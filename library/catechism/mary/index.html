Mary, Mother of God: fifteen questions

What the Church believes about Mary rests on what she believes about Christ, and in turn sheds light on him. Short answers in plain language, each with the paragraph of the Catechism of the Catholic Church where you can read further.

Why does the Church speak about Mary at all? CCC 487

Because what we believe about Mary is based on what we believe about Christ, and what we teach about her illumines our faith in him. Every truth about the Mother points to the Son.

How did God prepare for her? CCC 488–489

God chose from all eternity a daughter of Israel, a young Jewish woman of Nazareth, to be the mother of his Son, and he wanted her willing cooperation. Many holy women of the Old Covenant, from Eve to Sarah, Hannah, Deborah, Ruth, Judith and Esther, prepared for her mission.

What is the Immaculate Conception? CCC 490–492

From the first moment of her conception, by a singular grace of God and because of the merits of Christ, Mary was preserved from every stain of original sin. Pope Pius IX proclaimed this dogma in 1854. She too was redeemed by her Son, in a more exalted way.

Did Mary ever sin? CCC 493, 508

No. By the grace of God she remained without any personal sin all her life. The Fathers of the Eastern tradition call her the "All-Holy" (Panagia).

What was her "yes"? CCC 494, 511

At the Annunciation she answered with the obedience of faith: "Here am I, the servant of the Lord; let it be with me according to your word" (Luke 1:38). By that consent she became the mother of Jesus and gave herself wholly to the work of her Son. She cooperated in our salvation by faith and obedience, and so became the new Eve, mother of the living.

Why is she called Mother of God? CCC 495, 509

Because the one she conceived and bore as man is the eternal Son of the Father, the second Person of the Trinity, God himself. Elizabeth already greeted her as "the mother of my Lord" (Luke 1:43). The Council of Ephesus in 431 confessed her as Mother of God (Theotokos) (CCC 466).

What is the virginal conception? CCC 496–498

From the beginning the Church has confessed that Jesus was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit alone in the womb of the Virgin Mary, without a human father. It is a real event, not a legend, and a work of God beyond all human understanding, fulfilling Isaiah 7:14.

Why did God want his Son born of a virgin? CCC 502–506

It shows that the Incarnation is God's initiative: Jesus has only God as Father. He is the New Adam who begins a new creation. And Mary's virginity is the sign of her undivided faith and her whole gift of herself to God.

Did she remain a virgin? CCC 499, 510

Yes. The Church confesses Mary's real and perpetual virginity, even in giving birth to the Son of God. The liturgy honours her as the "Ever-virgin" (Aeiparthenos).

What about the "brothers and sisters" of Jesus? CCC 500–501

The Church has always understood these passages as not referring to other children of Mary. James and Joseph, called "brothers of Jesus", are sons of another Mary, a disciple of Christ. In the language of the Old Testament "brothers" can mean close relatives. Jesus is Mary's only son, but her spiritual motherhood reaches all whom he came to save.

Why is she called Mother of the Church? CCC 963–965

She is joined with her Son from his conception to his death, above all at the Cross, where Jesus gave her to the beloved disciple as his mother (John 19:26–27). After the Ascension she prayed with the Apostles for the Holy Spirit. Saint Paul VI, on 21 November 1964, proclaimed her "Mother of Christ, Mother of the Church".

What is the Assumption? CCC 966, 974

When the course of her earthly life was finished, Mary was taken up body and soul into heavenly glory. Pope Pius XII defined this in 1950. It is a singular share in her Son's Resurrection and a foretaste of ours. The Byzantine tradition keeps it as the Dormition, the "falling asleep" of the Mother of God, on 15 August.

Does Mary take anything away from Christ? CCC 968–970

No. Christ is the one Mediator. Mary's motherhood in the order of grace does not obscure or lessen his unique mediation but shows its power. All her influence flows from his merits, depends entirely on him and draws all its power from him.

Do Catholics worship Mary? CCC 971

No. The Church honours Mary with a special devotion, which differs in its very nature from the adoration given to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, and in fact helps that adoration. Her feasts and prayers such as the Rosary, called an "epitome of the whole Gospel", express this devotion.

How do we pray to her? CCC 2676–2679

The Hail Mary joins the angel's greeting and Elizabeth's blessing to our plea: "pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death". The Byzantine Churches pray the Akathistos and the Paraclesis, and the Syriac, Armenian and Coptic traditions sing their own hymns to her, such as those of Saint Ephrem and Saint Gregory of Narek. We can pray with Mary and to her, as the beloved disciple took her into his home.
