Jesus Christ: sixteen questions

Who is Jesus, why did he become man, why did he die, and what does his Resurrection mean for us? Short answers in plain language, each with the paragraph of the Catechism of the Catholic Church where you can read further.

What does the name "Jesus" mean? CCC 430

In Hebrew, Jesus means "God saves". The angel Gabriel gave him this name at the Annunciation. Since God alone can forgive sins, it is God who, in Jesus his eternal Son made man, saves his people from their sins.

What do "Christ" and "Lord" mean? CCC 436, 446

"Christ" is the Greek form of the Hebrew "Messiah", the Anointed One; in Israel, kings, priests and sometimes prophets were anointed for a mission from God, and Jesus fulfils all three. In the Greek Old Testament, God's holy name was rendered "Kyrios", Lord. When the New Testament calls Jesus "Lord", it recognises him as God himself.

In what sense is he the Son of God? CCC 441–445

Not as a title of honour only, as kings and holy people were sometimes called sons of God. Jesus is the only Son of the Father, eternally. Peter confessed it by a revelation from the Father, and the Resurrection showed it in power.

Why did the Son of God become man? CCC 456–460

For us and for our salvation: to reconcile us with God; so that we might know God's love; to be our model of holiness; and to make us "participants of the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4).

Is he God, or man, or something in between? CCC 464, 467, 469

He is true God and true man, not part God and part man, nor a mixture of the two. He became truly man while remaining truly God. The Council of Chalcedon (451) confessed one and the same Son, perfect in divinity and perfect in humanity.

Did Jesus have a human mind and will? CCC 470–475

Yes. He had a real human soul, with human knowledge that could grow, and a human will. The Third Council of Constantinople (681) confessed that he has two wills, divine and human, not opposed but in harmony: his human will follows his divine will.

Does he love each of us personally? CCC 478

Yes. Jesus knew and loved each one of us in his life, his agony and his Passion, and gave himself up for each of us. He has loved us with a human heart. That is why the Church honours his Sacred Heart as the great sign of his love.

Why do his hidden years and daily life matter? CCC 516, 520–521

Everything in his life, his words and deeds, his silences and sufferings, reveals the Father. He is our model in all of it, and he lets us live in him what he lived, so that we become one with him.

What were his miracles for? CCC 547–549

His miracles were signs that the kingdom of God was present in him and that the Father had sent him. They invite faith; they were not done to satisfy curiosity. He did not come to remove every evil on earth, but to release us from the worst slavery, which is sin.

Who is responsible for his death? CCC 597–598

The guilt of those who took part in his trial is known to God alone. The Church teaches that the Jewish people as a whole, then or now, cannot be held responsible. She teaches instead that all sinners caused his sufferings, and that Christians who sin bear the gravest responsibility.

Why did he die, and how does it save us? CCC 599–601, 613–615

His death was not an accident but part of God's plan, foretold in the Scriptures, without making those who handed him over mere puppets: "Christ died for our sins in accordance with the scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:3). It is the Paschal sacrifice and the sacrifice of the New Covenant, unique and surpassing all others. By his obedience to the end, out of love, he made amends for our disobedience and reconciled us to God.

Did he die for everyone? CCC 605, 616

Yes. God's love excludes no one. The Church teaches that there is not, never has been and never will be a single human being for whom Christ did not suffer. He knew and loved us all when he offered his life.

What does "he descended into hell" mean? CCC 632–635

"Hell" here means the realm of the dead (Sheol or Hades), not the hell of the damned. Jesus truly died and, in his soul, joined the dead. He went there as Saviour, to open heaven to the just who had gone before him.

Did he really rise from the dead? CCC 639–647

Yes. The Resurrection is a real event, with signs that were historically verified: the empty tomb and the appearances to many witnesses. No one saw the moment itself. It was not a return to earthly life, like that of Lazarus; Jesus rose in a glorified body that could no longer die.

What does the Resurrection mean for us? CCC 651–655

It confirms all that Christ did and taught, and it confirms the truth of his divinity. By his death he delivers us from sin; by his Resurrection he opens a new life for us. The risen Christ is the source of our own future resurrection.

What is the Ascension? CCC 659, 665–667

Forty days after Easter, Christ's humanity entered the glory of heaven, from where he will come again. He goes before us as our Head, so that we may hope to be with him for ever, and there he prays for us and sends us the Holy Spirit.

Sit with this

  1. He loved me with a human heart and gave himself up for me. Do I let that be personal?
  2. Which moment of his life do I most need to live in him today?
  3. How would my week look if I truly believed he is risen?
