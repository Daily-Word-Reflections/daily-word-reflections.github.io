Anointing of the Sick: thirteen questions

Christ the physician comes to the sick through the prayer and anointing of his Church. Each answer points to the paragraph of the Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC), and where the Eastern Churches have their own rule, to the Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches (CCEO).

What is this sacrament? CCC 1499, 1511

By the holy anointing of the sick and the prayer of the priests, the whole Church commends the sick to the suffering and glorified Lord, asking him to raise them up and save them. The Church believes that Christ instituted it as a true sacrament of the New Covenant, especially meant to strengthen those tried by illness.

Why does illness matter so much? CCC 1500–1501

In illness we feel our weakness and limits, and every serious illness lets us glimpse death. It can lead to anguish, turning in on oneself, even despair or revolt against God. It can also make a person more mature, helping to see what really matters, and very often it leads to a search for God.

How did Jesus treat the sick? CCC 1503–1505

His compassion and many healings were a sign that God had visited his people. He had power to heal and also to forgive sins: he came to heal the whole person, soul and body. He did not heal every sick person; his healings pointed to a deeper healing, his victory over sin and death by his Passion, in which he took our sufferings on himself.

Where is it found in Scripture? CCC 1506, 1510

Jesus sent the Twelve, who "anointed with oil many who were sick and cured them" (Mk 6:13). The apostolic Church had its own rite for the sick, described by St James: the sick are to call the elders of the Church, who pray over them and anoint them with oil in the name of the Lord, and the prayer of faith will save the sick (Jas 5:14-15).

Is it only for the dying? CCC 1512–1514

No. Over the centuries it came to be given almost only at the point of death, and so was called "Extreme Unction". The Church now teaches that it is not only for those about to die. As soon as anyone begins to be in danger of death from sickness or old age, the right time for it has certainly come.

Can it be received more than once? CCC 1515

Yes. If a sick person recovers and later falls seriously ill again, he can receive it again. If the illness grows worse, it may be repeated. It is fitting before a serious operation, and also for the elderly whose frailty becomes more marked.

Who may give it? CCC 1516; CCEO 739

Only priests, that is, bishops and presbyters. The Eastern Code says the same: all priests, and only priests, validly give it. Pastors should teach its value, and families should encourage the sick to call for a priest in good time.

Who may receive it? CCC 1514; CCEO 738, 740

The faithful who are seriously ill, or weak with old age. The Eastern Code asks pastors and those close to the sick to see that they receive it at the right time. It also presumes that the gravely ill who have lost consciousness or the use of reason would want it, so they may be anointed.

How is it celebrated? CCC 1513, 1517–1519; CCEO 737

It is a liturgy of the Church, at home, in hospital or in church, for one person or many, and very fittingly within the Eucharist. After the word of God and an act of repentance, the priests lay hands on the sick in silence, pray over them in the faith of the Church, and anoint them with oil blessed, if possible, by the bishop. In the Roman Rite the forehead and hands are anointed. Some Eastern Churches have the custom that several priests give it together, and the Eastern Code asks that this custom be kept where possible.

What does it do? CCC 1520, 1532

Its first grace is strength, peace and courage in serious illness or the frailty of old age, a gift of the Holy Spirit against discouragement and the fear of death. It forgives sins, if the sick person could not go to Confession. It can restore bodily health, if this helps the soul's salvation.

Does suffering have meaning? CCC 1521–1522

Yes. Through this sacrament the sick person is united more closely to Christ's Passion, and suffering takes on a new meaning: it becomes a share in Jesus' saving work. The sick who offer their suffering in this way contribute to the good of the whole People of God, while the Church, in the communion of saints, prays for them.

How does it prepare for death? CCC 1523

For those near the end of life it is also called the sacrament of those departing. It completes our conforming to the death and resurrection of Christ, begun in Baptism. It fortifies the end of our earthly life like a solid rampart for the final struggles before entering the Father's house.

What is Viaticum? CCC 1524–1525

Holy Communion given to one who is about to leave this life. Received at this moment of passing over to the Father, it is the seed of eternal life and the power of resurrection, for Jesus said that whoever eats his flesh and drinks his blood has eternal life (Jn 6:54). Penance, Anointing and the Eucharist as Viaticum together are the sacraments that prepare for our heavenly home.

Sit with this

  1. Is there someone sick or elderly near me who should be told about this sacrament now, not at the last hour?
  2. How do I carry my own pain: alone, or with Christ?
  3. Have I asked my family to call a priest for me when the time comes?
