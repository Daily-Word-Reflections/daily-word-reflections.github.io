Eucharistic miracles · Poland · 2013

Legnica

Church's position The Bishop of Legnica, Zbigniew Kiernikowski, announced on 10 April 2016 that the event "has the signs of a Eucharistic miracle" (Polish: ma znamiona cudu eucharystycznego). That is his wording, and it is careful. He said he had put the whole matter before the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith in January 2016, and that "according to the recommendations of the Holy See" a place should be prepared for veneration of the relic. Sources differ about how far the Holy See went (see "The Holy See's part" below). No text of a Vatican decision could be found when this page was written (November 2026). The relic has been venerated publicly since 2 July 2016. No Catholic is obliged to believe it.
  • Place today Shrine and parish of St Hyacinth (Święty Jacek), ul. Nadbrzeżna 3, Legnica, south-west Poland
  • Date Host fell 25 December 2013
  • What is kept A small red fragment separated from the Host, in a reliquary
  • Where In the shrine church; placed on the main altar in 2016
  • Anniversary Kept on or near 2 July; tenth year marked on 4 July 2026

A note on the name: the Polish c sounds like ts, so Legnica is said "leg-NEE-tsa". St Hyacinth is Święty Jacek in Polish, so some English reports call the church "St Jack's". Szczecin is said roughly "SHCHEH-cheen".

The account

The town and the church

Legnica is a town of about 100,000 people in Lower Silesia. It was German until 1945. Weekly Mass attendance in the diocese in 2016 was reported at 29.9 per cent, below the Polish average of 39.1, so it is a less churchgoing part of the country.

The church was finished in 1908 as a Protestant church, a memorial to Kaiser Friedrich. It is said that Red Army soldiers used it as a stable after they took the town in February 1945. The priest named to serve it in 1968, Fr Tadeusz Kisiński, became parish priest in 1972 and retired in 1997. The Diocese of Legnica was founded in 1992.

What happened

These are recent events, and the bishop's statement is the main witness. It was written over two years after the event. Small details vary between reports, as noted.

DateWhat is reported
25 Dec 2013At a Christmas Mass, about 8.45 in the morning by one account, a Host fell to the floor during Communion. The priest picked it up and put it in a vessel of water to dissolve, as the rules provide. Reports call it hot tap water or holy water. Bishop Kiernikowski's statement says only that it fell and was placed in water.

16 Jan 2014Bishop Cichy named a commission to watch and study it. Two days later, by one account, members went to Sokółka, where a like event had happened in 2008.
21 Jan 2014The diocese ordered scientific study.
Feb 2014A small fragment of the red material was separated and laid on a corporal. Samples went to research institutions.
June 2014Zbigniew Kiernikowski became Bishop of Legnica. He kept the commission in place.

10 Apr 2016The bishop's statement was read at Mass at St Hyacinth's. A press conference followed.

What the bishop's statement says and tells people to do

The statement describes the fall of the Host, the red marks, the commission and the samples. It then quotes the final report of the Department of Forensic Medicine. In a histopathological image, fragments of tissue were found with pieces of striated muscle. The whole picture is "most similar to the heart muscle", with changes that "often appear during the agony". Genetic research "indicates the human origin of the tissue".

The bishop then gives instructions. The parish priest is to:

  • prepare a suitable place to show the relic for veneration;
  • give information about it to those who come;
  • give regular teaching on Eucharistic worship;
  • start a book to record graces received and other such events.

The bishop asks for prayer. He speaks of an event with the signs of a miracle, and not of a defined miracle. The shrine's own page calls it both a "Eucharistic event" and a "Eucharistic miracle".

The Holy See's part: what each source says

This is the point most often overstated. Here is what each source actually says, in date order.

SourceWhat it says
Bishop's statement, April 2016He presented the matter to the Congregation in January. He orders a place for veneration "according to the recommendations of the Holy See". It reports no verdict.
PCh24, 12 April 2016The Congregation recommended a solemn announcement on 10 April. Fr Tadeusz Dąbski, judicial vicar of the diocese, is quoted: "we leave the final decision to the Holy See".
Radio Wrocław and WP, April 2016The Holy See recommended preparing a place for showing the relic. Fr Dąbski admits this is not a final proof, and more tests were planned.
National Catholic Register, July 2016The bishop says a decision of the Congregation is what must come next before any official declaration of a supernatural event. No Vatican verdict is reported.
Gość Niedzielny Legnica, March 2018Fr Ziombra: it is not for him to rule on a miracle, but to gather reports from the faithful. The title of miracle is said to belong to the Holy See.
Catholic News Agency, June 2020In 2016 the Congregation approved veneration of the Host. The Vatican is still weighing the matter, and the parish will send evidence of healings, conversions and pilgrimages to inform a final statement.
Niedziela, May and July 2026The Congregation found the event "has the signs of a Eucharistic miracle". Later: the relic was shown "after the Holy See approved the event". Neither piece cites a document.

Some other reports say that Rome recognised a supernatural event, or that the bishop officially declared a miracle. No published document that says so could be found. The safest statement is this. The bishop acted, saying he did so on the Holy See's recommendation, and veneration is allowed. Whether Rome has given a final judgment on the event itself cannot be confirmed from anything public.

The laboratory reports, and what critics say

No one has published the full reports in a scientific journal, as far as could be found. What we have comes through the bishop's statement, the press, and a devotional book of 2017 edited by Irena Chłopkowska, Bóg przemówił w Legnicy. None of this is proof. It is what named people reported, and they do not all agree.

Wrocław

The commission first went to the Department of Forensic Medicine of the Medical University of Wrocław, in January 2014 according to Niedziela. Reports of the result differ:

  • Niedziela (2026) and the National Catholic Register (2016) say that fungal and bacterial origin were excluded.
  • WP and Radio Wrocław (2016) say that Wrocław was unsure of the cause of the colour, or did not find human origin, and so the material went elsewhere.
  • Prof. Tadeusz Dobosz, who took part, said in a 2016 interview that his own genetic tests found no human DNA, and that any such trace would likely come from handling. He said the parish priest sought a second opinion because he found the Wrocław results unsatisfactory.
  • Dr Barbara Engel, a cardiologist on the commission, said that some images showed fibres resembling heart muscle. By 2016 she was calling it a miracle that secular terms cannot explain.
Szczecin

The material went on to the Department of Forensic Medicine of the Pomeranian Medical University in Szczecin. The names of those who signed have not been made public, as far as could be found. WP reports the use of ultraviolet light. It says they judged the fibres very like heart muscle and the tissue around them connective tissue, and that the fragmentation may point to a moment of agony.

The final report is the one the bishop quotes (see above). It says "most similar", not "is". Reports of the genetic test differ too. The book of 2017 speaks of mitochondrial human DNA. The Catholic News Agency in 2020 quotes the parish priest that a fragment of DNA sequence was found, and that saying whose it is would need more work. Blood group was not tested, as the claim is of heart tissue and not of blood.

What critics say
  • Martyna Franczuk, a biotechnologist at the Medical University of Gdańsk, said in 2016 that Serratia marcescens is the most likely cause, as flour and water are good ground for it. She found it hard to believe that an experienced pathologist would confuse bacteria with the much larger human cells. She asked the curia for details and, at the time of the report, had no answer.
  • Prof. Dobosz, as above, holds that the human DNA was contamination.
  • Mark Woods, writing in Christian Today in 2016, thought fraud or honest mistake more likely than a miracle. He accused no one in particular.
  • The 2025 study. Tomasz Grzybowski, Prof. Dobosz and colleagues (Applied Microbiology and Biotechnology) tested 25 cases from Poland, Germany, the United States and South Africa, all ordered by Church officials. They do not name the cases, so we cannot say if Legnica is among them. They found no proof of human blood, and no human material beyond single skin cells and blood cells, likely stray contamination. In most cases the structures were fungi and microbes of decay. They cite the Legnica book as an example of partial results spread in a popular form, and they say procedures were skipped or results ignored in some public cases, leading to hasty conclusions.
  • Fairness to the other side. The same paper says that in two cases it could not explain structures resembling heart muscle. It finds Serratia in only two cases, and calls the easy line "There are no miracles, there are Serratia marcescens" an unproven belief. The authors say the decision about any sign belongs to the Church's authorities.
  • Dr Maciej Krzywosz, a scholar of miracle claims at the University of Białystok, notes that the Church examines miracles in detail mainly in causes of saints, and leaves events like this to local bishops. He says that recognition may take a very long time, and pilgrimage has religious meaning either way.
  • Sociologists. Anna Królikowska and Tomasz Kalniuk have written on Sokółka and Legnica as new sacred places in a secularising Poland, without judging what happened.

What is known

A Host fell on Christmas Day 2013 and was placed in water. A red mark appeared. A commission was named in January 2014, and two forensic departments examined samples. The bishop's statement was read on 10 and 17 April 2016. The relic has been venerated since 2 July 2016.

What is less certain

What the red material is. The two laboratories did not plainly agree. Whether the DNA came from the sample or from handling. The full reports have not been published. Exactly what the Holy See said, as no text is public. Reported healings and conversions come from the parish and have not been independently checked.

How the Church has honoured it

  • Two bishops took part: Stefan Cichy, who ordered the study, and Zbigniew Kiernikowski, who made it public.
  • The liturgy of 2 July 2016 at the cathedral and shrine began with expiation, which suits an event that began with a fallen Host.
  • Perpetual adoration has gone on without a break since 1 December 2018, in a chapel with its own door on the river side.
  • A monthly service of reparation for offences against the Eucharist is held on first Fridays.
  • A thanksgiving for ten years of public veneration was held on 4 July 2026.

No papal visit or indulgence connected with it could be found.

The fruits

In March 2018 the local Gość Niedzielny reported more than ten thousand visitors since 2016, with over 700 organised groups, some from Vietnam, Singapore and Taiwan. It also reported about 10,000 prayer requests, read out before Masses, and a board with photographs of over 800 people. In 2026 Niedziela said that over 200 people share in the adoration, with pilgrims from North America, Singapore and the Philippines.

Fr Ziombra tells of sudden conversions. One is of a man hostile to the Church all his life who, after about 50 years, made his confession and received Communion and is now devout. These are the words of a pastor, and they are not independently checked. He reflects that the image of a dying heart speaks of the sacrifice of Jesus. As the parish priest also says, it is not his to rule on the miracle.

If you visit

The shrine is at ul. Nadbrzeżna 3, 59-220 Legnica. It is about 80 kilometres from Wrocław, the nearest large airport, with frequent trains. Its page lists these hours: weekdays 6.30 to 19.00, Saturday 8.15 to 19.00, Sunday 7.00 to 19.00. You may pray before the relic during those hours, and please stay still during Mass. The adoration chapel is open day and night. Groups should write ahead, and the parish will arrange a talk. See the shrine's page for pilgrims, in Polish.

What the Church teaches

Our faith rests on the words of Christ and not on any miracle, test or laboratory. At every Mass and Qurbana, Christ is truly present in the Eucharist, whether or not there is any sign (CCC 1374 to 1376). Christ is no less present in the Host on any altar near you than in anything that could be shown in a reliquary. No one is obliged to believe in the Legnica event. A careful person may pray there, or may wait, and remain a faithful Catholic either way.
