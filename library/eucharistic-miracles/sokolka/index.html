Eucharistic miracles · Poland · 2008

Sokółka

Church's position There is a local Church act and no published judgment of the Holy See. On 14 October 2009 the Metropolitan Curia of Białystok reported the findings of a commission named by Archbishop Edward Ozorowski. It said that the Host examined was the same Host, that no outside hand was found, and that the event does not oppose the faith of the Church but confirms it. The papers went to the Apostolic Nunciature in Warsaw. In 2018 Archbishop Tadeusz Wojda said that for now the word is "event", that he personally believes it to be a miracle, and that only the Holy See can decide. In 2023 the Polish Catholic news agency still described the matter as under study in Rome. No later decision of the Holy See could be found when this page was written (November 2026). No Catholic is obliged to believe it.
  • Place today Collegiate church and Shrine of the Blessed Sacrament, Grodzieńska 47, Sokółka, north-east Poland, Archdiocese of Białystok
  • Date Host fell 12 October 2008; red mark seen 19 October 2008
  • What is kept A fragment of the Host with a dried red mark, on a corporal, in a reliquary
  • Where Chapel of Our Lady of the Rosary, in the church, since 2 October 2011
  • Celebration Anniversary held in early October; in 2011, 2018 and 2023 it fell on the first Sunday

A note on the name: the Polish letter ł sounds like an English w, so the town is said roughly "so-KOOW-ka". Białystok is said "bya-WIS-tok".

The account

The town and the church

Sokółka is a small town about 40 kilometres north-east of Białystok, near the border with Belarus. It is a meeting place of peoples: Catholics, Orthodox and Muslims live there, with Belarusian and Tatar minorities.

The church of St Anthony of Padua was built between 1840 and 1848 and consecrated on 9 June 1850. It was enlarged from 1901 to 1904 and has an unusual plan of five naves.

Blessed Michał Sopoćko, the confessor of St Faustina, had been beatified in Białystok on 28 September 2008, two weeks before these events.

What happened, day by day

Everything here rests on the witnesses in the parish and on the curia's statement. The earliest public text is that statement, a year after the event. It is not a late legend. It is a recent event with named people, and we can read how it was reported from the start.

DateWhat is reported
Sun 12 Oct 2008At the Mass of about 8.30 in the morning, a priest giving Communion dropped a consecrated Host on the altar step. He picked it up and, as the rules provide, put it in the vasculum, the small vessel of water kept beside the tabernacle, so that it would dissolve. English Wikipedia names the priest as the vicar, Fr Jacek Ingielewicz.
After that MassSister Julia Dubowska, a Eucharistic Sister and the sacristan, was told by the parish priest, Canon Stanisław Gniedziejko, to pour the contents into another vessel and to lock it in the sacristy safe. Only she and the parish priest had keys.
Sun 19 Oct 2008Asked by the parish priest how the Host was, Sister Julia opened the safe. She noticed a slight smell of unleavened bread. In clear, uncoloured water she saw the Host dissolving, and on it a raised, deep red mark like a clot of blood. She called the parish priest. He came with the other priests and a visiting missionary, Fr Ryszard Górowski. They agreed to be discreet.
Following daysArchbishop Edward Ozorowski, the Metropolitan of Białystok, was told at once. He came with the chancellor of the curia, Fr Andrzej Kakareko, and others, and ordered that the Host be kept safe and watched.
29 Oct 2008The vessel was moved to the tabernacle of the Divine Mercy chapel in the rectory.
30 Oct 2008By the Archbishop's decision, the Host with its mark was taken out of the water, laid on a small corporal and put back in the tabernacle.
7 January 2009A small sample was taken for examination (see "What the two pathologists reported" below).
Mid-January 2009According to the shrine, the fragment had dried and stayed in the form of a clot. It says the look has not changed since.

For about a year nothing was made public. The curia's statement of 14 October 2009 made it known. Then the Polish and world press took it up, under the word "miracle", a word the statement itself did not use as a verdict.

The commission and the statement of 14 October 2009

On 30 March 2009 Archbishop Ozorowski named a Church commission. It questioned the witnesses and studied the doctors' findings. The statement, signed by the chancellor, gave these conclusions:

  • The Host from which the sample was taken was the same Host moved to the rectory chapel.
  • Third party intervention was not found.
  • The two pathologists jointly reported that the sample looks like heart muscle tissue, the living tissue it most resembles.
  • The files were sent to the Apostolic Nunciature in Warsaw.
  • The event does not oppose the faith of the Church but confirms it. The Church believes that the words of consecration, by the power of the Holy Spirit, change the bread into the Body of Christ.
  • It is a call to ministers to give Communion with faith and attention, and to the faithful to receive with reverence.

Read this carefully. The statement does not use the formula "this is a miracle". Some later articles say that the commission confirmed a supernatural character. The English text of the statement does not say this in those words. A 2026 article in the Polish weekly Niedziela says the event was approved at diocesan level. The church speaks of a "Eucharistic event".

The tests, and what critics say

These are the reports of named people. None of them is a proof of anything about the Body of Christ, for no microscope can see that (see the last box on this page).

What the two pathologists reported

Who: Prof. Maria Elżbieta Sobaniec-Łotowska and Prof. Stanisław Sulkowski, pathologists at the Medical University of Białystok. Who asked: the Archbishop. When: the sample was taken on 7 January 2009, and the work ended later that month.

How: according to later accounts, by light microscope and transmission electron microscope. An exhibition panel on Eucharistic miracles says that Prof. Sulkowski did not know the sample came from a Host. It also says that the two worked independently and agreed.

What: the curia's words are that the sample looks like heart muscle tissue. Later accounts by the doctors and their supporters go further. They speak of human heart tissue from a living person near death, with fibres of the tissue woven into the bread. A 2018 article in the Polish journal Teologia i Człowiek, with both pathologists among the authors, repeats this. It should be said plainly that the curia's statement is more careful than these later words.

Published? The original report was not published in an international scientific journal, as far as could be found. No DNA test has been reported.

What the critics say
  • The university. On 30 October 2009 the Polish science news service reported that the Medical University of Białystok distanced itself from the results. Its spokesman, Prof. Lech Chyczewski, head of the department, said the work was done "quietly", outside official channels, and was never ordered or registered by the university. He did not reject the diagnosis outright, but he said the doctors had an emotional approach to faith, and that pathology cannot say whose tissue it is.
  • Skeletal or heart muscle. The daily Rzeczpospolita (27 October 2009) reported Prof. Chyczewski's view that, after talking with Prof. Sulkowski, the tissue seemed more like skeletal than heart muscle. Prof. Sulkowski answered that no one should give such an opinion without examining it. Prof. Sobaniec-Łotowska said no one had told her of any doubts. The pathologists protested publicly against what they called false claims by people who had never seen the material.
  • No DNA test. Prof. Chyczewski proposed molecular tests, and Prof. Karol Śliwka's forensic department in Bydgoszcz offered to do them. The Archbishop declined. By one report he said that such matter, if held to be the Body of Christ, cannot be cut and tested. A priest and theologian, Fr Prof. Marian Rusecki, said the tests should have gone on, to answer charges of manipulation.
  • A natural cause. Dr Paweł Grzesiowski proposed the bacterium Serratia marcescens, which makes a red pigment and grows on bread. No test for it on this Host has been reported. Against this, the pathologists said the material they saw was not a bacterium.
  • A 2024 experiment. Kelly Kearse and Frank Ligaj of Knoxville Catholic High School in the United States published a paper in the Journal of Forensic Science and Research. They put unconsecrated wafers in water, and about 15 per cent grew red areas, caused by a fungus, Epicoccum. They point out that the water at Sokółka stayed clear, which they find odd if blood were involved. They did not test the Sokółka Host. They say plainly that they are not calling Eucharistic miracles false, and that faith and science need not conflict.
  • A 2025 study. Tomasz Grzybowski and colleagues (Applied Microbiology and Biotechnology) tested 25 cases of changed Hosts from Poland, Germany, the United States and South Africa, all at the request of Church officials. They do not name the cases, so we cannot say whether Sokółka is among them. They found no proof of human blood in any case, and no human material beyond single skin cells and blood cells, likely stray contamination. They found pigment-making microbes in several. In two cases they could not explain structures resembling heart muscle, and left them open. They also criticise the easy line "There are no miracles, there are Serratia marcescens" as an unproven belief, and say the decision about any sign belongs to the Church's authorities and not to them.
  • The authorities. A rationalist association asked for a criminal inquiry, including whether human remains were involved. The prosecutor in Sokółka closed the matter, as no evidence of a crime was found.

What is known

A Host fell on 12 October 2008 and went into water. A red mark was seen a week later. It was kept under lock and key. Two named pathologists examined a sample at the Archbishop's request. A commission reported in 2009. The Host has been shown publicly since 2 October 2011. The files went to Rome, by way of the nunciature.

What is less certain

What the red material is. Whether it is human, as no DNA test has been reported. Whether it is heart or other muscle, as one colleague doubted. Whether a natural cause was ruled out. What Rome will say, if it says anything. Reports of healings have not been independently checked.

How the Church has honoured it

  • 9 April 2009, Holy Thursday. The church was raised to a collegiate church, with a Collegiate Chapter of the Blessed Sacrament of priests of the archdiocese. Its task is care for Eucharistic worship.
  • 2 October 2011. The relic was carried from the rectory chapel to the chapel of Our Lady of the Rosary in the church. Archbishop Ozorowski led the field Mass. Thousands of pilgrims came from Poland, Lithuania and Belarus. He told them that for God nothing is impossible.
  • 25 March 2017. The Shrine of the Blessed Sacrament was established.
  • 2018. Ten years on, Archbishop Wojda ordered a Eucharistic novena on first Sundays in the archdiocese, ending at Sokółka on 7 October with a field Mass. He said then that the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith had not yet answered.
  • Today. The archdiocese keeps an Archdiocesan Day of the Eucharist in October, and in June a day of prayer for first communicants and priests.

No papal act or visit connected with this event could be found, and no indulgence attached to it.

The fruits

The parish priest said in 2016 and 2018 that about a thousand organised groups come each year, and pilgrims from Europe, China, the United States and Australia. A report of 2023 gives a firmer number for the year before: 460 groups, over 15,000 pilgrims in them, and about as many on their own. Two volumes of testimonies of graces have been printed. The shrine's web page lists accounts of healing from cancer, rescue from accidents and peace of heart. These are the words of those who sent them, and the page gives no medical papers.

The clearest fruit is the simplest. The statement of 2009 turned the event toward care at the altar. Priests are to give Communion with attention, and people are to receive with reverence. There is prayer before the Blessed Sacrament at the shrine, which anyone can share without settling the laboratory questions.

If you visit

The church is at ul. Grodzieńska 47, 16-100 Sokółka. Most travellers go by train from Warsaw through Białystok, about three hours in all. The relic is in the chapel of Our Lady of the Rosary. Times of Mass and adoration are on the shrine's web page in Polish. Please check there, as they change.

What the Church teaches

Our faith rests on the words of Christ and not on any miracle, test or laboratory. At every Mass and Qurbana, Christ is truly present in the Eucharist, whether or not there is any sign (CCC 1374 to 1376). If the red mark on the Host of Sokółka were shown tomorrow to have a natural cause, nothing in the faith would change. If the Holy See were to approve it, nothing would be added to the faith either. No one is obliged to believe in this event. Everyone is invited to the altar.
