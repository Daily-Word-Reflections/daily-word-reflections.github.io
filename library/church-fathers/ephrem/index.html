St Ephrem the Syrian

TimeAbout 306 to 373
WhereNisibis (today Nusaybin, Turkey), then Edessa (today Şanlıurfa, Turkey)
What he wasDeacon, teacher, poet and hymn writer
LanguageSyriac
Known asHarp of the Spirit
HonouredDoctor of the Church, named by Pope Benedict XV in 1920
His life

Ephrem was born at Nisibis and served there as a deacon and teacher, in the time of Bishop Jacob of Nisibis. In 363 the city passed to Persia, and many Christians left. He settled in Edessa, where he taught for his last ten years.

Tradition says that he died in 373 after caring for the sick during an epidemic in the city.

What he taught
  • God is beyond our definitions. We know him through what he has revealed, in Scripture and in creation.
  • Christ is truly God and truly man. Ephrem defends the faith of Nicaea against the errors of his day.
  • Mary. He sings of her as the Mother of the Lord, with great tenderness.
  • The Eucharist and Baptism. His hymns dwell on the holy mysteries as the place where we meet Christ.
How he taught

He taught through song. His teaching hymns (madrashe) and verse homilies (memre) were written for the assembly, and tradition says he trained choirs of women to sing them. He teaches through images and paradox: the Lord who holds all creation becomes small enough to be held by his mother.

His works

Hymns

On the Faith · On the Nativity · On Paradise · On Virginity · On Unleavened Bread · On the Church · Nisibene Hymns · Against Heresies · Against Julian

Scripture commentaries

On Genesis · On Exodus · On the Diatessaron (the Gospels woven into one account)

Prose and homilies

Homily on Our Lord · Prose Refutations

Why he matters today

He shows that theology can be prayed and sung. He is a father of the Syriac tradition shared by the Syro-Malabar and Syro-Malankara Churches, and is honoured in the Latin Church as well.
