Marian apparitions · France · 1858

Our Lady of Lourdes

Approval status Approved by the local bishop, Bertrand-Sévère Laurence of Tarbes, in a pastoral letter of 18 January 1862, after a commission of inquiry. He wrote that the Immaculate Mary, Mother of God, really appeared to Bernadette Soubirous, and he authorised devotion at the grotto. The feast was later extended to the whole Latin Church (1907). This judgment came long before the 2024 norms of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, under which the Church now usually gives a pastoral conclusion and not a declaration of supernatural origin.
  • Seer St Bernadette Soubirous, aged 14
  • Dates 18 appearances, 11 February to 16 July 1858
  • Place Grotto of Massabielle, Lourdes, Hautes-Pyrénées, France
  • Feast 11 February; St Bernadette, 16 April in most places

What happened

Bernadette and her family

All that we know of the apparitions comes through one witness. "She alone saw the Lady," says the Sanctuary, "and all depends on her testimony." So we start with her.

Bernadette Soubirous was born on 7 January 1844 at the Boly Mill in Lourdes, a market town at the foot of the Pyrenees. She was the first child of François Soubirous, a miller, and Louise Castérot, who had married for love. The Sanctuary stresses that her first ten years were happy and that this gave her a strong and balanced nature, in spite of what came next.

Then the family lost the mill and fell into real poverty. By 1858 they were living in one wretched room known as the Cachot. Bernadette was small, about 1.40 metres tall, and she had asthma. She helped at home and at her aunt's. She had scarcely been to school, and the abstract words of the catechism would not stay in her memory. At 14 she could not read or write, and she had not yet made her First Communion. She had been sent to the village of Bartrès in September 1857, and came home on 21 January 1858 because she wanted to prepare for it. She spoke the local dialect of the Pyrenees, not French.

She was lively, quick and honest, and sometimes proud and touchy. People who met her noticed her common sense.

The eighteen appearances, day by day

The dates and details below follow the Sanctuary's own list. The numbers of people are the Sanctuary's.

Date, 1858What happened
1. Thu 11 FebBernadette goes with her sister Toinette and a friend, Jeanne Abadie, to gather dead wood and bones by the river Gave. Taking off her stockings to cross a stream, she hears a noise like a gust of wind and looks up. "I saw a lady dressed in white," with a white veil, a blue belt, and a yellow rose on each foot. She makes the Sign of the Cross and says the Rosary with the Lady, who then vanishes.
2. Sun 14 FebHer parents have forbidden her to go, but her mother gives way. She sprinkles holy water towards the Lady, who smiles and bows her head.
3. Thu 18 FebThe Lady speaks for the first time. Bernadette offers pen and paper and asks her to write her name. She answers: "It is not necessary." She adds that she does not promise to make Bernadette happy in this world, but in the other, and asks: "Would you be kind enough to come here for a fortnight?"
4. Fri 19 FebBernadette comes with a blessed lighted candle. The Sanctuary traces the custom of candles at the grotto to this.
5. Sat 20 FebThe Lady teaches her a personal prayer. At the end Bernadette is overcome with great sadness.
6. Sun 21 FebVery early, about a hundred people are present. Afterwards the police commissioner, Jacomet, questions her. She will speak only of "Aquerò", "that thing" in dialect.
7. Tue 23 FebAbout 150 people. The Lady gives her a secret "only for her alone".
8. Wed 24 Feb"Penance! Penance! Penance! Pray to God for sinners. Kiss the ground as an act of penance for sinners!"
9. Thu 25 FebAbout 300 people. She is told to drink at the spring and wash there. She finds only muddy water, scrapes the ground, and at the fourth try can drink. She eats bitter herbs. The crowd thinks she has gone mad. "It is for sinners," she says. In the following days the spring grows and runs clear.
10. Sat 27 FebAbout 800 people. The Lady is silent. Bernadette drinks and does her acts of penance.
11. Sun 28 FebOver a thousand people. She prays, kisses the ground and moves on her knees. Afterwards Judge Ribes threatens her with prison.
12. Mon 1 MarAbout 1,500 people, and for the first time a priest. In the night Catherine Latapie, from Loubajac, dips her injured arm and hand in the spring and recovers their movement. This is the first of the healings later recognised.
13. Tue 2 Mar"Go and tell the priests that people are to come here in procession and to build a chapel here." The parish priest, Father Peyramale, wants one thing: her name. He asks also that the wild rose bush flower in winter.
14. Wed 3 MarAbout 3,000 people at 7 in the morning, but no vision. After school she goes back and sees the Lady, who answers her question with a smile.
15. Thu 4 MarAbout 8,000 people wait for a miracle at the end of the fortnight. The Lady is silent. No miracle comes. For twenty days Bernadette does not go back.
16. Thu 25 MarThe feast of the Annunciation. She asks the name again. The Lady stretches her arms toward the ground, joins them as in prayer, and says in dialect: "Que soy era Immaculada Councepciou", "I am the Immaculate Conception." The rose bush does not flower. Bernadette runs to the priest, repeating words she does not understand.
17. Wed 7 AprShe keeps her candle lit, and the flame licks her hand without burning it. Dr Dozous sees this.
18. Fri 16 JulThe feast of Our Lady of Mount Carmel. The grotto is closed off by a barrier, so she goes to the far bank of the Gave. "I saw only the Blessed Virgin," she says, "and she was more beautiful than ever!"

Bernadette did not call the Lady the Blessed Virgin at first. She said "Aquerò". When Jacomet wrote "the Holy Virgin", she corrected him: she had not said that.

Why the name mattered

Pope Pius IX had defined the Immaculate Conception on 8 December 1854: by a unique grace of God, in view of the merits of Christ, Mary was kept from all stain of original sin from the first moment of her existence. Bernadette, who had not had her First Communion, did not know the phrase. The Sanctuary says the words troubled the parish priest, Father Peyramale. It was now clear that the Lady had given a name the girl herself could not explain.

Questioning and opposition

Bernadette faced the police commissioner, and a judge who threatened prison. Later she had to answer civil and religious commissions, journalists and crowds. She was not moved by threats or by offers of money. "I'm charged with telling you, not with making you believe," she said. She accepted no payment and refused to bless rosaries. Of pictures of herself sold for ten sous she said: "Ten sous, that's all I'm worth!"

The grotto itself was closed. Common accounts date the barrier to 8 June 1858, by order of the local authorities acting for the prefect. They say the Emperor Napoleon III ordered it reopened in early October 1858. We have these dates from secondary accounts, not from the Sanctuary's own pages. The Sanctuary's list confirms the barrier on 16 July.

The Church's inquiry

Bishop Laurence moved slowly. On 28 July 1858 he set up a commission of inquiry. His final judgment drew on Bernadette's testimony and on the cures linked to the water. The mayor, Anselme Lacadé, had the water analysed in 1858. The Catholic Encyclopedia reports that an official chemist found no natural healing properties in it.

The bishop waited almost four years. His pastoral letter of 18 January 1862 weighs Bernadette herself: "the simplicity, the candour, the modesty of this child". He asks whether she could have been deceived or hallucinating, and he finds no sign of it. He notes that the visions did not depend on her wishes, since twice in the fortnight she went and the Lady did not come. An extract is engraved in marble in the Basilica of the Immaculate Conception.

Bernadette after the apparitions

Life in the Cachot had become impossible, with crowds always at the door. She made her First Communion on 3 June 1858, between the apparitions. On 15 July 1860 she was received at the hospice of the Sisters of Nevers in Lourdes "as a sick poor person". There, at 16, she learned to read and write. She nursed the sick and looked for her vocation. "My mission in Lourdes is finished," she said.

On 4 July 1866 she left Lourdes for good, and she reached the motherhouse at Nevers three days later. She told her story once to the assembled sisters, and then was not to speak of it. She took the name Sister Marie Bernard, and made her profession in October 1867. "I came here to hide myself," she said. She served in the infirmary and the sacristy until her own illness took over. The bishop had said her work would be prayer, and she took this seriously. She wrote to the Pope that her weapons were prayer and sacrifice. She never returned to the grotto.

She died at Nevers on 16 April 1879, aged 35, after long illness.

The main messages

18 FebShe asks Bernadette to come for a fortnight, and tells her she does not promise happiness in this world, but in the next.
24 Feb"Penance! Penance! Penance!" A call to pray for sinners and to kiss the ground for them.
25 FebShe tells Bernadette to drink at the spring and wash there. Bernadette digs in the mud, and the spring begins to flow. "It is for sinners."
2 MarShe asks the priests to build a chapel, and for people to come in procession.
25 MarShe gives her name: "I am the Immaculate Conception."

In one line

Conversion, prayer and penance, with a special love for the poor and the sick.

Nothing new in the faith

These messages repeat the Gospel's call to repent and believe. They add no new teaching. The name she gave repeats a doctrine the Church had already defined.

How the Church sees it

JudgmentBishop Laurence's pastoral letter of 18 January 1862: "We judge that the Immaculate Mary, Mother of God, really appeared." He authorised devotion at the grotto and called for a shrine, built by gifts of the faithful.
FeastLeo XIII granted a proper Office and Mass in 1892, as Pius XII recalls, and St Pius X extended the feast to the whole Latin Church on 13 November 1907. It is kept on 11 February in the Roman calendar.
Pius IXHe supported the shrine. A statue was crowned on 3 July 1876, by his legate Cardinal Meglia.
St Pius XHe stressed the bond between Lourdes and the Eucharist: people are drawn there by Mary "to adore Jesus Christ in the august Sacrament".
Pius XI, Pius XIIPius XI had come as a pilgrim before his election. He beatified Bernadette on 14 June 1925, canonised her on 8 December 1933, and ended the Jubilee of the Redemption at the grotto in 1935, with Cardinal Pacelli as his legate. As Pope, Pacelli wrote the encyclical Le pèlerinage de Lourdes (2 July 1957) for the centenary.
St John XXIIIAs Cardinal Roncalli he dedicated the great underground basilica of St Pius X in 1958.
Papal visitsSt John Paul II came as Pope in 1983 and 2004. Benedict XVI came in September 2008 for the 150th anniversary. Leo XIV came on 26 and 27 September 2026: he prayed in the grotto, drank at the spring, walked in the torchlight procession, and said Mass before a crowd reported at about 150,000.
World Day of the SickSt John Paul II established it in 1992, to be kept each year on 11 February.
The seerBernadette's holiness was judged apart from the visions. The Sanctuary says plainly that the Church proclaimed her a saint "not for having been chosen for the Apparitions, but for the way in which she responded to that grace".
Healings: how they are judged

The Church is slow here. Most people who go to Lourdes come home with the same illness, and many say they received something else: peace, strength, the grace to carry it.

The numbers. The Sanctuary says that more than 7,000 cases have been studied by the Medical Bureau, and 72 have been declared miraculous by the Church. The most recent is Antonietta Raco, an Italian woman with primary lateral sclerosis, cured after a pilgrimage in 2009, whose healing was proclaimed by Bishop Vincenzo Orofino of Tursi-Lagonegro on 16 April 2025.

The process. The Medical Bureau at Lourdes dates from 1883. Any doctor may take part, whatever their nationality or belief. Only a few of the cases it opens are studied in depth. Those go to the International Medical Committee of Lourdes, about twenty specialists of different nations. They ask whether the cure was unexpected, complete and lasting, and whether it is unexplained in the present state of medical knowledge. The study often takes many years.

The last step. Doctors do not declare miracles. They can only say a cure is unexplained. The bishop of the diocese of the person healed then judges whether it should be called a miracle, a sign from God.

The water. Analysis has found no natural healing power in it, and the Church does not teach that it has any power of its own. It is a sign, used with faith and prayer. Many who were cured never bathed in it, as the Catholic Encyclopedia notes.

Critics. Sceptics have long doubted these cures, and some argue that an unexplained cure simply awaits a future explanation. The method allows for this by setting the medical question apart from the judgment of faith. No Catholic is bound to accept any single healing.

Other questions honestly faced

Bernadette's body. Her body was exhumed in 1909, 1919 and 1925, during the process for her cause. The doctors' reports describe it as well preserved, though not unchanged: Dr Comte in 1919 wrote of parts as "practically mummified". For the 1925 exposition, the face and hands were covered with wax. It rests in a glass reliquary in the chapel at Nevers. The Church did not canonise her for this.

The statue. Joseph-Hugues Fabisch made the statue in the grotto, dedicated on 4 April 1864. It shows a taller and older figure than the one Bernadette described.

The rose bush. The sign Father Peyramale asked for was never given. That is worth remembering. The Lady gave her name, not a show.

The fruits

  • Pilgrims. By the end of 1908, 5,297 organised pilgrimages with 4,919,000 pilgrims had been recorded (Catholic Encyclopedia). The Sanctuary gives 3.5 million in 2019, 800,000 in 2020, 1.5 million in 2021, 3.2 million in 2024 and almost 4 million in 2025. Press reports put the sick and disabled at about 60,000 a year.
  • Care of the sick. The Sanctuary has accommodation and special facilities for the sick and for people with disabilities, such as the Accueil Notre-Dame. Volunteers called hospitaliers care for them.
  • The processions. The torchlight Marian procession and the Blessed Sacrament procession answer the Lady's request for people to come in procession. Pius XII and St Pius X both saw Mary here leading people to the Eucharist.
  • The buildings. The Crypt, opened at Pentecost 1866; the Basilica of the Immaculate Conception, consecrated in 1876; the Basilica of the Rosary, consecrated in 1901; and the underground Basilica of St Pius X, 1958, which holds about 25,000. In all there are 3 basilicas and 25 churches and chapels. The grotto itself stays bare.
  • Penance. Bernadette's "It is for sinners" still stands behind every pilgrimage, and the call to penance leads pilgrims to the sacrament of Reconciliation.
  • Grottoes round the world. Copies of the grotto stand in parishes everywhere, including one in the Vatican Gardens, where Leo XIV prayed on 11 February 2026.

If you visit

Lourdes is in the Hautes-Pyrénées in south-west France. It has its own railway station, and the Tarbes-Lourdes-Pyrénées airport is nearby. Walk to the grotto, touch the rock, take water at the taps, light a candle. Also see the places of Bernadette's life in the town, such as the Cachot and the Boly Mill. At Nevers, in central France, you can pray at her body at Espace Bernadette. The Sanctuary's website is lourdes-france.com.

Read it rightly

Approved apparitions are private revelations. The Catechism teaches that no new public revelation is to be expected before Christ's return, and that private revelations do not improve or complete the deposit of faith. They may help people live it more fully in a certain time (CCC 66 to 67). No Catholic is obliged to believe in Lourdes. It sends us back to what we already have: penance, prayer, the Eucharist, and love of the sick.
