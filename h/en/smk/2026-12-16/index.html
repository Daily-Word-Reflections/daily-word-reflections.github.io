Word for the day

“but he said to me, “My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness.” So, I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may dwell in me.” 2 Corinthians 12:9

Reflection

Jesus, after praying alone, asks his disciples two questions. First: “Who do the crowds say that I am?” They answer: John the Baptist, Elijah, one of the ancient prophets. Then the decisive one: “But who do you say that I am?” Peter answers, “The Messiah of God.” Opinions about Jesus are many; each of us must give our own answer. Christmas puts the same question to every person who looks at the child in the manger.

Paul tells of “a thorn in the flesh” that he begged the Lord three times to remove. The answer was not removal but grace: “My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness.” So Paul boasts of his weaknesses, “for whenever I am weak, then I am strong.” God chose to come to us as a weak infant; he still meets us in our weakness, not only in our strength.

Homily outline

Theme: “My grace is sufficient for you.”

Confessing Christ; strong in weakness.

1. Who do you say that I am?

  • Peter confesses the Messiah of God.
“I am the LORD, and there is no other; besides me there is no god.” Isaiah 45:5

  • God gives strength to the powerless.
  • His grace is sufficient.
“He gives power to the faint, and strengthens the powerless.” Isaiah 40:29
“but he said to me, “My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness.”” 2 Corinthians 12:9

Apply: Bring one persistent weakness to God.

3. God is my strength

  • Flesh and heart may fail; God remains.
  • When I am weak, then I am strong.
“My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.” Psalm 73:26
“Therefore I am content with weaknesses, insults, hardships, persecutions, and calamities for the sake of Christ; for whenever I am weak, then I am strong.” 2 Corinthians 12:10

Apply: Thank God for one weakness that taught you to trust him.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul speaks of a thorn in his flesh. Three times he begged the Lord to take it away, but the Lord said to him: my grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness. So Paul says: I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may dwell in me; for whenever I am weak, then I am strong (2 Corinthians 12:7–10).

Many of us carry thorns: illness, a difficult family situation, a temptation that returns, a loss that does not heal. We pray, and the thorn remains. God's answer to Paul is also for us: my grace is sufficient.

In the Gospel Jesus is praying alone with his disciples nearby. He asks them: who do the crowds say that I am? They give various answers: John the Baptist, Elijah, one of the prophets. Then he asks: but who do you say that I am? Peter answers: the Messiah of God (Luke 9:18–20).

Notice that this question comes after Jesus has been praying. Knowing who Jesus is comes from prayer, not just from information.

Jesus asks us the same question as Yaldo approaches: who do you say that I am? A baby in a crib? A good teacher? A distant God? Or the Messiah of God, my Lord and Saviour, who gives me grace in my weakness?

During this fast, let us answer Jesus personally, in prayer. And let us bring him our thorns, not only asking for their removal, but asking for the grace to carry them with him. His grace is sufficient. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus asks you: who am I for you? Tell Him today: Jesus, you are my friend and my God!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Messiah of God, we confess you as our Lord. When our thorns remain, let your grace be enough for us, and let your power be made perfect in our weakness. Amen.
