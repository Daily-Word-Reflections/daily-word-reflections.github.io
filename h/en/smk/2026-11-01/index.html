Word for the day

“And I tell you, you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it.” Matthew 16:18

Reflection

The Syro-Malankara year does not begin with Christmas or with Advent but with the Church. On Qudos Eetho Sunday we remember that God has sanctified a people for himself and made it his dwelling. Everything that follows in the year, the Annunciation, the Birth of the Lord, his Passion and Resurrection, is celebrated by this Church and for this Church.

The Old Testament readings trace the long story of God's dwelling. Moses sets up the Tabernacle; Solomon builds the Temple; Ezekiel sees the glory of the Lord rise from the Temple and depart because of the people's sins; Isaiah sees the Lord enthroned, hears the seraphim cry “Holy, holy, holy” and is sent. God wants to live among his people, but his presence is holy, and it cannot be taken for granted.

The New Testament shows where that glory finally comes to rest. Christ himself is the cornerstone, and believers become “living stones… built into a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood” (1 Peter 2:5). In the Gospel, Peter confesses, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God”, and Jesus answers, “On this rock I will build my church.” The Church is not a human association; it is built by Christ, on the faith of Peter, and the powers of death will not overcome it.

For the Syro-Malankara Catholic Church this Gospel has a special meaning. In 1930 Archbishop Mar Ivanios and his companions, keeping the ancient West Syriac liturgy, entered full communion with the Bishop of Rome, the successor of Peter. Unity with Peter and fidelity to our own Antiochene heritage are not opposed; together they express the one Catholic Church built on the rock.

Homily outline

Theme: “The Church: God's holy dwelling among us.”

Our liturgical year does not begin with Christmas but with the Church. Before we celebrate what God has done, we remember where he does it: in a people he has made holy and chosen as his home. Today we ask three questions: Where does God dwell? On what is that dwelling built? What does it ask of us?

1. God desires to dwell among his people

  • In the desert God told Moses to set up a Tabernacle, and his glory filled it.
  • Solomon built the Temple; but when the people sinned, Ezekiel saw the glory of the Lord leave the house.
  • God did not give up: in Jesus the glory returned to dwell among us in human flesh, and through him in the Church.
“Then the cloud covered the tent of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle.” Exodus 40:34
“And the Word became flesh and lived among us, and we have seen his glory, the glory as of a father's only son, full of grace and truth.” John 1:14

Apply: Our parish church is not a hall; it is the house where God's glory dwells. Enter it with reverence.

2. The Church is built on the rock of faith in Christ

  • Jesus asks: “Who do you say that I am?” Faith is a personal answer, not only a family custom.
  • Peter answers: “You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God”, and Jesus builds his Church on this rock.
  • The Church stands firm not because we are strong but because Christ promised: the gates of Hades will not prevail.
“See, I am laying in Zion a foundation stone, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone, a sure foundation: “One who trusts will not panic.”” Isaiah 28:16
“He said to them, “But who do you say that I am?” Simon Peter answered, “You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God.”” Matthew 16:15–16

Apply: This week, answer Jesus personally in prayer: “Lord, you are the Christ, my Lord and my God.”

3. We are living stones, shaped by God

  • Solomon's Temple was built of stones already shaped at the quarry; no hammer was heard at the holy place.
  • God shapes us beforehand through Baptism, the sacraments, trials and the Cross, so that we fit together in his house.
  • Peter, who wanted a Christ without the Cross, was corrected: the stone must accept the chisel.
“The house was built with stone finished at the quarry, so that neither hammer nor ax nor any tool of iron was heard in the temple while it was being built.” 1 Kings 6:7
“Like living stones, let yourselves be built into a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.” 1 Peter 2:5

Apply: Accept the “chisel” in your life (a difficult person, a sickness, a correction) as God shaping you for his house.

4. A holy God sends a holy people

  • Isaiah saw the Lord and heard: “Holy, holy, holy”; he knew his own sin and was purified.
  • Then God asked: “Whom shall I send?” and Isaiah answered: “Here am I; send me!”
  • A sanctified Church is a missionary Church: we are made holy in order to be light for others.
“Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory.” Isaiah 6:3
“You are the light of the world. A city built on a hill cannot be hid.” Matthew 5:14

Apply: At the Sanctus of the Qurbono, sing “Holy, holy, holy” with Isaiah, and say in your heart: “Here am I; send me.”

The Tabernacle, the Temple, the glory, the rock, the living stones: all point to one truth. God wants to live with us, and he has made us his home in Christ.

As the new liturgical year begins, let us love the Church as our mother, build it up with our lives, and let its light shine in our families and our land.

Take-home resolution: Make one visit to the church this week outside Sunday, and pray for your parish, your bishop and the whole Church.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, today a new liturgical year begins, and our Church begins it in a very wise way: not with Christmas, but with the Church herself. Qudos Eetho, the Sanctification of the Church. Before we celebrate the mysteries of Christ, we remember the house in which we celebrate them.

Listen to the long story told in our readings. Moses sets up the Tabernacle; Solomon builds the Temple; Ezekiel sees the glory of the Lord leave the Temple because the people had defiled it; Isaiah sees the Lord on his throne and hears the seraphim cry, “Holy, holy, holy.” God wants to live among his people, but his holiness can never be taken for granted.

Then St Peter tells us where God lives now: in us. “Like living stones, let yourselves be built into a spiritual house.” Every baptised person is a stone in God's house. Some stones carry weight; some stones crumble. This year, what kind of stone will I be?

And in the Gospel Jesus says to Peter: “On this rock I will build my church.” For us Syro-Malankara Catholics these words are dear. In 1930 Mar Ivanios led our forefathers into communion with the successor of Peter, while keeping our ancient Syriac liturgy. Unity with Peter and love for our own tradition are not two loyalties; they are one Catholic faith.

In a few moments we will sing with the seraphim, “Holy, holy, holy.” Heaven's song in our parish church. Let us begin this year as living stones, holy, united and faithful. Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, a new liturgical year begins today with Qudos Eetho, the Sanctification of the Church. Among the readings is the vision of the prophet Isaiah in the Temple (Isaiah 6:1–8). It is worth looking at closely, because we repeat its words in every Holy Qurbono.

In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord sitting on a throne, high and lifted up. Seraphim stood above Him and called to one another: holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts; the whole earth is full of His glory (Isaiah 6:1–3). The king had died; the nation was anxious. But Isaiah saw the true King, who never dies. When we sing Qadeesh in the Qurbono, we join the seraphim, and with them we see the King.

Isaiah's first reaction was not joy but fear: woe is me, for I am a man of unclean lips. Then a seraph took a burning coal from the altar and touched his lips, saying: your guilt has departed and your sin is blotted out (Isaiah 6:5–7). The Fathers of the Syriac Church saw in this burning coal an image of the Holy Mysteries, the Body of Christ received from the altar, which purifies and sets on fire. Before we receive, let us confess, as Isaiah did, that we are unworthy.

Then the Lord asked: whom shall I send? And Isaiah answered: here am I; send me (Isaiah 6:8). A holy God sends a holy people. In the Gospel Peter confesses Jesus as the Christ, the Son of the living God, and Jesus builds His Church on that faith (Matthew 16:16–18). The Church is not a club for the holy; she is a people being made holy and sent into the world.

So as this new year begins, let us do three things. Let us sing the Qadeesh with attention, knowing that heaven sings with us. Let us prepare well for Holy Communion, with a clean conscience. And let us say to God: here am I, send me. Offer yourself for one service in your parish this year.

Dear brothers and sisters, our liturgical year begins in a special way. In the West the year begins with Advent; in our West Syriac tradition it begins with two Sundays dedicated to the Church: Qudos Eetho, the Sanctification of the Church, today, and Hudos Eetho, the Dedication or Renewal of the Church, next Sunday. Only then do we move to the Sundays of the Annunciations and to Yaldo, the Nativity.

Why begin with the Church? Because it is in the Church that we meet Christ. When Moses finished the tabernacle, the cloud covered it and the glory of the Lord filled it (Exodus 40:34–35). The Church is the place where God's glory dwells among His people. Before we contemplate the mysteries of Christ through the year, we renew our love for the Church in which those mysteries are celebrated.

Our Church follows the West Syriac or Antiochene tradition. Our Holy Qurbono is celebrated principally with the Anaphora of St James, the brother of the Lord, one of the most ancient liturgies of the Church. In 1930 Mar Ivanios and his companions entered into full communion with the Catholic Church, bringing this heritage with them. In 2005 the Syro-Malankara Catholic Church was raised to a Major Archiepiscopal Church. We are fully Eastern and fully Catholic.

St Peter writes that we are living stones being built into a spiritual house, a holy priesthood (1 Peter 2:5). Each of us is part of this building. And our Church has a special vocation: to be a bridge of unity. Jesus prayed that all may be one (John 17:21). We share much of our heritage with our Orthodox brothers and sisters. Let us pray daily for the unity of all Christians, especially in Kerala.

So as this new year begins, let us love our Church, learn the meaning of our liturgy, take part faithfully in the Holy Qurbono, and live as living stones. May the God who sanctifies His Church sanctify each of us this year.
