Word for the day

“Jesus did this, the first of his signs, in Cana of Galilee, and revealed his glory; and his disciples believed in him.” John 2:11

Reflection

The Malankara Church begins the Great Fast with a wedding. On Kothine Sunday we read the miracle at Cana, where Jesus changed water into wine. It may seem strange to start a season of fasting at a feast, but it is deeply wise. The Fast is not about sadness; it is about transformation. The water of our ordinary, tasteless lives can become the wine of God's joy, if we do what Mary says: “Do whatever he tells you.”

The prophets tell us what kind of fast God wants. Joel: “Return to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning; rend your hearts and not your clothing.” Isaiah: “Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of injustice… to share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house?” Fasting from food is meant to open our hearts to God and our hands to the poor.

St Paul shows the inner work of Lent: “Put to death whatever in you is earthly… clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience… Above all, clothe yourselves with love.” The Fast is a change of clothes: putting off the old self and putting on Christ. Acts reminds us that it was in Antioch, the mother Church of our Syriac tradition, that the disciples were first called Christians.

The jars at Cana were filled “up to the brim” with water at Jesus' word. In these fifty days, let us fill our jars: with prayer, fasting, almsgiving and the Word of God. At the end of the Fast, at Qyomtho, Christ will give us the best wine, kept until now: the joy of his Resurrection.

Homily outline

Theme: “The Fast begins at a wedding.”

Our Great Fast does not begin with ashes but with a wedding at Cana. The Church tells us that Lent is not sadness but the joyful return of the Bride to the Bridegroom, who turns the water of our efforts into the wine of his grace.

1. Return with your whole heart

  • Joel calls: return with fasting, weeping and mourning; rend your hearts, not your clothing.
  • At Cana Mary notices: “They have no wine.” Lent begins by admitting what we lack.
  • Her counsel is the programme of the Fast: “Do whatever he tells you.”
“Yet even now, says the LORD, return to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning; rend your hearts and not your clothing.” Joel 2:12–13
“When the wine gave out, the mother of Jesus said to him, “They have no wine.” … His mother said to the servants, “Do whatever he tells you.”” John 2:3, 5

Apply: Name the “wine” that has run out in your life (joy, peace, love) and bring it to Jesus.

2. The fast that God chooses

  • Fasting from food has meaning when it frees us for justice and charity.
  • Isaiah: share your bread, shelter the homeless, clothe the naked.
  • Jesus: fast in secret; the Father who sees in secret will reward you.
“Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of injustice, to undo the thongs of the yoke, to let the oppressed go free … Is it not to share your bread with the hungry?” Isaiah 58:6–7
“But when you fast, put oil on your head and wash your face, so that your fasting may be seen not by others but by your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you.” Matthew 6:17–18

Apply: Set aside the money saved by fasting for the poor.

3. Water into wine: God transforms

  • The servants fill the jars to the brim; Jesus does the rest.
  • The best wine is kept for last: God's grace exceeds our efforts.
  • After the flood God gave a covenant sign; at Cana Jesus gives the first sign of the new covenant.
“I have set my bow in the clouds, and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and the earth.” Genesis 9:13
““You have kept the good wine until now.” Jesus did this, the first of his signs, in Cana of Galilee, and revealed his glory; and his disciples believed in him.” John 2:10–11

Apply: Fill your “jars” this Lent: daily prayer, Scripture, fasting and charity, and trust Jesus to transform them.

The Fast is a wedding feast in preparation: the Bridegroom is coming to his Passion and Resurrection.

Let us return with all our hearts, fast as God desires, and let him change our water into wine.

Take-home resolution: Choose three Lenten practices (prayer, fasting, almsgiving) and write them down today.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today our Church begins the Great Fast, and it begins it at a wedding. On Kothine Sunday we read how Jesus, at Cana, changed water into wine. Why begin fifty days of fasting with a wedding feast? Because the Fast is not a season of gloom. It is a season of transformation.

At Cana the wine ran out. Mary noticed and said to the servants: “Do whatever he tells you.” That is the best advice for Lent. The servants did a simple thing: they filled the jars with water, up to the brim. And Jesus changed that water into the best wine. In this Fast, let us fill our jars: with prayer, with fasting, with the Word of God, with works of mercy. And let Jesus change the water of our lives into wine.

The prophets tell us what true fasting is. Joel says: “Return to me with all your heart; rend your hearts and not your clothing.” Isaiah says: “Is not this the fast that I choose: to share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house?” Fasting from food is not a diet; it makes room in our hearts for God and in our hands for the poor.

Tonight the special prayers of the Fast begin, and tomorrow we celebrate Shubkono, when we ask forgiveness of one another. Let each family make a simple plan: one fast, one prayer, one act of charity. And at the end of these fifty days, at Qyomtho, Jesus will give us the best wine, kept until now: the joy of his Resurrection. Amen.

For children

Children, today Lent begins! And our Gospel is about a wedding party. At the party the wine ran out. Mary said: “Do whatever Jesus tells you.”

The servants filled six big jars with water, and Jesus changed it into wonderful wine! In Lent Jesus wants to change us too: to make us kinder, more patient, more prayerful.

What can you give up this Lent? Sweets? Television? And what good thing can you do? Let's say: “Jesus, change my heart!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus, at Cana you changed water into wine and revealed your glory. As we begin the Great Fast, change our hearts: teach us to do whatever you tell us, to fast with love, to share with the poor, and to reach with joy the feast of your Resurrection. Amen.
