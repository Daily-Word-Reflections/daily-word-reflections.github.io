Word for the day

“And my God will fully satisfy every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus.” Philippians 4:19

Reflection

St Paul ends his letter to the Philippians with a thank-you. “You Philippians indeed know that in the early days of the gospel, when I left Macedonia, no church shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving, except you alone. For even when I was in Thessalonica, you sent me help for my needs more than once.” The church at Philippi was poor and often persecuted, yet it was the one community that kept on supporting the apostle as he carried the gospel from town to town. Paul is careful about how he thanks them: “Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the profit that accumulates to your account.” What delights him is not the money but what their giving does to them; every gift for the gospel is written in God's book to their credit. Tomorrow the Panchangam keeps Seminary Day, and the offerings of the Holy Qurbono will go to the Major Seminary of our Church, where young men are formed to be priests. In the Apostles' Fast, when we remember the first men Christ sent, it is fitting that we support those who are being prepared to be sent today. The parish that gives to the seminary is doing exactly what the Philippians did for Paul: sharing in the gospel by giving and receiving.

Paul then describes their gift in the language of worship: “I have been paid in full and have more than enough; I am fully satisfied, now that I have received from Epaphroditus the gifts you sent, a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God.” A gift for the poor or for the mission is not only an act of kindness; it rises to God like the incense of the temple. And it never leaves the giver poorer in the end: “And my God will fully satisfy every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus. To our God and Father be glory forever and ever. Amen.” Paul does not promise that the Philippians will become wealthy. He promises that God, who is rich beyond measure, will not let them lack what they truly need. A family that shares even from a small income discovers this again and again, and that discovery is itself a reason to give glory to God.

The Gospel brings us into one of the sharpest disputes in St John's Gospel. Jesus is speaking with listeners who, a few verses earlier, are said to be looking for an opportunity to kill him, and he tells them: “If God were your Father, you would love me, for I came from God and now I am here.” Then he speaks plainly of the devil: “He was a murderer from the beginning and does not stand in the truth, because there is no truth in him. When he lies, he speaks according to his own nature, for he is a liar and the father of lies.” These words were spoken to particular opponents at a particular moment; the Church has always taught that they may not be turned against the Jewish people as a whole (Nostra Aetate 4; Catechism 597). Their warning is for us. From the first lie in the garden, the enemy has worked through falsehood. St Augustine observed that whoever tells a lie speaks from what is his own, while whoever speaks the truth speaks from God, the source of all truth (Tractates on John 42, in substance). In an age of rumours forwarded from phone to phone, the fast of the tongue matters as much as the fast of the stomach: no lie, no gossip, no message passed on without knowing it is true. “Whoever is from God hears the words of God.” Let us belong to God by listening to his word and by speaking only what is true.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul thanks the Philippians for their help: no church shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving, except you alone (Philippians 4:15). They were not a rich church, but they kept supporting the apostle so that the gospel could go further. Tomorrow is Seminary Day, and we will be asked to help the seminary where young men are formed to be priests. That is our way of sharing in the gospel, as the Philippians did.

Paul calls their gift a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God (Philippians 4:18). And he makes a promise: my God will fully satisfy every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus (Philippians 4:19). No one becomes truly poor by giving to God.

In the Gospel, Jesus speaks hard words to those who were opposing him, and he warns about the devil, who is a liar and the father of lies (John 8:44). These words are not a judgement on a people; they are a warning to every heart. And Jesus gives us a sign of those who belong to God: Whoever is from God hears the words of God (John 8:47).

So let us do two things. Let us give generously tomorrow for the seminary and pray for our future priests. And for the rest of this Apostles' Fast, let us keep the fast of the tongue: no lies, no gossip, no rumours passed on. Let us listen to the word of God and speak the truth in love. Amen.

For children

Children, a long time ago the Christians of a town called Philippi sent presents to St Paul so that he could keep going from place to place telling people about Jesus. Tomorrow our Church will collect money for the seminary, the big school where young men learn to become priests. You can bring a coin too! And Jesus tells us today that God's children always tell the truth. If you have told a lie, say sorry, and try to always speak the truth.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you came from the Father and you speak the truth. Make us children of God who hear your words and love you. Keep us from every lie, from gossip and from rumours that wound our neighbours, and let our speech be true and kind. Bless all who share in the work of the gospel by their gifts, and make their offering a fragrant sacrifice, pleasing to the Father. Bless our Major Seminary, its teachers and its students, and send faithful labourers into your harvest. According to the riches of your glory, supply every need of our families. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
