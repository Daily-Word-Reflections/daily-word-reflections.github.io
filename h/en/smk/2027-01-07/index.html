Word for the day

“I tell you, among those born of women no one is greater than John; yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he.” Luke 7:28

Reflection

On the day after Denho the Syriac tradition praises the one who baptised the Lord. Jesus himself gives John's praise: “What did you go out into the wilderness to look at? A reed shaken by the wind?” No, John was firm. “Someone dressed in soft robes?” No, he lived in poverty. “A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.” He is the messenger foretold to prepare the way. “Among those born of women no one is greater than John.”

And then a surprise: “yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he.” John stood at the threshold; we, by Baptism, have entered. The grace of the Kingdom is greater than any human greatness. Paul calls himself “the very least of all the saints”, yet a servant of the Gospel “according to the gift of God's grace”. The mystery hidden for ages is now revealed: the Gentiles are fellow heirs in Christ. John prepared the way; we are sent to walk it.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the day after Denho the Syriac Churches praise John the Baptist, who baptised the Lord in the Jordan. Jesus himself speaks his praise.

He asks the crowds: what did you go out into the wilderness to look at? A reed shaken by the wind? (Luke 7:24). No. John was not shaken by public opinion or by Herod's power. Someone dressed in soft robes? No. John lived in poverty.

A prophet? Yes, says Jesus, and more than a prophet. He is the messenger sent ahead to prepare the way. Among those born of women no one is greater than John (Luke 7:28).

Then a surprise: yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he. John stood at the door; by Baptism we have entered. This is not about our merit but about grace. Paul calls himself a servant of the gospel according to the gift of God's grace (Ephesians 3:7).

Let us honour John by being firm in faith, simple in life, and grateful for the Kingdom we have received. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus said John the Baptist was very great and strong, like a tree in a storm. Let us be strong for Jesus too!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you praised John the Baptist, your messenger and the friend who baptised you. Make us firm as he was, simple as he was, and grateful for the grace of the Kingdom we have received in Baptism. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

With Denho just kept, the Church turns to praise the one who baptised the Lord. In the Gospel Jesus himself gives the praise: “more than a prophet”, and no one born of woman greater. In the Epistle Paul, a prisoner, describes the same kind of service: to be a servant of a mystery larger than oneself. Both men are great because of what they point to.

Ephesians 3:1–7 · A servant of the gospel

Setting.

The letter speaks in Paul's voice from prison (on authorship, many modern scholars think of a close disciple, others defend Paul himself). Chapter 2 has just said that Gentiles and Jews are made one in Christ. Paul starts a prayer in 3:1, breaks off to explain his own mission (3:2–13), and takes up the prayer again in 3:14. This reading is the first part of that digression.

Structure.

  • v. 1: the prisoner for the sake of the Gentiles.
  • vv. 2–4: the commission and the mystery, made known by revelation.
  • v. 5: hidden in the past, revealed now.
  • v. 6: what the mystery is.
  • v. 7: Paul the servant.

Key words.

  • mystērion, mystery: In Paul this is not a puzzle or a secret for a few. It means God's plan, once hidden and now openly revealed. Here it has one precise content: the Gentiles belong.
  • oikonomia, commission: Literally the managing of a household. Paul is a steward: the grace is not his own, it passes through him “for you” (v. 2).
  • diakonos, servant: A word for one who waits on others or carries a message. It is the root of our “deacon”. Paul, like John, defines himself by the One he serves.

Verse by verse.

“In former generations this mystery was not made known to humankind, as it has now been revealed to his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit:” (3:5)

“As it has now been”: the Old Testament had already spoken of the nations coming to God (Isaiah 2:2–3), but not of this, that they would be one body with Israel in the Messiah.

“that is, the Gentiles have become fellow heirs, members of the same body, and sharers in the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel.” (3:6)

Three words in the Greek all begin with “together” (syn-): co-heirs, co-bodied, co-sharers. One of them, “of the same body”, may have been coined for this purpose. The Gentiles join in Israel's promises, and they do not replace Israel.

“Of this gospel I have become a servant according to the gift of God’s grace that was given me by the working of his power.” (3:7)

Paul was a persecutor, and in the very next verse he calls himself “the very least of all the saints”. The call is a gift, and the power is God's.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism sees in Israel the root onto which the Gentiles would be grafted (CCC 60; see Romans 11:17–24), and the Second Vatican Council recalls this same image when it speaks of the Church's bond with the Jewish people (Nostra Aetate 4). The readers of this passage are therefore guests of a promise made first to others, and this should make them grateful. Denho, with its manifestation to all the world, is the natural setting for this reading.

Luke 7:24–29 · More than a prophet

Setting.

John is in prison (3:20) and has sent messengers to ask, “Are you the one who is to come” (7:19)? Jesus answers them by pointing to his works (7:22–23). As soon as they leave, he turns to the crowd and defends John, in case anyone should think less of him for that question.

Structure.

  • vv. 24–26: three questions: what did you go out to see?
  • v. 27: Scripture's answer (Malachi 3:1).
  • v. 28: the greatest, and the least.
  • v. 29: the people's response.

Key words.

  • kalamos, reed: Tall canes grew along the Jordan and bent in every wind. The picture means someone with no spine. Some scholars also think of the reed on the early coins of Herod Antipas, the ruler who jailed John, but this remains a suggestion.
  • angelos, messenger: The same word as “angel”. Malachi's messenger comes ahead to clear the road for the Lord himself.
  • mikroteros, the least: Literally “the lesser”. Most interpreters take it of anyone in the Kingdom. Some early writers, St John Chrysostom among them, understood Jesus himself, younger than John and thought less of by people (in substance).

Verse by verse.

“What then did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.” (7:26)

Other prophets spoke of the One to come; John saw him. He is himself foretold as the messenger, and he could point with his finger at the One the others could only announce.

“I tell you, among those born of women no one is greater than John; yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he.”” (7:28)

This is not a demotion of John. It compares two moments: he stood at the door, and we have been brought in, by no merit of ours. The second half is praise of grace rather than a judgment on John, who is a saint in the Kingdom.

“And all the people who heard this, including the tax collectors, acknowledged the justice of God, because they had been baptized with John’s baptism.” (7:29)

The ones who took John's washing seriously are able to recognise God's work. His baptism prepared hearts, and that was its purpose.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism calls John the Lord's immediate forerunner (CCC 523) and says that he is “more than a prophet”, in whom the Holy Spirit concludes his speaking through the prophets (CCC 719). Here he receives the Lord's own praise. His greatness lies in the plainness of his own words: “He must increase, but I must decrease” (John 3:30).

Questions people ask

Is John outside the Kingdom?

No. Jesus says elsewhere that all the prophets will be seen in the kingdom of God (Luke 13:28). The verse compares John's place in the history of salvation, before the Cross and Pentecost, with the gift of those who live after them. The Church honours him among the greatest of the saints.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

On the day after Denho the Syriac tradition praises the one who baptised the Lord. It is fitting that a man who always stepped aside should receive his feast right after the day of the One he pointed to. Parents, godparents and catechists share John's work: to bring someone to the river and then to let that person go to Christ.

For study and teaching

  • “A reed shaken by the wind?” Where is my faith most easily bent by the opinion of those around me?
  • Paul and John both called themselves servants of something greater. Whom have I helped to come closer to Christ, and was I content to step aside?
