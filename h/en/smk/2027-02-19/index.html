Word for the day

“Is anything too wonderful for the Lord? At the set time I will return to you, in due season, and Sarah shall have a son.” Genesis 18:14

Reflection

The guests at Mamre ask a strange question: “Where is your wife Sarah?” She is “in the tent,” listening behind the entrance, and when she hears that she will have a son, she laughs to herself: “After I have grown old, and my husband is old, shall I have pleasure?” It is the laugh of a woman who has waited too long and stopped hoping. The Lord hears the laugh that no one saw, and asks: “Why did Sarah laugh?… Is anything too wonderful for the Lord?” Then a small, almost humorous exchange: “But Sarah denied, saying, ‘I did not laugh’; for she was afraid. He said, ‘Oh yes, you did laugh.’” God does not punish her for it. He simply keeps his word. Many of us carry a hidden laugh like Sarah's, a quiet disbelief that God can still do anything new in our lives. The Fast is a time to let him name it, and to hear again: nothing is too wonderful for the Lord.

On this Friday Paul shows where real hope is found: “I regard everything as loss because of the surpassing value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things, and I regard them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ.” He wants to know “the power of his resurrection and the sharing of his sufferings by becoming like him in his death.” St Ignatius of Antioch, on his way to martyrdom, wrote that it was better for him to die for Christ than to reign over the ends of the earth (in substance). This is not a love of loss for its own sake; it is the discovery that Christ is worth more than everything. And Christ holds those who belong to him: “My sheep hear my voice. I know them, and they follow me. I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one will snatch them out of my hand.” Today we also remember Mar Ishaq, and we pray for Joseph Mar Thomas on the anniversary of his episcopal ordination.

Homily outline

Theme: “Is anything too wonderful for the Lord?”

Sarah's hidden laugh, Paul's great exchange, and the hand that holds us fast.

1. Sarah laughs

  • Listening behind the tent.
  • The laugh of lost hope.
  • “Oh yes, you did laugh.”
“Ah Lord God! It is you who made the heavens and the earth by your great power and by your outstretched arm! Nothing is too hard for you.” Jeremiah 32:17
“For nothing will be impossible with God.” Luke 1:37

Apply: Bring your most hopeless situation before God every day of this week.

2. The surpassing value

  • Everything as loss.
  • Not my own righteousness.
  • The power of his resurrection.
“Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire other than you.” Psalm 73:25
“More than that, I regard everything as loss because of the surpassing value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord.” Philippians 3:8

Apply: Give up one comfort each Friday of the Fast and spend that time before the crucifix.

3. In the Father's hand

  • My sheep hear my voice.
  • Eternal life, never perish.
  • The Father and I are one.
“See, I have inscribed you on the palms of my hands; your walls are continually before me.” Isaiah 49:16
“I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one will snatch them out of my hand.” John 10:28

Apply: When fear comes this week, pray: Lord, I am in your hand.

God keeps his word even when we laugh in disbelief.

Those who count all as loss for Christ are held in a hand no one can open.

Take-home resolution: Each Friday of the Fast, spend time before the crucifix and renew your hope in God.

Full homily

Dear friends, Sarah heard behind the tent that she would have a son, and she laughed to herself. The Lord asked: why did Sarah laugh? Is anything too wonderful for the Lord? (Genesis 18:13–14).

She denied it, for she was afraid. He said: oh yes, you did laugh (Genesis 18:15). God does not condemn her; he simply keeps his word. Many of us carry such a hidden laugh, a quiet doubt that God can still do something new.

Paul shows where hope is found: I regard everything as loss because of the surpassing value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord (Philippians 3:8). He wanted to share Christ's sufferings so as to know the power of his resurrection.

And Jesus promises: I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one will snatch them out of my hand (John 10:28). On this Friday, let us place our doubts and losses into that hand. Amen.

For children

Children, Sarah laughed when God said she would have a baby, because she was very old. But God asked: is anything too wonderful for me? And baby Isaac was born! Jesus holds you in his hand, and nobody can take you away from him.

Prayer

Lord God, nothing is too wonderful for you. You heard Sarah's hidden laugh and still kept your promise. Heal our quiet disbelief. Give us the heart of Paul, who counted everything as loss for the surpassing value of knowing Christ. Keep us in the hand of your Son, from which no one can snatch us. Bless Joseph Mar Thomas and all our bishops. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
