Word for the day

“For this reason he is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance.” Hebrews 9:15

Reflection

Christ is risen! On this Friday of the Easter season the Letter to the Hebrews speaks of the Lord's death, but as something that has already borne its fruit. Jesus “is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance.” The Letter plays on a word that in Greek means both “covenant” and “will.” A will takes effect only when the one who made it has died; until then the heirs receive nothing. So with us: by his death on the Cross, Christ made the inheritance ours. Then the Letter recalls Mount Sinai, where Moses took the blood of the sacrifice, sprinkled the book and the people, and said, “This is the blood of the covenant that God has ordained for you.” In the Upper Room Jesus took the cup and used those very words of himself: “this is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins” (Matthew 26:28). And here is the wonder of Easter: in every other will the one who makes it stays dead. Our testator rose on the third day, and he himself now hands the inheritance to his heirs.

The Gospel takes us to a meal. Jesus is a guest in the house of a leader of the Pharisees, and he notices “how the guests chose the places of honor.” He tells them: “When you are invited... go and sit down at the lowest place, so that when your host comes, he may say to you, ‘Friend, move up higher.’” This is more than good manners. It describes the way of Jesus himself, who “humbled himself and became obedient to the point of death” (Philippians 2:8), and whom the Father raised and exalted. “For all who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted.” Then Jesus turns to his host: do not invite only those who can invite you back, but “invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, and the blind. And you will be blessed, because they cannot repay you, for you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous.” St John Chrysostom urged the families of Antioch to keep a room in their homes for Christ, that is, for the poor stranger, and to set a place for him at their table (in substance).

Notice the Easter word at the end of the Gospel: “the resurrection of the righteous.” Because Christ is risen, a kindness that is never returned on earth is not lost. It is stored up with God. Our Kerala celebrations know the struggle Jesus describes: the seating at a wedding, the chairs kept for important guests, the gift lists, the invitations sent with an eye to who will invite us back. This week, turn one of these customs around. Invite someone to your table who cannot repay you; give a gift that will not be recorded; at a function, take the lower chair gladly. And remember that at the Holy Qurbono every one of us is a guest who has been called up higher. We come not because we have earned the place, but because the host has said, “Friend, come.” The blood of the new covenant is given to sinners, and the inheritance is offered to all who are called.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! The Letter to the Hebrews says that Jesus is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance (Hebrews 9:15).

A will takes effect only after death. Christ died, and his will is in force: forgiveness, adoption, eternal life. But this testator rose again on the third day. He himself gives us what he left us, especially his own Body and Blood in the Holy Qurbono.

In the Gospel Jesus watches the guests choosing the best seats, and he says: go and sit down at the lowest place (Luke 14:10). That is his own way. He took the lowest place on the Cross, and the Father raised him up. Then he tells the host to invite the poor, the crippled, the lame and the blind, because they cannot repay, and you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous (Luke 14:14).

Because Christ is risen, no unreturned kindness is wasted. This week let us take a lower seat gladly, and invite to our table someone who cannot invite us back. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus once saw people pushing to get the best chairs at a party. He said: go and sit in the last chair, and the host will call you up! He also said: when you have a party, invite people who are poor or sick, who cannot invite you back. God will reward you. Can you share something today with someone who cannot give you anything in return?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, mediator of the new covenant, by your death you made us heirs of the eternal inheritance, and by your Resurrection you live to give it to us. Teach us your humility, that we may take the lowest place without bitterness and rejoice when others are honoured. Open our hands and our homes to the poor, the sick and the lonely, who cannot repay us, and keep our hope fixed on the resurrection of the righteous. You live and reign for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

On a Friday of Qyomtho the Church remembers the Passion in the light of the empty tomb. Hebrews explains the death of Christ as the blood of a new covenant, sealing God's promise as Moses once sealed the covenant at Sinai. Luke shows Jesus at a meal, telling guests to take the lowest place and hosts to invite those who cannot repay. The one who took the lowest place on the Cross is the host of the new covenant's table. The key words are covenant, blood and lowest place.

Hebrews 9:15–22 · The mediator of a new covenant

Setting.

Chapters 8–10 of Hebrews compare the worship of the first covenant with the work of Christ. Chapter 8 quoted at length Jeremiah's promise of a new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31–34). In 9:1–14 the writer described the tent of the old worship and the Day of Atonement, and said that Christ entered the true sanctuary “with his own blood” (9:12).

Today's verses explain why a death was needed. The writer uses the fact that one Greek word can mean both “covenant” and “will”, and then recalls the covenant ceremony at Sinai (Exodus 24:3–8).

Structure.

  • v. 15: Christ the mediator; his death redeems.
  • vv. 16–17: a will takes effect only at death.
  • vv. 18–21: the first covenant was also sealed with blood.
  • v. 22: no forgiveness without the shedding of blood.

Key words.

  • diathēkē, covenant, will: In the Greek Old Testament this word translates the Hebrew bᵉrît, covenant. In ordinary Greek it meant a last will. Hebrews plays on both: God's promises come to us as an inheritance through the death of the Son.
  • mesitēs, mediator: One who stands between two parties and brings them together. Christ is mediator because he is both God and man (1 Timothy 2:5).
  • haimatekchysia, shedding of blood: A word not found in any earlier Greek writing known to us. Behind it lies the teaching of Leviticus that “the life of the flesh is in the blood” (Leviticus 17:11): blood offered means life given.

Verse by verse.

“For this reason he is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance,” (9:15)

The goal is not only the removal of sin but an inheritance. Those who are called become heirs.

“Hence not even the first covenant was inaugurated without blood.” (9:18)

At Sinai, half the blood was thrown on the altar and half on the people (Exodus 24:6–8). God and people were bound together in one life.

“This is the blood of the covenant that God has ordained for you.” (9:20)

Moses said “See the blood of the covenant” (Exodus 24:8). The form Hebrews gives, “This is”, is close to the Lord's words over the cup at the Supper (Mark 14:24).

“without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins.” (9:22)

The writer states the principle of the old worship in order to show its fulfilment. The blood of animals pointed to the one gift of life that truly takes sin away.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that the sacrifice of Christ is the sacrifice of the New Covenant, which restores us to communion with God through “the blood of the covenant” poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins (CCC 613). In the Holy Qurbono the priest repeats the Lord's words over the cup, and the Church receives the blood of the new covenant as her Lord commanded.

Luke 14:7–14 · Take the lowest place; invite the poor

Setting.

Jesus has gone on a sabbath to eat at the house of a leader of the Pharisees (14:1). He accepts the hospitality of Pharisees more than once in Luke (7:36; 11:37), and they are watching him closely. At such dinners guests reclined on couches arranged around the table, and the places nearest the host were the places of honour.

Jesus speaks first to the guests (vv. 7–11) and then to the host (vv. 12–14). What sounds like advice on manners is a parable about the kingdom.

Structure.

  • vv. 7–11: to the guests: do not seek the first place.
  • vv. 12–14: to the host: invite those who cannot repay.

Key words.

  • prōtoklisia, place of honour: Literally “first couch”. Seeking it was a way of claiming status in front of others.
  • tapeinōn, humbling: The saying in v. 11 uses passives, “will be humbled”, “will be exalted”. It is God who does the lifting, as Mary sings: he “lifted up the lowly” (Luke 1:52).
  • antapodothēsetai, you will be repaid: The same root appears for repayment at the resurrection. God himself becomes the one who repays those who gave without return.

Verse by verse.

“When he noticed how the guests chose the places of honor, he told them a parable.” (14:7)

Luke calls it a parable. The table is an image of God's kingdom, where places are given, not grabbed.

“For all who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted.” (14:11)

Jesus says it again after the parable of the Pharisee and the tax collector (18:14). He lived it first, by taking the lowest place of all on the Cross.

“But when you give a banquet, invite the poor, the crippled, the lame, and the blind.” (14:13)

These are the very people the master brings in at the great dinner a few verses later (14:21).

“for you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous.” (14:14)

Generosity that expects nothing in return is a sign of faith in the resurrection. We give now because we believe God will answer later.

How the Church reads it.

The Church reads this teaching as part of her preferential love for the poor. The Catechism says that God blesses those who come to the aid of the poor (CCC 2443), and that love for the poor belongs to the constant tradition of the Church (CCC 2444). Every gathering of Christians, from the parish feast to the family table, is measured by this Gospel.

For study and teaching

  • Hebrews calls Christ's death the beginning of an inheritance. What does it mean to me that I am an heir of God's promises?
  • Whom could I invite, or help, this week who cannot repay me?
