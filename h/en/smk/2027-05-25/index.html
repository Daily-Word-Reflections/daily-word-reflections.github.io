Word for the day

“So we do not lose heart. Even though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day.” 2 Corinthians 4:16

Reflection

Today the Panchangam names a saint called Sophia and tells us nothing more. Several holy women of that name are honoured in the calendars of East and West, and we need not guess which one our Church has in mind. The name itself is enough to pray with. Sophia is the Greek word for wisdom, and the readings of the day are a lesson in the wisdom that looks beyond what can be seen. St Paul writes to the Corinthians from the middle of great hardship. He has been afflicted, perplexed, persecuted and struck down, and his body carries the marks. Yet he says: “So we do not lose heart. Even though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day.” Every one of us knows the outer nature that wastes away: the knees that complain on the church steps, the eyes that need stronger glasses, the strength that is not what it was. Paul does not pretend otherwise. But he sees something growing while the body declines: an inner person, renewed day by day by the Spirit who was poured out at Pentecost. Old age, illness and loss are not the end of the story for a Christian; they can be the very place where the inner person grows young.

Paul continues: “For this slight momentary affliction is preparing us for an eternal weight of glory beyond all measure, because we look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen; for what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal.” He calls his sufferings slight and momentary, not because they were small, but because he has placed them on a scale beside the glory that is coming, and the glory is heavier. St John Chrysostom, preaching on this letter, says that Paul makes affliction light by setting it beside the reward, and short by setting it beside eternity (Homilies on 2 Corinthians, in substance). That is true wisdom, the wisdom the name Sophia speaks of: to judge things by their weight in eternity. The house, the land, the results in the examination, the praise of the neighbours, all these can be seen, and all are temporary. Faith, hope and love, the soul of a child, a kindness done in secret, the grace of the Holy Qurbono, these cannot be seen, and they remain. A wise person spends the visible to gain the invisible.

The Gospel tells how to do that. Jesus is near Jerusalem, and his hearers think the kingdom is about to appear at once. So he tells of a nobleman who goes to a distant country to receive royal power and then return. Before leaving he gives ten servants a pound each and says: “Do business with these until I come back.” The time between his going and his return is our time, the time of the Church after the Ascension and Pentecost. When he comes back, one servant has made ten pounds and hears, “Well done, good slave! Because you have been trustworthy in a very small thing, take charge of ten cities.” Another wrapped his pound in a cloth, because he was afraid. The parable ends with hard words about the citizens who said, “We do not want this man to rule over us.” Jesus is not teaching violence; he is going up to Jerusalem to die for those who reject him, and on the Cross he will pray for them. The harsh ending tells us how serious it is to refuse the true King. St Gregory the Great explained that the gift entrusted to each of us is whatever we have received: let the one who has understanding take care not to keep silent, the one who has means not to be slow in mercy, the one who has a skill to share its use (Homilies on the Gospels 9, in substance). The pound is the faith, the gifts of the Spirit and the time given to us. Do not bury it. Trade with it until he comes.

Homily outline

Theme: “We look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen; for what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal.”

On Tuesday of the first week after Pentecost, with St Sophia, whose name means wisdom: the inner person renewed, the unseen that lasts, and the gifts we are to use until the Lord returns.

1. Renewed day by day

  • The outer nature wastes away.
  • The inner person grows young.
  • The Spirit poured out at Pentecost.
“He gives power to the faint, and strengthens the powerless.” Isaiah 40:29
“I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worth comparing with the glory about to be revealed to us.” Romans 8:18

Apply: Visit or call one elderly or sick person this week and pray with them.

2. Do business until I come back

  • A pound for each servant.
  • The time between his going and his coming.
  • Trustworthy in a very small thing.
“So teach us to count our days that we may gain a wise heart.” Psalm 90:12
“Like good stewards of the manifold grace of God, serve one another with whatever gift each of you has received.” 1 Peter 4:10

Apply: Offer one gift you have, a skill, some time, a little money, to the parish or to a neighbour in need.

3. The wisdom that sees the unseen

  • Sophia: the name means wisdom.
  • Weighing all on the scale of eternity.
  • Spending the visible to gain the invisible.
“Therefore I prayed, and understanding was given me; I called on God, and the spirit of wisdom came to me.” Wisdom 7:7
“If any of you is lacking in wisdom, ask God, who gives to all generously and ungrudgingly, and it will be given you.” James 1:5

Apply: Each morning this week ask the Holy Spirit for the gift of wisdom before you begin your work.

The body may grow weak, but the Spirit renews the inner person day by day and prepares a weight of glory.

The Lord has given us his gifts for the time until he returns: wisdom uses them, fear buries them.

Take-home resolution: Look at what cannot be seen, and do not bury the pound the Lord has given you.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Panchangam remembers St Sophia. Her name means wisdom, and St Paul shows us what Christian wisdom looks like: so we do not lose heart. Even though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day (2 Corinthians 4:16).

Paul calls his many sufferings a slight momentary affliction, because they are preparing us for an eternal weight of glory beyond all measure (2 Corinthians 4:17). He has put them on a scale next to heaven. And he tells us where to look: what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal (2 Corinthians 4:18).

In the Gospel Jesus tells of a nobleman who gave his servants a pound each and said: do business with these until I come back (Luke 19:13). Two used it well and were praised: because you have been trustworthy in a very small thing, take charge of ten cities (Luke 19:17). One hid it in a cloth because he was afraid. The Lord has given each of us faith, gifts and time. This is the time between his Ascension and his return.

Let us not lose heart when the body grows weak, and let us not bury what we have received. A visit to a sick person, a song in the choir, an hour with a child learning the catechism: these are the pounds we trade for the kingdom. With St Sophia, let us choose the wisdom that looks at what cannot be seen. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told a story about a king who gave his helpers some money and said: use it well until I come back. Two helpers worked hard and made more. One was scared and hid the money in a cloth. God has given each of you something special: maybe you sing well, or draw, or you are good at making friends. Do not hide it! Use it to make someone happy and to make God happy too.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you went away to receive your kingdom and you will come again in glory. While we wait, you have entrusted to us the gifts of your grace. Renew our inner nature day by day by your Holy Spirit, even when our outer nature grows weak. Give us the true wisdom that looks at what cannot be seen, and keep us from the fear that buries what you have given. With St Sophia and all your saints, let us be found trustworthy in small things, so that at your coming we may hear your word of welcome. You live and reign for ever. Amen.
