Word for the day

“He first found his brother Simon and said to him, “We have found the Messiah” (which is translated Anointed).” John 1:41

Reflection

Two disciples of John the Baptist hear him say, “Look, here is the Lamb of God!” and follow Jesus. He turns and asks, “What are you looking for?” They answer, “Rabbi, where are you staying?” and he says, “Come and see.” They spend the day with him. One of them is Andrew, and the first thing he does is find his brother Simon: “We have found the Messiah.” He brings him to Jesus. Andrew is the patron of everyone who brings a brother or sister to Christ.

Paul writes to the Galatians with a mother's tenderness: “My little children, for whom I am again in the pain of childbirth until Christ is formed in you.” Evangelisation is not only information; it is labour and love until Christ takes shape in another person. On the eve of the Christmas fast, we prepare for Christ to be born, not only in Bethlehem but in us.

Full homily

Dear friends, today is the feast of Mar Andrews Shleeha, St Andrew the Apostle. Two disciples of John the Baptist hear him say, look, here is the Lamb of God, and follow Jesus. He turns and asks: what are you looking for? Rabbi, where are you staying? Come and see, he says, and they remain with him that day. Andrew first finds his brother Simon and tells him: we have found the Messiah; and he brings him to Jesus (John 1:36–42).

Paul writes to the Galatians with a mother's heart: my little children, for whom I am again in the pain of childbirth until Christ is formed in you (Galatians 4:19). Bringing others to Christ carries the pain of love.

In this week after the Sunday of the Visitation, like Mary who went in haste to Elizabeth, like Andrew who ran to his brother, let us bring Christ to someone. Say to one person: come and see, to a prayer, to a Holy Qurbono. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

On the feast of Mar Andrews Shleeha, St Andrew the Apostle, the readings speak of how Christ is first found and then formed in us. In John's Gospel Andrew hears the Baptist's word, follows Jesus, stays with him, and at once brings his brother Simon. In Galatians Paul, like a mother in labour, longs for Christ to be “formed” in his converts. Finding Christ is the beginning; letting him take shape in us, and bringing others to him, is the whole Christian life.

Galatians 4:12–20 · Until Christ is formed in you

Setting.

After a long argument from Scripture (Galatians 3:1–4:11), Paul changes his tone. He turns from argument to a personal appeal, reminding the Galatians of their first meeting and of the love they once showed him. Teachers who demanded circumcision had won their hearts, and Paul fears he has lost his children.

Structure.

  • v. 12: “become as I am, for I also have become as you are”.
  • vv. 13–15: memories: Paul came to them ill, and they received him like an angel of God, like Christ himself.
  • vv. 16–18: has telling the truth made him their enemy? The rivals court them for the wrong reasons.
  • vv. 19–20: the image of childbirth, and Paul's wish to be with them.

Key words.

  • astheneia tēs sarkos, physical infirmity: Paul does not say what the illness was. Some suggest an eye disease because of v. 15, but this is only a guess. What matters is that God used his weakness to bring the Gospel.
  • ōdinō, to be in the pain of childbirth: A strong image. Paul is not only a father (1 Corinthians 4:15) but a mother, suffering the labour pains of bringing them to life in Christ, now a second time.
  • morphōthē, is formed: Set beside the image of childbirth, it suggests an unborn child taking shape. The goal of the Christian life is not only to believe in Christ but to have his own form, his likeness, grow within us.

Verse by verse.

“Friends, I beg you, become as I am, for I also have become as you are. You have done me no wrong.” (4:12)

Paul, a Jew, became like a Gentile, living without the Law's ceremonies among them. Now he asks them to become like him: children of God in Christ, not slaves again.

“though my condition put you to the test, you did not scorn or despise me, but welcomed me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus.” (4:14)

In the ancient world sickness could be seen as a curse, and the Greek of v. 14 literally says “you did not spit”, a gesture against evil. The Galatians saw Christ in a sick stranger, as Jesus taught (Matthew 25:40).

“My little children, for whom I am again in the pain of childbirth until Christ is formed in you,” (4:19)

How the Church reads it.

The Second Vatican Council teaches that the Church, contemplating Mary, “becomes herself a mother”: by preaching and baptism she brings to new and immortal life children conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of God (Lumen Gentium 64). Paul's labour pains are the Church's own: every pastor, catechist and parent shares in this work of forming Christ in others. Coming at the start of the journey to Yaldo, the verse reminds us that Christ who was formed in Mary's womb wants to be formed in each of us.

John 1:35–42 · Come and see

Setting.

John's Gospel opens with a sequence of days (1:29, 35, 43; 2:1). John the Baptist has already pointed Jesus out as “the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world” (1:29). Today's passage is the day after: two of the Baptist's disciples leave their master to follow Jesus, and one of them is Andrew. The first disciples come to Jesus through the witness of another.

Andrew, a fisherman from Bethsaida (1:44), was the brother of Simon Peter. The other disciple is not named; many think it is the Beloved Disciple himself, who never names himself in this Gospel.

Structure.

  • vv. 35–36: the Baptist sees Jesus and says, “Look, here is the Lamb of God!”
  • v. 37: two disciples hear and follow Jesus.
  • vv. 38–39: Jesus' first words: “What are you looking for?” “Come and see.” They stay with him.
  • vv. 40–41: Andrew finds his brother: “We have found the Messiah.”
  • v. 42: Andrew brings Simon to Jesus, who gives him a new name, Cephas.

Key words.

  • ho amnos tou theou, the Lamb of God: It gathers several Old Testament images: the Passover lamb (Exodus 12), the servant led like a lamb to the slaughter (Isaiah 53:7), and the lamb God himself will provide (Genesis 22:8).
  • menō, to stay, to remain, to abide: A key word in John. The disciples ask where Jesus “stays” and they “remained” with him. Later Jesus will say, “Abide in me as I abide in you” (15:4). Discipleship is staying with Jesus.
  • Kēphas, Cephas, Rock: The Aramaic kêpā means rock; in Greek it is Petros. A new name in the Bible means a new mission, as with Abram and Jacob.
  • Messias, Messiah: The Greek spelling of the Hebrew and Aramaic word for “anointed”, which the evangelist translates into Greek as Christos.

Verse by verse.

“When Jesus turned and saw them following, he said to them, “What are you looking for?” They said to him, “Rabbi” (which translated means Teacher), “where are you staying?”” (1:38)

The first words of Jesus in John's Gospel are a question about desire. He does not begin with a command but asks what we are seeking.

“He said to them, “Come and see.” They came and saw where he was staying, and they remained with him that day. It was about four o’clock in the afternoon.” (1:39)

Literally “the tenth hour”, as the POC keeps it. The detail sounds like the memory of an eyewitness who never forgot the hour his life changed.

“He first found his brother Simon and said to him, “We have found the Messiah” (which is translated Anointed).” (1:41)

Andrew's first act as a disciple is to bring someone else. He found Christ, and at once he wanted his brother to find him too.

“He brought Simon to Jesus, who looked at him and said, “You are Simon son of John. You are to be called Cephas” (which is translated Peter).” (1:42)

How the Church reads it.

The Eastern Churches call Andrew the Prōtoklētos, the “First-called”, because he was among the first to follow Jesus. St John Chrysostom, preaching on this Gospel, praises Andrew because, once he had learned from Jesus, he did not keep the treasure to himself but ran to share it with his brother (in substance). Every Christian vocation follows this pattern: someone points to Christ, we come and stay with him, and then we bring others.

Tradition says that Andrew preached in Greece and the lands around the Black Sea and was martyred at Patras on a cross; the X-shaped cross associated with him comes from later tradition.

Questions people ask

Why does Jesus change Simon's name before Simon has done anything?

Because the name is God's gift and mission, not a reward. Jesus sees what Simon will become by grace. Andrew brings him, but Jesus calls him.

What does “Lamb of God” mean?

It joins the Passover lamb whose blood saved Israel, the suffering servant of Isaiah 53 and the lamb God provides in Genesis 22. Jesus is the one sacrifice that takes away the sin of the world (CCC 608).

For the Syro-Malankara reader

Andrew and Peter were brothers, and the Church sees their brotherhood in the Churches that honour them. Peter is remembered in Antioch, the mother Church of the West Syriac tradition to which the Malankara Church belongs, and in Rome, while Constantinople honours Andrew as its patron. Every year on this feast the Pope sends a delegation to the Ecumenical Patriarch, and the Patriarch sends one to Rome for Sts Peter and Paul: a sign of the longing for full unity among Christians. On Mar Andrews' feast the Malankara faithful pray for that unity and ask for Andrew's zeal to bring their own brothers and sisters to Christ.

For study and teaching

  • Jesus asks, “What are you looking for?” How would I answer him honestly today?
  • Andrew first found his own brother. Who in my family or neighbourhood could I bring closer to Christ?
  • Paul longs for Christ to be “formed” in his people. What part of Christ's likeness is still taking shape in me?
