Monday after the Sunday of the Visitation · Mar Andrews Shleeha (St Andrew the Apostle)

30 November 2026 · Syro-Malankara Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle Galatians 4:12–20 · Until Christ is formed in you
  • Gospel John 1:35–42 · Come and see

Word for the day

“He first found his brother Simon and said to him, “We have found the Messiah” (which is translated Anointed).” John 1:41

Reflection

Two disciples of John the Baptist hear him say, “Look, here is the Lamb of God!” and follow Jesus. He turns and asks, “What are you looking for?” They answer, “Rabbi, where are you staying?” and he says, “Come and see.” They spend the day with him. One of them is Andrew, and the first thing he does is find his brother Simon: “We have found the Messiah.” He brings him to Jesus. Andrew is the patron of everyone who brings a brother or sister to Christ.

Paul writes to the Galatians with a mother's tenderness: “My little children, for whom I am again in the pain of childbirth until Christ is formed in you.” Evangelisation is not only information; it is labour and love until Christ takes shape in another person. On the eve of the Christmas fast, we prepare for Christ to be born, not only in Bethlehem but in us.

Homily outline

Theme: “We have found the Messiah.”

St Andrew, the one who brings others.

1. Come and see

  • The psalm invites us to taste and see.
  • Jesus invites the disciples to come and see.
“O taste and see that the LORD is good; happy are those who take refuge in him.” Psalm 34:8
“He said to them, “Come and see.”” John 1:39

Apply: Spend fifteen minutes alone with Jesus today.

2. Bring your brother

  • The nations say: come, let us go up to the Lord.
  • Andrew brings Simon to Jesus.
“Many peoples shall come and say, “Come, let us go up to the mountain of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob;” Isaiah 2:3
“He first found his brother Simon and said to him, “We have found the Messiah” (which is translated Anointed).” John 1:41

Apply: Invite one person to the Holy Qurbono.

3. The pain of childbirth

  • God never forgets the child of his womb.
  • Paul labours until Christ is formed in his people.
“Can a woman forget her nursing child, or show no compassion for the child of her womb? Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you.” Isaiah 49:15
“My little children, for whom I am again in the pain of childbirth until Christ is formed in you,” Galatians 4:19

Apply: Pray daily for one person you want to bring to faith.

Andrew found Jesus and brought his brother.

May Christ be formed in us this Christmas fast.

Take-home resolution: Pray for one person by name every day of the fast.

Full homily

Dear friends, today is the feast of Mar Andrews Shleeha, St Andrew the Apostle. Two disciples of John the Baptist hear him say, look, here is the Lamb of God, and follow Jesus. He turns and asks: what are you looking for? Rabbi, where are you staying? Come and see, he says, and they remain with him that day. Andrew first finds his brother Simon and tells him: we have found the Messiah; and he brings him to Jesus (John 1:36–42).

Paul writes to the Galatians with a mother's heart: my little children, for whom I am again in the pain of childbirth until Christ is formed in you (Galatians 4:19). Bringing others to Christ carries the pain of love.

In this week after the Sunday of the Visitation, like Mary who went in haste to Elizabeth, like Andrew who ran to his brother, let us bring Christ to someone. Say to one person: come and see, to a prayer, to a Holy Qurbono. Amen.

For children

Children, when Andrew found Jesus he ran and brought his brother. Tell someone too: come and see!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Lamb of God, you invited Andrew to come and see. Let us stay with you, bring others to you as he brought Peter, and let you be formed in us during this holy fast. Amen.
