Word for the day

“For the Son of Man came to seek out and to save the lost.” Luke 19:10

Reflection

Four days after Suloko, the Church is in the upper room. The Lord has gone up to the Father, the Spirit has not yet come, and the Panchangam reminds us that in these ten days no wedding celebrations are held: the whole Church holds its breath and prays. It is fitting that today we remember one of the men who sat in that very room. St Luke gives the list of those who waited: “Peter, and John, and James, and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, and Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James.” This is Mar Shemun Shleeha, whom our Church calls “the zealous one.” St Matthew and St Mark call him “the Cananaean,” from an Aramaic word for zeal; St Luke translates it as “the Zealot.” Whether Simon had belonged to the party of armed patriots who longed to drive out the Romans, or whether the name simply marked a man burning with zeal for God's Law, the Gospels do not say, and scholars still discuss it. What happened to him after Pentecost is told differently by different Churches: some ancient traditions send him with St Jude to preach in Persia and to die there as a martyr; others speak of Egypt or other lands. We hold to what is sure: Jesus chose him, Jesus changed the direction of his zeal, and on the day of Pentecost he was among those on whom the fire came down.

The Gospel shows us why such a man could follow Jesus. In Jericho there lived Zacchaeus, “a chief tax collector” who “was rich.” Tax collectors worked for the occupying power and often took more than was due; a zealot would have counted him a traitor to his people. Yet Zacchaeus, short in stature and long in curiosity, “ran ahead and climbed a sycamore tree to see him.” Jesus stopped beneath the tree: “Zacchaeus, hurry and come down; for I must stay at your house today.” The crowd grumbled, “He has gone to be the guest of one who is a sinner.” But grace had already done its work. Zacchaeus stood and said, “Look, half of my possessions, Lord, I will give to the poor; and if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I will pay back four times as much.” Then came the great word: “Today salvation has come to this house, because he too is a son of Abraham. For the Son of Man came to seek out and to save the lost.” Think of the company of the Twelve: Simon the Zealot and Matthew the tax collector sat at the same table and ate the same bread. Only Jesus could make brothers of such men. St Augustine says that Zacchaeus climbed the sycamore as we must climb the Cross: the humble wood that seems foolish to the proud is the place from which we see the Lord passing by (Sermon 174, in substance). Simon's zeal did not die when he met Jesus; it was turned from hatred of the sinner to love of the lost.

St Paul's words to the Corinthians come from the same season of the year. “I will stay in Ephesus until Pentecost,” he writes, “for a wide door for effective work has opened to me, and there are many adversaries.” Paul counted the days towards Pentecost, as we do now. He saw a door open, and he saw opposition too, and he did not run from either. Then he speaks with tenderness of his younger helper: “If Timothy comes, see that he has nothing to fear among you, for he is doing the work of the Lord just as I am; therefore let no one despise him.” And of Apollos, who would not come just then: “He will come when he has the opportunity.” Here is zeal that has learned patience and gentleness. It does not crush the young or force the reluctant; it waits for God's time. That is the lesson of these ten days. The apostles did not rush out of the upper room on the day after the Ascension to begin their own campaign. They waited, “constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus.” Holy zeal begins on its knees. Then the Spirit opens the wide door.

Homily outline

Theme: “For the Son of Man came to seek out and to save the lost.”

On the memory of Mar Shemun Shleeha the Zealot, in the days of waiting before Pentecost: the apostle with a fiery heart, the tax collector found by grace, and zeal that waits for the Spirit.

1. Simon the Zealot: a fire chosen by Christ

  • The Cananaean, the zealous one.
  • Waiting in the upper room with Mary.
  • Zeal turned from anger to love.
“It is zeal for your house that has consumed me; the insults of those who insult you have fallen on me.” Psalm 69:9
“Do not lag in zeal, be ardent in spirit, serve the Lord.” Romans 12:11

Apply: Name the thing you are most zealous about and offer it to Jesus in prayer each morning until Pentecost.

2. Today salvation has come to this house

  • A short man in a sycamore tree.
  • Jesus comes first; goodness follows.
  • Half to the poor, fourfold to the wronged.
“I will seek the lost, and I will bring back the strayed” Ezekiel 34:16
“The saying is sure and worthy of full acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners” 1 Timothy 1:15

Apply: Put right one thing you owe another person, in money, in time or in an apology, before Sunday.

3. A wide door at Pentecost

  • Paul counts the days to Pentecost.
  • Protecting the young worker.
  • Zeal that begins on its knees.
“Wait for the Lord; be strong, and let your heart take courage; wait for the Lord!” Psalm 27:14
“All these were constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus, as well as his brothers.” Acts 1:14

Apply: Join the novena of prayer for the Holy Spirit with your family each evening until Pentecost.

Jesus chose a zealot and a tax collector and made them brothers; the Spirit purifies our zeal without putting out its fire.

In these ten days the Church waits on its knees, and the Lord stops at every house that longs to see him.

Take-home resolution: Pray for one person you had given up on, put right one wrong, and wait for the Spirit in prayer with Mary.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! We are living in the ten days between Suloko and Pentecost. The apostles are in the upper room, waiting and praying, and today our Church remembers one of them: Mar Shemun Shleeha, St Simon the Zealot. We know little about his life, but his name tells us something important. He was a man of zeal, a man with fire in his heart. Before he met Jesus, that fire may have burned against the Romans and against anyone who served them. After he met Jesus, it burned for the Kingdom of God.

In the Gospel we meet the very kind of man a zealot would have despised: Zacchaeus, a chief tax collector, rich from other people's money. The crowd blocked his way, so he ran ahead and climbed a sycamore tree. And Jesus stopped. He looked up and said, “Zacchaeus, hurry and come down; for I must stay at your house today.” Notice: Jesus did not wait for Zacchaeus to become good before he came to his house. He came first, and his coming made Zacchaeus good. The man who had taken now began to give: half of his possessions to the poor, and four times back to anyone he had cheated.

Now picture the table of the Twelve. Simon the Zealot on one side, Matthew the former tax collector on the other. In the world they would have been enemies. At the table of Jesus they were brothers. This is what the Holy Spirit will do at Pentecost: not take away our zeal, but purify it. The anger goes; the fire stays. St Paul, waiting in Ephesus “until Pentecost,” shows us what purified zeal looks like. He sees a wide door and many adversaries, and he is not afraid. He tells the Corinthians to protect young Timothy and to let Apollos come in his own time. That is zeal with patience, zeal with gentleness.

So in these days of waiting, let us ask ourselves: is there a Zacchaeus in my life, someone I have written off? Let us pray for that person by name until Pentecost. And let us let Jesus say to us too, “I must stay at your house today.” He will say it at this altar, when he comes to us in his Body and Blood. Let us hurry down and welcome him with joy.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Have you ever climbed a tree to see something far away? In today's Gospel a short man named Zacchaeus climbed a tree to see Jesus, because the crowd was too big. Nobody liked Zacchaeus, because he took too much money from people. But Jesus stopped right under the tree, looked up and called him by his name! Jesus went to his house, and Zacchaeus was so happy that he decided to give money to the poor and pay back everyone he had cheated. Today we also remember St Simon, one of Jesus' twelve friends. He had a fiery heart, and Jesus taught him to use that fire for love. This week, be kind to someone others leave out. That is what Jesus did.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you came to seek out and to save the lost, and you stopped beneath the sycamore tree to call Zacchaeus by name. You chose Mar Shemun the Zealot and turned the fire of his heart towards your Kingdom. In these days of waiting between your Ascension and the coming of the Holy Spirit, gather us with your apostles and with your holy Mother in prayer. Purify our zeal from anger and pride, make it patient and gentle, and open for us the wide door of your work. By the prayers of Mar Shemun Shleeha, let salvation come to our homes today, and make us glad to welcome you. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory for ever. Amen.
