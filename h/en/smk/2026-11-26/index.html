Full homily

Dear friends, Paul warns the Corinthians to flee from idolatry, and gives a reason that is pure Eucharistic theology: the cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a sharing in the blood of Christ? The bread that we break, is it not a sharing in the body of Christ? Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body (1 Corinthians 10:16–17).

In the Gospel Jesus teaches with authority in the synagogue at Capernaum; a man with an unclean spirit cries out: I know who you are, the Holy One of God. Jesus drives it out with a word, and all are amazed: what kind of utterance is this (Luke 4:33–36)!

It is the Holy One of God whom we receive at the Holy Qurbono. Those who share the one cup cannot serve other masters: money, power, superstition. This Sunday let us come to church a moment early and prepare our hearts in silence. Amen.

For children

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Today's readings show two meals and two powers. Paul tells the Corinthians that they cannot share both the table of the Lord and the table of demons: the cup and the bread unite us with Christ and with one another. In Capernaum Jesus speaks with authority, and an unclean spirit has to leave at his word. The One who drives out evil is the One who gives himself at the table of the Holy Qurbono.

1 Corinthians 10:14–22 · The cup of blessing and the bread we break

Setting.

Corinth was a city full of temples. Meat sold in the market had often been offered to an idol, and social life included dinners in temple dining rooms. In chapters 8–10 Paul answers questions about this. Here he reaches the heart of the matter: taking part in a sacrificial meal in honour of an idol is incompatible with the Eucharist.

Structure.

  • vv. 14–15: the command: flee from idolatry; judge for yourselves.
  • vv. 16–17: the Lord's Supper: the cup and the bread are a sharing in Christ's blood and body, and make us one body.
  • v. 18: an example from Israel: those who eat the sacrifices share in the altar.
  • vv. 19–20: idols are nothing, but pagan sacrifice involves demons.
  • vv. 21–22: the conclusion: you cannot share both tables.

Key words.

  • koinōnia, sharing, communion: A real participation, not a mere reminder. From this verse the Church calls the reception of the Eucharist “Holy Communion”.
  • potērion tēs eulogias, cup of blessing: At a Jewish festive meal, the cup over which the thanksgiving was said after the meal; at Passover, one of the four cups. Jesus took such a cup at the Last Supper.
  • trapeza kyriou, table of the Lord: Malachi calls the altar of the Temple “the Lord's table” (Malachi 1:7). Paul uses the same words for the Christian Eucharist.

Verse by verse.

“The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a sharing in the blood of Christ? The bread that we break, is it not a sharing in the body of Christ?” (10:16)

Paul does not prove the Real Presence; he takes it for granted as something the Corinthians already believe, and draws a conclusion from it.

“Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread.” (10:17)

The body of Christ we receive makes us the body of Christ we are. Communion with Christ and communion with one another cannot be separated.

“You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons. You cannot partake of the table of the Lord and the table of demons.” (10:21)

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism uses these verses to explain why the Eucharist is called Holy Communion (CCC 1331) and how the Eucharist makes the Church (CCC 1396). St Augustine told the newly baptised: if you are the body of Christ and his members, it is your own mystery that is placed on the Lord's table; be what you see, and receive what you are (in substance, Sermon 272). In the Holy Qurbono the priest breaks the one bread, and the people who share it become one body.

Luke 4:31–37 · He commands with authority

Setting.

Jesus has just been rejected at Nazareth (4:16–30). He goes down to Capernaum, a fishing town on the north shore of the Sea of Galilee, which becomes the centre of his ministry. This is the first miracle Luke reports, and it takes place in a synagogue on the Sabbath.

Structure.

  • vv. 31–32: Jesus teaches, and the people are astounded at his authority.
  • vv. 33–34: a man with an unclean spirit cries out.
  • v. 35: Jesus rebukes the spirit, and it leaves without harming the man.
  • vv. 36–37: amazement, and the report spreads.

Key words.

  • exousia, authority: The right and the power to act. The teachers of the Law usually taught by quoting earlier teachers; Jesus speaks in his own name.
  • ho hagios tou theou, the Holy One of God: The demon knows who Jesus is better than the crowd. Knowledge alone is not faith (compare James 2:19).
  • epetimēsen, rebuked: The same verb is used when Jesus rebukes the fever (4:39) and the wind and waves (8:24). He speaks to evil as its Lord.

Verse by verse.

“They were astounded at his teaching, because he spoke with authority.” (4:32)
““Let us alone! What have you to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are, the Holy One of God.”” (4:34)

“What have you to do with us?” is a Semitic way of saying: we have nothing in common. The answer to “Have you come to destroy us?” is yes.

“But Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be silent, and come out of him!” When the demon had thrown him down before them, he came out of him without having done him any harm.” (4:35)

Only Luke adds “without having done him any harm”. Jesus' power over evil is always gentle with the person it frees.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that Jesus' exorcisms deliver people from the domination of demons and anticipate his great victory over “the ruler of this world”; the coming of God's Kingdom means the defeat of Satan's (CCC 550). The Church continues this ministry in Christ's name, in baptism and, when needed, in exorcism (CCC 1673).

Questions people ask

Is Paul saying idols have real power?

No. An idol is nothing (10:19; 8:4). But worship given to what is not God opens a door to evil. Christians must not divide their loyalty.

For study and teaching

  • Paul says that “we who are many are one body”. How does my reception of the Holy Qurbono change the way I treat the people beside me?
  • Jesus' word releases the man “without having done him any harm”. Where do I need his word to release me today?
