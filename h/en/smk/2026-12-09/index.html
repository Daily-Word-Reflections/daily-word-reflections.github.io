Readings

  • Epistle Hebrews 1:1–4 · God has spoken to us by a Son

Word for the day

“And he answered them, “Go and tell John what you have seen and heard: the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised,” Luke 7:22

Reflection

Hebrews opens with a sweeping vision: “Long ago God spoke to our ancestors in many and various ways by the prophets, but in these last days he has spoken to us by a Son,” through whom he made the worlds. The Son is “the reflection of God's glory and the exact imprint of God's very being”; having made purification for sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high. The child of Bethlehem is God's final and complete Word.

John the Baptist, in prison, sends disciples to ask Jesus: “Are you the one who is to come, or are we to wait for another?” Jesus does not answer with a title but with signs: the blind see, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor hear good news, all fulfilling Isaiah. “And blessed is anyone who takes no offense at me.” Even great saints have dark hours; Jesus answers them with his works.

Homily outline

Theme: “Are you the one who is to come?”

God's final Word answers with works.

1. Seated at the right hand

  • The psalm sees the Lord seated at God's right hand.
  • Hebrews: God has spoken by a Son.
“The LORD says to my lord, “Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies your footstool.”” Psalm 110:1
“Long ago God spoke to our ancestors in many and various ways by the prophets, but in these last days he has spoken to us by a Son, whom he appointed heir of all things, through whom he also created the worlds.” Hebrews 1:1–2

Apply: Read Hebrews 1 slowly today.

2. Good news to the poor

  • Isaiah: the Spirit anoints the Servant.
  • Jesus points to the signs of the kingdom.
“The spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me; he has sent me to bring good news to the oppressed,” Isaiah 61:1
“And he answered them, “Go and tell John what you have seen and heard: the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised,” Luke 7:22

Apply: Do one work of mercy for a poor or sick person.

3. The rejected stone

  • The stone the builders rejected became the cornerstone.
  • Blessed is the one who takes no offence.
“The stone that the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone.” Psalm 118:22
“And blessed is anyone who takes no offense at me.” Luke 7:23

Apply: Tell Jesus honestly one doubt you carry.

God's last Word is his Son.

His works answer our doubts.

Take-home resolution: Note each evening one sign of God's goodness.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Letter to the Hebrews begins: long ago God spoke to our ancestors in many and various ways by the prophets, but in these last days He has spoken to us by a Son (Hebrews 1:1–2). The Son is the reflection of God's glory and the exact imprint of His being.

God's final word is not a book or a law, but a Person. At Yaldo we celebrate the Word made flesh.

In the Gospel John, in prison, sends disciples to ask Jesus: are you the one who is to come? Jesus answers: go and tell John what you have seen and heard: the blind see, the lame walk, the poor have good news. And blessed is anyone who takes no offence at me (Luke 7:19–23).

Sometimes Jesus does not act as we expect, and we are tempted to take offence. Let us trust Him even when we do not understand. He is the Son through whom God speaks to us. Amen.

For children

Children, God speaks to us through Jesus. When we read the Gospel, we hear Jesus speaking to us!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Son through whom the Father speaks, you answered John's doubt with signs of mercy. Answer our questions, open our eyes to your works, and keep us from taking offence at your ways. Amen.
