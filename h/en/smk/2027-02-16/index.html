Word for the day

“For by grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God” Ephesians 2:8

Reflection

God now turns to Sarai: “you shall not call her Sarai, but Sarah shall be her name. I will bless her, and moreover I will give you a son by her.” And the father of believers falls on his face and laughs: “Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? Can Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?” Then he makes a quieter, more human prayer: “O that Ishmael might live in your sight!” He already has a son; perhaps he would settle for that. God's answer is both firm and tender: “No, but your wife Sarah shall bear you a son, and you shall name him Isaac.” Isaac means “he laughs”: God will turn Abraham's doubtful laugh into the laughter of joy. And Ishmael is not forgotten: “As for Ishmael, I have heard you; I will bless him.” God's plan is larger than our hopes, and kinder than our fears.

Paul tells the Ephesians that every Christian life begins the way Isaac's did: with something impossible that only God can do. “You were dead through the trespasses and sins in which you once lived… But God, who is rich in mercy, out of the great love with which he loved us even when we were dead through our trespasses, made us alive together with Christ.” Our fasting does not buy this life: “by grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God.” Yet grace is not idle: “we are what he has made us, created in Christ Jesus for good works.” That is why Jesus gives a simple test: “You will know them by their fruits… every good tree bears good fruit.” St Augustine says that when God crowns our merits he is crowning his own gifts (in substance). In the Fast we do not try to grow fruit by our own strength; we stay grafted to the One who makes the tree good.

Homily outline

Theme: “By grace you have been saved through faith.”

God's impossible gift to Abraham, God's impossible gift to us, and the fruit that grace bears.

1. Abraham laughs

  • Sarai becomes Sarah.
  • “O that Ishmael might live in your sight!”
  • Isaac: God turns doubt into joy.
“The nations shall see your vindication, and all the kings your glory; and you shall be called by a new name that the mouth of the Lord will give.” Isaiah 62:2
“So if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation: everything old has passed away; see, everything has become new!” 2 Corinthians 5:17

Apply: Bring to God the promise you find hardest to believe, and pray over it each day this week.

2. By grace

  • Dead through trespasses.
  • Rich in mercy.
  • Not your own doing.
“A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you; and I will remove from your body the heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh.” Ezekiel 36:26
“For we are what he has made us, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand to be our way of life.” Ephesians 2:10

Apply: Make a good confession this week, receiving mercy as pure gift.

3. Known by their fruits

  • Wolves in sheep's clothing.
  • Good tree, good fruit.
  • Grafted into Christ.
“They are like trees planted by streams of water, which yield their fruit in its season, and their leaves do not wither. In all that they do, they prosper.” Psalm 1:3
“By contrast, the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, generosity, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.” Galatians 5:22–23

Apply: Choose one fruit of the Spirit to practise deliberately for the rest of the Fast.

What is impossible for us is the beginning of God's work.

Grace received in faith becomes fruit in good works.

Take-home resolution: Thank God each night of the Fast for one gift you did not earn.

Full homily

Dear friends, God promised Abraham a son by Sarah, and Abraham fell on his face and laughed: can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? (Genesis 17:17). God did not take back the promise. He named the child Isaac, he laughs.

Abraham also prayed: O that Ishmael might live in your sight! And God answered: as for Ishmael, I have heard you; I will bless him (Genesis 17:18, 20). God's plan is larger than our hopes and kinder than our fears.

Paul says that we too were given life when it was impossible: you were dead through the trespasses and sins... but God, who is rich in mercy, made us alive together with Christ. By grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God (Ephesians 2:1, 4–5, 8).

Grace bears fruit. Jesus says: you will know them by their fruits; every good tree bears good fruit (Matthew 7:16–17). Our fasting does not earn God's love; it opens us to receive it, so that we may bear the fruit he has prepared. Amen.

For children

Children, Abraham was a hundred years old and Sarah was ninety when God promised them a baby. Abraham laughed! But God kept his promise, and baby Isaac was born. Nothing is impossible for God. God's love is a gift; we cannot buy it, only say thank you.

Prayer

God rich in mercy, you gave Abraham a son when it was impossible and turned his laughter of doubt into the laughter of joy. You made us alive with Christ when we were dead in our sins. Let us never forget that all is your gift. Make us good trees, rooted in your grace, bearing the fruit of good works which you prepared for us. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
