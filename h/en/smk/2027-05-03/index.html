Word for the day

“See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” 2 Corinthians 6:2

Reflection

Three days before Suloko the readings speak of time and of light. St Paul begins with an appeal: “As we work together with him, we urge you also not to accept the grace of God in vain.” Then he quotes the prophet Isaiah, “At an acceptable time I have listened to you, and on a day of salvation I have helped you,” and turns the old promise into a present fact: “See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” We are always tempted to place God's time somewhere else: in the past, when we were more fervent, or in the future, when the children are settled, the loan is paid and life is quieter. Paul says no. The day of salvation is today, this Monday morning, with the work and the worries it brings. The Risen Lord does not wait for better circumstances to meet us. In these last days of the fifty days of Qyomtho the Church is preparing to see him ascend and to receive his Spirit; the preparation happens now or it does not happen at all.

Then Paul draws a portrait of the apostolic life, and it is not what anyone would choose. He lists “great endurance, in afflictions, hardships, calamities, beatings, imprisonments, riots, labors, sleepless nights, hunger.” Alongside these he names the weapons of a servant of God: “purity, knowledge, patience, kindness, holiness of spirit, genuine love, truthful speech, and the power of God.” And he ends with a chain of paradoxes that only Easter can explain: “as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything.” How can someone who has nothing possess everything? Only if the one thing he has is Christ, in whom all things hold together. St John Chrysostom, commenting on this passage, said that Paul lists the hardships not to boast but to show that the grace of God was strong enough to carry him through them all (Homilies on 2 Corinthians 12, in substance). The Christian is not someone protected from trouble; he is someone in whom joy survives trouble.

In the Gospel Jesus says in the temple: “I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.” He spoke these words, John notes, “in the treasury of the temple,” close to where the great lamps were lit at the feast of Tabernacles. The Pharisees object that he is testifying for himself, and Jesus answers that he does not stand alone: “I testify on my own behalf, and the Father who sent me testifies on my behalf.” The argument is a reminder that faith is not a private opinion; it rests on the witness of the Father to the Son, and soon, at Pentecost, on the witness of the Spirit. Our Church begins each day with the morning prayer of the Shehimo, greeting Christ as the light that has dawned on us. Let the light be practical today: switch off one thing that darkens your mind, open one page of the Gospel, and walk in the light by one honest act. “No one arrested him, because his hour had not yet come.” His hour came on the Cross, and its light still shines from the empty tomb.

Homily outline

Theme: “See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!”

On Monday of the fifth week after Qyomtho, three days before Suloko: God's today, the riches of the poor apostle, and the light of the world.

1. Now is the day of salvation

  • Grace not received in vain.
  • Not yesterday, not someday.
  • Preparing now for the Ascension and the Spirit.
“In a time of favor I have answered you, on a day of salvation I have helped you;” Isaiah 49:8
“Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion.” Hebrews 3:15

Apply: Write down one thing you have postponed for God and set a day this week to do it.

2. Having nothing, and yet possessing everything

  • The hardships of an apostle.
  • The weapons of righteousness.
  • Joy that survives sorrow.
“The Lord is my chosen portion and my cup; you hold my lot.” Psalm 16:5
“I can do all things through him who strengthens me.” Philippians 4:13

Apply: In one difficulty you face this week, thank God aloud for something he has given you in it.

3. The light of the world

  • Spoken in the temple treasury.
  • The Father bears witness.
  • Walking in the light.
“The Lord is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?” Psalm 27:1
“but if we walk in the light as he himself is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin.” 1 John 1:7

Apply: Pray the morning prayer of the Shehimo, or a short morning prayer, each day this week, greeting Christ as your light.

God's time is today; grace postponed is grace wasted.

The one who has Christ has everything and walks in his light.

Take-home resolution: Do today the good you have postponed, rejoice in trouble, and walk in the light of Christ as you prepare for Suloko.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! St Paul begs the Corinthians: do not accept the grace of God in vain. Then he says: see, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation! (2 Corinthians 6:1–2). We like to put God's time in the past or the future. Paul puts it here: today, this Monday, with all its ordinary work.

Paul describes his life as an apostle: afflictions, hardships, beatings, sleepless nights, hunger. And yet he says: as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything (2 Corinthians 6:10). Only someone who has Christ can say that. Having him, he has everything.

In the Gospel Jesus says: I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life (John 8:12). He is not one light among many; he is the light by which we see everything else. And his witness is true, because the Father who sent him testifies to him.

In three days we celebrate Suloko, the Ascension, and then we wait for the Holy Spirit. Let us not wait for a better time to turn to God. Today let us do the good we have been postponing, and let us walk in his light. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Have you ever been in a room when the lights suddenly went off? It is hard to find the door, and you might bump into a chair. Jesus says: “I am the light of the world.” When we follow Jesus, we do not get lost. St Paul also says something important: “Now is the time!” Don't wait until tomorrow to say sorry or to help someone. Do it today, and Jesus' light will shine through you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, you are the light of the world, and whoever follows you will not walk in darkness. Do not let us receive your grace in vain. Teach us that now is the acceptable time and today the day of salvation. When we are sorrowful, keep us rejoicing; when we are poor, make us rich in you; when we have nothing, let us possess everything by possessing you. Prepare our hearts to see you ascend and to receive your Holy Spirit. You live and reign for ever. Amen.
