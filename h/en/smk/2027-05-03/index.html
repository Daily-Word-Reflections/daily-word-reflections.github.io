Word for the day

“See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” 2 Corinthians 6:2

Reflection

Three days before Suloko the readings speak of time and of light. St Paul begins with an appeal: “As we work together with him, we urge you also not to accept the grace of God in vain.” Then he quotes the prophet Isaiah, “At an acceptable time I have listened to you, and on a day of salvation I have helped you,” and turns the old promise into a present fact: “See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” We are always tempted to place God's time somewhere else: in the past, when we were more fervent, or in the future, when the children are settled, the loan is paid and life is quieter. Paul says no. The day of salvation is today, this Monday morning, with the work and the worries it brings. The Risen Lord does not wait for better circumstances to meet us. In these last days of the fifty days of Qyomtho the Church is preparing to see him ascend and to receive his Spirit; the preparation happens now or it does not happen at all.

Then Paul draws a portrait of the apostolic life, and it is not what anyone would choose. He lists “great endurance, in afflictions, hardships, calamities, beatings, imprisonments, riots, labors, sleepless nights, hunger.” Alongside these he names the weapons of a servant of God: “purity, knowledge, patience, kindness, holiness of spirit, genuine love, truthful speech, and the power of God.” And he ends with a chain of paradoxes that only Easter can explain: “as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything.” How can someone who has nothing possess everything? Only if the one thing he has is Christ, in whom all things hold together. St John Chrysostom, commenting on this passage, said that Paul lists the hardships not to boast but to show that the grace of God was strong enough to carry him through them all (Homilies on 2 Corinthians 12, in substance). The Christian is not someone protected from trouble; he is someone in whom joy survives trouble.

In the Gospel Jesus says in the temple: “I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.” He spoke these words, John notes, “in the treasury of the temple,” close to where the great lamps were lit at the feast of Tabernacles. The Pharisees object that he is testifying for himself, and Jesus answers that he does not stand alone: “I testify on my own behalf, and the Father who sent me testifies on my behalf.” The argument is a reminder that faith is not a private opinion; it rests on the witness of the Father to the Son, and soon, at Pentecost, on the witness of the Spirit. Our Church begins each day with the morning prayer of the Shehimo, greeting Christ as the light that has dawned on us. Let the light be practical today: switch off one thing that darkens your mind, open one page of the Gospel, and walk in the light by one honest act. “No one arrested him, because his hour had not yet come.” His hour came on the Cross, and its light still shines from the empty tomb.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! St Paul begs the Corinthians: do not accept the grace of God in vain. Then he says: see, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation! (2 Corinthians 6:1–2). We like to put God's time in the past or the future. Paul puts it here: today, this Monday, with all its ordinary work.

Paul describes his life as an apostle: afflictions, hardships, beatings, sleepless nights, hunger. And yet he says: as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything (2 Corinthians 6:10). Only someone who has Christ can say that. Having him, he has everything.

In the Gospel Jesus says: I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life (John 8:12). He is not one light among many; he is the light by which we see everything else. And his witness is true, because the Father who sent him testifies to him.

In three days we celebrate Suloko, the Ascension, and then we wait for the Holy Spirit. Let us not wait for a better time to turn to God. Today let us do the good we have been postponing, and let us walk in his light. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Have you ever been in a room when the lights suddenly went off? It is hard to find the door, and you might bump into a chair. Jesus says: “I am the light of the world.” When we follow Jesus, we do not get lost. St Paul also says something important: “Now is the time!” Don't wait until tomorrow to say sorry or to help someone. Do it today, and Jesus' light will shine through you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, you are the light of the world, and whoever follows you will not walk in darkness. Do not let us receive your grace in vain. Teach us that now is the acceptable time and today the day of salvation. When we are sorrowful, keep us rejoicing; when we are poor, make us rich in you; when we have nothing, let us possess everything by possessing you. Prepare our hearts to see you ascend and to receive your Holy Spirit. You live and reign for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Today Paul cries out, “See, now is the acceptable time”, and describes an apostle's life as a string of paradoxes: sorrowful yet always rejoicing, having nothing yet possessing everything. In the Gospel Jesus says, “I am the light of the world.” The light of Christ shines most clearly in lives like Paul's, where human weakness and God's power meet. The key words are now and light.

2 Corinthians 6:1–10 · Now is the acceptable time

Setting.

Paul has just written that God “reconciled us to himself through Christ” and made him an ambassador with the message “be reconciled to God” (5:18–20). Now he urges the Corinthians not to let that grace go to waste, and he quotes Isaiah 49:8 in its Greek form. He then defends his ministry, not by listing successes, but by listing what he has suffered and how God has carried him through.

Structure.

  • vv. 1–2: do not accept God's grace in vain; now is the day of salvation.
  • vv. 3–7: the servants of God commend themselves by endurance and by the gifts of the Spirit.
  • vv. 8–10: seven paradoxes of the apostolic life.

Key words.

  • euprosdektos, acceptable: Isaiah's Greek text had dektos, “acceptable”. Paul repeats the word with a stronger form, “most acceptable”. The time of God's favour that the prophet promised has arrived.
  • nyn, now: Paul says it twice: “now is the acceptable time; now is the day of salvation”. The grace of God is never only for later. It meets us today.

Verse by verse.

“As we work together with him, we urge you also not to accept the grace of God in vain.” (6:1)

Paul calls himself a co-worker with God. Grace is pure gift, but it can be received “in vain” if it does not change the life of the one who receives it.

“See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” (6:2)

Paul adds his own comment to the prophet's words. He does not wait for a better moment to call his readers to conversion.

“as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything.” (6:10)

These lines describe Christ himself, who “though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor” (8:9). The apostle shares the pattern of his Lord.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism quotes 2 Corinthians 6:2 when it speaks of the Last Judgement: the message of the Judgement calls people to conversion while God is still giving them “the acceptable time” and “the day of salvation” (CCC 1041). The Church has always seen in Paul's list of paradoxes a portrait of every faithful pastor and every Christian who suffers for Christ. What looks like loss is gain, because the life of Jesus is made visible in mortal flesh (4:10–11).

John 8:12–20 · I am the light of the world

Setting.

John sets these words at the festival of Booths (7:2). During that feast great lamps were lit in the Court of the Women in the Temple, and the light was said to shine over all Jerusalem. Jesus speaks “in the treasury of the temple” (8:20), which was in that same court. In the oldest manuscripts today's passage (8:12) follows directly on 7:52; the story of the woman caught in adultery (7:53–8:11) is missing there and was placed here later, though the Church receives it as inspired Scripture.

The Pharisees object that a man cannot be a witness in his own case. Jesus answers that he is not alone: the Father bears witness with him.

Structure.

  • v. 12: “I am the light of the world.”
  • vv. 13–18: the debate about valid testimony: Jesus and the Father as two witnesses.
  • vv. 19–20: “Where is your Father?”; his hour has not yet come.

Key words.

  • egō eimi to phōs tou kosmou, I am the light of the world: One of the great “I am” sayings in John. In the Old Testament light belongs to God: “The Lord is my light and my salvation” (Psalm 27:1). The Law too was called a lamp (Psalm 119:105). Jesus claims to be what the feast's lamps only pointed to.
  • martyria, testimony, witness: A key word of John's Gospel. The Law required two witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15). Jesus' second witness is the Father, who speaks through his works and through the Scriptures (5:36–39).

Verse by verse.

“I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.” (8:12)

The promise is for those who “follow”, a word for discipleship. The pillar of fire led Israel through the desert at night (Exodus 13:21). Now the light walks ahead of us in person.

“You judge by human standards; I judge no one.” (8:15)

Literally, “according to the flesh”, that is, by appearances. Jesus came not to condemn but to save (3:17).

“You know neither me nor my Father. If you knew me, you would know my Father also.” (8:19)

To know Jesus is to know the Father. Later Jesus says to Philip, “Whoever has seen me has seen the Father” (14:9).

How the Church reads it.

The Church confesses Christ as “Light from Light” in the Creed. The Fathers called Baptism “enlightenment”, because those who receive it are filled with the light of the Word (CCC 1216). The Second Vatican Council begins its document on the Church with the words Lumen Gentium, “Christ is the light of the nations”, and adds that this light shines on the face of the Church (Lumen Gentium 1). The Church has no light of her own; she reflects his.

Questions people ask

Is Jesus attacking the Pharisees in this passage?

John shows a sharp debate, but Jesus is not rejecting the Pharisees as people. Many of them shared his faith in the resurrection and his love for the Law, and Nicodemus, a Pharisee, became a disciple (3:1; 19:39). The question at stake is who Jesus is, and it is put to every reader.

For study and teaching

  • Paul says “now is the acceptable time”. What step toward God have I been putting off for later?
  • Where in my home or work do I most need the light of Christ to drive out darkness?
