Word for the day

“One does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God.” Matthew 4:4

Reflection

Today the forty days are complete. Since Shubkono on 8 February the Church has fasted, prayed and made her prostrations, walking with the Lord who “fasted forty days and forty nights, and afterwards he was famished.” The Panchangam sets side by side two scenes of temptation. In a garden full of fruit the serpent asks, “Did God say…?” and promises, “You will not die… you will be like God.” The woman sees that the tree is “good for food,” “a delight to the eyes,” and “to be desired to make one wise”; she takes and eats, and gives to her husband. In a desert without bread the same tempter comes to Jesus with the same three hungers: for bread, for display, for power. Where Adam doubted the word of God, Jesus answers each time with that word: “It is written, ‘One does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God.’” St Irenaeus taught that the Lord fought as a man against the enemy who had taken us captive in Adam, and conquered him, so that what was lost at a tree in a garden was won back in obedience (in substance). Luke adds a sober line: “When the devil had finished every test, he departed from him until an opportune time.” That time will come in Gethsemane and on the Cross.

The other readings ask what our fast has been for. Isaiah hears the people complain, “Why do we fast, but you do not see?” and God answers: “Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of injustice… Is it not to share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house?” Jeremiah, at the Temple gate, warns against trusting in holy places while living unholy lives: “Amend your ways and your doings, and let me dwell with you in this place.” David, buying the threshing floor where the Temple would later stand, refuses a free gift: “I will not offer burnt offerings to the Lord my God that cost me nothing.” And Peter shows us the price of our own redemption: “not with perishable things like silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without defect or blemish.” So Paul can say: “If we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord; so then, whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord’s.” The forty days end today, but the Fast is not over: tomorrow we stand at the tomb of Lazarus, and on Sunday we enter Jerusalem with the Lord and begin the Week of the Passion.

This year the Fortieth Friday falls on the feast of Mar Yauseph, St Joseph, and so the Church does not fast or make the prostrations today. Joseph is the right companion for this day. Like Abraham, of whom Paul writes, “Hoping against hope, he believed,” Joseph trusted God's word when nothing made sense. Finding Mary with child, “being a righteous man and unwilling to expose her to public disgrace,” he planned to dismiss her quietly. Then the angel spoke in a dream: “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife, for the child conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.” And Matthew gives the whole of Joseph in one line: “When Joseph awoke from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him.” Where Adam heard God's word and doubted it, Joseph heard and obeyed at once, without a single recorded word. Pope Francis, in Patris Corde, called him a father in the shadows, beloved, tender, obedient, accepting, creatively courageous and a working man (in substance). The guardian of the Child in Bethlehem stood near the Mother at the beginning of the road that ends at Calvary. On the last day of the Forty, we ask him to guard the Church as he guarded the Holy Family.

Homily outline

Theme: “One does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God.”

On the Fortieth Friday and the feast of St Joseph: the tempter overcome, the fast God chooses, and the righteous man who obeyed.

1. A garden and a desert

  • Adam doubts the word in a garden of plenty.
  • Christ answers with the word in a desert of hunger.
  • Until an opportune time.
“I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head, and you will strike his heel.” Genesis 3:15
“For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but we have one who in every respect has been tested as we are, yet without sin.” Hebrews 4:15

Apply: Learn one verse by heart today and use it against temptation in Holy Week.

2. The fast that I choose

  • Bread shared with the hungry.
  • Amend your ways.
  • An offering that costs; the precious blood.
“Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of injustice, to undo the thongs of the yoke, to let the oppressed go free, and to break every yoke?” Isaiah 58:6
“You know that you were ransomed from the futile ways inherited from your ancestors, not with perishable things like silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without defect or blemish.” 1 Peter 1:18–19

Apply: Give what you saved in the forty days to the hungry before Sunday.

3. Joseph did as the angel commanded

  • A righteous man, unwilling to shame.
  • Do not be afraid.
  • Obedience without words.
“Your house and your kingdom shall be made sure forever before me; your throne shall be established forever.” 2 Samuel 7:16
“When Joseph awoke from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him; he took her as his wife,” Matthew 1:24

Apply: Do at once the one thing God has long been asking of you.

The forty days end in the victory of the word over the tempter.

With St Joseph we go on, obedient and hoping, toward Lazarus, Jerusalem and the Cross.

Take-home resolution: Live by every word from the mouth of God, and obey it at once, like Joseph.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the forty days of the Great Fast are complete. Jesus fasted forty days and forty nights, and the devil came to tempt him. Long ago, in a garden full of fruit, our first parents listened to the tempter and doubted God's word (Genesis 3:1–6). In a desert without bread, Jesus answered: One does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God (Matthew 4:4).

What was our fast for? Isaiah tells us: Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of injustice… to share your bread with the hungry? (Isaiah 58:6–7). David said: I will not offer burnt offerings to the Lord my God that cost me nothing (2 Samuel 24:24). And Peter reminds us what our salvation cost: not silver or gold, but the precious blood of Christ (1 Peter 1:18–19).

Today is also the feast of St Joseph, so we do not fast today. Joseph is a man of few words and quick obedience. When the angel spoke to him in a dream, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him (Matthew 1:24). Adam heard God's word and doubted; Joseph heard and obeyed.

The forty days end, but our journey goes on. Tomorrow we stand with Martha and Mary at the tomb of Lazarus; on Sunday we wave palms and go up to Jerusalem with the Lord. Let us ask St Joseph to walk with us as he walked with Mary and the Child. Whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's (Romans 14:8). Amen.

For children

Children, when the devil tempted Jesus, Jesus answered with words from the Bible. You can do the same: when you feel like lying or being unkind, say a Bible word, like: Jesus, I love you more. And St Joseph, whose feast is today, did what God asked right away. Can you obey your parents right away today, like Joseph?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, for forty days and forty nights you fasted in the wilderness and overcame the tempter by the word of God, where our first parents fell. Thank you for bringing us to the end of these forty days. Let our fast bear fruit in mercy to the hungry, in amended lives, and in offerings that cost us something, for you have ransomed us with your precious blood. By the prayers of Mar Yauseph, the righteous man who did at once what the Lord commanded, give us silent and ready obedience, guard our families, and lead us with you into Jerusalem, to your Cross and your Resurrection. Amen.
