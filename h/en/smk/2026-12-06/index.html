Word for the day

“And you, child, will be called the prophet of the Most High; for you will go before the Lord to prepare his ways.” Luke 1:76

Reflection

Elizabeth's time comes and she bears a son. The neighbours rejoice, because “the Lord had shown his great mercy to her”. The child whose birth seemed impossible is born, and his name is chosen not by custom but by God: “His name is John”, which means “God is gracious”.

For nine months Zechariah had been silent. When he writes “His name is John” on a tablet, obeying the angel's word, “immediately his mouth was opened and his tongue freed, and he began to speak, praising God.” Doubt had closed his mouth; obedience opens it. His first words are praise, the Benedictus that the Church still prays every morning: “Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has looked favorably on his people and redeemed them.”

The Old Testament readings show God's way with children of promise. Moses was drawn out of the water to deliver his people. Hannah, who prayed for a son, gives him back: “For this child I prayed… therefore I have lent him to the LORD” (1 Samuel 1:27–28). The psalm says: “Sons are indeed a heritage from the LORD, the fruit of the womb a reward.” Every child is a gift and has a mission from God.

Zechariah's song ends looking towards Christ: “By the tender mercy of our God, the dawn from on high will break upon us, to give light to those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death.” John is not the light; he prepares for the Dawn. The Church in this season is called to the same task: to point beyond herself to Christ who comes.

Homily outline

Theme: “What then will this child become?”

Elizabeth's son is born, and a family argument about a name becomes a moment of faith. The birth of John teaches us that every child is a gift, that obedience opens our lips to praise, and that every life has a mission in God's plan.

1. Every child is a gift from God

  • The neighbours rejoice because the Lord has shown great mercy to Elizabeth.
  • Hannah, after long prayer, gave her son back to the Lord.
  • Children are not property or burdens; they are God's heritage entrusted to us.
“Sons are indeed a heritage from the LORD, the fruit of the womb a reward.” Psalm 127:3
“All who heard them pondered them and said, “What then will this child become?” For, indeed, the hand of the Lord was with him.” Luke 1:66

Apply: Bless your children each night with the sign of the Cross.

2. Obedience opens our lips to praise

  • Zechariah writes, “His name is John,” meaning “God is gracious”, against family custom.
  • At once his tongue is freed, and he blesses God.
  • When we obey God's word, even against pressure, our silence turns into praise.
“For this child I prayed; and the LORD has granted me the petition that I made to him. Therefore I have lent him to the LORD.” 1 Samuel 1:27–28
“He asked for a writing tablet and wrote, “His name is John.” And all of them were amazed. Immediately his mouth was opened and his tongue freed, and he began to speak, praising God.” Luke 1:63–64

Apply: Where are you silent because of disobedience? Obey, and find your voice of praise.

3. Every life has a mission: to prepare the way

  • John will be “the prophet of the Most High”, going before the Lord.
  • God makes his people a crown of beauty in his hand.
  • Each of us is sent to prepare a way for Christ in our home, work and nation.
“You shall be a crown of beauty in the hand of the LORD, and a royal diadem in the hand of your God.” Isaiah 62:3
“And you, child, will be called the prophet of the Most High; for you will go before the Lord to prepare his ways.” Luke 1:76

Apply: Ask in prayer: “Lord, for whom am I to prepare your way this Christmas?”

Zechariah's song ends with light: the dawn from on high will break upon those in darkness and guide our feet into the way of peace.

May our families welcome every child as a gift, obey God's word, and prepare the way for the Lord.

Take-home resolution: Pray Zechariah's Benedictus (Luke 1:68–79) each morning this week.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today we celebrate a birthday: the birth of John the Baptist. The neighbours rejoiced with Elizabeth, because God had shown her great mercy. And then came a small argument about the name. The relatives said, “Call him Zechariah, after his father.” Elizabeth said, “No; he is to be called John.”

Zechariah, still unable to speak, asked for a tablet and wrote: “His name is John.” And immediately his mouth was opened, his tongue was freed, and he began to praise God. For nine months his doubt had kept him silent. When he obeyed the word of God, he could speak again, and his first words were praise.

Our readings speak of other children of promise: Moses drawn out of the water, Samuel whom Hannah gave back to the Lord. The psalm says, “Children are a heritage from the Lord.” Dear parents, your children are not your possession; they are God's gift, and each one has a mission. Bless them, pray for them by name, and help them discover what God wants of them.

John's father sang of the coming of “the dawn from on high” to give light to those who sit in darkness. That is what we wait for in this Nombu. Like John, let us prepare the way: with a good Confession, with prayer, with kindness. And let our tongues, like Zechariah's, be freed to bless the Lord. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what your name means? John's name means “God is kind”. His father Zechariah could not talk for many months.

When baby John was born, Zechariah wrote on a board: “His name is John.” And suddenly he could talk again! The first thing he did was to praise God.

Tonight, ask your parents why they chose your name. And let's say together: “Thank you, God, for my name!”

Prayer

Lord God, you gave Zechariah and Elizabeth a son and named him John, for you are gracious. Loose our tongues to bless you, bless the children you entrust to us, and prepare our hearts for the Dawn from on high, your Son Jesus Christ. Amen.
