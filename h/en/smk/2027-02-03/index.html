Wednesday after Aneede Sunday · Mar Shem'on and Morth Hanna

3 February 2027 · Syro-Malankara Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle Ephesians 3:14–21 · Rooted and grounded in love
  • Gospel Luke 2:25–38 · Simeon and Anna meet the child in the temple

Word for the day

“At that moment she came, and began to praise God and to speak about the child to all who were looking for the redemption of Jerusalem.” Luke 2:38

Reflection

Yesterday we kept Mayaltho, the Entrance of our Lord into the Temple. Today our Church turns to the two old people who were waiting there. Simeon “was righteous and devout, looking forward to the consolation of Israel, and the Holy Spirit rested on him.” He had been promised “that he would not see death before he had seen the Lord’s Messiah,” and he came into the temple “guided by the Spirit.” Beside him is Anna, “of a great age”, a widow of eighty-four who “never left the temple but worshiped there with fasting and prayer night and day.” When she saw the child, “she came, and began to praise God and to speak about the child to all who were looking for the redemption of Jerusalem.” Long waiting did not make them bitter; it made them ready.

Paul's prayer to the Ephesians shows what such a life is built on. He bows his knees before the Father and prays “that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith, as you are being rooted and grounded in love,” so that we may “know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge.” Simeon and Anna were rooted in that love long before they held it in their arms. Our Church calls them Mar Shem'on and Morth Hanna and keeps them close to the feast. An ancient antiphon of the Church says that the old man carried the child, but the child was guiding the old man (in substance). On this day we thank God for the grandparents and elders of our parishes, whose prayer holds up more than we know.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday Jesus was carried into the temple. Today we honour the two old people who were waiting for him: Simeon, righteous and devout, on whom the Holy Spirit rested, and Anna, the widow of eighty-four (Luke 2:25, 37).

Simeon had been promised that he would not see death before he saw the Lord’s Messiah. He came into the temple guided by the Spirit, and the promise was kept. God does not forget those who wait for him.

Anna never left the temple but worshiped there with fasting and prayer night and day. When she saw the child she began to praise God and to speak about him to all who were looking for redemption (Luke 2:37–38). Her prayer turned into proclamation.

Paul prays that Christ may dwell in our hearts through faith, as we are being rooted and grounded in love (Ephesians 3:17). As the Fast draws near, let us learn from Simeon and Anna to pray with patience, and thank God for the elders who pray for us. Amen.

For children

Children, Simeon and Anna were very old, and they waited all their lives to see Jesus. When they saw the baby, they were full of joy! Pray today for your grandparents.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Light of the nations, you came into the temple and were welcomed by Simeon and Anna. Through their prayers give us patient and faithful hearts, rooted and grounded in love. Bless the grandparents and elders of our families and parishes, and let their long prayer bear fruit in us. Amen.
