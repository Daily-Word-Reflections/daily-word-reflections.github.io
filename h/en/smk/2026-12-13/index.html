Word for the day

“Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife, for the child conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.” Matthew 1:20

Reflection

Luke told us the story through Mary; Matthew tells it through Joseph. Joseph discovers that Mary, his betrothed, is with child, and he does not yet know how. St Matthew describes him in one word: he was “a righteous man”. His righteousness is shown in mercy: “unwilling to expose her to public disgrace, he planned to dismiss her quietly.”

Then God speaks to him in a dream: “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife, for the child conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit. She will bear a son, and you are to name him Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins.” Joseph is given two tasks: to receive Mary and to name the child. By naming him, Joseph gives Jesus his legal place in the house of David.

Joseph never speaks a word in the Gospels. But he acts: “When Joseph awoke from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him; he took her as his wife.” His obedience is immediate and complete. Like the young Samuel he says, in deeds if not in words, “Speak, for your servant is listening.” The patriarch Joseph, the dreamer of Genesis, saved his family by going down to Egypt; this Joseph will save the Holy Family by taking them to Egypt.

Matthew adds: “All this took place to fulfill what had been spoken by the Lord through the prophet: ‘Look, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall name him Emmanuel,’ which means, ‘God is with us.’” Through Joseph's quiet faith, God-with-us receives a home, a family name and a father's protection.

Homily outline

Theme: “Joseph: the just man who listened and obeyed.”

Joseph never speaks a word in the Gospels, yet his silence is full of faith. Faced with a crisis he did not understand, he chose mercy, listened to God in the night, and acted at once.

1. A just man chooses mercy

  • Joseph discovers that Mary is with child and does not understand.
  • Being righteous, he will not expose her to disgrace: his justice is clothed in mercy.
  • Micah sums up God's will: do justice, love kindness, walk humbly.
“He has told you, O mortal, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?” Micah 6:8
“Her husband Joseph, being a righteous man and unwilling to expose her to public disgrace, planned to dismiss her quietly.” Matthew 1:19

Apply: In a family conflict this week, choose the way that protects the other's dignity.

2. God speaks in the night

  • While Joseph is still deciding, the angel speaks in a dream.
  • Like the boy Samuel, Joseph has a listening heart.
  • God still speaks in silence, in Scripture, in conscience and through the Church; we must make room to listen.
“Now the LORD came and stood there, calling as before, “Samuel! Samuel!” And Samuel said, “Speak, for your servant is listening.”” 1 Samuel 3:10
“An angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream and said, “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife, for the child conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.”” Matthew 1:20

Apply: Before sleeping, spend five minutes in silence with the Lord, without your phone.

3. Obedience in action

  • Joseph wakes and does exactly as the angel commanded.
  • He takes Mary into his home and gives the child the name Jesus, “God saves”.
  • Fathers of families are called to the same quiet courage: to protect, provide and lead their homes to God.
“Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth! For I am God, and there is no other.” Isaiah 45:22
“When Joseph awoke from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him; he took her as his wife.” Matthew 1:24

Apply: Fathers: pray with your family this week and bless your children.

St Joseph teaches us that holiness is often silent: justice with mercy, listening in the night, obedience in the morning.

As Christmas draws near, let us ask him to guard our families as he guarded the Holy Family.

Take-home resolution: Entrust your family to St Joseph each day this week with an Our Father.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, this Sunday the Gospel turns from Mary to Joseph. Joseph has made his plans: he is betrothed to Mary, he is preparing a home. Then he discovers that she is with child, and he does not understand.

What does he do? St Matthew says that Joseph, being a righteous man and unwilling to expose her to public disgrace, decided to send her away quietly. Notice: his righteousness is mercy. He does not want to shame her. How different from our world, where people love to expose others on social media and in gossip!

Then God speaks to him in a dream: “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife.” And Joseph, who never speaks a word in the Gospels, simply obeys: he woke up and did as the angel commanded. No delay, no argument. Like young Samuel he could say: “Speak, Lord, your servant is listening.”

Joseph gave Jesus a home, a family name and a father's protection. Dear fathers, your quiet work, your sacrifices, your faithfulness are part of God's plan. And all of us: let us give God some silence each night, so that, like Joseph, we may hear him and obey. Amen.

For children

Children, Joseph was a carpenter who loved Mary. One night an angel came to him in a dream and said: “Don't be afraid! Take care of Mary and her baby Jesus.”

When Joseph woke up, he did exactly what God said, straight away. He became Jesus' father on earth. He kept Jesus safe and taught him to work.

Let's thank God for our fathers. And let's be like Joseph: when Mummy or Daddy asks, do it straight away! Let's say: “Like Joseph, I will listen and obey!”

Prayer

Lord God, you entrusted your Son and the Virgin Mary to the faithful care of Joseph, the righteous man. Give us his listening heart, his merciful justice and his prompt obedience, and bless all fathers with his tender strength. Amen.
