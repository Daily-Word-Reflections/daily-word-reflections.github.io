Word for the day

“for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.” Colossians 3:3

Reflection

Three weeks ago we kept Suloko, the Ascension of our Lord, and saw him go up in glory. St Paul draws from that mystery a rule for daily life: “So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God. Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth.” Paul is not telling us to despise the earth, to neglect our work or our families. He is telling us where to set the compass. A person whose heart is fixed on Christ at the Father's right hand works, studies, cooks and cares for the sick in a different way, because none of it is the last word. St Augustine, preaching on the Ascension, said that Christ is now exalted above the heavens and yet still feels on earth the sufferings of his members, and that we, while we are still here, may already be with him there by faith, hope and love (Sermon on the Ascension of the Lord, in substance). Then comes the line that describes every baptized person: “for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ who is your life is revealed, then you also will be revealed with him in glory.” In Baptism we went down into the water with Christ and rose with him. The deepest part of our life is now hidden, kept safe with Christ in God, like a seed under the soil. It will be seen on the day he appears.

The Gospel shows what it looks like when someone seeks Christ with her whole heart. Jesus returns across the lake, and the crowd welcomes him, “for they were all waiting for him.” A leader of the synagogue named Jairus falls at his feet and begs him to come to his house, “for he had an only daughter, about twelve years old, who was dying.” On the way, the crowds press in on him. And in that crush is a woman whom nobody notices. She “had been suffering from hemorrhages for twelve years; and though she had spent all she had on physicians, no one could cure her.” For twelve years, as long as Jairus' daughter had been alive, she had been weak, poor and, by the law of purity, kept at a distance from others. “She came up behind him and touched the fringe of his clothes, and immediately her hemorrhage stopped.” The fringe was the tassel that every faithful Jewish man wore on the corners of his cloak, as the Law commanded (Numbers 15:38): Jesus, a son of Israel, wore it too, and through it the power of God reached her. Then Jesus asks, “Who touched me?” Peter is almost amused: “Master, the crowds surround you and press in on you.” But Jesus says: “Someone touched me; for I noticed that power had gone out from me.” St Augustine put the difference in a few words: the crowd presses, faith touches (Sermons, in substance). Many were close to Jesus that day; one touched him.

Jesus does not let her slip away healed but hidden. “When the woman saw that she could not remain hidden, she came trembling; and falling down before him, she declared in the presence of all the people why she had touched him, and how she had been immediately healed.” Jesus wants more than her body to be healed. He wants her to be restored to the community, and above all he wants to meet her. “He said to her, ‘Daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace.’” Daughter: no one had called her that for twelve years. That word is the second healing. Today our Syro-Malabar brothers and sisters keep the feast of the Body and Blood of Christ, and in many countries the Latin Church keeps Corpus Christi on this Thursday too; in India the Latin Church keeps it on Sunday. Whatever the calendar, the Gospel tells us what happens at every Holy Qurbono. We come in a crowd, and it is possible to stand very near the altar and only press. Or we can come as she came, from behind, with nothing but need and faith, and touch him. When we receive his Body and Blood, power goes out from him into us. And he wants to say to each of us what he said to her: your faith has made you well; go in peace.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul writes: so if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God (Colossians 3:1). We saw the Lord ascend three weeks ago. Now we must live with our hearts turned towards him.

Paul tells us a secret about ourselves: for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God (Colossians 3:3). In Baptism our true life was hidden in God, like a seed under the ground. One day, when Christ who is your life is revealed, then you also will be revealed with him in glory (Colossians 3:4).

In the Gospel a great crowd is pressing round Jesus. But one woman, sick for twelve years, came up behind him and touched the fringe of his clothes, and immediately her hemorrhage stopped (Luke 8:44). Jesus knew: someone touched me; for I noticed that power had gone out from me (Luke 8:46). Many were near him; only she touched him with faith. And he called her: daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace (Luke 8:48).

At every Holy Qurbono we are in a crowd near Jesus. Let us not only press. Let us reach out with faith, bring our hidden wounds, and receive him who is our life. Then we too will hear his word of peace. Amen.

For children

Children, imagine a huge crowd pushing around Jesus. A lady who was very sick for a long time crept up behind him and touched just the edge of his clothes, and she was healed! Jesus stopped and said: who touched me? He wanted to meet her and make her happy. Jesus is never too busy for you. When you pray, it is like touching his cloak. He always notices you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen and ascended, you are seated at the right hand of the Father, and our life is hidden with you in God. Turn our hearts to the things that are above, so that we may do our earthly work with heavenly hope. As the woman reached out to touch the fringe of your clothes, let us come to you with humble faith, especially in the Holy Qurbono. Let your power go out to heal the sick, the ashamed and the forgotten, and speak to each of us your word of peace. When you appear in glory, may we appear with you. You live and reign for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Paul tells the Colossians that their life “is hidden with Christ in God”. In the Gospel a woman tries to stay hidden in the crowd, touches the fringe of Jesus' clothes, and is healed; but Jesus will not let her slip away unnoticed, because he wants to give her more than a cure. The life hidden in God is not a life hidden from God. The key words are hidden and touch.

Colossians 3:1–4 · Your life is hidden with Christ in God

Setting.

Colossae was a small town in the Lycus valley in Asia Minor, near Laodicea and Hierapolis (4:13). The letter names Paul as its author, writing from prison. Many scholars think it was written by a disciple in his name, perhaps soon after his death; others hold that Paul wrote it himself. Either way, the Church has always received it as inspired Scripture.

In chapter 2 the letter has said that in Baptism the Colossians were “buried with him” and “raised with him” (2:12). Today's verses are the hinge of the letter: they turn from teaching to daily life. What follows in 3:5–17 is very practical: put away anger and lies, put on compassion, forgive one another.

Structure.

  • v. 1: you have been raised with Christ, so seek what is above.
  • v. 2: set your minds there.
  • v. 3: you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.
  • v. 4: when Christ appears, you will appear with him in glory.

Key words.

  • synēgerthēte, you have been raised with: A verb with the prefix “with”. The believer's rising is not a separate event from Christ's; it is a share in his. The same compound verb is used in 2:12.
  • ta anō, the things above: Not a place in the sky, but the world where Christ now reigns “at the right hand of God”, the words of Psalm 110:1. To seek them is to let Christ's reign shape how we live now.
  • kekryptai, is hidden: Hidden as treasure is hidden, kept safe, not lost. The new life is real already, but not yet visible to the world. It will be revealed when Christ appears (v. 4).

Verse by verse.

“So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.” (3:1)

The “if” is not a doubt. It means “since”. Paul is reminding the baptized of what has already happened to them.

“for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.” (3:3)

A Christian's deepest identity cannot be seen on the surface. It is kept in God, where no failure or loss can reach it.

“When Christ who is your life is revealed, then you also will be revealed with him in glory.” (3:4)

Some ancient manuscripts read “our life” instead of “your life”; the POC follows that reading. Either way, Christ is not only the giver of life. He is our life.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism quotes these verses when it speaks of the resurrection of the dead: united with Christ by Baptism, believers already share truly in the heavenly life of the risen Christ, but this life remains “hidden with Christ in God” until it is revealed (CCC 1002–1003). Seeking “the things above” does not mean neglecting the earth. The verses that follow fill everyday life, at home and at work, with the virtues of Christ. The Holy Qurbono itself joins earth to heaven: we lift up our hearts to where Christ is, and he comes down to us.

Luke 8:40–48 · Daughter, your faith has made you well

Setting.

Jesus has crossed back over the lake after healing the man among the tombs in the country of the Gerasenes (8:26–39). A crowd is waiting for him. Jairus, a leader of the synagogue, begs him to come to his dying daughter, and on the way the woman's story is set inside his. Mark tells the same two stories in the same way (Mark 5:21–43). Both the girl and the woman's illness are marked by the number twelve.

The Law of Moses counted a woman with a long flow of blood as ritually unclean, and the same applied to what she touched (Leviticus 15:25–27). This concerned worship and the Temple, and the Gospel does not dwell on it. But it may help explain why she came quietly from behind.

Structure.

  • vv. 40–42a: the crowd; Jairus' plea for his daughter.
  • vv. 42b–44: the woman touches the fringe and is healed at once.
  • vv. 45–46: “Who touched me?”; power has gone out.
  • v. 47: she comes trembling and tells everything.
  • v. 48: “Daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace.”
  • ¶ The story of Jairus' daughter continues in 8:49–56.

Key words.

  • kraspedon, fringe: Probably the tassel that the Law told every Israelite man to wear on the corners of his cloak, to remember the commandments (Numbers 15:38–39). Jesus wore it as a faithful Jew. Matthew says that many were healed by touching it (Matthew 14:36).
  • dynamis, power: The same word Luke uses of the Spirit's power (1:35; 24:49). Healing is not magic in the cloth. It is the power of God going out from the person of Jesus.
  • sesōken, has made you well, has saved you: The Greek verb means both to heal and to save. The POC renders it “has saved you”. The woman came for a cure of the body and received salvation and peace.

Verse by verse.

“She came up behind him and touched the fringe of his clothes, and immediately her hemorrhage stopped.” (8:44)

Twelve years of failure end in a moment. Verse 43 adds that she had spent all she had on physicians; some important early manuscripts leave out those words, and Luke, whom tradition calls a physician (Colossians 4:14), is gentler with doctors than Mark is (Mark 5:26).

“Someone touched me; for I noticed that power had gone out from me.” (8:46)

Many pressed on him; only one touched him in faith. St Augustine put it this way: the crowd presses, faith touches (in substance).

“When the woman saw that she could not remain hidden, she came trembling;” (8:47)

Jesus calls her out not to shame her but to complete her healing in front of everyone, so that she is restored to the community.

“Daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace.” (8:48)

In all three Gospels that tell this story, she is the only person Jesus addresses as “daughter”. She came as an outsider and leaves as one of the family.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism recalls that the sick tried to touch Jesus, “for power came out from him and healed all of them” (Luke 6:19), and teaches that in the sacraments Christ continues to “touch” us in order to heal us (CCC 1504). The woman's faith was humble and bodily: she wanted to touch. That is the logic of the sacraments, where the grace of Christ reaches us through water, oil, bread and wine, and the hands of his priests.

For study and teaching

  • Where am I hiding from the Lord, as the woman tried to hide in the crowd, and what would it mean to come forward to him?
  • Paul says my life is hidden with Christ in God. How does that change the way I see my failures and my successes?
