Word for the day

“for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.” Colossians 3:3

Reflection

Three weeks ago we kept Suloko, the Ascension of our Lord, and saw him go up in glory. St Paul draws from that mystery a rule for daily life: “So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God. Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth.” Paul is not telling us to despise the earth, to neglect our work or our families. He is telling us where to set the compass. A person whose heart is fixed on Christ at the Father's right hand works, studies, cooks and cares for the sick in a different way, because none of it is the last word. St Augustine, preaching on the Ascension, said that Christ is now exalted above the heavens and yet still feels on earth the sufferings of his members, and that we, while we are still here, may already be with him there by faith, hope and love (Sermon on the Ascension of the Lord, in substance). Then comes the line that describes every baptized person: “for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ who is your life is revealed, then you also will be revealed with him in glory.” In Baptism we went down into the water with Christ and rose with him. The deepest part of our life is now hidden, kept safe with Christ in God, like a seed under the soil. It will be seen on the day he appears.

The Gospel shows what it looks like when someone seeks Christ with her whole heart. Jesus returns across the lake, and the crowd welcomes him, “for they were all waiting for him.” A leader of the synagogue named Jairus falls at his feet and begs him to come to his house, “for he had an only daughter, about twelve years old, who was dying.” On the way, the crowds press in on him. And in that crush is a woman whom nobody notices. She “had been suffering from hemorrhages for twelve years; and though she had spent all she had on physicians, no one could cure her.” For twelve years, as long as Jairus' daughter had been alive, she had been weak, poor and, by the law of purity, kept at a distance from others. “She came up behind him and touched the fringe of his clothes, and immediately her hemorrhage stopped.” The fringe was the tassel that every faithful Jewish man wore on the corners of his cloak, as the Law commanded (Numbers 15:38): Jesus, a son of Israel, wore it too, and through it the power of God reached her. Then Jesus asks, “Who touched me?” Peter is almost amused: “Master, the crowds surround you and press in on you.” But Jesus says: “Someone touched me; for I noticed that power had gone out from me.” St Augustine put the difference in a few words: the crowd presses, faith touches (Sermons, in substance). Many were close to Jesus that day; one touched him.

Jesus does not let her slip away healed but hidden. “When the woman saw that she could not remain hidden, she came trembling; and falling down before him, she declared in the presence of all the people why she had touched him, and how she had been immediately healed.” Jesus wants more than her body to be healed. He wants her to be restored to the community, and above all he wants to meet her. “He said to her, ‘Daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace.’” Daughter: no one had called her that for twelve years. That word is the second healing. Today our Syro-Malabar brothers and sisters keep the feast of the Body and Blood of Christ, and in many countries the Latin Church keeps Corpus Christi on this Thursday too; in India the Latin Church keeps it on Sunday. Whatever the calendar, the Gospel tells us what happens at every Holy Qurbono. We come in a crowd, and it is possible to stand very near the altar and only press. Or we can come as she came, from behind, with nothing but need and faith, and touch him. When we receive his Body and Blood, power goes out from him into us. And he wants to say to each of us what he said to her: your faith has made you well; go in peace.

Homily outline

Theme: “Daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace.”

On Thursday of the first week after Pentecost: the things that are above, the life hidden with Christ in God, and the faith that touches him.

1. Seek the things that are above

  • Christ seated at the right hand of God.
  • Not despising the earth, but setting the compass.
  • With him there while we are still here.
“To you I lift up my eyes, O you who are enthroned in the heavens!” Psalm 123:1
“But our citizenship is in heaven, and it is from there that we are expecting a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ.” Philippians 3:20

Apply: Begin each piece of work today with a short offering to Christ at the Father's right hand.

2. The crowd presses, faith touches

  • Twelve years of sickness and shame.
  • The fringe of a faithful Israelite's cloak.
  • Power went out from him.
“But for you who revere my name the sun of righteousness shall rise, with healing in its wings.” Malachi 4:2
“and begged him that they might touch even the fringe of his cloak; and all who touched it were healed.” Matthew 14:36

Apply: At your next Holy Qurbono, come with a hidden need and receive the Lord with deliberate faith.

3. Daughter, go in peace

  • He would not let her stay hidden.
  • A word that restored her dignity.
  • The peace that Christ gives.
“Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” Isaiah 43:1
“Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you.” John 14:27

Apply: Speak a word of welcome this week to someone who feels on the edge of the parish.

Our life is hidden with Christ in God, and our hearts are turned to him above.

In the crowd at the altar, faith reaches out, touches him, and hears his word of peace.

Take-home resolution: Set your heart on the things above, and come to the Holy Qurbono to touch Christ with faith.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul writes: so if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God (Colossians 3:1). We saw the Lord ascend three weeks ago. Now we must live with our hearts turned towards him.

Paul tells us a secret about ourselves: for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God (Colossians 3:3). In Baptism our true life was hidden in God, like a seed under the ground. One day, when Christ who is your life is revealed, then you also will be revealed with him in glory (Colossians 3:4).

In the Gospel a great crowd is pressing round Jesus. But one woman, sick for twelve years, came up behind him and touched the fringe of his clothes, and immediately her hemorrhage stopped (Luke 8:44). Jesus knew: someone touched me; for I noticed that power had gone out from me (Luke 8:46). Many were near him; only she touched him with faith. And he called her: daughter, your faith has made you well; go in peace (Luke 8:48).

At every Holy Qurbono we are in a crowd near Jesus. Let us not only press. Let us reach out with faith, bring our hidden wounds, and receive him who is our life. Then we too will hear his word of peace. Amen.

For children

Children, imagine a huge crowd pushing around Jesus. A lady who was very sick for a long time crept up behind him and touched just the edge of his clothes, and she was healed! Jesus stopped and said: who touched me? He wanted to meet her and make her happy. Jesus is never too busy for you. When you pray, it is like touching his cloak. He always notices you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen and ascended, you are seated at the right hand of the Father, and our life is hidden with you in God. Turn our hearts to the things that are above, so that we may do our earthly work with heavenly hope. As the woman reached out to touch the fringe of your clothes, let us come to you with humble faith, especially in the Holy Qurbono. Let your power go out to heal the sick, the ashamed and the forgotten, and speak to each of us your word of peace. When you appear in glory, may we appear with you. You live and reign for ever. Amen.
