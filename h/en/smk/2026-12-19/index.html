Word for the day

“in whom you also are built together spiritually into a dwelling place for God.” Ephesians 2:22

Reflection

On the last Saturday before Christmas the Church reads again Paul's vision of reconciliation: Christ is our peace; he has broken down the dividing wall and made the far and the near one people, “so he came and proclaimed peace to you who were far off and peace to those who were near.” In him the whole structure grows into a holy temple, and we are “built together spiritually into a dwelling place for God.” The God who will lie in a manger wants to dwell in a people.

John, in prison, sends to ask if Jesus is the one who is to come. Jesus answers by pointing to what is happening: the blind see, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor have good news brought to them. Isaiah had promised that on that day the deaf would hear and the eyes of the blind would see. The kingdom has begun; Christmas is the arrival of the one who heals.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul writes: Christ is our peace; in his flesh he has made both groups into one and has broken down the dividing wall, that is, the hostility between us. He has reconciled both groups to God in one body through the cross (Ephesians 2:14–16).

Jew and Gentile were deeply divided. Christ did not merely make a truce between them; he created one new humanity in himself. You are no longer strangers and aliens, but citizens with the saints and members of the household of God, built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone (Ephesians 2:19–20).

In the Gospel John the Baptist, in prison, sends to ask Jesus: are you the one who is to come? Jesus answers with signs: the blind see, the lame walk, the poor hear good news. And blessed is anyone who takes no offence at me (Matthew 11:3–6).

John had expected a fiery judge. Jesus came as a healer and peacemaker. Sometimes Jesus does not match our expectations either. Blessed are we if we accept him as he is.

In our country and even in our communities, there are walls: caste, language, region, rite, politics. Christ came to break them down. The Child of Yaldo was visited by Jewish shepherds and Gentile Magi: the first sign of one new humanity.

In these last days before Yaldo, let us break down one wall: reach out to someone from a different background, community or Church. And let us accept Jesus as he comes, not as we imagine him. Amen.
