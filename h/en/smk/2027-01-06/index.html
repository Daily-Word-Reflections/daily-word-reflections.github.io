Word for the day

“This is my Son, the Beloved, with whom I am well pleased.” Matthew 3:17

Reflection

Denho means “rising” or “manifestation”. At the Jordan the hidden Child of Bethlehem is revealed to the world. When Jesus comes up from the water, “the heavens were opened to him and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and alighting on him. And a voice from heaven said, ‘This is my Son, the Beloved, with whom I am well pleased.’” For the first time the whole Holy Trinity is manifested: the Father speaks, the Son stands in the water, the Spirit descends.

John protests: “I need to be baptized by you.” Jesus answers: “Let it be so now; for it is proper for us in this way to fulfill all righteousness.” The sinless One goes down into the waters of repentance with sinners. He does not need to be cleansed; he cleanses the waters. The Fathers say that by his Baptism Christ sanctified the waters for our Baptism.

The Old Testament readings are full of water: Rebekah drawing water for a stranger and his camels; water filling the dry valley; Ezekiel's river flowing from the Temple, where “everything will live where the river goes”; Isaiah's promise: “With joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation.” All these streams flow into the Jordan, and from the Jordan into our baptismal font.

St Paul tells Titus: “the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all”, and God saved us “through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit”. Denho is our feast too. In Baptism the Father said to each of us, “You are my beloved child”, and the Spirit came to dwell in us. Today we renew that grace.

Homily outline

Theme: “The heavens opened: the Son, the Spirit, the Father, and our Baptism.”

Denho means “dawning” or “manifestation”. At the Jordan Jesus is revealed as the beloved Son, the Spirit rests on him, and the Father speaks. Today we also remember our own Baptism.

1. The sinless One enters our waters

  • John protests: “I need to be baptized by you.”
  • Jesus answers: “Let it be so now; it is proper for us to fulfil all righteousness”: he stands with sinners.
  • The Fathers say: he did not need the waters to make him holy; he made the waters holy for us.
“I will sprinkle clean water upon you, and you shall be clean from all your uncleannesses, and from all your idols I will cleanse you.” Ezekiel 36:25
“But Jesus answered him, “Let it be so now; for it is proper for us in this way to fulfill all righteousness.” Then he consented.” Matthew 3:15

Apply: Thank God for the humility of Christ, who stands in line with sinners.

2. The Trinity revealed

  • The heavens are torn apart: God's distance is overcome.
  • The Spirit descends like a dove; the Father's voice declares the beloved Son.
  • Isaiah's servant, filled with the Spirit, is revealed: Jesus is the Christ, the Anointed.
“Here is my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my spirit upon him; he will bring forth justice to the nations.” Isaiah 42:1
“And just as he was coming up out of the water, he saw the heavens torn apart and the Spirit descending like a dove on him. And a voice came from heaven, “You are my Son, the Beloved; with you I am well pleased.”” Mark 1:10–11

Apply: Make the sign of the Cross today slowly, thinking of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

3. Our Baptism: drawing water from the wells of salvation

  • Isaiah promised joy from the wells of salvation.
  • In Baptism the same Spirit came upon us; in Chrismation we were sealed with holy Myron.
  • St Paul's disciples at Ephesus received the Spirit when hands were laid on them.
“With joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation.” Isaiah 12:3
“He saved us, not because of any works of righteousness that we had done, but according to his mercy, through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.” Titus 3:5

Apply: Find out the date of your Baptism and celebrate it every year.

At the Jordan heaven opened; at our Baptism heaven opened for us.

Let us live as beloved children of the Father, filled with the Spirit, following the Son.

Take-home resolution: Renew your baptismal promises at home this week: reject sin and profess the Creed.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today is Denho, the feast of the manifestation of the Lord. The Child of Bethlehem, hidden for thirty years in Nazareth, comes to the Jordan and is revealed to the world. When he comes up out of the water, the heavens open, the Spirit descends like a dove, and the Father's voice says: “This is my Son, the Beloved, with whom I am well pleased.”

John did not want to baptise him: “I need to be baptised by you!” But Jesus went down into the water with sinners. Why? He had no sin to wash away. The Fathers answer: he went down to make the waters holy for us. The Jordan became the source of every baptismal font in the world.

Our Old Testament readings are full of water: Rebekah at the well, a river flowing from the Temple bringing life wherever it goes, Isaiah's joyful promise: “With joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation.” In our Baptism we drank from those wells. And the same voice spoke over each of us: “You are my beloved child.”

Today, as the waters are blessed, let us renew our baptismal promises with faith. Take the blessed water home; bless your children with it each night. Find out the date of your Baptism and thank God for it every year. Live as beloved children of the Father, filled with the Holy Spirit. Amen.

For children

Children, today we remember the day Jesus was baptised in the river Jordan. When he came out of the water, heaven opened!

The Holy Spirit came down like a white dove, and God the Father said: “This is my beloved Son!” Father, Son and Holy Spirit, all together.

When you were baptised, God said: “You are my beloved child!” Do you know your baptism day? Ask your parents tonight! Let's say: “I am God's beloved child!”

Prayer

Eternal Father, at the Jordan you revealed your beloved Son and sent your Holy Spirit upon him. Renew in us the grace of our Baptism, make us live as your beloved children, and let the river of your life flow through our homes and our Church. Amen.
