Word for the day

“you show that you are a letter of Christ, prepared by us, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts.” 2 Corinthians 3:3

Reflection

At the end of the first week of the season of Pentecost, St Paul gives us a picture of what the Holy Spirit does in a Christian. Some people in Corinth had begun to ask for Paul's credentials: where were his letters of recommendation? Paul answers: “Surely we do not need, as some do, letters of recommendation to you or from you, do we? You yourselves are our letter, written on our hearts, to be known and read by all; and you show that you are a letter of Christ, prepared by us, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts.” The tablets of stone recall Sinai, where God gave the Law to Moses; the Jewish people still keep the feast of Weeks, Shavuot, in memory of that gift, and it was on that very feast that the Spirit came upon the apostles. The prophets had promised a day when God would write his law within his people: “I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts” (Jeremiah 31:33). Paul says that day has come. The Spirit of Pentecost is a writer, and his paper is the human heart.

Paul goes on: “our competence is from God, who has made us competent to be ministers of a new covenant, not of letter but of spirit; for the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.” This is not contempt for the Law, which Paul elsewhere calls “holy” (Romans 7:12), nor for the people to whom it was given. The Law shone with glory, so that the people “could not gaze at Moses' face because of the glory of his face.” But a commandment written outside us can only tell us what to do; it cannot give us the strength to do it. St Augustine put the difference simply: by the law of works God says, Do what I command; by the law of faith we say to God, Give what you command (On the Spirit and the Letter 13.22, in substance). The Spirit does not only show us the way; he becomes the life within us that walks in it. “How much more will the ministry of the Spirit come in glory?” The glory once seen on Moses' face is now meant to shine, quietly, in ordinary Christian lives, so that anyone who reads us reads Christ.

The Gospel tells us how to keep that letter legible: keep the lamp burning. Ten bridesmaids wait for the bridegroom; five take flasks of oil, five do not. “As the bridegroom was delayed, all of them became drowsy and slept.” Everyone sleeps; the difference is in the oil. When the cry comes at midnight, the foolish ask to borrow, and the wise cannot lend, because the oil of a life cannot be handed over at the last minute. St John Chrysostom saw in the oil the works of mercy, the kindness and almsgiving that keep faith burning (Homilies on Matthew 78, in substance). Today the Panchangam remembers St Rita of Cascia, a woman whose lamp burned through a long night. Born in Umbria about 1381, she was married young to a hard and violent man, and for years she answered him with patience and prayer; he changed, and then was murdered. She forgave his killers and prayed that her sons would not take revenge. After their death she entered the Augustinian monastery at Cascia, where she lived in prayer and penance until her death on 22 May 1457. Many in Kerala call on her as the patron of impossible causes. Her life was a letter written by the Spirit on a human heart, in a hand that anger and grief could not erase. Ask her prayers today for a marriage under strain, for a family divided by revenge, for a cause that looks hopeless, and keep your own lamp filled: “Keep awake therefore, for you know neither the day nor the hour.”

Homily outline

Theme: “You are a letter of Christ... written... with the Spirit of the living God.”

On Saturday after the Sunday of Pentecost, with St Rita: the letter written on hearts, the Spirit who gives life, and the oil that keeps the lamp burning.

1. A letter of Christ

  • Not ink, but the Spirit.
  • Not stone, but hearts.
  • Read by all.
“I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts” Jeremiah 31:33
“In the same way, let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.” Matthew 5:16

Apply: At the end of each day this week ask: what did someone read about Christ in me today?

2. The Spirit gives life

  • The holy Law and its glory.
  • Command and strength.
  • “Give what you command.”
“A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you” Ezekiel 36:26
“And all of us, with unveiled faces, seeing the glory of the Lord as though reflected in a mirror, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another” 2 Corinthians 3:18

Apply: Name the commandment you find hardest to keep, and ask the Holy Spirit each morning this week for the strength to keep it.

3. Oil in the lamp

  • All of them slept.
  • Oil that cannot be borrowed.
  • St Rita's long night.
“my soul waits for the Lord more than those who watch for the morning, more than those who watch for the morning.” Psalm 130:6
“Be dressed for action and have your lamps lit” Luke 12:35

Apply: Do one work of mercy every day until next Saturday, and ask St Rita's prayers for one family you know in difficulty.

Faith kept burning by prayer and mercy is the oil no one can lend us; St Rita kept hers through the longest night.

Take-home resolution: Let the Spirit write on your heart, keep your lamp filled day by day, and be a letter in which others read Christ.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul tells the Corinthians that he does not need letters of recommendation. You yourselves are our letter, written on our hearts, to be known and read by all (2 Corinthians 3:2). They are a letter of Christ, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts (2 Corinthians 3:3). This is what Pentecost does: the Spirit writes God's law on our hearts.

For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life (2 Corinthians 3:6). A command written outside us tells us what to do; the Spirit within gives us the strength to do it. As St Augustine said (in substance), we can now pray: Lord, give what you command. The glory that shone on Moses' face is meant to shine quietly in our ordinary lives.

In the Gospel, ten bridesmaids wait for the bridegroom. All of them fall asleep; the difference is the oil. The foolish ask to borrow at the last moment, but the oil of a lifetime cannot be borrowed. Keep awake therefore, for you know neither the day nor the hour (Matthew 25:13). Our oil is prayer and works of mercy, kept up day after day.

Today we remember St Rita, whose lamp burned through a long night: a hard marriage, the murder of her husband, the loss of her sons, and through it all patience, prayer and forgiveness. Let us ask her prayers for families in difficulty and for causes that seem impossible, and let us keep our lamps filled, so that our lives are letters in which others can read Christ. Amen.

For children

Children, St Paul says something surprising: you are a letter! Not a letter written with a pen, but a letter written by the Holy Spirit on your heart. When people see how you behave, kind or unkind, patient or angry, they are reading that letter. Today we remember St Rita, who stayed gentle and kept praying even when her life was very hard. Let's be letters that tell everyone how good Jesus is!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, by your Holy Spirit you write your law on the tablets of our hearts and make us letters of your love for all to read. Write in us what you will. Do not let the letter of the law lie dead in us, but let your Spirit give us life and strength to do what you command. Keep our lamps filled with the oil of faith and good works, so that when you, the Bridegroom, come at midnight, we may go in with you to the wedding feast. By the prayers of St Rita, give patience to those who suffer at home, forgiveness to those who are wronged, and hope to those whose cause seems impossible. With your Father and your Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
