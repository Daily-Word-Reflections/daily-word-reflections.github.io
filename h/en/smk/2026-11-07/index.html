Word for the day

“They came to Philip, who was from Bethsaida in Galilee, and said to him, “Sir, we wish to see Jesus.”” John 12:21

Reflection

The Church in Corinth was splitting into parties: “I belong to Paul,” “I belong to Apollos,” “I belong to Cephas,” “I belong to Christ.” Paul's answer is sharp: “Has Christ been divided? Was Paul crucified for you?” Every preacher is only a servant. Paul was sent “to proclaim the gospel, and not with eloquent wisdom, so that the cross of Christ might not be emptied of its power.”

In the Gospel some Greeks come to Philip with a request that every parish should hear: “Sir, we wish to see Jesus.” People do not come to church to see us, our quarrels or our cleverness, but to see him. A united community that points away from itself to Christ is the best answer to that request. The martyrs we remember today gave the same answer with their lives.

Homily outline

Theme: “Sir, we wish to see Jesus.”

Unity that points to Christ.

1. Live in unity

  • Unity among brothers is good and pleasant.
  • Paul begs the Corinthians to end their divisions.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“Now I appeal to you, brothers and sisters, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you be in agreement and that there be no divisions among you,” 1 Corinthians 1:10

Apply: Speak kindly to someone you are estranged from.

2. The word does its work

  • God's word never returns empty.
  • The Cross does not need our cleverness.
“so shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty, but it shall accomplish that which I purpose,” Isaiah 55:11
“For Christ did not send me to baptize but to proclaim the gospel, and not with eloquent wisdom, so that the cross of Christ might not be emptied of its power.” 1 Corinthians 1:17

Apply: Read the Sunday Gospel in advance.

3. We wish to see Jesus

  • The psalmist longs to behold the Lord's beauty.
  • The Greeks ask Philip to see Jesus.
“One thing I asked of the LORD, that will I seek after: to live in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD,” Psalm 27:4
“They came to Philip, who was from Bethsaida in Galilee, and said to him, “Sir, we wish to see Jesus.”” John 12:21

Apply: Invite one person to the Holy Qurbono.

A divided Church hides Christ.

A united Church shows him.

Take-home resolution: Pray daily this week for unity in your parish.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Church in Corinth was splitting into parties: I belong to Paul, I belong to Apollos, I belong to Cephas, I belong to Christ. Paul's answer is sharp: has Christ been divided? Was Paul crucified for you (1 Corinthians 1:12–13)?

In the Gospel some Greeks who came up for the feast ask Philip: sir, we wish to see Jesus (John 12:21). This is what the world wants from us: not our parties, but to see Jesus.

Today the Church remembers the 33 martyrs of Melitene, who, tradition says, gave their lives together for the one Christ. This week take one step to heal a division in the parish or in the family; then others will see Jesus in us. Amen.

For children

Children, some people said: we wish to see Jesus. Let others see Jesus through your kindness!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you are not divided. Heal the divisions in our families and parishes, keep us faithful like the martyrs, and let all who wish to see you find you in us. Amen.
