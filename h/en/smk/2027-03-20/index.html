Lazarus Saturday · The Saturday of the Raising of Lazarus

Open in the Daily Word app

  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 15:20–28 · Christ the first fruits; the last enemy is death
  • Gospel John 11:28–44 · “Lazarus, come out!”

Word for the day

“Jesus said to her, “Did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see the glory of God?”” John 11:40

Reflection

The Forty Days ended yesterday; Hosanna Sunday is tomorrow. Between them, like the Churches of the East, our Church keeps the Saturday of Lazarus. The order is the order of John's Gospel: the raising of Lazarus is the last and greatest of Jesus' signs, and it opens the road to Jerusalem. John tells us that the crowd who came out with palms came “not only because of Jesus but also to see Lazarus, whom he had raised from the dead.” Today's Gospel begins as Martha goes to call her sister: “The Teacher is here and is calling for you.” Mary rises quickly, and the friends who were consoling her follow, thinking she is going to the tomb to weep. She falls at Jesus' feet with the same words Martha had said: “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.” Then John shows us the heart of God made flesh: “When Jesus saw her weeping, and the Jews who came with her also weeping, he was greatly disturbed in spirit and deeply moved.” He asks, “Where have you laid him?” They answer, “Lord, come and see.” And “Jesus began to weep.” The mourners, neighbours and friends of the family who had come from Jerusalem to share their grief, say, “See how he loved him!” Many of them, John tells us, believed in him that day.

At the tomb, a cave with a stone across it, Jesus says, “Take away the stone.” Practical Martha protests: “Lord, already there is a stench because he has been dead four days.” Jesus answers: “Did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see the glory of God?” He lifts his eyes and thanks the Father, and “he cried with a loud voice, ‘Lazarus, come out!’ The dead man came out, his hands and feet bound with strips of cloth.” St Leo the Great wrote that it belongs to the man in Christ to weep over a dead friend, and to the God in Christ to call him back to life by a word (in substance). One Person, true God and true man, weeps and raises. And the last command is given to the bystanders: “Unbind him, and let him go.” St Augustine saw here an image of the sinner who comes out of the tomb of sin by confessing: Christ alone gives life, but he has entrusted to his Church the ministry of unbinding (in substance). Before Easter, there is no better way to keep Lazarus Saturday than to come out of our tomb in the sacrament of Confession and let the Church unbind us.

Lazarus would die again; his raising was a sign. Paul proclaims the reality it points to: “But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the first fruits of those who have died… for as all die in Adam, so all will be made alive in Christ.” Christ's Resurrection is not a return to this life but the first fruits of the harvest of the risen body, and “the last enemy to be destroyed is death.” Genesis today closes the story of Rebekah. Her family asks her one question: “Will you go with this man?” and she answers in one word: “I will.” She leaves her home for a bridegroom she has never seen. St Ambrose saw in Rebekah the Church, and every soul, going out in faith to meet Christ (in substance). Abraham's servant says, “Do not delay me.” Tomorrow the Lord rides into Jerusalem. Will we go with this man? With Rebekah, let us answer, “I will.”

Full homily

Dear friends, the forty days ended yesterday, and tomorrow is Hosanna Sunday. Today, Lazarus Saturday, we stand at a tomb in Bethany. Mary falls at Jesus' feet and says: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died (John 11:32). And Jesus began to weep (John 11:35).

Jesus is true man: he feels our grief, he cries with us. And Jesus is true God: he says, Take away the stone, and then he cries with a loud voice: Lazarus, come out! (John 11:39, 43). The dead man comes out. Jesus said to Martha: Did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see the glory of God? (John 11:40).

Then Jesus says to the people: Unbind him, and let him go (John 11:44). St Augustine saw here a picture of Confession: Christ gives us new life, and the Church unbinds us (in substance). Holy Week begins tomorrow. Let us come out of our tombs and be unbound in Confession before Easter.

Lazarus would die again one day. But Christ has been raised from the dead, the first fruits of those who have died (1 Corinthians 15:20), and the last enemy to be destroyed is death (1 Corinthians 15:26). And like Rebekah, who was asked, Will you go with this man? and answered, I will (Genesis 24:58), let us tomorrow take our palms and say to Jesus: I will go with you. Amen.

For children

Children, when Jesus' friend Lazarus died, Jesus cried. Jesus cries with us when we are sad. But then he called: Lazarus, come out! and Lazarus came out alive. Jesus is stronger than death. Tomorrow bring a palm leaf to church and wave it for Jesus, our King who gives life!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you wept at the tomb of your friend Lazarus and called him out by your word. You are true man, who shares our grief, and true God, who gives life to the dead. Call us out of every tomb of sin and fear, and let your Church unbind us in the sacrament of mercy. Strengthen our faith in the resurrection of the body, for you are the first fruits of those who have died. And as we come to Hosanna Sunday, give us Rebekah's ready answer, that we may say, “I will,” and go with you into Jerusalem, to your Cross and your Resurrection. Amen.
