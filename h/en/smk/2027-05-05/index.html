Word for the day

“But the Lord is faithful; he will strengthen you and guard you from the evil one.” 2 Thessalonians 3:3

Reflection

On the eve of Suloko St Paul asks his people for something he could not do without: “Finally, brothers and sisters, pray for us, so that the word of the Lord may spread rapidly and be glorified everywhere, just as it is among you, and that we may be rescued from wicked and evil people; for not all have faith.” The great apostle does not stand above the community; he leans on its prayer. The Greek picture behind “spread rapidly” is of a runner: Paul wants the word of the Lord to run through the world. Tomorrow Jesus will be lifted up from his disciples and will send them, in St Mark's words, to “go into all the world and proclaim the good news.” The mission belongs to the whole Church, and the first way each of us shares in it is by prayer for those who preach. Pray this week for your bishop and your parish priest by name, for missionaries in the north of India and abroad, for catechism teachers preparing children for First Holy Qurbono. Paul knew that the word does not spread by human cleverness. It runs when the Church prays.

Then Paul turns from his own needs to the faithfulness of God: “But the Lord is faithful; he will strengthen you and guard you from the evil one.” People may fail, not all have faith, but the Lord does not fail. The phrase “the evil one” is the same as in the Lord's Prayer, which our Church prays many times each day, when we ask to be delivered from the evil one. Paul ends with a blessing worth learning by heart: “May the Lord direct your hearts to the love of God and to the steadfastness of Christ.” The word for direct means to straighten a path, to clear the road ahead. Love of God and the steadfastness, the patient endurance, of Christ are the two rails on which the Christian life runs. St Augustine wrote that our hearts are directed to the love of God when we love God for his own sake and everything else for his sake (in substance, after his teaching on the order of love in On Christian Doctrine I). The Lord who is faithful will do the straightening, if we let him.

The Gospel speaks of light and of seeing. “No one after lighting a lamp puts it in a cellar, but on the lampstand so that those who enter may see the light. Your eye is the lamp of your body. If your eye is healthy, your whole body is full of light.” In Kerala homes the nilavilakku is lit at dusk and set where all can see it; no one hides it under a cot. Jesus says the eye works like that lamp: what we look at, and how we look, fills us either with light or with darkness. “Therefore consider whether the light in you is not darkness.” A sobering question for an age of screens. On the eve of the Ascension, when the disciples will stand gazing up into heaven, Jesus asks us to look with a clear eye: at God, at our neighbour, at the world, without greed or envy or impurity. Tomorrow, at Suloko, the Church will lift its eyes to the ascending Lord. Prepare tonight with a short examination of what your eyes have taken in today, and ask for a healthy eye, so that your whole life may become a lamp on the stand.

Homily outline

Theme: “But the Lord is faithful; he will strengthen you and guard you from the evil one.”

On the eve of Suloko: prayer for the word that runs, the faithfulness of God, and the healthy eye that fills the body with light.

1. Pray for us

  • The apostle leans on the Church's prayer.
  • The word that runs.
  • Mission begins on our knees.
“so shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty, but it shall accomplish that which I purpose, and succeed in the thing for which I sent it.” Isaiah 55:11
“At the same time pray for us as well that God will open to us a door for the word, that we may declare the mystery of Christ,” Colossians 4:3

Apply: Pray each day this week by name for your bishop, your parish priest and one missionary.

2. The Lord is faithful

  • Not all have faith; God never fails.
  • Guarded from the evil one.
  • Hearts directed to love and steadfastness.
“The steadfast love of the Lord never ceases, his mercies never come to an end; they are new every morning; great is your faithfulness.” Lamentations 3:22–23
“God is faithful, and he will not let you be tested beyond your strength, but with the testing he will also provide the way out so that you may be able to endure it.” 1 Corinthians 10:13

Apply: Learn 2 Thessalonians 3:5 by heart and pray it over your family this week.

3. Your eye is the lamp of your body

  • The lamp on the stand.
  • A healthy eye.
  • Looking up to the ascending Lord.
“Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.” Psalm 119:105
“In the same way, let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.” Matthew 5:16

Apply: Make a short examination of your eyes each night this week, and give up one thing that darkens you.

The word of the Lord runs when the Church prays, and the faithful Lord guards those who carry it.

A healthy eye makes the whole life a lamp for others.

Take-home resolution: Pray for those who preach, trust the faithful Lord, guard your eyes, and come to Suloko with your heart lifted up.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Tomorrow is Suloko. Tonight St Paul asks us for something simple: pray for us, so that the word of the Lord may spread rapidly and be glorified everywhere (2 Thessalonians 3:1). The apostle needed the prayer of ordinary believers. Our priests and missionaries need ours.

Paul knew that not all have faith. But he says: the Lord is faithful; he will strengthen you and guard you from the evil one (3:3). And he blesses them: may the Lord direct your hearts to the love of God and to the steadfastness of Christ (3:5). God himself clears the road for our hearts.

In the Gospel Jesus says: your eye is the lamp of your body. If your eye is healthy, your whole body is full of light (Luke 11:34). What we look at shapes who we become. A lamp is not hidden in a cellar; it is put on a stand so that all may see.

Tomorrow we will lift our eyes to the ascending Lord. Let us prepare tonight: pray for those who preach, trust the faithful Lord, and keep our eyes clear and our lives full of light. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! At home, when your family lights the evening lamp, where do you put it? In the middle, where everyone can see. Jesus says our eyes are like a lamp. When we look at good things, like the beautiful world God made or kind people, our hearts fill with light. Tomorrow we celebrate Jesus going up to heaven. Tonight, say a little prayer for your priest, so that he can tell many people about Jesus.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, tomorrow we celebrate your going up to the Father. Let your word run through the world and be glorified, and strengthen all who proclaim it. You are faithful: strengthen us and guard us from the evil one. Direct our hearts to the love of God and to your own steadfastness. Make our eyes healthy and our lives full of light, so that we may be lamps on the stand for all who enter your house. You live and reign for ever. Amen.
