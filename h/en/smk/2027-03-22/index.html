Word for the day

“But you are the same, and your years will never end.” Hebrews 1:12

Reflection

Last evening, as Hosanna Sunday closed, our Church entered Hasha Week. Hasho is the Syriac word for suffering: this is the week of the Lord's Passion, and the Church walks beside him day by day. On Monday the Gospel finds Jesus in the temple at Jerusalem, teaching, a few days before his death. The chief priests and the elders come with a question: “By what authority are you doing these things, and who gave you this authority?” Jesus answers with a question of his own: “Did the baptism of John come from heaven, or was it of human origin?” They weigh the answer by what it will cost them, not by what is true, and at last say: “We do not know.” St John Chrysostom notes that the Lord did not refuse to answer people who wished to learn, but people who asked only in order to trap him (in substance). The question is not only theirs. Each of us knows how to say “We do not know” when the truth would ask too much. This week Jesus will answer the question of his authority, not with argument, but with the Cross and the empty tomb.

Isaiah shows why some do not hear. God offers himself as “a garland of glory, and a diadem of beauty, to the remnant of his people,” but the leaders of the city are drunk: “the priest and the prophet reel with strong drink.” They mock the prophet's plain teaching as baby talk, “precept upon precept... line upon line, here a little, there a little.” Yet the message God sent was simple and kind: “This is rest; give rest to the weary; and this is repose”; “yet they would not hear.” The Letter to the Hebrews then lifts our eyes to who it is that stands in the temple. To the Son the Father says: “In the beginning, Lord, you founded the earth, and the heavens are the work of your hands; they will perish, but you remain.” Everything wears out like clothing, “But you are the same, and your years will never end.” The man questioned in the temple courts is the Lord who laid the foundations of the earth. He has every authority, and he uses it this week to lay down his life for us.

How does a family keep Hasha Week? First, by going with the Church to the prayers of the hours. This week our Church prays the Evening, Compline, Night, Morning, Third, Sixth and Ninth Hours with special solemnity, and the new Hasha Week lectionary gives readings from the Old Testament, the Apostle and the Gospel for each hour; follow your parish timetable for the hours and for the Holy Qurbono. Second, by keeping the fast and the abstinence of the Great Lent to its end, with the prostrations (kumbideel) as your parish practises them; the Panchangam asks priests to remind the faithful of the special prayers and abstinences of this week. Third, by preparing for Pesaha with a good confession before Thursday. At home, light the lamp in the evening, read the day's Gospel aloud, pray the evening prayer from the Shehimo, and end with a few moments of silence before the cross. Today we also remember Fr Seraphion Attupurathu OIC, known as Alexander Shemmashan, who came into Catholic communion in the reunion of 1930 and served the Church until his death in 1975.

Full homily

Dear friends, last evening Hasha Week began, the week of our Lord's Passion. Today the Gospel shows Jesus in the temple, and the chief priests and elders ask him: by what authority are you doing these things, and who gave you this authority? (Matthew 21:23).

Jesus asks them about John's baptism, and they answer: we do not know (Matthew 21:27). They knew, but the truth would cost them. Let us be honest: we too say “I do not know” when God asks something hard of us.

Isaiah tells of a people to whom God said: this is rest; give rest to the weary; yet they would not hear (Isaiah 28:12). And Hebrews sings to the Son: they will perish, but you remain (Hebrews 1:11). The Lord who made heaven and earth stands in the temple and lets himself be questioned.

His answer will come on Friday: not a speech, but the Cross; and on Sunday, the empty tomb. This week let us go to the prayers of the hours, keep the fast, make a good confession, and give rest to someone who is weary. Amen.

For children

Children, this week is called Hasha Week. It is the most special week of the year, because Jesus suffered and died for us, and then rose again. Some people asked Jesus: who gave you the power to do these things? Jesus is God's Son. All power belongs to him, and he used it to love us. Can you say a short prayer to Jesus every evening this week?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, in the beginning you founded the earth, and the heavens are the work of your hands; you remain when all things wear out. In these holy days you walked into Jerusalem to suffer for us. Give us hearts that hear your word and do not evade it. Make us a people who give rest to the weary. Keep us close to you through this week of your Passion, that we may rejoice in your Resurrection. Grant eternal rest to Fr Seraphion and to all who have served your Church faithfully. Amen.
