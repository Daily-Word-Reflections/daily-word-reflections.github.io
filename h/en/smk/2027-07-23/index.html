Word for the day

“But the one who endures to the end will be saved.” Matthew 24:13

Reflection

Jesus is sitting on the Mount of Olives when the disciples come to him privately, saying: “Tell us, when will this be, and what will be the sign of your coming and of the end of the age?” He does not give them a timetable. He gives them a way to live: “Beware that no one leads you astray. For many will come in my name, saying, ‘I am the Messiah!’ and they will lead many astray. And you will hear of wars and rumors of wars; see that you are not alarmed; for this must take place, but the end is not yet.” Wars, famines and earthquakes are “but the beginning of the birth pangs”, which means that they are pains that lead to birth, not to death. Then comes the harder word about the disciples themselves: they will be hated, many will fall away, false prophets will arise, and “because of the increase of lawlessness, the love of many will grow cold.” In the floods that have come upon Kerala in recent years, we have seen both: fear, and also the warm love of neighbour for neighbour, the fishermen with their boats and the people who opened their churches and homes. Jesus says: “But the one who endures to the end will be saved. And this good news of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the world, as a testimony to all the nations; and then the end will come.” The Church has never turned these words into a calendar. She hears in them a call to watchfulness, to endurance, and to mission.

Today the Panchangam remembers Ezekiel, a prophet for hard times. He was a priest, “Ezekiel son of Buzi,” who was carried into exile with the leaders of Judah, and in the fifth year of the exile of King Jehoiachin, as he was “among the exiles by the river Chebar,” he writes, “the heavens were opened, and I saw visions of God.” He saw the glory of the Lord, not in the Temple in Jerusalem, but beside a canal in Babylon: the Lord had not been left behind in the ruined city, and he came to his people in exile. Ezekiel was told to eat a scroll, and he says: “Then I ate it; and in my mouth it was as sweet as honey.” The word of God is sweet even when it announces hard things. And he was made a sentinel: “Mortal, I have made you a sentinel for the house of Israel; whenever you hear a word from my mouth, you shall give them warning from me.” The watchman on the wall is the picture of today's Gospel: beware, do not be led astray, stay awake. A watchman does not frighten people for the sake of it. He cries out so that they may live.

But Ezekiel is also the prophet of the new heart. To the exiles who thought that all was over, the Lord promised: “A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you; and I will remove from your body the heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh. I will put my spirit within you, and make you follow my statutes and be careful to observe my ordinances.” In the vision of the dry bones, the Lord asks: “Mortal, can these bones live?” and he commands the prophet to speak: “I will cause breath to enter you, and you shall live.” The Church has long heard in this vision the promise of resurrection and of the gift of the Holy Spirit, which we remember in this season after Pentecost. At the end of the book, Ezekiel sees water flowing from below the threshold of the temple toward the east, growing into a river, so that “everything will live where the river goes.” In July in Kerala, when our rivers are full and the land is green, we know what a river can do for a dry land. The Fathers saw in this river baptism and the water that flowed from the side of Christ, and Saint John saw in the Book of Revelation “the river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb.”

Today's Epistle gives the ground of our endurance: “giving thanks to the Father, who has enabled you to share in the inheritance of the saints in the light. He has rescued us from the power of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of his beloved Son, in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins.” The exiles in Babylon were transferred back to their land; we have been transferred into the kingdom of the Son by baptism. This is why we can endure to the end: our endurance does not rest on our own strength, but on what has already been done for us, the rescue, the transfer, the redemption. The Lord who spoke to the exiles by the river Chebar, who gave them a new heart and spoke of a river that heals, is the same Lord who tells us today: do not be alarmed. Let us ask him for the heart of flesh, for the watchfulness of the sentinel, and for the love that does not grow cold.

Full homily

Dear friends, in today's Gospel the disciples ask Jesus on the Mount of Olives: when will this be, and what will be the sign of your coming? Jesus gives no timetable. He says: Beware that no one leads you astray. You will hear of wars and rumors of wars; see that you are not alarmed (Matthew 24:4, 6). All this is but the beginning of the birth pangs, the pains that lead to a birth. Many will fall away, and because of the increase of lawlessness, the love of many will grow cold. But the one who endures to the end will be saved (Matthew 24:12–13).

Today the Church remembers Ezekiel, a prophet for hard times. He was a priest among the exiles by the river Chebar in Babylon, and there the heavens were opened and he saw visions of God (Ezekiel 1:1). The Lord was not left behind in Jerusalem; he came to his people in exile. God made him a sentinel for the house of Israel (Ezekiel 3:17), and promised: A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you (Ezekiel 36:26). At the end of the book Ezekiel sees a river flowing from the temple, and everything will live where the river goes (Ezekiel 47:9).

St Paul tells us why we can endure: God has rescued us from the power of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of his beloved Son, in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins (Colossians 1:13–14). Our endurance rests on what Christ has done for us. And the good news of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the world, as a testimony to all the nations (Matthew 24:14).

Let us ask for the heart of flesh, for the watchfulness of the sentinel, and for the love that does not grow cold. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what a watchman does? He stands high on a wall and looks out, and if he sees danger coming, he shouts so that everyone is safe. God made Ezekiel a watchman for his people. Jesus also asks us to stay awake and not to be afraid when we hear scary news. And God promised a new heart, a soft one that can love. You can ask Jesus every day: Please give me a soft and loving heart.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you sat on the Mount of Olives and taught your disciples to watch and to endure. We thank you for the prophet Ezekiel, who saw your glory among the exiles, who ate your word as sweet as honey, who was a sentinel for your people, and who promised a new heart and a river of life. Keep us from being led astray, and from alarm. Give us a heart of flesh, and let your love never grow cold in us. You have rescued us from the power of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of your beloved Son; give us strength to endure to the end and to proclaim the good news to all the nations. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
