Word for the day

“Consider the lilies, how they grow: they neither toil nor spin; yet I tell you, even Solomon in all his glory was not clothed like one of these.” Luke 12:27

Reflection

Paul says that the ascended Christ gave gifts to his Church, “some would be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, some pastors and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ.” The goal is maturity: “speaking the truth in love, we must grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ.” Each member has a part; when each works properly, the body grows in love.

In the Gospel Jesus tells his disciples not to worry about food or clothing: look at the ravens, consider the lilies. God feeds and clothes them; how much more will he care for you, “you of little faith”. Today we remember the erection of our Eparchy in the USA and Canada in 2016: thousands of our families live far from Kerala, often under great pressure of work and money. The word of Jesus is for them too: your Father knows what you need. On the eve of Denho, let us lay our worries before the Father who will speak tomorrow at the Jordan: “This is my beloved Son.”

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul tells the Ephesians that the risen Christ gave gifts to his Church: apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ (Ephesians 4:11–12). Every gift is for others.

The goal is maturity: speaking the truth in love, we must grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ (Ephesians 4:15). Not truth without love, and not love without truth.

In the Gospel Jesus says: do not worry about your life, what you will eat, or about your body, what you will wear. Consider the ravens; consider the lilies. They neither toil nor spin, yet even Solomon in all his glory was not clothed like one of these (Luke 12:22, 27).

Today we remember the erection of our Eparchy of St Mary, Queen of Peace, in the USA and Canada. Many of our families live far from home under heavy pressure. Jesus says to them and to us: your Father knows what you need.

Tonight is the eve of Denho. Tomorrow the Father will speak at the Jordan: this is my beloved Son. Let us lay down our worries tonight and come tomorrow as children who trust their Father. Amen.

For children

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

On the eve of Denho the readings speak of growing and of being cared for. Paul describes the ascended Christ giving gifts so that the whole Body may reach maturity. Jesus tells the disciples to look at ravens and lilies. A person who has stopped being anxious about food and clothing is able to give attention to the Body; a Church built up in love is one in which the anxious are carried.

Ephesians 4:7–16 · Grow up in every way into Christ

Setting.

The letter comes to us under Paul's name from prison (on authorship, many modern scholars think of a close disciple, others defend Paul himself). Just before this reading he has listed what unites Christians: “one body and one Spirit… one Lord, one faith, one baptism” (4:4–5). Now he turns from what all share to what each receives, and shows that the differences are for the sake of the unity.

Structure.

  • v. 7: to each a measure of grace.
  • vv. 8–10: Psalm 68 and the Christ who came down and went up.
  • vv. 11–12: the ministries and their purpose.
  • v. 13: the goal: unity and maturity.
  • vv. 14–16: children tossed about, or a body growing in love.

Key words.

  • katartismos, to equip: The related verb is used for mending nets (Mark 1:19). Ministers exist to put the saints in working order. The “work of ministry” belongs to the whole body.
  • alētheuontes, speaking the truth: A single Greek word that means more than speech: living and dealing truly. Joined with “in love”, it rules out both soft lies and hard truths told without care.
  • kephalē, head: Christ is not only the ruler of the body but also the source of its growth: “from whom the whole body” grows (v. 16).

Verse by verse.

“Therefore it is said, “When he ascended on high he made captivity itself a captive; he gave gifts to his people.”” (4:8)

Psalm 68:18 in the Hebrew and Greek says that God received gifts. Here it says “gave”. An Aramaic Jewish paraphrase of the psalm also reads “gave”, and the Conqueror who receives tribute now shares it out.

“The gifts he gave were that some would be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, some pastors and teachers,” (4:11)

The gifts are persons. Greek grammar joins “pastors and teachers” closely, perhaps as one ministry.

“But speaking the truth in love, we must grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ,” (4:15)

The reading's central image. The Christian life is not standing still, and its direction is Christ himself.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that Christ provides in his Body, the Church, the gifts and helps by which we support one another on the way of salvation (CCC 794), and it uses vv. 9–10 of the descent of Christ among the dead (CCC 631). The Second Vatican Council places all ministries inside the one people of God, for the service of the whole Body (Lumen Gentium 7, 32). St John Chrysostom, preaching on this letter, warns that nothing divides the Church as much as the love of power (Homilies on Ephesians 11, in substance).

Luke 12:22–28 · Consider the lilies

Setting.

“Therefore” connects these words to the parable just before: the rich man who built bigger barns and died that night (12:16–21). The rich man's sin was greed; the disciple's danger may be the same fear in a poorer form. Matthew has a parallel in the Sermon on the Mount (6:25–33).

Structure.

  • vv. 22–23: the command, and the reason: life is more than food.
  • vv. 24–26: the ravens, and the futility of worry.
  • vv. 27–28: the lilies and the grass; the argument “how much more”.
  • ¶ The reading stops just short of the positive command: “strive for his kingdom” (12:31).

Key words.

  • merimnaō, to worry: Anxious care that pulls the mind in many directions. Jesus does not forbid work: the birds and flowers are pictures of trust, and elsewhere his followers work and give.
  • korax, raven: Matthew says “birds”. Luke names the raven, an unclean bird in the Law (Leviticus 11:15). Yet God feeds even these (Psalm 147:9). The choice sharpens the point.
  • pēchys, cubit: The word in v. 25 is literally a cubit, a measure of length, which the POC keeps as “മുഴം”. The NRSV takes it as a stretch of time, “a single hour”.

Verse by verse.

“Consider the ravens: they neither sow nor reap, they have neither storehouse nor barn, and yet God feeds them.” (12:24)

“Barn” echoes the rich fool. He built bigger barns and lost his life; the raven has none and is fed. The word “consider” asks for careful, patient looking.

“Consider the lilies, how they grow: they neither toil nor spin; yet I tell you, even Solomon in all his glory was not clothed like one of these.” (12:27)

The flower cannot be identified with certainty; it may be any wild bloom of the Galilean spring. Solomon's splendour (1 Kings 10) is the highest human measure, and a weed outdoes it.

“But if God so clothes the grass of the field, which is alive today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, how much more will he clothe you” (12:28)

Dry grass served as fuel for the clay oven. “How much more” is the logic of the kingdom, from God's care for little things to his care for you.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism says that Jesus asks for childlike abandonment to the providence of the Father, who takes care of his children's smallest needs (CCC 305). On the petition “give us this day our daily bread” it explains that he is not inviting us to idleness, but wants to relieve us of nagging worry (CCC 2830). St Paul's own hard work shows that trust and labour go together (1 Thessalonians 2:9). God often feeds those who have little through those who have more.

Questions people ask

Is it wrong to plan, save, or insure one's family?

No. Jesus speaks against anxiety and greed, not against prudence; he himself speaks of counting the cost (14:28). The test is what we do with the fear: whether it drives us to trust God and care for others, or to build barns.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

Our Eparchy of St Mary, Queen of Peace, in the USA and Canada was erected on 4 January 2016. Many of our families live far from Kerala under heavy pressure of work and money, and the words on the ravens are spoken for them too. Tomorrow is Denho: this evening is a good time to recall the day and place of one's own Baptism.

For study and teaching

  • Paul says that ministers are there “to equip the saints”. What is my part in “the work of ministry”, and who is equipping me for it?
  • What worry occupies me most this week? How would I name it to God, and what might “consider the ravens” mean for it?
