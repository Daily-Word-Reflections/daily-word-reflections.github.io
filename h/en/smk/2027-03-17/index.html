Readings

Word for the day

“Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children, and live in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God.” Ephesians 5:1–2

Reflection

God answers the servant's prayer at once: “Before he had finished speaking, there was Rebekah,” coming out with her water jar on her shoulder. He runs to her and asks for a little water. Watch how she answers: “Drink, my lord,” she says, and “quickly” lowers her jar; then, without being asked, “I will draw for your camels also, until they have finished drinking.” She empties her jar into the trough and runs again to the well, again and again, until all ten camels have drunk. The text is full of her speed: quickly, ran, quickly. Her generosity is not measured; it goes to the end. The servant “gazed at her in silence,” and when he learns that she is of Abraham's kindred, he bows his head and worships: “Blessed be the Lord, the God of my master Abraham, who has not forsaken his steadfast love and his faithfulness toward my master.” Origen loved this scene. He noted that Rebekah came to the wells every day, and that is why the servant of Abraham found her there; so the soul that comes daily to the wells of Scripture will meet the messenger of the Bridegroom (in substance).

Paul tells us whose generosity Rebekah reflects: “be imitators of God, as beloved children, and live in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God.” Christ did not give a measured love; he gave himself, to the end, and that self-offering is made present on our altar in every Holy Qurbono. In the Gospel, Jesus goes to the synagogue at Nazareth “on the sabbath day, as was his custom,” and reads from Isaiah: “The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives.” Today we remember St Patrick, who knew captivity from the inside. Seized from Britain by raiders at about sixteen, he spent six years as a slave herding flocks in Ireland, and there, he tells us in his Confession, he learned to pray many times a day and through the night (in substance). He escaped, became a priest and bishop, and then did the most Christ-like thing: he went back to the land of his slavery to bring the people who had enslaved him the good news of release. That is what it means to be an imitator of God.

Full homily

Dear friends, God answers prayer quickly. Before the servant had finished speaking, there was Rebekah (Genesis 24:15). He asks for a little water. She says: Drink, my lord. Then, without being asked: I will draw for your camels also, until they have finished drinking (Genesis 24:18–19).

Ten thirsty camels! She runs to the well again and again. Her generosity does not stop halfway. That is what God's love is like, and what our love should be like.

Paul says: be imitators of God, as beloved children, and live in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us (Ephesians 5:1–2). Christ did not give a little; he gave himself, and he gives himself still in the Holy Qurbono.

Today we remember St Patrick. He was taken as a slave to Ireland when he was a boy. He escaped, became a priest and bishop, and then went back to the same land to bring them Jesus. Like Jesus in Nazareth he brought release to the captives (Luke 4:18). May we also love even those who hurt us. Amen.

For children

Children, Rebekah gave water to a tired man, and then to all his ten camels! That is a lot of running. When Mummy asks you to help with one thing, try doing two. And remember St Patrick: he went back to the people who had made him a slave, to tell them about Jesus. That is real love.

Prayer

Lord God, you answered the servant's prayer before he had finished speaking and showed him Rebekah's generous heart. Make us imitators of you, as beloved children, living in love as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us. By the prayers of St Patrick, who returned with the gospel to the land of his captivity, give us courage to bring good news to the poor and release to captives, and to love even those who have wronged us. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
