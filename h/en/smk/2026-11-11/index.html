Word for the day

“For he is our peace; in his flesh he has made both groups into one and has broken down the dividing wall, that is, the hostility between us.” Ephesians 2:14

Reflection

The temple in Jerusalem had a wall that kept Gentiles out of the inner courts. Paul says that on the Cross Christ has broken down every such wall: Jews and Gentiles are reconciled “in one body”, no longer strangers and aliens but “members of the household of God”, built on the apostles and prophets with Christ as cornerstone. The Church is a living temple, still under construction, where God dwells in the Spirit.

In the Gospel John's disciples ask why Jesus' disciples do not fast. While the bridegroom is with them they cannot mourn; the days will come when he is taken away, and then they will fast. Jesus brings something new that cannot be squeezed into old forms: new wine needs fresh wineskins. At the start of a new liturgical year we ask him to renew the wineskins of our hearts.

Homily outline

Theme: “He is our peace.”

Walls broken, a temple built, hearts renewed.

1. The Prince of Peace

  • Isaiah names the child Prince of Peace.
  • Paul says Christ himself is our peace.
“and he is named Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.” Isaiah 9:6
“For he is our peace; in his flesh he has made both groups into one and has broken down the dividing wall, that is, the hostility between us.” Ephesians 2:14

Apply: Greet someone you usually avoid.

2. Christ the cornerstone

  • God lays a tested cornerstone in Zion.
  • We are built on it into one household.
“See, I am laying in Zion a foundation stone, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone, a sure foundation:” Isaiah 28:16
“So then you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are citizens with the saints and also members of the household of God,” Ephesians 2:19

Apply: Pray for a parish group you seldom think of.

3. Something new

  • God does a new thing in the wilderness.
  • New wine needs new wineskins.
“I am about to do a new thing; now it springs forth, do you not perceive it?” Isaiah 43:19
“Neither is new wine put into old wineskins; otherwise, the skins burst, and the wine is spilled, and the skins are destroyed; but new wine is put into fresh wineskins, and so both are preserved.” Matthew 9:17

Apply: Choose one new spiritual habit for this liturgical year.

Christ breaks walls and builds a home.

Let him renew us this year.

Take-home resolution: Keep one small fast this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, the temple in Jerusalem had a wall that kept Gentiles out of the inner courts. Paul says that on the Cross Christ broke down every such wall: he is our peace; he has made both groups into one and has broken down the dividing wall of hostility, reconciling both to God in one body (Ephesians 2:14–16).

In the Gospel Jesus speaks of newness: no one sews a piece of unshrunk cloth on an old cloak, nor is new wine put into old wineskins; new wine is put into fresh wineskins (Matthew 9:16–17). The new life Jesus brings does not fit inside old habits; it makes the heart new.

Is there still a wall standing in our heart: of caste, of Church group, of an old wound? We are one house, built on Christ the cornerstone. This week let us take one step of friendship towards someone who stands apart from us. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus broke down all the walls and made everyone one family. Today make friends with a child from another group!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you are our peace and the cornerstone of your Church. Break down the walls between us, build us into your temple, and pour your new wine into renewed hearts. Amen.
