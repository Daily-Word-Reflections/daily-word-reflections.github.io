Word for the day

“And he said to them, “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” Mark 16:15

Reflection

Paul, free in Christ, made himself a slave to all in order to win more of them: a Jew to the Jews, weak to the weak. “I have become all things to all people, that I might by all means save some.” This is the missionary's freedom: not changing the gospel, but changing himself, his comfort, his language and his habits, so that others can receive it.

The risen Lord rebukes the Eleven for their unbelief and then sends them anyway: “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” St Francis Xavier took those words to the letter. Sailing from Lisbon, he arrived in Goa in 1542, walked the fishing villages of South India, including the coast of Travancore, then went on to the Malay world and Japan. He died on an island off the coast of China, longing to enter it.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we remember St Francis Xavier, the great missionary who preached on the coasts of South India. St Paul describes the heart of a missionary: though I am free with respect to all, I have made myself a slave to all, so that I might win more of them. I have become all things to all people, that I might by all means save some. I do it all for the sake of the gospel (1 Corinthians 9:19, 22–23).

Francis Xavier lived this. He learnt the prayers in the language of the fishermen, sat with the poor, nursed the sick, and walked from village to village. He became one with the people so that they might meet Christ.

In the Gospel the risen Jesus appears to the Eleven and says: go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation. And he promises signs: in my name they will cast out demons, speak in new tongues, lay hands on the sick and they will recover (Mark 16:15–18).

The mission is not finished. Many in our country and in the world have not truly heard the Gospel. Many baptised Christians have forgotten it. The Lord still says: go.

We are not all called to cross oceans, but we are all sent. A Malankara Catholic nurse in Germany, a student in Delhi, a worker in the Gulf, a farmer in Pathanamthitta: each can become all things to all people, out of love for Christ.

Let us pray today for missionaries, and for our own Church's mission in North India and abroad. And let us ask St Francis Xavier to give us his zeal and his joy. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

On the day the Church remembers St Francis Xavier, the readings show what a missionary is. Paul changes himself, not the Gospel, so that all kinds of people can receive it. The risen Lord sends disciples whose faith he has just had to rebuke, to tell the news to the whole creation. The mission rests on Christ's sending and not on the strength of those he sends.

1 Corinthians 9:19–23 · All things to all people

Setting.

Chapters 8–10 deal with a practical quarrel in Corinth: may Christians eat meat offered to idols? Paul says that knowledge is not enough; love for the weaker brother or sister must decide. In chapter 9 he gives himself as an example of someone who gives up his rights: as an apostle he could expect to be supported, yet he earns his own living. These verses state the principle behind it.

Structure.

  • v. 19: the paradox: free, yet a slave of all.
  • vv. 20–22a: the groups he serves: Jews, those under the Law, those outside the Law, the weak.
  • vv. 22b–23: the aim: to save some, for the sake of the Gospel.

Key words.

  • edoulōsa, I have made myself a slave: A strong word. In a society with many slaves, a free man choosing this for himself was startling. Compare Christ, who took “the form of a slave” (Philippians 2:7).
  • kerdainō, to win, to gain: A word from business, used five times in these verses. Paul's only profit is people won for Christ.
  • synkoinōnos, sharer (v. 23): Paul does not stand outside the Gospel as its salesman. He hopes to share in its blessings himself, alongside those he serves.

Verse by verse.

“To the Jews I became as a Jew, in order to win Jews.” (9:20)

Paul is himself a Jew and proud of it (Romans 11:1). He means that he kept Jewish customs among Jews where doing so was no obstacle to the Gospel (compare Acts 16:3; 21:26).

“I have become all things to all people, that I might by all means save some.” (9:22)

This is not saying whatever pleases people. In v. 21 Paul insists he is “under Christ’s law”, and in this same letter he rebukes the Corinthians firmly. What changes is the messenger: his habits, his comfort, his rights. The message does not change.

“I do it all for the sake of the gospel, so that I may share in its blessings.” (9:23)

How the Church reads it.

The Church speaks of this today as inculturation: the Gospel enters a people's culture, purifying and raising what is good in it (CCC 854; St John Paul II, Redemptoris Missio 52). St Francis Xavier lived the passage in his own way. Sailing east for the first time at 35, he had the basic prayers and commandments put into the language of the fishing people of South India, and later went on to the Malay world and Japan.

Mark 16:14–18 · Go into all the world

Setting.

A note on the text. These verses belong to the “longer ending” of Mark (16:9–20), which is missing from two of the oldest Greek manuscripts (Sinaiticus and Vaticanus) and differs in style from the rest of the Gospel. Many scholars therefore think it was added early by another hand. It was known by the second century and the Church receives it as inspired Scripture, part of the canon. It gathers up what the other Gospels and Acts report of the risen Lord.

Structure.

  • v. 14: the rebuke of the Eleven at table.
  • vv. 15–16: the sending, and what depends on the response.
  • vv. 17–18: the signs that will accompany believers.

Key words.

  • apistia, sklērokardia, lack of faith, stubbornness: Literally “unbelief” and “hardness of heart”. Jesus has used the second word of others (Mark 10:5). Now it describes his own closest friends, and he sends them anyway.
  • ktisis, creation: “The whole creation”: no nation, people or corner of the world lies outside the good news.
  • sēmeia, signs: Not displays of power for their own sake, but pointers that confirm the word (16:20).

Verse by verse.

“And he said to them, “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” (16:15)

This comes straight after the rebuke. The Church's mission has never depended on the disciples being impressive.

“The one who believes and is baptized will be saved; but the one who does not believe will be condemned.” (16:16)

Faith and Baptism go together, and the Gospel calls for a decision. On those who through no fault of their own do not know Christ, see the question below.

“they will lay their hands on the sick, and they will recover.”” (16:18)

Acts reports several of these signs: tongues (Acts 2:4), demons cast out (16:18), the snake at Malta (28:3–6) and hands laid on the sick (28:8). Nothing here tells Christians to test God by handling snakes on purpose; Jesus himself refused to put God to the test (Matthew 4:7).

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism quotes v. 16 in teaching that the Lord tied the forgiveness of sins to faith and Baptism (CCC 977), and v. 18 in teaching about the sacrament for the sick (CCC 1507). St Francis Xavier, whom Pope Pius XI named a patron of the missions in 1927 together with St Thérèse of Lisieux, took “all the world” literally: he died in 1552 on the island of Shangchuan, within sight of the China he longed to enter.

Questions people ask

Does this mean that everyone who has not believed is condemned?

No one is condemned for what he could not know. Vatican II teaches that those who, through no fault of their own, do not know the Gospel, yet seek God sincerely and try to follow their conscience, moved by grace, may attain eternal salvation (Lumen Gentium 16; CCC 847). This never lessens the Church's duty to proclaim Christ (CCC 848).

For the Syro-Malankara reader

St Francis Xavier belonged to the Latin Church, but his story touches Kerala and South India closely. He worked on the Fishery Coast and in Travancore and, according to his own letters, visited the tomb of St Thomas at Mylapore in 1545. The faith of the St Thomas Christians was far older than his coming. Today Malankara Catholics may honour him as a brother in the same mission, and ask what it means in our own time to go to those who have not yet heard.

For study and teaching

  • Paul gave up his rights so that others could hear the Gospel. Which of my comforts or habits stand in the way of someone meeting Christ?
  • Jesus rebukes the Eleven and sends them in the same scene. What does that say to me when I feel unworthy to speak of my faith?
