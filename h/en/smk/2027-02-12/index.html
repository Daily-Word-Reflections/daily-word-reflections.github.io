Friday of the first week of the Great Fast

12 February 2027 · Syro-Malankara Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Old Testament Genesis 16:7–16 · The God who sees Hagar in the wilderness
  • Epistle Romans 2:12–16 · The law written on their hearts
  • Gospel Luke 12:8–12 · Everyone who acknowledges me before others

Word for the day

“And I tell you, everyone who acknowledges me before others, the Son of Man also will acknowledge before the angels of God;” Luke 12:8

Reflection

Yesterday Hagar ran away into the wilderness. Today “the angel of the Lord found her by a spring of water in the wilderness” and asks two questions that every soul in the Fast should hear: “Hagar, slave-girl of Sarai, where have you come from and where are you going?” She can answer only the first: “I am running away.” God sends her back, but not empty: he promises her a son and names him Ishmael, “for the Lord has given heed to your affliction.” And Hagar, the foreign slave whom no one else noticed, does what almost no one else in Scripture dares: she gives God a name of her own: “You are El-roi,” the God who sees. “Have I really seen God and remained alive after seeing him?” On a Friday of the Fast we remember that the God who sees is the God who looked down from the Cross on all the afflicted.

Paul says that God sees the heart of every human being, not only of those who have the written law: Gentiles “show that what the law requires is written on their hearts, to which their own conscience also bears witness,” on the day “when, according to my gospel, God, through Jesus Christ, will judge the secret thoughts of all.” The Fast is a time to let that light into our secret thoughts. In the Gospel Jesus asks for courage in the open: “everyone who acknowledges me before others, the Son of Man also will acknowledge before the angels of God.” When we are brought before authorities, we need not worry: “the Holy Spirit will teach you at that very hour what you ought to say.” The God who sees Hagar in the wilderness also stands beside those who confess him before the world.

Homily outline

Theme: “Everyone who acknowledges me before others, the Son of Man also will acknowledge before the angels of God.”

The God who sees Hagar, the law written on the heart, and courage to confess Christ.

1. The God who sees

  • Found by a spring in the wilderness.
  • Where have you come from, where are you going?
  • El-roi.
“So she named the Lord who spoke to her, “You are El-roi”; for she said, “Have I really seen God and remained alive after seeing him?”” Genesis 16:13
“But whenever you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you.” Matthew 6:6

Apply: Spend ten minutes in hidden prayer each day of the Fast.

2. Written on their hearts

  • Hearers and doers.
  • Conscience bears witness.
  • God judges the secret thoughts.
“But this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, says the Lord: I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.” Jeremiah 31:33
“They show that what the law requires is written on their hearts, to which their own conscience also bears witness;” Romans 2:15

Apply: Make a careful examination of conscience and go to confession.

3. Acknowledge me before others

  • Confessing and denying.
  • Brought before authorities.
  • The Spirit teaches what to say.
“The Lord God helps me; therefore I have not been disgraced; therefore I have set my face like flint, and I know that I shall not be put to shame;” Isaiah 50:7
“for the Holy Spirit will teach you at that very hour what you ought to say.” Luke 12:12

Apply: Speak of your faith openly in one conversation this week.

No one is invisible to the God who sees.

He who sees our hearts gives us courage to confess his Son.

Take-home resolution: Make a good confession before the middle of the Fast.

Full homily

Dear friends, Hagar ran away into the wilderness. The angel of the Lord found her by a spring and asked: where have you come from and where are you going? (Genesis 16:8). In this first week of the Fast, God asks us the same.

God heard her affliction, and Hagar named him: You are El-roi, the God who sees (Genesis 16:11, 13). No one is invisible to God.

Paul says that God's law is written even on the hearts of those who have not heard it, and their conscience bears witness (Romans 2:15). In the Fast we let God's light into our secret thoughts.

And Jesus says: everyone who acknowledges me before others, the Son of Man also will acknowledge before the angels of God (Luke 12:8). On this Friday, let us not hide our faith, and let us trust the Spirit to teach us what to say. Amen.

For children

Children, Hagar was alone in the desert, but God found her! She called him “the God who sees me.” God sees you too, always, and he loves you.

Prayer

Lord, you are the God who sees. You found Hagar by the spring in the wilderness and heard her affliction. See us in our wilderness, search our secret thoughts, and heal what you find there. Give us courage to acknowledge your Son before others, and teach us by your Holy Spirit what to say. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
