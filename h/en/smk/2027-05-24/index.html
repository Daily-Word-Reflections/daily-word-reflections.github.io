Word for the day

“in whom you also are built together spiritually into a dwelling place for God.” Ephesians 2:22

Reflection

A week ago the Holy Spirit came down on the apostles gathered with the Mother of God in the upper room, and the Church went out into the streets of Jerusalem. Yesterday, on the First Sunday after Pentecost, we heard of the fruit the Spirit wants to grow in us. Today St Paul shows us what the Spirit is building. He is writing to Gentile Christians in Ephesus, people who had once stood outside the covenant of Israel, and he tells them who they are now: “So then you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are citizens with the saints and also members of the household of God, built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone.” A stranger waits at the gate; a member of the household sits at the table. Every one of us came into the Church as a stranger, carried to the font as a baby or walking in as an adult, and was made a son or daughter of the house. In the Holy Qurbono we stand together before one altar, old families and new, those who sing well and those who cannot sing at all, and not one of us is a guest. The foundation is not our own merit but the faith handed on by the apostles and prophets, and the stone that holds every corner together is Christ himself.

Paul goes on: “In him the whole structure is joined together and grows into a holy temple in the Lord; in whom you also are built together spiritually into a dwelling place for God.” Behind the word “spiritually” stands Paul's phrase “in the Spirit”: it is the Holy Spirit who joins the stones. A building is not a heap. Stones lying in a quarry are only stones; fitted together, they become a house where someone can live. St Augustine, preaching at the dedication of a church, told his people that what had happened to the timber and the stone while the building went up happens to believers: they are cut from the forest and the hillside when they come to faith, shaped and squared in instruction and Baptism, and they become the house of the Lord only when they are bound together by charity (Sermon 336, in substance). That is the work of these weeks after Pentecost: the Spirit fitting us together. It can hurt to be fitted to other stones: to forgive a neighbour, to work with a difficult committee, to bear with a relative who tests our patience. But without that fitting there is no house, only a pile of stones. And the Gospel shows how a stone can hold back from being built in.

St John looks back over the public ministry of Jesus with sorrow: “Although he had performed so many signs in their presence, they did not believe in him.” He sees in this the mystery that Isaiah had already met, that the word of God can be heard and not received. Then he adds a sentence that cuts close in every age: “Nevertheless many, even of the authorities, believed in him. But because of the Pharisees they did not confess it, for fear that they would be put out of the synagogue; for they loved human glory more than the glory that comes from God.” These people were not unbelievers. They believed, but quietly, and they would not say so, because it would cost them their place. John is not passing judgement on a whole people; he is describing a temptation as common in our towns today as in Jerusalem then. St Augustine warned, commenting on this passage, that the love of human praise can stop a person on the very threshold of faith (Tractates on John 53, in substance). How many of us believe at home and fall silent at the office, in the hostel or at the family gathering when the talk turns against the Church? Members of the household do not hide which house they belong to. Ask today for the courage to make the sign of the Cross before your meal in the canteen, to say a good word for the Church when it is not popular, to admit that you were at the Qurbono on Sunday. The Spirit who came at Pentecost turned frightened men into witnesses; he can do the same with us.

Theme: “You are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are citizens with the saints and also members of the household of God.”

On Monday of the first week after Pentecost: the household of God, the stones fitted together by the Spirit, and the courage to confess Christ.

  • From the gate to the table.
  • Citizens with the saints.
  • Built on the apostles, held by Christ the cornerstone.
“for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples.” Isaiah 56:7
“like living stones, let yourselves be built into a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.” 1 Peter 2:5

Apply: Welcome one newcomer or one person who sits alone at church next Sunday.

2. Built together in the Spirit

  • A house, not a heap.
  • The Spirit fits stone to stone.
  • Charity is the mortar.
“My dwelling place shall be with them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.” Ezekiel 37:27
“Do you not know that you are God's temple and that God's Spirit dwells in you?” 1 Corinthians 3:16

Apply: Make peace this week with one person in the family or the parish from whom you have drawn apart.

3. The glory that comes from God

  • Believers who would not confess.
  • The fear of losing our place.
  • The Spirit makes witnesses.
“Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory, for the sake of your steadfast love and your faithfulness.” Psalm 115:1
“Everyone therefore who acknowledges me before others, I also will acknowledge before my Father in heaven” Matthew 10:32

Apply: Choose one place where you usually stay silent about your faith and speak one simple word of witness there.

Take-home resolution: Let the Spirit fit you to your neighbour, and do not be ashamed to confess Christ before others.

Full homily

Dear friends, a week ago the Holy Spirit came at Pentecost. Today St Paul tells us what the Spirit is doing: you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are citizens with the saints and also members of the household of God (Ephesians 2:19). Each one of us has been brought in from outside and given a place at the table.

Paul says we are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone (Ephesians 2:20). And the building is still going up: in him the whole structure is joined together and grows into a holy temple in the Lord (Ephesians 2:21). The Spirit is the builder, and he fits us together, stone beside stone. Sometimes that fitting is painful, when we must forgive or work patiently with others. But that is how a house is made.

The Gospel shows a sad picture. Many, even of the authorities, believed in Jesus, but because of the Pharisees they did not confess it, for fear that they would be put out of the synagogue (John 12:42). John explains why: for they loved human glory more than the glory that comes from God (John 12:43). They believed, but they hid it. That temptation is not far from us.

So let us ask two things today. First, let the Holy Spirit fit us together in love, in our homes and in our parish. Second, let us not be ashamed of the house we belong to. A small sign of the Cross, a kind word for the Church, a quiet witness at work: these are the stones of a living temple. Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever watched people building a house? First they lay a strong stone at the corner, and then they put the other stones next to it, one by one. St Paul says the Church is God's house, and Jesus is the corner stone. Each of you is a stone too! The Holy Spirit puts us next to each other, so we must be kind and not push each other away. And when your friends ask about Jesus, do not be shy. Be proud to belong to God's house.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, cornerstone of the house of God, through Baptism you made us no longer strangers but members of your household. Send your Holy Spirit to fit us together, stone with stone, in patience and in love, so that our parish and our families may become a dwelling place for God. Take from us the fear that makes us silent and the love of human praise that holds us back. Give us the courage to confess you before others and to seek only the glory that comes from the Father. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
