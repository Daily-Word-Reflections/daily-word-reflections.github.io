Readings

Word for the day

“But the hour is coming, and is now here, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father seeks such as these to worship him.” John 4:23

Reflection

Today the Panchangam asks us to remember the founding of the first church built in the name of the holy Mother of God. It gives no place and no date, and we do not need to supply them. What it wants us to remember is enough: very early, the disciples of Jesus began to raise houses of prayer and to give them the name of his Mother. Think how many churches in our own villages and towns are called St Mary's, Marth Mariam, the church of the Mother of God. Every one of them is a grandchild of that first church. And the Gospel the Church gives us for this memory is, surprisingly, a word of Jesus about the place of worship. At the well of Sychar the Samaritan woman raises the old dispute between her people and the Jews: is God to be worshipped on this mountain or in Jerusalem? Jesus answers: “Woman, believe me, the hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.” He does not despise the faith of Israel; he says plainly that “salvation is from the Jews.” But he announces something new: “the hour is coming, and is now here, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father seeks such as these to worship him.”

Does this mean that church buildings no longer matter? Not at all. Worship “in spirit and truth” does not mean a vague prayer anywhere and nowhere. The Spirit is the Holy Spirit poured out at Pentecost, and the Truth is Christ himself, who says to the woman, “I am he, the one who is speaking to you.” True worship is worship offered in Christ and through the Spirit, and that happens above all when the Body of Christ gathers around the altar. Solomon knew that no house could hold God: “Even heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain you, much less this house that I have built!” A church is not a box for God; it is a home for his people, a sign on earth of the Father's house. St Augustine, preaching on this Gospel, said: if you want to pray in a temple, pray within yourself, but first become a temple of God (Tractates on John 15, in substance). That is why a church is fitting for the Mother of God. She was the first temple of the new covenant, the one in whom the Word dwelt for nine months. St Cyril of Alexandria, preaching at Ephesus in 431, greeted her as the Mother of God, the temple that cannot be destroyed, holding him whom no place can hold (Homily at Ephesus, in substance). A church that bears her name does not compete with her Son; like her at Cana it says, “Do whatever he tells you.”

The Epistle brings the lesson home. The Christians of Corinth were tempted to think that holiness meant changing their circumstances: the circumcised wanted to hide it, the uncircumcised wanted to be circumcised, the married wondered whether to separate. Paul says: stop looking elsewhere. “Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing; but obeying the commandments of God is everything.” And again: “In whatever condition you were called, brothers and sisters, there remain with God.” God is to be worshipped in spirit and truth in the church, and then in the kitchen, the paddy field, the shop, the office and the classroom that has just reopened after the summer. The holy Mother of God herself lived for years in an ordinary house at Nazareth, cooking, drawing water and keeping the words of God in her heart. Her first sanctuary was a home. So today, thank God for the church where you were baptised, and then make your own house and work a place where the Father finds a true worshipper.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Panchangam remembers the founding of the first church built in the name of the holy Mother of God. We do not know where it stood. But we know its children: so many churches in our land carry her name. And the Gospel today is about worship. Jesus says to the Samaritan woman: the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth (John 4:23).

Worship in spirit and truth is not prayer without a church. The Spirit is the Holy Spirit of Pentecost, and the Truth is Jesus himself, who says: I am he, the one who is speaking to you (John 4:26). We worship in spirit and truth above all when we gather at the altar for the Holy Qurbono. The church building is not a box for God. Solomon said that heaven itself cannot contain him (1 Kings 8:27). The church is a home for God's family.

Why name a church after Mary? Because she was the first house of God among us. The Word of God lived in her womb. A church with her name does not take us away from Jesus. Mary says what she said at Cana: do whatever he tells you (John 2:5).

And St Paul adds: in whatever condition you were called, brothers and sisters, there remain with God (1 Corinthians 7:24). Our worship must not stay inside the church. Like the holy Mother of God in her house at Nazareth, let us make our homes, our fields, our offices and our classrooms places where God is honoured. Amen.

For children

Children, what is the name of your parish church? Many churches in Kerala are named after Mother Mary. Do you know why? Because Mary was the very first home of Jesus. Before any church was built, Jesus lived inside his mother, close to her heart. Today Jesus tells us that God loves people who pray with true hearts. So when you go to church, pray with all your heart. And at home, before you study or play, say a little prayer too. Then your home also becomes a small church.

Prayer

Father in heaven, you seek true worshippers who will worship you in spirit and truth. Pour your Holy Spirit on us, who gather in houses of prayer built in your name and in the name of the holy Mother of God. As she welcomed your Word and became his first dwelling, let us welcome him in the Holy Qurbono and carry him into our homes and our work. Wherever you have called us, keep us with you, faithful to your commandments and glad in your service. Through Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Today we remember the first church built in the name of the holy Mother of God, and the Gospel speaks of true worship: “the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth.” Paul, writing to Corinth, tells each believer to remain with God in the condition in which God called them, since what counts is “obeying the commandments of God”. Both readings ask where God is truly found and served. The key words are calling and worship.

1 Corinthians 7:17–24 · Remain with God where you were called

Setting.

In chapter 7 Paul answers questions the Corinthians had sent him: “Now concerning the matters about which you wrote” (7:1). Some thought that becoming a Christian meant changing everything at once: leaving marriage, changing one's social state, undoing one's past. In the middle of his answers about marriage Paul gives a general rule and illustrates it with two examples: circumcision and slavery.

Structure.

  • v. 17: the rule: live the life to which God called you.
  • vv. 18–19: circumcised or uncircumcised: what matters is keeping God's commandments.
  • vv. 20–23: slave or not: in the Lord the slave is the Lord's freed person, and every Christian belongs to Christ.
  • v. 24: remain with God in your calling.

Key words.

  • klēsis, calling: For Paul the “calling” is first God's call to faith and to belong to Christ. Our outward condition is the place where we answer that call; it is not the call itself.
  • timē, price: “You were bought with a price” (7:23; also 6:20). The price is the blood of Christ. Those who have been bought by him cannot again sell themselves to any human master.

Verse by verse.

“let each of you lead the life that the Lord has assigned, to which God called you.” (7:17)

Paul says this is his rule “in all the churches”. Holiness does not wait for perfect circumstances; it begins where God finds us.

“Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing; but obeying the commandments of God is everything.” (7:19)

A striking sentence from a Jew who had been circumcised on the eighth day (Philippians 3:5). Paul does not despise his heritage; he says that what saves is God's grace received in faith and lived in obedience.

“Even if you can gain your freedom, make use of your present condition now more than ever.” (7:21)

The Greek of the second half is brief and can be read two ways: “make use of your present condition” (NRSV text) or “avail yourself of the opportunity” (NRSV note). The POC takes the second: if you can gain your freedom, use the chance. Either way, Paul does not praise slavery.

“In whatever condition you were called, brothers and sisters, there remain with God.” (7:24)

The last words, “with God”, are the key. Whatever our state of life, the real question is whether we remain in his presence.

How the Church reads it.

The Church reads this passage as a teaching on vocation: every state of life, married or single, rich or poor, can become the place of holiness (Lumen Gentium 41). Paul's words about slaves are not a defence of slavery. In his letter to Philemon he asks a master to receive his slave back “no longer as a slave but more than a slave, a beloved brother” (Philemon 16), and the Church teaches plainly that enslaving human beings is a sin against their dignity (CCC 2414). Paul's point is that no human condition can stand between a person and the call of Christ.

John 4:21–26 · In spirit and truth

Setting.

Jesus is resting at Jacob's well near the Samaritan town of Sychar. He has asked a Samaritan woman for a drink, crossing two boundaries at once, since “Jews do not share things in common with Samaritans” (4:9). As the conversation deepens, she raises the old question that divided the two peoples: “Our ancestors worshiped on this mountain, but you say that the place where people must worship is in Jerusalem” (4:20).

The mountain is Gerizim. The Samaritans accepted only the five books of Moses and had built a temple on Gerizim, which was destroyed by the Jewish ruler John Hyrcanus in the late second century BC. Jesus' answer moves the question from the place of worship to the person in whom God is worshipped.

Structure.

  • v. 21: neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.
  • v. 22: salvation is from the Jews.
  • vv. 23–24: true worshippers will worship the Father in spirit and truth.
  • vv. 25–26: the woman speaks of the Messiah; Jesus says, “I am he.”

Key words.

  • proskynein, to worship: The word first meant to bow down in reverence. It comes again and again in these verses: the whole conversation is about worship.
  • en pneumati kai alētheia, in spirit and truth: Not simply “sincerely” or “inwardly”. In John, the Spirit is the Holy Spirit whom Jesus will give, and the Truth is Jesus himself (14:6). True worship is worship in the Spirit, through Christ.
  • egō eimi, I am he: Literally “I am”. In John this phrase often recalls the name God revealed to Moses (Exodus 3:14). Here it is the first time in the Gospel that Jesus openly says who he is, and he says it to a Samaritan woman.

Verse by verse.

“Woman, believe me, the hour is coming when you will worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.” (4:21)

“Woman” is not a cold word in John; Jesus uses it also for his mother (2:4; 19:26). The “hour” in John is the hour of his death and glorification. From the cross a new worship will begin.

“for salvation is from the Jews.” (4:22)

Jesus does not set aside Israel's faith; he affirms it. The Messiah comes from Israel, and the Church never forgets this root (CCC 528; Nostra Aetate 4).

“But the hour is coming, and is now here, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth,” (4:23)

“And is now here”: because Jesus is present, the new worship has already begun. The Father himself “seeks” such worshippers; worship begins with God's desire for us.

“I am he, the one who is speaking to you.” (4:26)

The woman had placed the Messiah in the future. Jesus places him in the present, in front of her. She will leave her water jar and become a witness to her town (4:28–29).

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that the worship “in Spirit and in truth” of the New Covenant is not tied exclusively to any one place, because the whole earth is sacred and entrusted to the children of men. Yet when believers gather, they are the living stones built into a spiritual house, and so, where religious liberty allows, Christians build churches as visible signs of the Church living in that place (CCC 1179–1180). Churches dedicated to the Mother of God belong to this picture. The Council of Ephesus in 431 confessed Mary as Theotokos, Mother of God, because the one she bore is truly God; a church in her name is a confession of faith in her Son.

Questions people ask

If true worship is “in spirit and truth”, do we still need church buildings?

Yes. Jesus does not abolish places of prayer; he himself went up to the Temple for the feasts. He says that worship is no longer bound to one mountain or one city, because it is offered in him and through the Spirit. A church is the house where the Church, the people of God, gathers for that worship, above all for the Holy Qurbono (CCC 1180–1181). The building serves the living temple; it does not replace it.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

Our calendar keeps today the memory of the founding of the first church in the name of the holy Mother of God. It does not say where or when that church was built, and we need not supply what it does not say. What it asks us to remember is that, very early, the disciples of Jesus gave houses of prayer the name of his Mother. In the Syriac tradition she is called Yoldath Aloho, the one who gave birth to God, and many of our parishes bear her name. Each such church is a sign that Christians come to the Son in the company of his Mother.

For study and teaching

  • Paul tells me to remain with God in the condition in which I was called. Am I waiting for better circumstances before I try to be holy?
  • Jesus says the Father seeks true worshippers. How do I prepare to worship in spirit and truth when I come to the Holy Qurbono?
