Word for the day

“Why does this generation ask for a sign? Truly I tell you, no sign will be given to this generation.” Mark 8:12

Reflection

Today's Epistle is among the hardest passages in all of Paul, and it helps to remember why he wrote it. Some in Thessalonica had been shaken by a rumour that the day of the Lord had already come, and Paul writes to steady them. He speaks of “the mystery of lawlessness”: “For the mystery of lawlessness is already at work, but only until the one who now restrains it is removed. And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will destroy with the breath of his mouth, annihilating him by the manifestation of his coming.” Christians have read this passage in many ways over the centuries, and the Church has never turned it into a timetable. What she takes from it is plain. Evil is real and works through deception, with “signs” and “wonders” that are false. But the Lord Jesus needs no great army to overcome it: the breath of his mouth is enough. And the reason why some are lost is not that God hides the truth but that they “refused to love the truth and so be saved.” The danger Paul names is a heart that has stopped loving what is true.

That is exactly the heart we meet in the Gospel. The Pharisees came and began to argue with Jesus, “asking him for a sign from heaven, to test him.” Only a few verses earlier, Mark has told us that Jesus fed four thousand people with seven loaves, with baskets left over. The signs were there. What they wanted was not a sign but a proof on their own terms, so that they could stay in charge of the question. Mark tells us something we do not often hear about the Lord: “And he sighed deeply in his spirit and said, ‘Why does this generation ask for a sign? Truly I tell you, no sign will be given to this generation.’” It is not anger; it is the deep sigh of one who loves and is grieved by a closed door. Then he does a quiet and painful thing: “And he left them, and getting into the boat again, he went across to the other side.” The Lord does not force himself on those who test him. In the other Gospels he adds that the one sign given will be the sign of Jonah, his own death and rising. In the Holy Qurbono that sign is given to us again every Sunday.

On this day the Panchangam remembers Morth Makrina, whom the Church calls Macrina the Younger. Her brother, St Gregory of Nyssa, wrote her Life soon after she died in the year 379, and he tells us how a young woman of a Christian family in Cappadocia became the teacher of her whole family. After the death of her fiancé she chose to remain unmarried, and with her mother, Emmelia, she turned the family estate on the river Iris into a community in which women and men lived by prayer, psalm-singing and work, sharing their goods with the poor. She helped her brother Basil, who had come home proud of his learning in the schools, to turn towards a life with Christ; she raised her youngest brother, Peter, who became bishop of Sebaste; and Gregory, bishop of Nyssa, calls her his teacher. When he visited her in her last illness, he found her lying on a plank on the ground, and he heard her speak so clearly about the soul and the resurrection that he later wrote their conversation as a book. She died after a prayer of thanksgiving, signing herself with the cross.

Macrina never asked for a sign, and she never performed one that we would call a show. Her life was the sign: a quiet, steady, hidden holiness in which the whole family came to see Christ. That is what the Lord means when he refuses a sign to the testing crowd and offers himself to those who love the truth. In our own time many look for a sign in every new claim and every dramatic report of a vision, and forget the ordinary signs God has already given: the Gospel, the Holy Qurbono, the forgiveness of sins, and the faithful lives of people like Macrina. Let us ask for what she had: a heart that loves the truth and does not need to test the Lord, and a faith that shows itself in sharing the household's bread with the poor. As the monsoon rain wakes the fields each year without any announcement, so God's grace works quietly in a hidden life.

Homily outline

Theme: “Why does this generation ask for a sign?”

On Monday of the ninth week after Pentecost: the mystery of lawlessness, the love of the truth, the sign refused, and Morth Makrina.

1. The breath of the Lord's mouth

  • The mystery of lawlessness is at work.
  • False signs and wonders deceive.
  • The Lord Jesus overcomes without an army.
“with the breath of his lips he shall kill the wicked.” Isaiah 11:4
“But the Lord is faithful; he will strengthen you and guard you from the evil one.” 2 Thessalonians 3:3

Apply: When you feel afraid of the evil around you, say the name of Jesus and trust his faithfulness.

2. Love of the truth

  • The danger is a heart that does not love what is true.
  • God hides nothing from those who seek.
  • Truth is a person, Jesus.
“Lead me in your truth, and teach me, for you are the God of my salvation; for you I wait all day long.” Psalm 25:5
“Jesus said to him, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.” John 14:6

Apply: Take up one question of faith you have put off and read the Gospel on it.

3. The sign refused

  • The Pharisees test him.
  • The deep sigh of the Lord.
  • The only sign: the sign of Jonah.
“do not harden your hearts, as at Meribah, as on the day at Massah in the wilderness, when your ancestors tested me, and put me to the proof, though they had seen my work.” Psalm 95:8–9
“Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion.” Hebrews 3:15

Apply: Do what you already know is right without asking God for proof first.

4. Macrina, the quiet sign

  • Teacher of her brothers.
  • A home of prayer, psalms and charity.
  • Her life was the sign.
“She opens her mouth with wisdom, and the teaching of kindness is on her tongue.” Proverbs 31:26
“In the same way, let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.” Matthew 5:16

Apply: Do one hidden good deed at home this week, without comment.

The Lord gives himself to those who love the truth, and gives no show to those who only test.

A quiet life of prayer and charity, like Macrina's, is a sign that God is near.

Take-home resolution: Love the truth, stop testing the Lord, and shine quietly in good works.

Full homily

Dear friends, in today's Gospel the Pharisees ask Jesus for a sign from heaven, to test him. And Mark tells us that Jesus sighed deeply in his spirit and said: Why does this generation ask for a sign? Truly I tell you, no sign will be given to this generation (Mark 8:12). Then he left them. He had fed four thousand people with seven loaves only a few verses before. The signs were there. The problem was not a lack of signs but a lack of love for the truth.

That is also the warning in the Epistle. Paul says that the mystery of lawlessness is already at work, with false signs and wonders and deceptions for those who are perishing, because they refused to love the truth and so be saved (2 Thessalonians 2:10). He does not tell us to fear. The Lord Jesus will destroy the lawless one with the breath of his mouth (2 Thessalonians 2:8). What the Lord wants from us is a heart that loves what is true.

Today the Church remembers Morth Makrina, the sister of St Basil and St Gregory of Nyssa. Gregory wrote her Life, and he calls her his teacher. She turned her family's home into a place of prayer, psalms and charity, and she never asked for a sign. Her whole life was a sign. Let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven (Matthew 5:16).

Let us ask the Lord for a heart like hers: honest, quiet and faithful. And let us not look for wonders when the Lord has already given us the Gospel and the Holy Qurbono. Amen.

For children

Children, imagine your friend says, I will only believe you love me if you do something amazing. That would make you sad, wouldn't it? That is how Jesus felt when some people asked him for a special sign. He had already done so many good things! Today we remember Morth Makrina. She showed Jesus to her brothers just by praying, teaching and sharing. You can also show Jesus to your family, just by being kind and honest every day.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you sighed in your spirit over those who asked for a sign, and you give yourself to those who love the truth. Keep us from the deception of false wonders, and guard us from the evil one. Give us a heart that loves what is true and does not set conditions for you. Through the prayers of Morth Makrina, who taught her whole family by a hidden life of prayer, psalms and charity, make our homes places where your light is seen, and make us ready for the day of your coming. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
