Word for the day

“But Jesus said to him, “Follow me, and let the dead bury their own dead.”” Matthew 8:22

Reflection

Twice in five verses Genesis repeats the same words: “that very day.” Abraham “circumcised the flesh of their foreskins that very day, as God had said to him… That very day Abraham and his son Ishmael were circumcised.” He was ninety-nine years old. He did not form a committee or wait for a more convenient season. God spoke, and Abraham obeyed the same day, beginning with himself and then with all his household. Delay is one of the quiet enemies of the Fast. We mean to pray more, to make our confession, to be reconciled, to give alms; and the days slip by. The Psalmist prays the opposite: “I hurry and do not delay to keep your commandments.”

In the Gospel two men approach Jesus. The first is eager: “Teacher, I will follow you wherever you go.” Jesus tells him the cost: “Foxes have holes, and birds of the air have nests; but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head.” The second wants to follow, but later: “Lord, first let me go and bury my father.” Jesus answers: “Follow me, and let the dead bury their own dead.” St John Chrysostom explains that the Lord was not teaching contempt for parents; he was teaching that nothing, not even the most honourable duty, may be placed before following him (in substance). Why is he worth it? Paul gives the answer: in Christ “are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.” Paul struggles in prayer so that the hearts of the Colossians may be “encouraged and united in love,” and he rejoices to see “the firmness of your faith in Christ.” The one who has no place to lay his head is himself the treasure.

Full homily

Dear friends, Genesis says it twice: that very day. Abraham, ninety-nine years old, obeyed God's command that very day, beginning with himself and his household (Genesis 17:23, 26).

Delay is a quiet enemy of the Fast. We mean to pray, to confess, to forgive, to give; and the days slip by. The Psalmist says: I hurry and do not delay to keep your commandments (Psalm 119:60).

In the Gospel one man promises: I will follow you wherever you go. Another says: first let me go and bury my father. Jesus answers: the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head... Follow me (Matthew 8:19–22). Nothing may come before him, not even good things.

Why is he worth it? Because in him are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge (Colossians 2:3). Let us not wait for a better season. Today is the day to follow. Amen.

For children

Children, when God asked Abraham to do something, he did it that very day! When Jesus says follow me, let us not say later, later. Is there something good your mum or dad asked you to do? Do it today, with a smile.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you had nowhere to lay your head, yet in you are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. Give us the promptness of Abraham, who obeyed that very day. Free us from excuses and delays, unite our hearts in love, and make our faith firm, so that we may follow you wherever you go. Amen.
