Word for the day

“Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” John 2:19

Reflection

Last Sunday we celebrated the Church as God's holy dwelling. Today, Hudos Eetho, we celebrate her renewal. The two belong together. The Church is holy because Christ sanctifies her, yet she is made of sinners and is always in need of purification. The Second Vatican Council says it plainly: the Church, “holding sinners in her bosom, is at the same time holy and always in need of being purified” (Lumen Gentium 8).

The readings show both the danger and the remedy. At the foot of Sinai the people make a golden calf, worshipping a god they can control. Solomon, dedicating the Temple, admits that “heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain you, much less this house that I have built” (1 Kings 8:27). God's house can never be our possession. The risen Christ says to the Church of Laodicea: “Because you are lukewarm… I am about to spit you out of my mouth” (Revelation 3:16). The danger of a Church is not only sin but comfort.

Jesus enters the Temple with a whip of cords and drives out the traders. His anger is not against worship but against worship turned into business. Then he points to himself: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” He was speaking of the temple of his body. The true renewal of the Church is always a return to Christ, crucified and risen, whose blood, says Hebrews, purifies “our conscience from dead works to worship the living God” (Hebrews 9:14).

Renewal does not come from outside the Church but from her own sources: the Word, the Mysteries, prayer and repentance. That is why the Lord, after rebuking Laodicea, says: “Listen! I am standing at the door, knocking; if you hear my voice and open the door, I will come in to you and eat with you.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Renew the house of God, beginning with yourself.”

Last Sunday we celebrated the Church as God's holy dwelling. Today, Hudos Eetho, the Church asks to be renewed. Jesus enters the Temple, drives out the traders and speaks of a new temple, his own body. Renewal is not a new building or a new programme; it is a return to God.

1. Zeal for the Father's house

  • Jesus makes a whip and overturns the tables: he will not allow God's house to become a market.
  • At Sinai the people exchanged the living God for a golden calf; idols always creep into the house of God.
  • Our idols today: money, status, rivalry and noise in God's house, and the busy “trade” in our own hearts.
“And they said, “These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt!”” Exodus 32:4
“He told those who were selling the doves, “Take these things out of here! Stop making my Father's house a marketplace!”” John 2:16

Apply: Name one idol that crowds God out of your heart, and remove it this week.

2. Christ is the true temple

  • Solomon knew that no building could contain God.
  • Jesus says: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up”: he speaks of his body.
  • Through his body, crucified and risen, we meet God; in Baptism we become members of that body, the Church.
“But will God indeed dwell on the earth? Even heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain you, much less this house that I have built!” 1 Kings 8:27
“Jesus answered them, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” … But he was speaking of the temple of his body.” John 2:19, 21

Apply: Before Holy Qurbono, remember: you are entering the body of Christ, and you will receive him into your own.

3. Renewal begins with repentance

  • Moses interceded and God relented; there is always a way back.
  • Isaiah invites: “Seek the LORD while he may be found.”
  • The risen Christ knocks at the door of a lukewarm Church; renewal is opening the door to him.
“Seek the LORD while he may be found, call upon him while he is near; let the wicked forsake their way, and the unrighteous their thoughts; let them return to the LORD, that he may have mercy on them.” Isaiah 55:6–7
“Listen! I am standing at the door, knocking; if you hear my voice and open the door, I will come in to you and eat with you, and you with me.” Revelation 3:20

Apply: Make a good confession before the Annunciation season advances.

The Church is renewed when each of us is renewed. Jesus still cleanses the temple, not with a whip of cords now, but with his word and his sacraments.

Let us open the door to him, cast out our idols, and become again a house of prayer for all peoples.

Take-home resolution: Spend ten minutes in silence in church this week, asking: “Lord, what must you cleanse in me?”

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, last Sunday we celebrated the Church as God's holy house. Today, Hudos Eetho, we celebrate the renewal of the Church. Both are true at once: the Church is holy because Christ makes her holy, and she is always in need of renewal because she is made of us, sinners.

Our readings show two dangers. At Sinai the people made a golden calf, a god they could see and control. And the risen Lord says to the Church of Laodicea: “Because you are lukewarm, I will spit you out of my mouth.” The danger of the Church is not only sin, but comfort.

Then Jesus enters the Temple and overturns the tables of the traders. He was not against worship; he was against worship turned into business. And he said: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” He was speaking of his body. Every true renewal of the Church is a return to Christ, crucified and risen.

So where does renewal begin? Not with someone else. With me. With a good Confession, with silence and reverence in church, with a heart that opens when the Lord knocks. “If anyone hears my voice and opens the door, I will come in and eat with him.” In a few moments he will do exactly that at this altar. Amen.

For children

Children, what happens if nobody cleans the house for a long time? Dust everywhere! Jesus once went into the Temple and cleaned it, because people had made it a market.

Our hearts are God's house too. We clean them by saying sorry to Jesus and by praying. Jesus says: “I am standing at your door, knocking.” Shall we open the door to him? Let's say: “Come in, Jesus!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you cleansed the Temple and raised up the temple of your body. Purify your Church, free us from idols and lukewarmness, and come in to eat with us at your holy table. Amen.
