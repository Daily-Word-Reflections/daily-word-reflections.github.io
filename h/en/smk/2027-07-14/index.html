Word for the day

“I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.” 1 Corinthians 3:6

Reflection

The Church at Corinth had split into parties. “What I mean is that each of you says, ‘I belong to Paul,’ or ‘I belong to Apollos,’ or ‘I belong to Cephas,’ or ‘I belong to Christ’” (1 Corinthians 1:12). Today St Paul answers with a picture that every farmer in Kerala understands: “What then is Apollos? What is Paul? Servants through whom you came to believe, as the Lord assigned to each. I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. So neither the one who plants nor the one who waters is anything, but only God who gives the growth.” In these weeks the seedlings of paddy are being transplanted into the flooded fields and the coconut saplings are going into the wet earth. Anyone who has done that work knows that the planter and the person who waters each have a task, and that both together cannot make a single seed grow. Life comes from God, and the servants are only servants. Paul does not make light of their labor: “The one who plants and the one who waters have a common purpose, and each will receive wages according to the labor of each.” The work is real, the reward is real, and the praise belongs to the Lord of the field.

Paul then gives two pictures of the Church: “For we are God's servants, working together; you are God's field, God's building.” A field and a building: a field that must be tended, patiently, season after season; a building that is raised stone upon stone. He goes on to say that he himself laid a foundation and another is building on it, and that “no one can lay any foundation other than the one that has been laid; that foundation is Jesus Christ” (1 Corinthians 3:10–11). The faith we received in India was planted and watered by many hands: the Apostle Thomas, according to tradition, who first sowed the Gospel on our shores; the mothers who taught prayers; the priests who kept the Qurbono alive in hard times. Tomorrow the Church of the Syro-Malankara tradition remembers one of those planters, Archbishop Mar Ivanios, who died on 15 July 1953. He founded the Bethany Ashram in 1919 and the Bethany Sisters in 1925, was received with Mar Theophilos and their companions into full communion with the Catholic Church on 20 September 1930, keeping the Syriac liturgy of the Malankara tradition, and in 1932 became the first Archbishop of Trivandrum. He planted in poverty and in tears, and the growth, in eparchies across India and beyond, has been God's. Tonight is a good evening to thank him, and to ask which part of the field has been given to us to plant or to water.

The Gospel brings us two men who could not see. “As Jesus went on from there, two blind men followed him, crying loudly, ‘Have mercy on us, Son of David!’” They cannot see the road, but they see who he is, for “Son of David” is the title of the promised Messiah, and the prophet had said that “the eyes of the blind shall be opened” (Isaiah 35:5). They follow him all the way into a house, and there Jesus asks them one question: “Do you believe that I am able to do this?” They said to him, “Yes, Lord.” “Then he touched their eyes and said, ‘According to your faith let it be done to you.’ And their eyes were opened.” Faith here is not a payment that buys a miracle; it is an open hand. Then Jesus sternly orders them: “See that no one knows of this.” Probably he wished to avoid becoming a mere wonder-worker before his hour had come. But the two men “went away and spread the news about him throughout that district.” Their gratitude was too great to keep quiet. The cry of the blind men is the cry that rises in every Holy Qurbono: Lord, have mercy. And like the Corinthians whose eyes were dim with party spirit, we too need our eyes opened to see that all growth is God's gift, and that we are one field, one building, one body in the one Lord.

Homily outline

Theme: “I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.”

On Wednesday of the eighth week after Pentecost, the eve of the commemoration of Archbishop Mar Ivanios: the planters of our faith, God's field and building, and the two blind men whose eyes are opened.

1. God gave the growth

  • Paul and Apollos are servants.
  • The one who plants and the one who waters share a purpose.
  • Every harvest is God's gift.
“Unless the Lord builds the house, those who build it labor in vain.” Psalm 127:1
“I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.” 1 Corinthians 3:6

Apply: Do your part of a task this week faithfully, and hand over the result to God in prayer.

2. God's field, God's building

  • A field tended season after season.
  • One foundation, Jesus Christ.
  • Those who planted our faith, and the memory of Mar Ivanios.
“They will be called oaks of righteousness, the planting of the Lord, to display his glory.” Isaiah 61:3
“For no one can lay any foundation other than the one that has been laid; that foundation is Jesus Christ.” 1 Corinthians 3:11

Apply: Thank one person who planted or watered your faith, and say a prayer for the unity of the Church.

3. According to your faith

  • The blind men see who Jesus is.
  • Faith is an open hand.
  • Gratitude cannot keep silent.
“Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped;” Isaiah 35:5
“Then he touched their eyes and said, “According to your faith let it be done to you.”” Matthew 9:29

Apply: Pray the cry of the blind men three times a day: Lord Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on us.

The Church is God's field and God's building; we plant and we water, and God gives the growth, on the one foundation which is Christ.

The cry Have mercy on us opens our eyes to see his hand in all the growth we have received.

Take-home resolution: Plant and water faithfully, thank those who planted before us, and give the glory to God.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul speaks to a divided community: some said, I belong to Paul, and others, I belong to Apollos. And Paul answers with a picture from the field: I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. So neither the one who plants nor the one who waters is anything, but only God who gives the growth (1 Corinthians 3:6–7). In these weeks of rain, our fields are being planted. We know that no farmer makes the seed grow. He prepares the soil, he plants, he waters, and then he waits for God.

Paul adds: you are God's field, God's building (1 Corinthians 3:9). A field needs care in every season; a building rises stone by stone. The faith we have received was planted and watered by many hands that we never saw: the Apostle Thomas, the first Christians of our land, parents and grandparents, priests who kept the Qurbono alive. Tomorrow the Church remembers Archbishop Mar Ivanios, who died on 15 July 1953. He worked and prayed for the unity of the Church and for the Malankara tradition, and he began in poverty. What he planted has grown, and the growth is God's.

In the Gospel, two blind men follow Jesus, crying, Have mercy on us, Son of David! (Matthew 9:27). They could not see the road, but they saw who he was. Jesus asks them: Do you believe that I am able to do this? Yes, Lord, they say. And he touches their eyes: According to your faith let it be done to you (Matthew 9:29). Faith is an open hand that receives. And when they were healed, they could not keep silent about him.

So tonight, let us thank someone who planted our faith. Let us do our part in the field faithfully, and give the glory to the Lord who gives the growth. And let us say, with the blind men, Lord, have mercy on us. Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever planted a seed and watered it every day? You cannot make the seed grow yourself; only God does that. But you plant it and you water it, and God does the rest. St Paul says that we are all workers in God's garden: some plant, some water, and God makes it grow. Somebody planted our faith: our grandparents, our parents, the priests, the catechism teachers. Tomorrow we remember Archbishop Mar Ivanios, who planted something very important in our Church. In the Gospel two men who were blind cried, Jesus, have mercy on us! And Jesus opened their eyes. This week, plant something small, a seed or a flower, and water it, and say a thank-you prayer for the people who planted your faith.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you are the only foundation, and from you comes all growth. We thank you for those who planted and watered the faith in our land, and especially for Archbishop Mar Ivanios, whose memory we shall keep tomorrow. Make us faithful servants in your field, glad to plant and glad to water, and never proud of the harvest. Open our eyes, as you opened the eyes of the two blind men, to see you and to see one another as members of one building. Lord, have mercy on us. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
