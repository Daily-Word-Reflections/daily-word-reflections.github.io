Word for the day

“Do not be afraid; you are of more value than many sparrows.” Luke 12:7

Reflection

Today our Church keeps the feast of Mar Addai Shleeha, and with him we stand at the very fountain of our own Syriac Christian tradition. The story is told by Eusebius of Caesarea, the first great historian of the Church, who says he found it in the archives of Edessa, written in Syriac (Church History 1.13). Abgar, the king of Edessa, a city in the north of Mesopotamia, was gravely ill. He had heard of the healings worked by Jesus, and he sent a letter asking him to come. The answer came that the Lord must first fulfil his mission, but that after he had been taken up he would send one of his disciples. “After the Ascension of Jesus,” Eusebius writes, Judas Thomas, one of the Twelve, sent Thaddaeus, one of the Seventy, to Edessa as herald and evangelist (in substance). The Syriac Churches know this Thaddaeus as Addai. He healed the king, preached Christ crucified and risen, and gathered the first Church of Edessa, which handed on its faith to his disciples Aggai and Mari. Historians judge that the letters themselves belong to legend; but behind the legend stands a fact: from the earliest generations Edessa was a Christian city, and from it the Syriac-speaking Church spread east and south. For us there is a further thread. The same tradition that sends Addai to Edessa sends Thomas to India, and it was in the Syriac world of Edessa that the story of Thomas the Apostle of India was first written down. Mar Addai is a brother in the family tree of our own faith.

Notice when Addai was sent: after the Ascension. That is the season we are in. Before he went up, the Lord told his disciples that repentance and the forgiveness of sins were to be proclaimed in his name to all nations, “beginning from Jerusalem,” and that they would be his witnesses “to the ends of the earth.” The Gospel today shows the courage that such witness needs. Jesus says: “Nothing is covered up that will not be uncovered... what you have whispered behind closed doors will be proclaimed from the housetops.” What the apostles heard quietly in the upper room was meant to be shouted in the streets of Jerusalem and Edessa and Kodungallur. Then he says, “I tell you, my friends, do not fear those who kill the body, and after that can do nothing more.” And he gives the reason, in one of the tenderest pictures in the Gospel: “Are not five sparrows sold for two pennies? Yet not one of them is forgotten in God's sight. But even the hairs of your head are all counted. Do not be afraid; you are of more value than many sparrows.” The missionary is not brave because he is strong, but because he knows he is counted, hair by hair, in the hands of the Father.

St Paul shows the other side of mission: the support of the Church at home. From prison he thanks the Philippians for their gift: “I rejoice in the Lord greatly that now at last you have revived your concern for me.” He wants them to know that he does not depend on comfort: “I have learned to be content with whatever I have. I know what it is to have little, and I know what it is to have plenty... I can do all things through him who strengthens me.” And yet he adds, with gratitude, “it was kind of you to share my distress.” The Philippians could not travel with Paul, but they shared in his mission by prayer and by giving. St Augustine turned this strength into a prayer: “Give what you command, and command what you will” (Confessions 10.29, in substance). On the feast of an apostle who was sent, we who stay at home are asked two things: to speak of Christ without fear in our own place, and to share the distress of those who carry him further. In two days the Spirit of Pentecost will come to make both possible.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today our Church celebrates Mar Addai Shleeha, the apostle of Edessa. An old story, told by the historian Eusebius, says that the king of Edessa, Abgar, was very ill and sent a letter to Jesus asking him to come and heal him. After the Ascension of the Lord, the apostle Thomas sent Addai, one of the seventy disciples, to Edessa. Addai healed the king and preached the Gospel, and Edessa became one of the first Christian cities in the world. From there the faith spread to the Syriac-speaking peoples. That is why this feast matters to us: we too belong to the family of the Syriac Churches, and we too received the faith from the apostle Thomas.

Notice when Addai was sent: after the Ascension. The Lord had gone up to heaven, and his disciples went out to the ends of the earth. In the Gospel today Jesus tells them not to be afraid: “do not fear those who kill the body, and after that can do nothing more.” Many of the apostles did die for Christ. But Jesus gives them a reason for courage. God does not forget even a sparrow, a little bird sold for a few coins. “But even the hairs of your head are all counted. Do not be afraid; you are of more value than many sparrows.”

St Paul, writing from prison, shows us another part of mission. He thanks the Philippians for sending him help. He says, “I have learned to be content with whatever I have,” and then the famous words, “I can do all things through him who strengthens me.” Paul is not boasting of his own strength. He is saying that Christ is his strength in hunger and in plenty. And he thanks them because they “shared his distress.” They could not travel with him, but by their prayer and their gifts they were part of his mission.

So today we are asked two things. First, to speak of Jesus without fear in our own place: our home, our office, our school. Second, to share in the work of those who go further: our missionary priests and sisters in North India and abroad. Pray for them, write to them, support them. In two days the Holy Spirit will come at Pentecost to give us courage. Let us receive the Lord in this Holy Qurbono and ask him to make us apostles like Mar Addai.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Have you ever watched little sparrows hopping around looking for grains of rice? They are small and seem unimportant. But Jesus said that God never forgets even one sparrow. And then he said something amazing: God has counted every hair on your head! Can you count your hair? No one can! But God knows you that well. So Jesus says, “Do not be afraid.” Today we remember Mar Addai, who travelled far away to tell a sick king about Jesus. He was not afraid, because he knew God loved him. When you feel scared, remember the sparrows, and say, “Jesus, I trust in you.”

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, after your Ascension you sent your apostles to the ends of the earth, and through Mar Addai Shleeha you brought the light of the Gospel to Edessa and to the Syriac-speaking peoples. We thank you for the faith that came to us from the apostles, through Mar Thoma and the Church of our fathers. Take away our fear of those who can harm only the body, and teach us that we are counted in your hands, more precious than many sparrows. Give us the contentment of St Paul and his strength in you, and make us generous in sharing the distress of those who carry your name to others. By the prayers of your holy Mother and of Mar Addai, send your Holy Spirit upon your Church, and make us your witnesses where we live. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory for ever. Amen.
