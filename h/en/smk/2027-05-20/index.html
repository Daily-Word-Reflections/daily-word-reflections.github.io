The Four Evangelists · Thursday after the Sunday of Pentecost

20 May 2027 · Syro-Malankara Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Also: Syro-Malabar Rite · Latin Rite · മലയാളം

Readings

  • Epistle Romans 10:14–20 · “How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!”; faith comes from what is heard
  • Gospel Matthew 28:16–20 · “Go therefore and make disciples of all nations”; “I am with you always”

Word for the day

“So faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ.” Romans 10:17

Reflection

In the first week of the season of Pentecost our Church gathers four saints into one feast: the Four Evangelists, Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, through whose writing the good news of Jesus reaches every generation. Matthew was the tax collector whom Jesus called from his booth (Matthew 9:9). Mark, ancient writers tell us, set down the preaching of St Peter, whose interpreter he had been (Papias, in Eusebius, Church History 3.39, in substance). Luke was the physician and companion of St Paul (Colossians 4:14), who also wrote the Acts of the Apostles. John is the disciple whom Jesus loved, who “has testified to these things and has written them” (John 21:24). Four men, four styles, four points of view, and one Lord. St Irenaeus, writing in the second century, already insisted that the Gospel is fourfold and yet one, held together by one Spirit, like the four winds or the four pillars of a single house (Against Heresies 3.11.8, in substance). The Church gave each evangelist a sign from the four living creatures that Ezekiel saw around the throne of God: the human face for Matthew, the lion for Mark, the ox for Luke, the eagle for John.

The Epistle explains why the evangelists matter. St Paul builds a chain of questions: “But how are they to call on one in whom they have not believed? And how are they to believe in one of whom they have never heard? And how are they to hear without someone to proclaim him? And how are they to proclaim him unless they are sent? As it is written, ‘How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!’” Then the conclusion: “So faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ.” Nobody invents faith for himself. It comes by hearing, and we hear because someone was sent and someone wrote. Paul goes on to speak with sorrow and hope of his own people Israel, who had heard and not yet believed; elsewhere he insists that “the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable” (Romans 11:29). He also applies to the preachers a line from the psalm about the heavens: “Their voice has gone out to all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world.” The four Gospels are that voice. They have gone out in hundreds of languages; in our own Syriac tradition the Gospels were read for some centuries in a single woven harmony, until in the fifth century bishops such as Rabbula of Edessa saw to it that the four separate Gospels were read in the churches. Today they are read in Malayalam at our altars.

The Gospel is the last page of St Matthew. The eleven go to the mountain in Galilee: “When they saw him, they worshiped him; but some doubted.” Even at the end, the evangelist is honest about the disciples' weakness. Jesus does not wait for their doubts to vanish. He comes near and gives them everything: “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything that I have commanded you. And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” He is with us in the Holy Qurbono, and he is with us in the word. That is why, in our Qurbono, the Gospel is not simply read like a notice: it is called the Evangelion, it is proclaimed by the priest, and the people stand to hear the Lord himself speak. St Jerome said that ignorance of the Scriptures is ignorance of Christ (Commentary on Isaiah, prologue, in substance). On this feast, take one of the four Gospels and begin to read it through, a chapter a day. Mark can be read in under three weeks. Let the evangelists do for you what they have done for the whole Church: bring you face to face with Jesus.

Homily outline

Theme: “So faith comes from what is heard.”

On the feast of the Four Evangelists: four voices and one Gospel, the word that is heard and believed, and the Lord who sends and stays with us.

1. Faith comes from what is heard

  • Sent, proclaimed, heard, believed.
  • Beautiful feet.
  • The word that stands forever.
“The grass withers, the flower fades; but the word of our God will stand forever.” Isaiah 40:8
“So faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ.” Romans 10:17

Apply: Listen to the Evangelion at your next Qurbono as if you were hearing it for the first time, and remember one sentence of it all day.

2. Four Gospels, one Christ

  • Matthew, Mark, Luke and John.
  • Four living creatures around the throne.
  • Written so that we may believe.
“As for the appearance of their faces: the four had the face of a human being, the face of a lion on the right side, the face of an ox on the left side, and the face of an eagle” Ezekiel 1:10
“But these are written so that you may come to believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that through believing you may have life in his name.” John 20:31

Apply: Choose one Gospel and read it through, one chapter a day, starting today.

3. Go and make disciples

  • Some doubted, and still he sent them.
  • To all nations.
  • With us always.
“yet their voice goes out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world.” Psalm 19:4
“And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” Matthew 28:20

Apply: Share one Gospel verse this week with someone who is far from faith, simply and without argument.

Through the four evangelists the voice of Christ has gone out to all the earth and reaches us today.

Faith comes from what is heard: what we hear we must also hand on, trusting the Lord who is with us always.

Take-home resolution: Read a Gospel through, listen to the Evangelion as the voice of the Lord, and bring the good news to one person this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, today our Church honours the Four Evangelists, Matthew, Mark, Luke and John. Matthew the tax collector, Mark who wrote down Peter's preaching, Luke the physician and companion of Paul, John the beloved disciple. Four voices, one Lord. St Irenaeus said that the Gospel is fourfold, held together by one Spirit.

St Paul tells us why they matter: how are they to believe in one of whom they have never heard? And how are they to hear without someone to proclaim him? (Romans 10:14). So faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ (Romans 10:17). Someone was sent, someone wrote, someone read it to us. How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news! (Romans 10:15).

In the Gospel the risen Lord meets the eleven on the mountain. They worshiped him; but some doubted (Matthew 28:17). Even so, he sends them: Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, and he promises: I am with you always, to the end of the age (Matthew 28:19–20). He is with us in the Qurbono and in the word. That is why we stand when the Evangelion is proclaimed.

Let us honour the evangelists by reading what they wrote. Take one Gospel and read a chapter a day. Read it with your family. Then share it: faith comes from what is heard, and someone near you is waiting to hear. Amen.

For children

Children, who wrote the stories of Jesus that we hear in church? Four friends of Jesus: Matthew, Mark, Luke and John! We call them the evangelists, which means people who bring good news. In pictures, Matthew has a man, Mark a lion, Luke an ox and John an eagle. At the end of Matthew's book, Jesus says: I am with you always. Whenever you hear the Gospel, Jesus is talking to you! Can you ask your parents to read you one story from a Gospel tonight?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, living Word of the Father, you gave your Church the four holy Gospels through Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, so that all who hear may believe and have life in your name. We thank you for the evangelists and for all who have handed on your word in every language, and in ours. Open our ears to hear you, our hearts to believe, and our lips to proclaim you. Send us, as you sent the eleven, to make disciples, and keep your promise to be with us always, to the end of the age. With your Father and your Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
