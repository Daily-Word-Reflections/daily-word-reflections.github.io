Readings

  • Epistle Hebrews 10:1–4 · The law has only a shadow of the good things to come
  • Gospel John 15:11–17 · I have called you friends; love one another as I have loved you

Word for the day

“I do not call you servants any longer, because the servant does not know what the master is doing; but I have called you friends, because I have made known to you everything that I have heard from my Father.” John 15:15

Reflection

Today our Church keeps the memory of Mar Aprem Malpan, St Ephrem the Syrian, the greatest poet of our Syriac tradition. He was born at Nisibis in Mesopotamia at the beginning of the fourth century, served the Church there as a deacon and as a teacher of the faith, and when Nisibis was handed over to Persia in 363 he went with many Christians to Edessa, where he taught and wrote until his death in 373. He never became a priest or a bishop; he remained a deacon, a servant, all his life. Yet his hymns, the madrashe, taught the faith to whole peoples, and they are still sung in the prayers of the Syriac Churches, ours among them. He wrote them for choirs of women and girls, so that the doctrine of the Church might be carried home on melodies that people loved. The Syriac tradition calls him the Harp of the Spirit. In 1920 Pope Benedict XV declared him a Doctor of the universal Church. It is fitting that his memory falls in the Apostles' Fast: Ephrem was a man of fasting and prayer, and tradition remembers that in his last years, when famine struck Edessa, he left his cell to organise help for the hungry and the sick.

The Epistle of the day is a passage Ephrem would have loved. “Since the law has only a shadow of the good things to come and not the true form of these realities, it can never, by the same sacrifices that are continually offered year after year, make perfect those who approach.” The sacrifices of the old covenant were true worship given by God to Israel, but they were shadows cast ahead by the coming light. “For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins.” Only the one offering of Christ does that. Ephrem saw the whole Bible and the whole of creation as full of such shadows and symbols, which our tradition calls raze, mysteries. The lamb, the ark, the manna, the rock in the desert, the dew, the pearl, the olive and its oil: everything, for him, was a window through which Christ could be seen. He wrote that the Word of God is like a living spring: whoever drinks from it takes only a little and leaves far more behind, and should give thanks for what he has received instead of grieving over what he could not take in (Commentary on the Diatessaron 1, in substance). That is how he read Scripture, with wonder and thanksgiving, never pretending to have exhausted it. And Ephrem sang tenderly of the holy Mother of God, through whom the reality came into the world. Addressing Christ, he wrote that only he and his Mother are beautiful in every way, for in him there is no blemish and in his Mother no stain (Nisibene Hymns 27, in substance).

The Gospel shows what the shadows were pointing to: not only a perfect sacrifice but a friendship. “This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you. No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends. You are my friends if you do what I command you. I do not call you servants any longer, because the servant does not know what the master is doing; but I have called you friends.” The old sacrifices could not make the worshippers perfect; the sacrifice of Christ makes servants into friends. And Jesus adds: “You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last.” Ephrem's fruit has lasted for sixteen centuries; every time the Syriac Churches sing his hymns, the friend of Christ is still bearing fruit. Ours may be smaller and hidden: a prayer taught to a child, a hymn sung with love, a meal given to a hungry neighbour. On this Friday of the fast, let us give thanks for the friendship Christ offers us, and ask Mar Aprem to teach us to sing it.

Homily outline

Theme: “I do not call you servants any longer… but I have called you friends.”

On the memory of Mar Aprem Malpan, in the Apostles' Fast: the shadow and the reality, the friendship of Christ, and the Harp of the Spirit.

1. The shadow and the true form

  • Sacrifices offered year after year.
  • What the blood of animals could not do.
  • The one offering of Christ.
“Sacrifice and offering you do not desire, but you have given me an open ear.” Psalm 40:6
“he entered once for all into the Holy Place, not with the blood of goats and calves, but with his own blood, thus obtaining eternal redemption.” Hebrews 9:12

Apply: At the next Holy Qurbono, unite your own small sacrifices of the fast with the one offering of Christ.

2. Friends, not servants

  • The greatest love.
  • Knowing the Master's heart.
  • Chosen to bear fruit that will last.
“Thus the Lord used to speak to Moses face to face, as one speaks to a friend.” Exodus 33:11
“We love because he first loved us.” 1 John 4:19

Apply: Speak to Jesus today for five minutes as to a friend, telling him plainly what is in your heart.

3. The Harp of the Spirit

  • A deacon all his life.
  • Christ in every symbol.
  • Hymns that carried the faith home.
“Awake, my soul! Awake, O harp and lyre! I will awake the dawn.” Psalm 57:8
“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly; teach and admonish one another in all wisdom; and with gratitude in your hearts sing psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs to God.” Colossians 3:16

Apply: Learn by heart one hymn of our liturgy, or teach one to a child, this week.

The shadows of the law led to Christ, and Christ leads us into friendship.

Mar Aprem sang that friendship for the whole Church; we are invited to live it and to sing it.

Take-home resolution: Live as a friend of Jesus: love one another as he loved us, and praise him with your whole heart.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we remember Mar Aprem Malpan, St Ephrem the Syrian. He was a deacon in Nisibis and Edessa in the fourth century, and he wrote hundreds of hymns that taught the faith. Our Syriac tradition calls him the Harp of the Spirit, and the whole Church honours him as a Doctor.

The Epistle says that the law has only a shadow of the good things to come (Hebrews 10:1). The old sacrifices pointed forward, but it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins (Hebrews 10:4). Only Jesus, by his one offering, takes away sin. Mar Aprem loved to find these shadows and symbols in Scripture and in nature, and to show how all of them point to Christ.

And what do they point to? To friendship. Jesus says: I do not call you servants any longer, but I have called you friends (John 15:15). No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends (John 15:13). On the Cross, Jesus made us his friends. And friends of Jesus must love one another as he loved us.

Mar Aprem was such a friend. He prayed, fasted, sang and, when famine came, cared for the hungry. In this Apostles' Fast, let us ask him to teach us to see Christ in the Scriptures, to praise him with joy, and to love one another in deeds. Amen.

For children

Children, do you like to sing? Today we remember a saint who loved to sing about Jesus: Mar Aprem. He lived long, long ago and wrote many, many songs to teach people about Jesus and Mother Mary. People called him the harp of the Holy Spirit, because his songs were like music played by God. Jesus says today: you are my friends. A friend of Jesus sings his praises and helps others. So today, sing a hymn for Jesus, and help someone who needs you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, the shadows of the law have passed and you have come, the true and only sacrifice that takes away sin. You no longer call us servants but friends. Through the prayers of Mar Aprem Malpan, the Harp of the Spirit, open our eyes to see you in the Scriptures and in all your creation, teach us to praise you with joy, and make us love one another as you have loved us. Let our lives bear fruit that will last, to the glory of your Father and the Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
