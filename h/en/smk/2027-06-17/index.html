Word for the day

“As you therefore have received Christ Jesus the Lord, continue to live your lives in him, rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, abounding in thanksgiving.” Colossians 2:6–7

Reflection

The monsoon has come to Kerala, and with it the yearly lesson of the rivers. We have seen how quickly water rises, how a canal becomes a river and a river becomes a sea, and how some houses stand while others crack and fall. Jesus knew such floods in Galilee, and he uses them in today's Gospel: “Why do you call me ‘Lord, Lord,’ and do not do what I tell you? I will show you what someone is like who comes to me, hears my words, and acts on them. That one is like a man building a house, who dug deeply and laid the foundation on rock; when a flood arose, the river burst against that house but could not shake it, because it had been well built.” The other man built “on the ground without a foundation,” and “immediately it fell, and great was the ruin of that house.” Notice that both men hear the words of Jesus. Both build. Both houses face the same flood. The difference is hidden underground: one dug deeply, the other did not bother. The difference between them is not hearing but doing. It is possible to call Jesus “Lord, Lord” every Sunday, to sing beautifully in the Qurbono, to know many hymns by heart, and still to be building on sand, because the words we hear never reach the place where we decide how to live.

St Paul uses the same picture, and adds another: “continue to live your lives in him, rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, abounding in thanksgiving.” Rooted like a tree, built up like a house: the Christian life needs both depth and growth. And Paul tells the Colossians why Christ alone can be the foundation: “For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, and you have come to fullness in him.” There were teachers in Colossae offering something more, philosophies, special days, powers of the universe to be feared and placated. Paul answers: you lack nothing in Christ. Then he describes what happened in Baptism, in words our Church sings at every font: “when you were buried with him in baptism, you were also raised with him through faith in the power of God, who raised him from the dead.” And he gives one of the most moving images in all his letters. Our sins were like a written record, a bond of debt signed in our own hand, standing against us. God took that record, “erasing the record that stood against us with its legal demands. He set this aside, nailing it to the cross.” St John Chrysostom, preaching on this verse, says that Christ did not merely cancel the bond but tore it in pieces and nailed it up for all to see, as a sign that it could never be used against us again (Homilies on Colossians 6, in substance).

Here is the rock under the house. Not our good resolutions, which are often sand, but what Christ has done: the debt nailed to the cross, the powers disarmed, the new life given in Baptism. Our part is to dig deeply: to let his word go down past our feelings and habits to the place where we choose. The Apostles' Fast, which began yesterday, is a time for digging. When we fast, we feel the ground shift a little; we discover how much of our peace depended on comfort, food, our phone, other people's approval. Underneath, if we keep digging, we find the rock that does not move. In these monsoon days, when you hear the rain on the roof at night, let it remind you to ask: on what is my life built? And pray for those whose houses are threatened by water this season, and for all who have lost homes in floods. A Christian who is rooted in Christ is not spared the floods, but is not swept away by them.

Full homily

Dear friends, the monsoon has come, and today Jesus speaks about floods. He tells of two builders. One dug deeply and laid the foundation on rock; when a flood arose, the river burst against that house but could not shake it (Luke 6:48). The other built without a foundation, and immediately it fell (Luke 6:49). Both men heard the words of Jesus. Only one acted on them.

Jesus asks a hard question: why do you call me ‘Lord, Lord,’ and do not do what I tell you? (Luke 6:46). We can come to church, sing well and know many prayers, and still be building on sand, if the word of God never reaches our daily choices. Hearing is not enough. We must dig deeply.

St Paul tells us what the rock is. Continue to live your lives in him, rooted and built up in him and established in the faith (Colossians 2:6–7). Christ is the rock. In Baptism we were buried with him and raised with him. And God took the record of our sins and set it aside, nailing it to the cross (Colossians 2:14). Our foundation is not our own strength but what Christ has done for us.

In this Apostles' Fast, let us dig deeply. Let us find one word of Jesus that we hear but do not live, and live it. And when the rain falls at night, let us remember to pray for those whose homes are in danger. A house built on Christ will stand. Amen.

For children

Children, have you seen how strong the water is in the monsoon? It can carry away mud, trees and even houses. Jesus told a story about two houses. One was built on a big rock, and when the flood came it stood strong. The other was built on soft ground, and it fell down with a crash! Jesus says: if you listen to me and do what I say, you are like the house on the rock. So when Jesus says, be kind, share, forgive, let us really do it. Then we will be strong like that house.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, in you the whole fullness of God dwells bodily, and in you we have come to fullness. You nailed to the cross the record that stood against us and raised us with you in Baptism. Do not let us call you Lord, Lord, and fail to do what you say. Help us in these days of fasting to dig deeply and build our lives on the rock of your word, so that when the floods come we may stand firm. Protect all who are threatened by the waters of this season, and give shelter to those who have lost their homes. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Both readings today are about foundations. Paul tells the Colossians to live “rooted and built up” in Christ, in whom “the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily”, and not to be carried off by teachings that do not come from him. Jesus ends the Sermon on the Plain with two houses: one built on rock by someone who hears his words and acts on them, the other built without a foundation. The key words are rooted and doing.

Colossians 2:6–15 · Rooted and built up in him

Setting.

Colossae was a small town in the Lycus valley of Asia Minor, near Laodicea and Hierapolis. The church there was founded not by Paul but by his co-worker Epaphras (1:7). The letter warns against a teaching that called itself “philosophy” and mixed reverence for the “elemental spirits”, special festivals, food rules and the worship of angels (2:8, 16–18). Paul's answer is Christ: in him the Colossians already have everything.

The letter presents itself as written by Paul from prison. Many scholars think it was written by a disciple in Paul's name, while others hold that Paul wrote it himself; the Church receives it as inspired Scripture either way.

Structure.

  • vv. 6–7: continue to live in Christ, as you were taught.
  • v. 8: a warning against empty philosophy.
  • vv. 9–10: the fullness of God dwells in Christ, and you are filled in him.
  • vv. 11–12: the circumcision of Christ: buried and raised with him in Baptism.
  • vv. 13–15: God forgave us, cancelled the record against us, and triumphed over the powers.

Key words.

  • stoicheia tou kosmou, elemental spirits of the universe: The phrase is debated. It can mean the basic elements of the world, or the first principles of a teaching, or spiritual powers thought to rule the world. In every case Paul's point is the same: nothing in creation can take the place of Christ.
  • plērōma, fullness: God's whole being and life. It does not dwell in Christ partly or for a time, but “bodily”, really and permanently, in the Word made flesh.
  • cheirographon, the record: A handwritten note of debt, signed by the debtor. Our sins had made us debtors before God; God has wiped out the bond and nailed it to the cross.

Verse by verse.

“As you therefore have received Christ Jesus the Lord, continue to live your lives in him,” (2:6)

“Received” is the word for receiving a tradition that is handed on. The Colossians did not receive only teaching about Christ; they received Christ himself.

“For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily,” (2:9)

One of the clearest statements of the divinity of Christ in the New Testament. The Church has always read it with the faith of the Councils: Christ is true God and true man in one person.

“when you were buried with him in baptism, you were also raised with him through faith in the power of God,” (2:12)

Baptism is not a symbol of something done elsewhere. In it the believer is joined to the death and resurrection of Christ.

“erasing the record that stood against us with its legal demands. He set this aside, nailing it to the cross.” (2:14)

The picture is bold: the note of our debt is nailed up beside the Crucified, cancelled. What condemned us has been taken away.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that Baptism signifies and brings about the Christian's descent into the tomb with Christ, dying to sin in order to walk in newness of life (CCC 628; Colossians 2:12). It also sees in the circumcision of Jesus a sign that prefigures the “circumcision of Christ”, which is Baptism (CCC 527). Paul's warning against empty philosophy does not condemn reason, which the Church honours; it warns against any system that would put something other than Christ at the centre.

Luke 6:46–49 · The house built on rock

Setting.

These verses close the Sermon on the Plain (6:20–49), Luke's shorter form of the teaching that Matthew gives as the Sermon on the Mount. Matthew's parable speaks of rock and sand (Matthew 7:24–27); Luke's builder digs deep to lay a foundation, and the danger is a river in flood. The image fits a land where dry stream beds can suddenly fill with water after heavy rain.

Structure.

  • v. 46: the question: why call me “Lord” and not do what I say?
  • v. 47: the one who comes, hears and acts.
  • v. 48: like a man who dug deep and built on rock.
  • v. 49: the one who hears and does not act: a house without a foundation.

Key words.

  • Kyrie, Kyrie, Lord, Lord: The title of faith and worship. Jesus does not reject it; he asks that it be true in life as well as on the lips.
  • petra, rock: Solid bedrock under the soil. The rock is his word heard and done; behind it stands Jesus himself, the sure foundation.

Verse by verse.

“Why do you call me ‘Lord, Lord,’ and do not do what I tell you?” (6:46)

A gentle but searching question. Prayer and worship are not opposed to action; they are meant to lead into it.

“That one is like a man building a house, who dug deeply and laid the foundation on rock;” (6:48)

Digging deeply takes time and effort and cannot be seen once the house stands. So it is with prayer, Scripture and the sacraments: the hidden work holds the visible life up.

“But the one who hears and does not act is like a man who built a house on the ground without a foundation.” (6:49)

Both men heard the word. The difference is not knowledge but obedience. The storm does not create the difference; it reveals it.

How the Church reads it.

St James says the same: “be doers of the word, and not merely hearers who deceive themselves” (James 1:22). The Second Vatican Council teaches that the word of God is the support and energy of the Church, the food of the soul and the pure and lasting source of spiritual life (Dei Verbum 21). The house built on rock is the life that receives this word not only in the ear but in the hands.

Questions people ask

Does “nailing the record to the cross” mean that God's commandments no longer bind Christians?

No. What is nailed to the cross is the record of our debt, the sentence that stood against us, not God's holy will. Jesus himself said, “If you wish to enter into life, keep the commandments” (Matthew 19:17), and the Church teaches that the Ten Commandments bind Christians (CCC 2068). Grace does not abolish the commandments; it gives us the strength to keep them out of love.

For study and teaching

  • What is one word of Jesus that I have heard many times but have not yet put into practice?
