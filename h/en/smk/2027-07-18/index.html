Word for the day

“For all who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted.” Luke 14:11

Reflection

At a dinner Jesus watches the guests choosing the places of honour and says: “When you are invited, go and sit down at the lowest place, so that when your host comes, he may say to you, ‘Friend, move up higher’; then you will be honoured in the presence of all who sit at the table with you. For all who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted.” Humility is not thinking little of ourselves; it is letting God decide our place.

In the evening Gospel Jesus tells three small parables of the kingdom. A man sows good seed, but while everyone sleeps an enemy sows weeds among the wheat. The servants want to pull them up at once. The master says: “No; for in gathering the weeds you would uproot the wheat along with them. Let both of them grow together until the harvest.” God is patient. The Church on earth is a field of wheat and weeds; judgment belongs to God at the harvest, not to us now.

The kingdom is also like a mustard seed, “the smallest of all the seeds, but when it has grown it is the greatest of shrubs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and make nests in its branches”; and like yeast that a woman mixed into three measures of flour until all of it was leavened. God works through small and hidden things: a family Rosary, a catechism class, one honest worker in an office.

Paul reaches Rome as a prisoner, but believers walk out to meet him, and “on seeing them, Paul thanked God and took courage.” He reminds the Corinthians, who were taking one another to court, of what they had become: “you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and in the Spirit of our God.” A humble and patient Church, aware of the grace she has received, will grow like the mustard seed.

Homily outline

Theme: “The lowest place and the hidden seed.”

At a dinner Jesus teaches humility; in the parables he shows how God's kingdom grows quietly, patiently, from small beginnings. In two weeks the Fifteen Days' Fast in honour of the Mother of God begins.

1. Take the lowest place

  • Guests rush for places of honour; Jesus tells them to sit at the lowest.
  • Humility is not thinking little of ourselves but seeing ourselves truly before God.
  • The greater one's position, the greater the need for humility.
“The greater you are, the more you must humble yourself; so you will find favor in the sight of the Lord.” Sirach 3:18
“For all who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted.” Luke 14:11

Apply: This week let someone else have the honour, the credit or the last word.

2. Wheat and weeds: the patience of God

  • Good and evil grow together in the world and even in the Church until the harvest.
  • God is patient, giving time for repentance; judgement belongs to him.
  • Our task is not to uproot others but to be wheat.
“Through such works you have taught your people that the righteous must be kind, and you have filled your children with good hope, because you give repentance for sins.” Wisdom 12:19
“Let both of them grow together until the harvest; and at harvest time I will tell the reapers, Collect the weeds first and bind them in bundles to be burned, but gather the wheat into my barn.” Matthew 13:30

Apply: Replace one judgemental thought about someone with a prayer for them.

3. Mustard seed and yeast

  • The kingdom begins small and grows great; it works quietly like yeast.
  • Keeping God's law faithfully is Israel's wisdom before the nations; our faithful lives are a witness too.
  • Mary, the humble handmaid, is the perfect example of this hidden growth.
“You must observe them diligently, for this will show your wisdom and discernment to the peoples, who, when they hear all these statutes, will say, “Surely this great nation is a wise and discerning people!”” Deuteronomy 4:6
“The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed that someone took and sowed in his field; it is the smallest of all the seeds, but when it has grown it is the greatest of shrubs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and make nests in its branches.” Matthew 13:31–32

Apply: Begin now to plan how your family will keep the Fifteen Days' Fast from 1 August.

God's kingdom grows through the humble, the patient and the faithful in small things.

Let us take the lowest place, trust God's patience, and be yeast in the world.

Take-home resolution: Pray the Magnificat (Luke 1:46–55) each evening this week.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, Jesus is at a dinner and notices the guests rushing for the best seats. He says: Sit in the lowest place, and let the host call you higher. “For all who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted.” In families, parishes and offices there is often a quiet competition for the best seat. Jesus offers us freedom: let God decide your place.

In the evening Gospel a farmer's enemy sows weeds among his wheat. The servants want to pull them out at once. The master says: no, you will pull out the wheat too; let them grow together until the harvest. We are often impatient servants. We want to separate the good and the bad in the Church and in our families right now. But God is patient. Many who look like weeds today will become wheat tomorrow. Judgment belongs to him.

Then Jesus speaks of the tiny mustard seed that becomes a tree, and of the yeast hidden in flour. God's kingdom grows quietly: a mother's prayer, a catechism class on Sunday morning, a young person's honest word. Do not despise small beginnings.

St Paul reminds the Corinthians: “you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ.” Remember who you are. And remember: on 1 August the Fifteen Days' Fast begins, in honour of our Mother's Assumption. Let us prepare our hearts with humility. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus said: when you go to a party, don't run for the best chair! Sit in a simple place, and the host may say, “Come up here, friend!”

He also told a story about a tiny mustard seed that grew into a big tree where birds could build nests.

Small kind acts can grow big too! This week, let someone else go first.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you lifted up the humble and were patient with the field of wheat and weeds. Teach us to take the lowest place, to leave judgment to you, and to trust the small seeds of your kingdom. Wash, sanctify and justify us in your Spirit, and prepare us to keep the fast in honour of your Mother. Amen.
