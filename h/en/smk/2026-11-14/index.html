Word for the day

“Jesus said to him, “Have I been with you all this time, Philip, and you still do not know me? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.” John 14:9

Reflection

Paul asks to be regarded “as servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries”, and the one thing required of stewards is that they be found trustworthy. He does not even judge himself; the Lord will judge, and he “will bring to light the things now hidden in darkness and will disclose the purposes of the heart.” Every priest, catechist and parent is such a steward: not owner, but servant.

Philip, the apostle we honour today, makes an honest request at the Last Supper: “Lord, show us the Father, and we will be satisfied.” Jesus answers with one of the most astonishing sentences of the Gospel: “Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.” In the face of Jesus we see God. And he promises that whatever we ask in his name, he will do, so that the Father may be glorified in the Son.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.”

Faithful stewards who look on Christ.

1. Faithful stewards

  • God promised a faithful priest.
  • Stewards of mysteries must be trustworthy.
“I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who shall do according to what is in my heart and in my mind.” 1 Samuel 2:35
“Think of us in this way, as servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries. Moreover, it is required of stewards that they be found trustworthy.” 1 Corinthians 4:1–2

Apply: Pray for your parish priest by name.

2. The Lord searches the heart

  • God tests the mind and heart.
  • Do not judge before the time.
“I the LORD test the mind and search the heart,” Jeremiah 17:10
“Therefore do not pronounce judgment before the time, before the Lord comes, who will bring to light the things now hidden in darkness and will disclose the purposes of the heart.” 1 Corinthians 4:5

Apply: Leave one judgment to God today.

3. Show us the Father

  • Moses asked to see God's glory.
  • Philip asked to see the Father.
“Moses said, “Show me your glory, I pray.”” Exodus 33:18
“Philip said to him, “Lord, show us the Father, and we will be satisfied.”” John 14:8

Apply: Spend five minutes before an icon of Christ.

We are stewards, not owners.

In Jesus' face we see the Father.

Take-home resolution: Look at Jesus in silent prayer each day this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul asks to be regarded as servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries; and it is required of stewards that they be found trustworthy. He does not even judge himself; when the Lord comes, he will bring to light the things now hidden in darkness and disclose the purposes of the heart (1 Corinthians 4:1–5).

Today is the memory of Mar Philipose Shleeha, St Philip the Apostle. At the Last Supper Philip says to Jesus: Lord, show us the Father, and we will be satisfied. Jesus answers: have I been with you all this time, Philip, and you still do not know me? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father (John 14:8–9).

The face of the Father is in Jesus. Like Philip we too are sometimes close to Jesus without truly knowing him. Today, at the Holy Qurbono and in prayer, let us look at the face of Jesus: there we see the Father. Amen.

For children

Children, Philip said: show us God. Jesus said: whoever sees me sees the Father. When we look at Jesus, we see God!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, in you we see the Father. Make us faithful stewards of your mysteries, and through the prayers of St Philip, satisfy our hearts with the sight of your face. Amen.
