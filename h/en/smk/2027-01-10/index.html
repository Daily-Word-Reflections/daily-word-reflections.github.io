Word for the day

“Follow me, and I will make you fish for people.” Matthew 4:19

Reflection

After his Baptism Jesus begins his public ministry in Galilee. Matthew sees Isaiah fulfilled: “the people who sat in darkness have seen a great light.” Jesus' first preaching is short: “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near.” His first action is to call people: by the Sea of Galilee he sees Simon and Andrew, James and John at work, and says, “Follow me, and I will make you fish for people.” “Immediately they left their nets and followed him.”

The Old Testament readings show God calling people in the middle of ordinary work. Moses was keeping his father-in-law's sheep when he saw the bush that burned and was not consumed, and heard: “Remove the sandals from your feet, for the place on which you are standing is holy ground.” God reveals his name, “I AM WHO I AM”, and sends Moses to free his people. Jacob too was a shepherd, and God blessed the work of his hands.

St Paul says that this call did not begin by the lake. God “chose us in Christ before the foundation of the world to be holy and blameless before him in love” (Ephesians 1:4). And Acts shows what the call produces: a community that “devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers” (Acts 2:42).

Denho is the season of the manifestation of Christ. He is manifested not only at the Jordan, but every time someone leaves their nets and follows him. Each of us is called: to holiness, to a vocation, to bring others to Christ.

Homily outline

Theme: “Follow me.”

After his Baptism Jesus begins his ministry in Galilee. Light dawns on a land in darkness, and ordinary fishermen hear a call that changes their lives. The same call reaches us.

1. The light dawns in Galilee

  • Jesus begins not in Jerusalem but in “Galilee of the Gentiles”, a mixed and despised region.
  • Isaiah's promise is fulfilled: a light for the nations.
  • Christ still begins where people sit in darkness: in our struggles and our neighbourhoods.
“I will give you as a light to the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth.” Isaiah 49:6
“The people who sat in darkness have seen a great light, and for those who sat in the region and shadow of death light has dawned.” Matthew 4:16

Apply: Pray for one person you know who is in darkness: illness, addiction, despair.

2. The call: “Follow me”

  • God called Moses by name from the bush, and he answered: “Here I am.”
  • Jesus calls fishermen at their work: “Follow me, and I will make you fish for people.”
  • They leave their nets “immediately”: a call delayed is often a call lost.
“God called to him out of the bush, “Moses, Moses!” And he said, “Here I am.”” Exodus 3:4
“And he said to them, “Follow me, and I will make you fish for people.” Immediately they left their nets and followed him.” Matthew 4:19–20

Apply: Ask: what “net” holds me back from following Jesus more closely? Leave it this week.

3. A community born of the call

  • Those who answered became the first Church: teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread and prayer.
  • God heals the brokenhearted and gathers his people.
  • Every call to follow Christ is also a call into his Church.
“He heals the brokenhearted, and binds up their wounds.” Psalm 147:3
“They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers.” Acts 2:42

Apply: Join one parish activity (prayer group, catechism, charity) this month. Pray for vocations.

The Light has come, the call has been given, and the community is gathered.

Let us answer like Moses, “Here I am”, and like the apostles, leave our nets to follow him.

Take-home resolution: Pray daily this week for priestly and religious vocations from your parish.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, last Wednesday we celebrated Denho, the Baptism of the Lord. Now Jesus begins his work. He goes to Galilee, a region of ordinary working people, and begins to preach: “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near.” And the first thing he does is call people.

He walks by the lake and sees fishermen at work: Simon and Andrew casting their nets, James and John mending theirs. He says: “Follow me, and I will make you fish for people.” And immediately they left their nets and followed him. No delay, no long negotiation.

God always calls people at work. Moses was looking after sheep when he saw the burning bush and heard: “Remove your sandals, for the place where you stand is holy ground.” The ordinary place of his work became holy because God was there. Your kitchen, your office, your field can become holy ground too.

What are your nets? Your phone, your worries, your ambitions, your comforts? Jesus does not always ask us to leave our jobs, but he always asks us to put him first. And some of our young people he calls to leave everything for the priesthood or religious life. Let us pray for them, and let us answer him: “Lord, I will follow you.” Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus walked by a big lake and saw fishermen catching fish. He said: “Come, follow me! I will make you fishers of people!”

The fishermen left their nets straight away and went with Jesus. They became his friends, the apostles.

Jesus calls you too: to be his friend and bring others to him. Let's say: “Jesus, I will follow you!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus, light of Galilee, you called fishermen to follow you and made them fishers of people. Call us again today: give us courage to leave our nets, reverence before your holiness, and joy in your Church. Raise up many vocations from our families. Amen.
