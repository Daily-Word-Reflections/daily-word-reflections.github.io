Word for the day

“Is not this the carpenter’s son? Is not his mother called Mary?” Matthew 13:55

Reflection

On the first of May the whole country remembers its workers. Offices close, unions march, and the radio plays songs about the farmer and the mill hand. On the same morning our Church turns to a workman of Nazareth: the Panchangam keeps the memory of Mar Yauseph, Patron of Workers. Pope Pius XII placed the feast of St Joseph the Worker on 1 May in 1955, so that the day of labour would not belong only to slogans but also to a saint who worked with his hands for the Son of God. The Gospel lets us hear what the neighbours said about that family. When Jesus came home and taught in the synagogue, the people “were astounded and said, ‘Where did this man get this wisdom and these deeds of power? Is not this the carpenter’s son? Is not his mother called Mary?’” They meant it as a put-down. They knew the workshop, the tools and the plain house; how could wisdom come from there? But the Church hears the same words as a title of honour. The eternal Son chose to be known in his own town as the son of a carpenter. Most of his life on earth was spent not in preaching but in work, under the eye of a just man who taught him a trade. Mar Yauseph never speaks a word in the Gospels. His holiness was done with his hands: a door fitted true, a plough beam shaped well, a fair price, a day's labour offered to God.

St Paul writes to the Thessalonians as a fellow worker. Some in that church had stopped working, perhaps thinking the Lord's return was so close that daily labour no longer mattered. Paul reminds them how he lived among them: “we were not idle when we were with you, and we did not eat anyone’s bread without paying for it; but with toil and labor we worked night and day, so that we might not burden any of you.” He gives them a rule they could not forget: “Anyone unwilling to work should not eat.” And to those who were “living in idleness, mere busybodies, not doing any work,” he says: “do their work quietly and to earn their own living.” This is not harshness towards the poor or the sick or those who cannot find work; the same Paul organised a collection for the hungry church in Jerusalem. It is a word against idleness, which St Benedict called the enemy of the soul (Rule 48, in substance). Work is part of our dignity. In the beginning God put the man in the garden “to till it and keep it” (Genesis 2:15), before there was any sin. The Catechism says that work honours the Creator's gifts and that, borne in union with Jesus, the carpenter of Nazareth and the one crucified on Calvary, it can become a way of holiness (CCC 2427).

The Panchangam also remembers today Jeremiah the Prophet, whom our tradition calls Yeremiah Nbiya. His life matches the Gospel's last line: “Prophets are not without honor except in their own country and in their own house.” Jeremiah was a priest's son from Anathoth, near Jerusalem, called as a young man: “Before I formed you in the womb I knew you” (Jeremiah 1:5). He spoke for some forty years, and his own townspeople plotted against him; he was beaten, put in the stocks and lowered into a muddy cistern. He wanted to stop, but could not: “within me there is something like a burning fire shut up in my bones” (Jeremiah 20:9). God sent him to a potter's house and showed him the clay being reworked on the wheel: “Just like the clay in the potter’s hand, so are you in my hand, O house of Israel” (Jeremiah 18:6). A workman's bench became a prophet's pulpit. And it was Jeremiah who foretold the new covenant, God's law written on the heart (31:33), which we shall hear again at Suloko on Thursday. Joseph at his bench, Jeremiah at the potter's wheel, Jesus in the carpenter's house: on Labour Day the Church reminds us that ordinary work, done faithfully, is where God shapes us. Pray today for all workers, for the unemployed and for those who are underpaid or unsafe at work, and offer your own day's work at the Holy Qurbono or in your morning prayer.

Homily outline

Theme: “Is not this the carpenter’s son?”

On May Day, with Mar Yauseph, Patron of Workers, and Jeremiah the Prophet: the hidden workshop, the dignity of labour, and the clay in the potter's hand.

1. The carpenter's son

  • The Son of God in a workshop.
  • Holiness done with the hands.
  • A title of honour, not of shame.
“The Lord God took the man and put him in the garden of Eden to till it and keep it.” Genesis 2:15
“Whatever your task, put yourselves into it, as done for the Lord and not for your masters,” Colossians 3:23

Apply: Choose one ordinary task today and do it as carefully as if Jesus were working beside you.

2. Work quietly and earn your living

  • Paul the tentmaker.
  • Idleness, the enemy of the soul.
  • Work that has something to share.
“Go to the ant, you lazybones; consider its ways, and be wise.” Proverbs 6:6
“Thieves must give up stealing; rather let them labor and work honestly with their own hands, so as to have something to share with the needy.” Ephesians 4:28

Apply: Set aside a small part of this month's earnings or pocket money for someone in need.

3. The prophet and the potter

  • Known before the womb.
  • Rejected in his own town.
  • Clay on the wheel.
“Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations.” Jeremiah 1:5
“Truly I tell you, no prophet is accepted in the prophet’s hometown.” Luke 4:24

Apply: Where you are not appreciated at home or at work, keep doing what is right, and let God shape you there.

Mar Yauseph shows that the hidden work of the hands can serve the Son of God.

Jeremiah reminds us that God shapes his servants like clay, even through rejection.

Take-home resolution: Offer each day's work to God, work honestly, share with the needy, and pray for all workers.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today is May Day, Labour Day, and our Church remembers Mar Yauseph, Patron of Workers. In the Gospel the people of Nazareth say of Jesus: is not this the carpenter's son? (Matthew 13:55). They meant to belittle him. But think of it: the Son of God was known in his own town by the work of his foster father. For most of his life on earth he worked with his hands.

St Paul says to the Thessalonians: we were not idle when we were with you; we worked night and day so as not to burden anyone (2 Thessalonians 3:7–8). And he gave them a rule: anyone unwilling to work should not eat. Honest work is not a curse; God gave the first man a garden to till and keep. Work done well and offered to God becomes holy.

The Panchangam also remembers Jeremiah the Prophet. Like Jesus, he was not honoured in his own town. God sent him to a potter's house and showed him the clay on the wheel: so are you in my hand, O house of Israel (Jeremiah 18:6). God shapes us in our ordinary work, in our patience, in our honesty when no one is watching.

So today let us offer our work to God. Let us pray for workers who are underpaid, for those who have no job, for those far from home earning for their families. And let us ask Mar Yauseph to teach us to work quietly, faithfully and for the love of Jesus. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Today is Labour Day. Do you know what job St Joseph did? He was a carpenter! He made tables and doors, and Jesus helped him in the workshop. People in Nazareth said, “Isn't he the carpenter's son?” Jesus did ordinary work, just like your parents do. When you do your homework neatly, or help to sweep the house without grumbling, you can say, “Jesus, this is for you!” Then your work becomes a prayer.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, you were known in Nazareth as the carpenter's son and learned to work with the hands of Mar Yauseph. By his prayers bless the work of our hands and the labour of every worker; give work to those who seek it, justice to those who are underpaid, safety to those in danger, and rest to the weary. Keep us from idleness and from greed, and like your prophet Jeremiah let us be clay in the Father's hands, shaped by your word. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
