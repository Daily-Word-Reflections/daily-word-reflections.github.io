Word for the day

“I have been crucified with Christ; and it is no longer I who live, but it is Christ who lives in me.” Galatians 2:19–20

Reflection

In three verses St Paul tells us where the centre of his life has moved. “For through the law I died to the law, so that I might live to God. I have been crucified with Christ; and it is no longer I who live, but it is Christ who lives in me.” He does not say that his person has been wiped out; he says that the centre has changed, that someone else has taken the first place. Every human being has a centre, and the question of the whole spiritual life is who or what stands there. Then Paul says something that no theory could say: “And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.” Notice the small word me. The cross was not only for the world in general; it was for him, by name, and it is for each of us. Everything therefore is grace: “I do not nullify the grace of God.” We do not earn a place at the centre; Christ takes it, because he loves us.

In the Gospel Jesus says the same thing in the language of the field: “Very truly, I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.” Every farmer in Kerala knows what happens to the seed that is sown in the wet earth of the monsoon. For some days nothing shows; the seed seems to have rotted and disappeared; then one morning the first green shoots come up, and later the field is full. Jesus says this first about himself: the Cross is the grain that falls into the earth, and the Resurrection is the harvest. Then he says it about his disciples: “Those who love their life lose it, and those who hate their life in this world will keep it for eternal life. Whoever serves me must follow me, and where I am, there will my servant be also.” This is not contempt for life, which is God's gift; it is a refusal to clutch at it. The disciple goes where Jesus goes, to the Cross, and through the Cross to the Father, who honours those who serve his Son.

Today the Panchangam prints the name of Morth Alphonsa in bold. Anna Muttathupadathu was born on 19 August 1910 at Kudamaloor near Kottayam, in a Syro-Malabar Christian family. Her mother died when she was only a few months old, and she grew up among her mother's relatives, who loved her. From her girlhood she wanted nothing except to belong wholly to Christ, and, according to the accounts of her life, when a marriage was being arranged for her she injured her foot so that the plan would be dropped; the Church does not hold this up as an example to copy, but it shows how single-hearted her love was. In 1927 she entered the Franciscan Clarist convent at Bharananganam and took the name Alphonsa of the Immaculate Conception. Then came long years of illness, which she spent in prayer and patience; the sisters who nursed her remembered that she never complained, and that she smiled. She made her perpetual vows in 1936 and died on 28 July 1946, aged thirty-five. Pope John Paul II beatified her at Kottayam on 8 February 1986, and Pope Benedict XVI canonised her on 12 October 2008, the first woman of Indian origin to be named a saint. Her tomb at Bharananganam draws thousands of pilgrims a year, many from other Churches and other faiths, and in these rainy days of Karkidakam they come to keep her feast.

Alphonsa did not go looking for suffering; it came to her, and she handed it to the Lord. That is the difference between the grain of wheat and waste: the grain falls into the earth for love. Lying on her sickbed she lived what Paul describes: Christ was living in her, and the life she lived she lived by faith in the Son of God who loved her and gave himself for her. The Church teaches that Christ, by his passion and death, has given a new meaning to suffering, so that it can unite us with his redemptive Passion (Catechism 1505). Her hidden life, which looked like a long waiting, has borne a harvest that she could not have imagined, and it speaks to all who are sick, to all who care for them, and to everyone whose plans have been broken. We honour her not by admiring her pain but by learning her trust, and we ask her, as one of the saints who have finished the journey, to pray for us (Catechism 956).

Homily outline

Theme: “It is no longer I who live, but it is Christ who lives in me.”

On Wednesday of the tenth week after Pentecost, with Morth Alphonsa: the new centre of life, the grain of wheat, the Clarist sister of Bharananganam, and the pain that is given in love.

1. Christ lives in me

  • Crucified with Christ.
  • The Son of God who loved me.
  • Everything is grace.
“I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.” Jeremiah 31:33
“For to me, living is Christ and dying is gain.” Philippians 1:21

Apply: Start each day this week with the prayer: Lord Jesus, live in me today.

2. The grain of wheat

  • The seed that seems to rot in the wet earth.
  • The Cross as seed, the Resurrection as harvest.
  • The disciple goes where Jesus goes.
“May those who sow in tears reap with shouts of joy.” Psalm 126:5
“Fool! What you sow does not come to life unless it dies.” 1 Corinthians 15:36

Apply: Name one thing you clutch at and let it fall into the earth for love of Christ.

3. Morth Alphonsa of Bharananganam

  • From Kudamaloor to the Clarist convent.
  • Years of illness in prayer and patience.
  • Canonised on 12 October 2008, the first woman of Indian origin to be a saint.
“But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and no torment will ever touch them.” Wisdom 3:1
“For those who want to save their life will lose it, and those who lose their life for my sake will find it.” Matthew 16:25

Apply: Visit or ring one sick person this week and give them an hour.

  • Christ has given suffering a new meaning.

  • The saints pray for us.

“always carrying in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be made visible in our bodies.” 2 Corinthians 4:10

Apply: Offer one small pain today for a person you name.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul says something astonishing: I have been crucified with Christ; and it is no longer I who live, but it is Christ who lives in me (Galatians 2:20). He has not lost himself; he has found the true centre. The life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me. Notice the word me: Christ died for each of us by name.

In the Gospel Jesus says that unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit (John 12:24). In these monsoon days our farmers know this: the seed disappears into the mud, and then the green shoots come. Jesus is that seed, and he calls us to follow him: whoever serves me must follow me, and where I am, there will my servant be also.

Today the Panchangam remembers Morth Alphonsa. She was born in 1910 at Kudamaloor, lost her mother as a baby, entered the convent at Bharananganam, and spent long years in illness, in prayer and in patience, dying at thirty-five in 1946. Pope Benedict XVI canonised her in 2008. She gave her pain to the Lord with a smile, and her hidden life has borne a great harvest.

So let us learn from her to give our small pains and our whole lives to the Lord, to visit and serve the sick, and to let Christ live in us. Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever planted a seed? You put it into the soil and cover it, and for some days you see nothing. But then a tiny green shoot comes up! Jesus says that he is like that seed. He died and rose again and now gives life to everyone. St Alphonsa was a sister who was often sick, but she gave all her pain to Jesus and kept smiling, and now many people pray at her tomb. When something hurts you this week, say, “Jesus, this is for you,” and give a smile.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, grain of wheat fallen into the earth, you died and rose to bear fruit for the whole world. Through the prayers of Morth Alphonsa, who gave you her pain and her whole life, make us live no longer for ourselves but for you who loved us and gave yourself for us. Comfort all who are sick, strengthen those who care for them, and bring our hidden sacrifices to a harvest of joy. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
