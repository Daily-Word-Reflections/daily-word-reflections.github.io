Word for the day

“so that you may know and understand that the Father is in me and I am in the Father.” John 10:38

Reflection

Today is the First Friday of the month. Every Friday recalls the Cross, and many Catholic parishes give the first Friday of each month to special prayer, the Holy Qurbono and devotion to the Heart of Jesus, the heart that loved us to the end. Today's Epistle shows what such a heart looks like in those who follow him. St Paul writes to Corinth about the churches of Macedonia, the communities of Philippi, Thessalonica and Berea, the first that he founded on European soil. They were small, poor, and already suffering. And Paul says of them: “for during a severe ordeal of affliction, their abundant joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity on their part.” Joy and poverty side by side, and out of them a flood of giving. They “voluntarily gave according to their means, and even beyond their means.” Usually it is the collector who has to beg. Here the givers do the begging: they were “begging us earnestly for the privilege of sharing in this ministry to the saints.”

Something more precious than money is named in the next verse: “they gave themselves first to the Lord and, by the will of God, to us.” The gift of self comes first and the gift of money follows from it. A parish that has given itself to the Lord is never short of gifts; a parish that gives only money, without itself, soon finds that even the money is hard to find. Paul does not command. He appeals to their pride in what is best in them: “Now as you excel in everything, in faith, in speech, in knowledge, in utmost eagerness, and in our love for you, so we want you to excel also in this generous undertaking.” A few verses later he gives the reason that stands behind all Christian giving: “For you know the generous act of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty you might become rich” (2 Corinthians 8:9). Jesus gave himself first. The First Friday keeps this before our eyes: the Heart that was pierced on a Friday gave everything.

The Gospel carries us to the Temple at Jerusalem, in winter, at the Feast of the Dedication (John 10:22). Jesus has said, “The Father and I are one,” and some of his hearers pick up stones. When John writes “the Jews” in such a scene he means those in Jerusalem who opposed Jesus on that occasion; we must remember that Jesus himself, his Mother, the Apostles and the first Christians were all Jews, and the Church teaches that what happened in the Passion cannot be charged against all the Jews of that time, nor against the Jews of today (Nostra Aetate 4; Catechism 597). Jesus answers with calm: “I have shown you many good works from the Father. For which of these are you going to stone me?” The works are his credentials: the sick healed, the hungry fed, the sinner forgiven. Then he argues from their own Scripture: “Is it not written in your law, ‘I said, you are gods’?” If the Scripture could call those to whom the word of God came “gods”, can anyone say that “the one whom the Father has sanctified and sent into the world is blaspheming because I said, ‘I am God's Son’?” The Fathers of the Church, especially in the East, drew from this the great truth that the Son of God became human so that we might share in his own life: “the Son of God became man so that we might become God,” wrote St Athanasius (On the Incarnation 54, in substance; Catechism 460).

Jesus ends with an invitation: “If I am not doing the works of my Father, then do not believe me. But if I do them, even though you do not believe me, believe the works, so that you may know and understand that the Father is in me and I am in the Father.” Believe the works. The same test is put to us. The Macedonians' joy in their poverty was such a work: a sign that Christ was alive in them. Few people in our days are argued into faith; many are drawn when they see a neighbour who gives more than he can afford and ask where that comes from. John says that they tried to arrest Jesus again “but he escaped from their hands.” His hour had not yet come. Later in the same discourse he says, “No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord” (John 10:18), and on a Friday he did. Today we ask for a heart like his, and for works that point to him.

Theme: “so that you may know and understand that the Father is in me and I am in the Father.”

On the First Friday, Friday of the sixth week after Pentecost: the joy of Macedonia, the gift of self, and the works that point to the Father.

  • Affliction, poverty and abundant joy.
  • Giving according to their means and beyond.
  • Begging for the privilege of giving.

Apply: Choose one gift this week that costs you something and give it with joy.

  • The gift of self before the gift of money.
  • Christ became poor for our sake.
  • The pierced Heart of the First Friday.
“You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your might.” Deuteronomy 6:5
“For you know the generous act of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty you might become rich.” 2 Corinthians 8:9

Apply: Before the next gift you give, offer yourself to the Lord in a short prayer.

  • “I have shown you many good works from the Father.”
  • The law that calls God's people “gods”.

“I say, “You are gods, children of the Most High, all of you;” Psalm 82:6
“Believe me that I am in the Father and the Father is in me; but if you do not, then believe me because of the works themselves.” John 14:11

Apply: Do one quiet work of love today so that someone may see the Father in it.

Our works are the way many people first meet the Father who is in Christ.

Take-home resolution: Give yourself first, give with joy, and let your works lead others to the Father.

Full homily

Dear friends, today is the First Friday, and we look to the Heart of Jesus, the Heart that gave everything. St Paul tells the Corinthians about the churches of Macedonia. They were poor and in affliction, and yet their abundant joy and their extreme poverty overflowed in a wealth of generosity (2 Corinthians 8:2). They gave according to their means, and even beyond their means, and they begged for the privilege of giving (2 Corinthians 8:3–4).

What was best of all is that they gave themselves first to the Lord (2 Corinthians 8:5). That is where all true giving begins. And Paul reminds us why: Jesus Christ, though he was rich, became poor for our sake, so that by his poverty we might become rich (2 Corinthians 8:9). He gave himself first.

In the Gospel Jesus is threatened with stones, and he answers calmly: I have shown you many good works from the Father; for which of these are you going to stone me? (John 10:32). He asks that people believe the works, so that they may know and understand that the Father is in him and he is in the Father (John 10:38). We too are to be known by our works. Many who do not read the Scriptures read our lives.

So today let us offer ourselves to the Lord first, and then let us give, with joy, even beyond our means, something that costs us. It will be a work that points to the Father. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know that today is the First Friday? We remember that Jesus loves us so much that he gave everything for us, even his life on the Cross. St Paul tells about some people who were very poor, but so happy in Jesus that they shared what little they had. And best of all, they gave themselves to the Lord. Today, do one kind thing without telling anyone: give a toy, help at home, or say a prayer for someone who is sad. That is how we show the Heart of Jesus to others.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, though you were rich, for our sake you became poor, so that by your poverty we might become rich. On this First Friday we look to your pierced Heart, which gave everything. Teach us to give ourselves first to you, and then whatever we have for the sake of your saints and your poor. Give us the joy of the churches of Macedonia, who found in their poverty a wealth of generosity. Let our works be so full of your love that those who see them may believe that you are in the Father and the Father is in you. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
