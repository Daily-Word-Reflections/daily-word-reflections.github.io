Word for the day

“Remember your leaders, those who spoke the word of God to you; consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith.” Hebrews 13:7

Reflection

Before the Great Fast the Malankara Church keeps two Sundays of remembrance. The first is Kohne Sunday, the commemoration of the departed priests (kohne in Syriac). We remember the bishops and priests who baptised us, taught us, absolved our sins, celebrated the Holy Qurbono for us, and buried our dead. Hebrews tells us: “Remember your leaders, those who spoke the word of God to you; consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith.”

The readings recall the death of Israel's great servants. Aaron, the first high priest, dies on Mount Hor, and Moses places his vestments on his son Eleazar (Numbers 20:28): the priesthood continues though the priest dies. Joshua dies after leading the people into the land. Sirach remembers Aaron anointed “for an everlasting covenant… to minister to the Lord and serve as priest and bless his people in his name” (Sirach 45:15).

In Acts Paul says farewell to the elders of Ephesus: “Keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock, of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God that he obtained with the blood of his own Son.” And he reminds them of the Lord's words: “It is more blessed to give than to receive.” The Gospel asks the question every priest will be asked: “Who then is the faithful and wise servant, whom his master has put in charge of his household, to give them their food at the proper time? Blessed is that servant whom his master will find at work when he arrives.”

We pray today for the departed priests, that the Lord who called them may forgive their weaknesses and say to them: “Well done, good servant!” (Luke 19:17). And we pray for our living priests, and for new vocations. “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever” (Hebrews 13:8): priests pass, but the one High Priest remains.

Homily outline

Theme: “Remembering the priests who served us.”

On Kohne Sunday the Church remembers her departed priests. We thank God for them, we pray for them, and we reflect on the gift of the priesthood that continues among us.

1. A priesthood handed on

  • On Mount Hor Moses took Aaron's vestments and put them on Eleazar: the ministry continues after death.
  • Every priest is taken from among the people and appointed for the people, to offer gifts and sacrifices.
  • The priests who baptised us, absolved us and fed us with the Eucharist have handed on Christ's priesthood to the next generation.
“Moses stripped Aaron of his vestments, and put them on his son Eleazar; and Aaron died there on the top of the mountain.” Numbers 20:28
“Every high priest chosen from among mortals is put in charge of things pertaining to God on their behalf, to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins.” Hebrews 5:1

Apply: Remember by name the priests who baptised you, gave you First Communion, married you or buried your loved ones.

2. Faithful stewards of the Master's gift

  • Aaron was anointed to minister to the Lord and bless the people in his name.
  • In the parable of the pounds, the faithful servant is praised for trading with what he received.
  • Priests are judged by faithfulness, not success; so are all of us in our vocations.
“Moses ordained him, and anointed him with holy oil; it was an everlasting covenant for him and for his descendants … to minister to the Lord and serve as priest and bless his people in his name.” Sirach 45:15
“He said to him, “Well done, good slave! Because you have been trustworthy in a very small thing, take charge of ten cities.”” Luke 19:17

Apply: Encourage a priest this week with a word of thanks, and pray for one who is struggling.

3. Praying for the departed

  • Judas Maccabeus offered sacrifice for the dead, “a holy and pious thought”.
  • The Church prays for the faithful departed, that they may be purified and enter God's rest.
  • Our prayers and Holy Qurbono offered for departed priests are an act of gratitude and charity.
“But if he was looking to the splendid reward that is laid up for those who fall asleep in godliness, it was a holy and pious thought. Therefore he made atonement for the dead, so that they might be delivered from their sin.” 2 Maccabees 12:45
“Blessed are the dead who from now on die in the Lord. “Yes,” says the Spirit, “they will rest from their labors, for their deeds follow them.”” Revelation 14:13

Apply: Have a Holy Qurbono offered, or pray a Rosary, for a departed priest.

The priests we remember today gave their lives so that Christ might be present among us.

Let us thank God for them, pray for their eternal rest, and pray that the Lord will send good and holy priests to his Church.

Take-home resolution: Visit the grave of a priest in your parish cemetery and pray for him.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today is Kohne Sunday, when our Church remembers the priests who have died. Think for a moment of the priests in your own life: the one who baptised you, who gave you your First Holy Communion, who heard your confessions, who blessed your marriage, who buried your parents. Many of them are now with the Lord.

The letter to the Hebrews says: “Remember your leaders, those who spoke the word of God to you; consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith.” We remember them with gratitude. They were human, with weaknesses like ours, but through their hands Christ gave us his Body and Blood and his forgiveness.

Our reading from Numbers tells how Aaron, the first high priest, died, and Moses placed his vestments on his son Eleazar. The priest dies; the priesthood continues. For Christ is the one eternal High Priest, “the same yesterday and today and forever”. And the Gospel asks every priest one question: were you a faithful and wise servant, giving God's household their food at the proper time?

Today let us offer this Qurbono for our departed priests, that the Lord may say to them: “Well done, good servant.” Let us thank and support our living priests. And let us pray that from our families God may call new priests, to stand at this altar when we are gone. Amen.

For children

Children, who baptised you? A priest! Who gives you Jesus in Holy Communion? A priest!

Today we remember priests who have died and gone to Jesus. We thank God for them and pray that they are happy in heaven.

Let's pray for our parish priest too. Maybe one day God will call one of you to be a priest or sister! Let's say: “Thank you, Jesus, for our priests!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, eternal High Priest, we remember before you the bishops and priests who served your people and have gone to their rest. Forgive their weaknesses, reward their labours, and give them a place of light. Keep our living priests faithful, and raise up new labourers for your harvest. Amen.
