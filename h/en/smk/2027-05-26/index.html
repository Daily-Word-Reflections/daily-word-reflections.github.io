Word for the day

“Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life.” John 6:68

Reflection

The Gospel today is only three verses long, but it comes at the hinge of St John's sixth chapter. On Sunday we heard Jesus speak of the true bread from heaven. In the verses that follow he says plainly that the bread is his flesh, given for the life of the world, and that whoever eats his flesh and drinks his blood has eternal life. Many disciples find this too hard and walk away. Then Jesus turns to the Twelve: “Do you also wish to go away?” It is one of the loneliest questions in the Gospel, and Jesus does not soften what he has said in order to keep them. Simon Peter answers for them all: “Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life. We have come to believe and know that you are the Holy One of God.” Peter does not claim to understand everything. He has simply looked at every other road and found that none of them leads to life. St John Chrysostom notes that Peter's words are not those of a man with nowhere else to go but of a man who loves: who else, he means, will teach us such things (Homilies on John 47, in substance). St Augustine observed that Peter says “we have come to believe and know” and not the other way round: we believe in order to understand (Tractates on John 27, in substance). That is the faith we bring to the Holy Qurbono every Sunday. We do not see how bread becomes his Body; we believe his word, and the believing opens our eyes.

St Paul gives thanks for the same kind of faith in the Thessalonians: “We also constantly give thanks to God for this, that when you received the word of God that you heard from us, you accepted it not as a human word but as what it really is, God's word, which is also at work in you believers.” The word of God is not only read; it works. It changes those who receive it. The Catechism takes up this very verse to say that the Church welcomes Sacred Scripture not as a human word but as the word of God, and finds in it her nourishment and her strength (CCC 104). The verses that follow speak with pain of opponents in Judea who had resisted the apostles. Paul, himself a son of Israel who loved his people deeply (Romans 9:1-5), is describing particular adversaries of his own day. The Church teaches firmly that what happened in the Passion of Christ cannot be charged against all the Jews then living, nor against the Jews of today, and that the Jewish people must never be presented as rejected or accursed by God (Nostra Aetate 4; CCC 597). We read such passages with that teaching, and with humility, because every one of us has also resisted the word at times.

Today the Panchangam remembers a saint in whom that word worked with joy: St Philip Neri. Born in Florence in 1515, he came to Rome as a young man and lived for years as a layman, teaching, visiting the sick in the hospitals, and praying at night in the catacombs. The tradition of his life records that on the eve of Pentecost 1544, while he prayed in the catacomb of San Sebastiano, he felt the fire of the Holy Spirit enter his heart, and from then on his heart burned with a love that people could almost feel. He founded a confraternity to care for poor pilgrims, was ordained a priest in 1551, and began to gather people in a simple room for prayer, the reading of Scripture, the lives of the saints and music. From those meetings came the Congregation of the Oratory, approved by Pope Gregory XIII in 1575. He heard confessions for hours each day, laughed easily, played jokes on himself to keep from pride, and was loved by cardinals and street boys alike. People called him the Apostle of Rome. He died in the early hours of 26 May 1595, after celebrating Corpus Christi, and was canonised in 1622. This week, in the season of Pentecost, he reminds us that the Holy Spirit does not make Christians gloomy. Where the Word is welcomed and the Eucharist is loved, joy follows.

Homily outline

Theme: “Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life.”

On Wednesday of the first week after Pentecost, with St Philip Neri: the Twelve who stayed, the word that works in believers, and the joy of the Spirit.

1. Not a human word but God's word

  • Received, not merely heard.
  • A word at work in believers.
  • Read with the Church's teaching and with humility.
“so shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty” Isaiah 55:11
“Indeed, the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword” Hebrews 4:12

Apply: Read the Gospel of the coming Sunday at home before you hear it in church.

2. Lord, to whom can we go?

  • Many left; the Twelve stayed.
  • Believing in order to understand.
  • The bread of life on the altar.
“You show me the path of life. In your presence there is fullness of joy; in your right hand are pleasures forevermore.” Psalm 16:11
“I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will never be hungry, and whoever believes in me will never be thirsty.” John 6:35

Apply: Spend ten minutes before the Blessed Sacrament this week and repeat Peter's words slowly.

3. St Philip Neri: the joy of the Spirit

  • A heart set on fire at Pentecost.
  • Hours in the confessional, a room of prayer and music.
  • Humble, cheerful, loved by all.
“for the joy of the Lord is your strength.” Nehemiah 8:10
“Rejoice in the Lord always; again I will say, Rejoice.” Philippians 4:4

Apply: Bring cheer to one person who is sad or lonely this week, and go to confession if you have been putting it off.

Peter stayed because no other road leads to life; St Philip Neri stayed and found that the road was full of joy.

The word of God and the bread of life are given at one table, and both work in those who receive them.

Take-home resolution: When it is hard, stay with Jesus, and let the joy of the Spirit be seen in you.

Full homily

Dear friends, in the Gospel today many disciples have left Jesus because his teaching about the bread of life seemed too hard. Jesus does not run after them and change his words. He turns to the Twelve and asks: do you also wish to go away? (John 6:67).

Peter answers: Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life. We have come to believe and know that you are the Holy One of God (John 6:68–69). Peter does not understand everything. But he knows that no other road leads to life. That is our faith when we come to the Holy Qurbono.

St Paul thanks God that the Thessalonians received the gospel not as a human word but as what it really is, God's word, which is also at work in you believers (1 Thessalonians 2:13). The word of God works. It changed the Thessalonians, and it changed a young man in Rome named Philip Neri, whom we remember today. On the eve of Pentecost 1544, his life tells us, the fire of the Spirit filled his heart. He became a priest who heard confessions for hours, who loved the Eucharist, and who made a whole city smile.

So when following Jesus is hard, let us not walk away. Let us say with Peter: to whom can we go? And let us ask St Philip Neri to pray for us, that our faith may be joyful, humble and full of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

For children

Children, once Jesus said something that was hard to understand, and many people went away. Jesus asked his friends: are you going too? Peter said: no, Lord, we will stay with you! Today we also remember St Philip Neri. He was a priest who loved Jesus so much that he was always happy. He laughed and played with children and told them: be good, if you can! When you love Jesus, you can be joyful too.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Holy One of God, you have the words of eternal life, and you give us your flesh as the bread of life. When others turn away, and when we ourselves are tempted to leave, keep us with you. Let us receive your word not as a human word but as the word of God, at work in us. Through the prayers of St Philip Neri, whose heart you set on fire with your Spirit, fill us with joy in your service, make us humble and cheerful, and draw many to you through our love for the Holy Qurbono. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
