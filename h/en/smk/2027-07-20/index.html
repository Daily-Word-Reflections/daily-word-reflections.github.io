Word for the day

“Elijah is indeed coming first to restore all things.” Mark 9:12

Reflection

Peter, James and John are coming down the mountain, and their heads are still full of what they have seen: the Lord shining like light, and with him Moses and Elijah, “who were talking with Jesus.” Jesus tells them to say nothing “until after the Son of Man had risen from the dead,” and they keep the matter to themselves, “questioning what this rising from the dead could mean.” Then comes the question that has been burning in them: “Why do the scribes say that Elijah must come first?” The scribes were reading the prophet Malachi: “Lo, I will send you the prophet Elijah before the great and terrible day of the Lord comes. He will turn the hearts of parents to their children and the hearts of children to their parents.” They had just seen Elijah on the mountain. Why was he gone again? Jesus answers in two steps. First: “Elijah is indeed coming first to restore all things.” The promise stands. Then: “But I tell you that Elijah has come, and they did to him whatever they pleased, as it is written about him.” Elijah has come in the person of John the Baptist, who, as the angel told Zechariah, would go before the Lord “with the spirit and power of Elijah.” And the way of the forerunner prepares the way of the Lord: they did to John whatever they pleased, and so the Son of Man “is to go through many sufferings and be treated with contempt.”

Who was Elijah, and why does the Church keep his memory in the season of Pentecost? He appears suddenly in the First Book of Kings, “the Tishbite, of Tishbe in Gilead,” announcing a drought in the days of King Ahab. He is fed first by ravens, then by a poor widow of Zarephath, whose jar of meal “was not emptied, neither did the jug of oil fail, according to the word of the Lord.” On Mount Carmel he stands alone against the prophets of Baal and asks the people the question that he asks still: “How long will you go limping with two different opinions? If the Lord is God, follow him; but if Baal, then follow him.” Fire falls and the people fall on their faces. Then Elijah climbs to the top of Carmel, bows with his face between his knees, and sends his servant to look at the sea, seven times. At the seventh time the servant says: “Look, a little cloud no bigger than a person's hand is rising out of the sea.” In July in Kerala, when the whole land waits for the rain and listens to it on the tiles, we know exactly what that cloud is. St James adds a word for ordinary believers: “Elijah was a human being like us, and he prayed fervently that it might not rain, and for three years and six months it did not rain on the earth. Then he prayed again, and the heaven gave rain and the earth yielded its harvest.”

The same prophet then sits exhausted under a broom tree and begs to die: “It is enough; now, O Lord, take away my life, for I am no better than my ancestors.” The great prophet is a person like us. And God does not answer with a speech. An angel touches him and says: “Get up and eat.” A second time: “Get up and eat, otherwise the journey will be too much for you.” “He got up, and ate and drank; then he went in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights to Horeb the mount of God.” There, God is not in the wind, nor the earthquake, nor the fire, but in “a sound of sheer silence.” The Fathers saw in the cake and the jar of water a sign of the bread that sustains the pilgrim, and the Church gives the Holy Qurbono to the dying as food for the last journey. The Second Book of Kings tells that Elijah went up in a whirlwind, with a chariot of fire, and on the mountain of the Transfiguration he appears again, to listen to the Son.

Today's Epistle gives the key. Paul tells the Colossians not to let anyone judge them in matters of food and drink, festivals, new moons or sabbaths: “These are only a shadow of what is to come, but the substance belongs to Christ.” Paul is not despising the feasts of Israel, which the same Paul calls gifts that belong to Israel (Romans 9:4); he is telling Gentile believers not to let any human group make them anxious about what Christ has already fulfilled. The prophets, too, are a shadow, a light thrown ahead by the one who is coming. The voice at the Transfiguration says of Jesus: “Listen to him!” and then, “they saw no one with them any more, but only Jesus.” We honour Elijah, we pray with him, and we learn courage from him; but we follow the one to whom Moses and Elijah pointed, “the head, from whom the whole body, nourished and held together by its ligaments and sinews, grows with a growth that is from God.” Let us ask the Lord to give us the courage of Elijah to choose, the humility of Elijah to rest and to eat what God provides, and the stillness of Elijah to hear a sound of sheer silence.

Homily outline

Theme: “they saw no one with them any more, but only Jesus.”

On Tuesday of the ninth week after Pentecost, the memory of Elijah the Prophet: Elijah has come, the prayer for rain, the bread for the journey, and the shadow and the substance.

1. Elijah must come first

  • Malachi's promise.
  • Elijah has come in John the Baptist.
  • The forerunner shares the Lord's fate.
“Lo, I will send you the prophet Elijah before the great and terrible day of the Lord comes.” Malachi 4:5
“With the spirit and power of Elijah he will go before him, to turn the hearts of parents to their children,” Luke 1:17

Apply: Ask the Lord to turn the hearts in your own family towards one another.

2. Prayer and the little cloud

  • Alone on Mount Carmel.
  • How long will you go limping?
  • A human being like us.
“How long will you go limping with two different opinions? If the Lord is God, follow him; but if Baal, then follow him.” 1 Kings 18:21
“Elijah was a human being like us, and he prayed fervently that it might not rain, and for three years and six months it did not rain on the earth.” James 5:17

Apply: Pray persistently this week for one good thing you are waiting for.

3. Get up and eat

  • Despair under the broom tree.
  • The angel and the bread.
  • A sound of sheer silence.
“Get up and eat, otherwise the journey will be too much for you.” 1 Kings 19:7
“I am the living bread that came down from heaven. Whoever eats of this bread will live forever; and the bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh.” John 6:51

Apply: When you are weary, rest and eat, and receive the Holy Qurbono as food for the way.

4. Shadow and substance

  • The prophets point ahead.
  • The substance belongs to Christ.
  • Listen to him.
“The Lord your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your own people; you shall heed such a prophet.” Deuteronomy 18:15
“Suddenly when they looked around, they saw no one with them any more, but only Jesus.” Mark 9:8

Apply: Put down one thing you cling to that is only a shadow, and hold fast to Christ.

Elijah teaches us to choose, to pray with persistence and to eat the bread God gives.

All the prophets point ahead; the substance is Christ, the head of the body.

Take-home resolution: Choose the Lord, pray without giving up, eat the bread for the way, and follow Jesus alone.

Full homily

Dear friends, in today's Gospel the disciples are coming down the mountain after seeing Jesus shining, with Moses and Elijah beside him. They ask: Why do the scribes say that Elijah must come first? Jesus answers: Elijah is indeed coming first to restore all things. But I tell you that Elijah has come, and they did to him whatever they pleased (Mark 9:12–13). He means John the Baptist, who came in the spirit and power of Elijah (Luke 1:17).

Who was Elijah? A prophet who stood alone for the living God. On Mount Carmel he asked the people: How long will you go limping with two different opinions? If the Lord is God, follow him (1 Kings 18:21). He prayed fervently, and after a long drought he saw a little cloud no bigger than a person's hand rising out of the sea (1 Kings 18:44). In these monsoon days we know what that cloud means. St James says that Elijah was a human being like us (James 5:17). He also became tired and sad, and God sent him bread and water with the words: Get up and eat, otherwise the journey will be too much for you (1 Kings 19:7).

St Paul tells the Colossians that festivals, new moons and sabbaths are only a shadow of what is to come, but the substance belongs to Christ (Colossians 2:17). The prophets, too, are lights thrown ahead by the one who is coming. When the disciples looked up on the mountain, they saw no one with them any more, but only Jesus (Mark 9:8). We honour Elijah, but we follow Jesus, the head of the body.

Let us ask the Lord for Elijah's courage to choose, his persistence in prayer and his humility to eat the bread God gives. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what Elijah did when the land was dry and there was no rain? He prayed and prayed, and he sent his servant to look at the sea. Seven times! At last the servant saw a tiny cloud, as small as a hand. And soon there was a big rain, just as in our monsoon! Elijah teaches us to keep praying and not to give up. And when Elijah was tired, an angel gave him bread and said, Get up and eat. God gives us strength for our journey, too.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you spoke with Moses and Elijah on the holy mountain, and the Father said of you, Listen to him. We thank you for the prophet Elijah, who stood alone for the living God, who prayed that the rain might come, and whom you strengthened with bread on the way. Give us the courage to choose you without dividing our hearts, the perseverance to pray when the heavens seem closed, and the humility to rise and eat when we are weary. Let us not be led by shadows, but hold fast to you, the head from whom the whole body grows with a growth that is from God. Through the prayers of the prophet Elijah, bless the farmers and the fields in this season of rain. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
