Word for the day

“Do not be afraid, little flock, for it is your Father’s good pleasure to give you the kingdom.” Luke 12:32

Reflection

As the guests leave Mamre, Abraham walks with them “to set them on their way,” and the Lord speaks almost to himself: “Shall I hide from Abraham what I am about to do?” God treats Abraham as a friend, someone who may know his plans. And he explains why he chose him: “that he may charge his children and his household after him to keep the way of the Lord by doing righteousness and justice.” Faith is meant to be handed on at home, from parents to children, in words and in the way a household lives. Then God speaks of “the outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah.” The cry of those who suffer injustice rises to heaven, and God says: “I must go down and see.” The God of Abraham is not indifferent to the cry of the oppressed.

Paul reminds Titus how a Christian household should look to its neighbours: “to speak evil of no one, to avoid quarreling, to be gentle, and to show every courtesy to everyone.” He does not pretend we were always so: “we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, led astray… But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved us, not because of any works of righteousness that we had done, but according to his mercy, through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.” In the Gospel Jesus speaks tenderly to that small, renewed household: “Do not be afraid, little flock, for it is your Father’s good pleasure to give you the kingdom. Sell your possessions, and give alms… For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” St Augustine urged his people to move their treasure to heaven through the hands of the poor, so that their hearts would follow (in substance). Almsgiving in the Fast is not only charity; it is moving our heart to where God is.

Theme: “Do not be afraid, little flock.”

Full homily

Dear friends, the Lord said: shall I hide from Abraham what I am about to do? I have chosen him, that he may charge his children and his household after him to keep the way of the Lord by doing righteousness and justice (Genesis 18:17, 19). God trusts Abraham as a friend, and expects faith to be passed on at home.

God also hears the outcry against Sodom: I must go down and see (Genesis 18:20–21). The cry of the oppressed reaches heaven.

Paul says we were once foolish and led astray, but when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved us, according to his mercy, through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit (Titus 3:3–5). So we are to be gentle and courteous to everyone.

And Jesus says: do not be afraid, little flock. Sell your possessions, and give alms. For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also (Luke 12:32–34). As we end this second week of the Fast, let us move our treasure, and our heart, toward God. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus calls us his little flock, like little sheep. He says: do not be afraid! God wants to give you his kingdom. When we share our things with poor people, we are storing treasure in heaven. What can you share this week?

Prayer

God our Savior, you did not hide your plans from Abraham, and you chose him to teach his household your way. Your goodness and loving kindness appeared in Christ, and you renewed us through the water of rebirth. Make our homes schools of righteousness and justice, free us from fear, and draw our hearts to the treasure of your kingdom. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
