Monday of the first week of the Great Fast · Shubkono

8 February 2027 · Syro-Malankara Rite

  • Epistle Ephesians 4:25–32 · Forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you
  • Gospel Matthew 4:1–11 · Forty days in the wilderness

Word for the day

“and be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you.” Ephesians 4:32

Reflection

Today the fast begins, and our Church opens it not with food rules but with forgiveness. In Shubkono, the service of reconciliation, the priest bows before the people and asks their pardon, and the faithful ask pardon of one another before they begin the forty days together. Paul gives the words for it: “Be angry but do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger, and do not make room for the devil… Put away from you all bitterness and wrath and anger and wrangling and slander, together with all malice, and be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you.” A fast kept with an unforgiving heart is only hunger.

The first reading of the Fast is about Abram's faith. Childless and old, he hears: “Do not be afraid, Abram, I am your shield; your reward shall be very great.” God brings him outside: “Look toward heaven and count the stars… So shall your descendants be.” And “he believed the Lord; and the Lord reckoned it to him as righteousness.” The Gospel shows Jesus in the wilderness, fasting forty days and forty nights and answering the tempter three times from the Scriptures: “One does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God.” This is the shape of our Fast: trust like Abram, peace with one another, and the word of God as our food. St John Chrysostom asks: do you fast? Then show it by your works; let not only the mouth fast, but also the eye, the ear, the hands and the feet (in substance).

Homily outline

Theme: “Forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you.”

The Great Fast opens with faith, forgiveness and the word of God.

1. Count the stars

  • Do not be afraid.
  • A promise beyond sight.
  • Faith reckoned as righteousness.
“After these things the word of the Lord came to Abram in a vision, “Do not be afraid, Abram, I am your shield; your reward shall be very great.”” Genesis 15:1
“For what does the scripture say? “Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness.”” Romans 4:3

Apply: Bring one fear to God in prayer each day of this first week.

2. Forgiving one another

  • Shubkono: the priest asks pardon.
  • Not letting the sun go down on anger.
  • Kind and tenderhearted.
“You shall not take vengeance or bear a grudge against any of your people, but you shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am the Lord.” Leviticus 19:18
“Be angry but do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger,” Ephesians 4:26

Apply: Make peace with one person before sunset today.

3. Not by bread alone

  • Forty days and forty nights.
  • Three answers from Scripture.
  • Angels came and waited on him.
“He humbled you by letting you hunger, then by feeding you with manna, with which neither you nor your ancestors were acquainted, in order to make you understand that one does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of the Lord.” Deuteronomy 8:3
“But he answered, “It is written, ‘One does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God.’”” Matthew 4:4

Apply: Read one Gospel chapter every day of the Fast.

The Fast begins with trust in God's promise and peace with one another.

Jesus has gone before us into the wilderness and overcome.

Take-home resolution: Keep the Fast with a forgiving heart and a daily portion of Scripture.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Great Fast begins, and our Church opens it with Shubkono, asking forgiveness of one another. Paul says: be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you (Ephesians 4:32).

Abram, old and childless, is told: look toward heaven and count the stars; so shall your descendants be. He believed the Lord; and the Lord reckoned it to him as righteousness (Genesis 15:5–6). The Fast begins with faith.

Jesus fasts forty days in the wilderness and answers the tempter: one does not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God (Matthew 4:4).

So let us begin: trusting like Abram, forgiving one another as we did at Shubkono, and feeding each day on God's word. A fast without love is only hunger; a fast with love becomes a road to Easter. Amen.

For children

Children, today the Great Fast begins! Before we give up sweets or meat, Jesus asks us to say sorry and forgive. Is there someone you can hug and say sorry to today?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you fasted forty days in the wilderness and overcame the tempter by the word of God. As we begin the Great Fast, take away all bitterness, anger and malice from our hearts. Make us kind, tenderhearted and forgiving, as the Father has forgiven us in you, and give us the faith of Abram, who believed your promise. Amen.
