Friday after the Sunday of the Visitation · St John of Damascus

  • Gospel Matthew 5:21–26 · First be reconciled

Word for the day

“leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift.” Matthew 5:24

Reflection

“So faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ.” Paul quotes Isaiah: God let himself be found by those who did not seek him. The word has gone out to the ends of the earth. St John of Damascus, whom we remember today, spent his life handing on that word faithfully; he also defended the holy icons, teaching that because the invisible God became visible in Christ, his face may be painted and venerated.

Jesus goes beneath the commandment “You shall not murder” to its root: anger and contempt. Whoever is angry with a brother or calls him a fool is already on the road to murder. And then a teaching for every worshipper: if at the altar you remember that your brother has something against you, “leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled”. Our offering at the Holy Qurbono is not acceptable while we refuse to be reconciled. A Friday of the fast is a good day to make peace.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we remember St John of Damascus, the great Syrian monk and teacher of the faith, who defended the holy icons and wrote beautiful hymns still sung in the Eastern Churches. He taught that because the invisible God became visible in Christ, we may honour images of Christ and his saints.

St Paul writes: faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ (Romans 10:17). Then he quotes Isaiah: I have been found by those who did not seek me (Romans 10:20). God's grace often reaches those we least expect.

In the Gospel Jesus deepens the commandment: you have heard that it was said, you shall not murder. But I say to you that if you are angry with a brother or sister, you will be liable to judgement (Matthew 5:21–22). Anger and insults are the roots of murder.

Then he says something very practical: if you are offering your gift at the altar, and there remember that your brother or sister has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled, and then come and offer your gift (Matthew 5:23–24).

In our Holy Qurbono we exchange the kiss of peace before the anaphora. This is the moment Jesus speaks of. It is not a formality; it is a commitment to be reconciled.

During this fast, let us look for one broken relationship and take a step towards peace: a phone call, a visit, an apology. Then our offering at Yaldo will be pleasing to God. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says: make peace first, then come to pray. If you had a fight with someone, say sorry today!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you call us to be reconciled before we bring our gifts. Heal our anger, give us courage to make peace, and through the prayers of St John of Damascus, let us see your face. Amen.
