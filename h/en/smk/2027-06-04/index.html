Word for the day

“Therefore Jesus also suffered outside the city gate in order to sanctify the people by his own blood. Let us then go to him outside the camp and bear the abuse he endured.” Hebrews 13:12–13

Reflection

Three remembrances meet on this first Friday of June. The Panchangam asks us to remember Most Rev. Geevarghese Mar Timotheos, a bishop of our Church who went to the Lord in 2019. It notes that the Latin Church celebrates today the solemnity of the Most Sacred Heart of Jesus, and June has long been kept by Catholics of every rite in Kerala as the month of the Sacred Heart. And it is the First Friday, when many of our families go to confession and receive Holy Communion in reparation and love. The readings chosen for the memory of a bishop gather all three together. The Letter to the Hebrews begins: “Remember your leaders, those who spoke the word of God to you; consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith. Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.” We remember a bishop not only with gratitude for what he did, but with a prayer that the Lord who called him may receive him, and with a resolve to imitate what was Christlike in him. The memory of a shepherd always sends us back to the Shepherd whose heart was the source of his.

The letter then says something remarkable: “We have an altar from which those who officiate in the tent have no right to eat.” The Christian altar is not a table of ordinary food; it is the place where the one sacrifice of Christ is made present. And where was that sacrifice offered? “Therefore Jesus also suffered outside the city gate in order to sanctify the people by his own blood. Let us then go to him outside the camp and bear the abuse he endured.” Outside the gate, on the hill of Calvary, one of the soldiers opened his side with a spear, and blood and water flowed out. That pierced side is what the Church contemplates in the devotion to the Sacred Heart: not a sentimental picture but the wound through which we see the love of God made flesh. St Bonaventure wrote that his side was pierced so that, through the visible wound, we might see the invisible wound of love (The Tree of Life, in substance). Hebrews draws the practical conclusion: “Through him, then, let us continually offer a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that confess his name. Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God.” Praise and sharing: this is how a heart that has been loved answers.

The Gospel shows the same love in the shape of a steward's faithfulness: “Who then is the faithful and wise slave, whom his master has put in charge of his household, to give the other slaves their allowance of food at the proper time? Blessed is that slave whom his master will find at work when he arrives.” A bishop is such a steward; so is a priest, a parent, a teacher, a nurse on night duty. The wicked servant thinks “My master is delayed” and begins to treat others as he pleases. The faithful one keeps feeding the household, because he knows whose household it is. The alternative Gospels printed today show the heart of the Master who appoints the stewards: “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” “I am the way, and the truth, and the life.” “No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends.” On this First Friday, let us thank God for the shepherds who fed us with his word and his Body, pray for the soul of Mar Timotheos, and bring our own hearts to the pierced Heart of Christ, so that they may become a little more like his: gentle, humble, faithful, and ready to share.

Homily outline

Theme: “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.”

On Friday of the second week after Pentecost, the memory of Most Rev. Geevarghese Mar Timotheos, the First Friday, and the day the Latin Church keeps the Sacred Heart: remembering our shepherds, the Heart pierced outside the gate, and the faithful steward.

1. Remember your leaders

  • Those who spoke the word of God to us.
  • Consider the outcome; imitate the faith.
  • Shepherds pass; the Shepherd remains.
“I will give you shepherds after my own heart, who will feed you with knowledge and understanding.” Jeremiah 3:15
“And when the chief shepherd appears, you will win the crown of glory that never fades away.” 1 Peter 5:4

Apply: Write down this week the name of one bishop, priest or catechist who gave you the faith, and one thing in them you want to imitate.

2. Outside the gate: the pierced Heart

  • Jesus suffered outside the city gate.
  • Blood and water from the opened side.
  • We have an altar: praise and sharing.
“so that, when they look on the one whom they have pierced, they shall mourn for him” Zechariah 12:10
“Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.” John 19:34

Apply: Keep the First Friday this month and the next with confession, Communion and one act of sharing.

3. The faithful steward

  • Put in charge of the household.
  • Food at the proper time.
  • Found at work when the master comes.
“The Lord is my shepherd, I shall not want.” Psalm 23:1
“Moreover, it is required of stewards that they be found trustworthy.” 1 Corinthians 4:2

Apply: Name the people the Lord has put in your care, and do one faithful, unnoticed act of care for each this week.

We remember our shepherds and pray for them, and their memory sends us to the Heart of the Good Shepherd, pierced outside the gate.

From that Heart comes the strength to be faithful stewards in the household of God.

Take-home resolution: Imitate the faith of your shepherds, bring your heart to the Heart of Jesus, and be found faithful at work.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Panchangam asks us to remember Most Rev. Geevarghese Mar Timotheos, a bishop of our Church who went to the Lord in 2019. It is also the First Friday, and the Latin Church celebrates today the Sacred Heart of Jesus. The Letter to the Hebrews tells us: Remember your leaders, those who spoke the word of God to you; consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith (Hebrews 13:7).

Then it says: Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever (Hebrews 13:8). Bishops and priests come and go; the Lord who sends them remains. And Hebrews shows us where his love was poured out: Therefore Jesus also suffered outside the city gate in order to sanctify the people by his own blood (Hebrews 13:12). There, on Calvary, a soldier pierced his side, and blood and water came out. That open Heart is the source of every shepherd's love.

The Gospel asks: Who then is the faithful and wise slave, whom his master has put in charge of his household? (Matthew 24:45). A bishop is such a servant, but so is every father and mother, every teacher, every one of us who has someone to care for. Blessed is that slave whom his master will find at work when he arrives (Matthew 24:46).

So today let us pray for the soul of Mar Timotheos and for all our shepherds. Let us bring our hearts to the Heart of Jesus in confession and Communion on this First Friday. And let us do what Hebrews asks: offer a sacrifice of praise, and do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God (Hebrews 13:16). Amen.

For children

Children, today we remember a bishop called Mar Timotheos, who has gone to be with Jesus. A bishop is like a shepherd who looks after God's people. But the best shepherd of all is Jesus. Jesus loves us so much that he gave his life for us on the Cross. Today is the First Friday, when many people remember the Heart of Jesus, full of love for us. You can give Jesus a gift from your heart today: share something with a friend, or say thank you to Jesus for loving you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, good shepherd, whose side was pierced outside the city gate so that blood and water might flow for the life of the world, we remember today your servant, Most Rev. Geevarghese Mar Timotheos, whom you called to shepherd your people. Grant him rest and light in your presence. On this First Friday we bring our hearts to your Heart: make them gentle and humble like yours, faithful in the tasks you have given us, and generous in praise and in sharing. Give your Church shepherds after your own heart. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
