Word for the day

“For just as Jonah became a sign to the people of Nineveh, so the Son of Man will be to this generation.” Luke 11:30

Reflection

The fast ends with a surprise: the prophet is angry that God was merciful. Nineveh has repented and been spared, and Jonah sulks under a bush. God asks him: “Is it right for you to be angry?” and ends the book with a question that is addressed to us: “should I not be concerned about Nineveh, that great city, in which there are more than a hundred and twenty thousand persons who do not know their right hand from their left, and also many animals?” The fast is complete only when our heart becomes as wide as God's mercy, even for those we dislike.

Abraham pleads for Sodom: “Suppose ten are found there.” Joshua falls on his face before the ark. Saul, the persecutor, is thrown down on the road and called; he later writes that God “was pleased to reveal his Son to me, so that I might proclaim him among the Gentiles.” And Jesus says: “something greater than Jonah is here!” The Ninevites believed a reluctant prophet; we have the Son of God himself. Today we also remember St Agnes, the young Roman girl who gave her life for Christ. She shows that the response to Christ can be total even in the young.

Full homily

Dear friends, the fast ends with a surprise. Nineveh repents and is spared, and Jonah is angry. God asks him: is it right for you to be angry? (Jonah 4:4). And the book ends with God's question to all of us: should I not be concerned about Nineveh, that great city? (Jonah 4:11).

The fast is not finished until our heart becomes as wide as God's mercy, even for those we dislike.

The other readings show us intercessors. Abraham pleads for Sodom: suppose ten are found there (Genesis 18:32). Paul, once a persecutor, writes that God was pleased to reveal his Son to me (Galatians 1:15–16).

And Jesus says: something greater than Jonah is here! (Luke 11:32). With St Agnes, who loved Christ with her whole young heart, let us leave the fast with wide hearts and listening ears. Amen.

For children

Children, God loved all the people of Nineveh, even their animals! God wants us to love everyone, even people who are not nice to us.

Prayer

Merciful God, you spared Nineveh and rebuked the narrow heart of your prophet. As the fast ends, make our hearts as wide as your mercy, teach us to intercede like Abraham, and through the prayers of St Agnes give us her undivided love for Christ, who is greater than Jonah. Amen.
