Word for the day

“He replied, ‘Sir, let it alone for one more year, until I dig around it and put manure on it.” Luke 13:8

Reflection

On the last day of the year the Church reads one of the most hopeful parables. A man has a fig tree that has borne no fruit for three years. “Cut it down! Why should it be wasting the soil?” But the gardener pleads: “Sir, let it alone for one more year, until I dig around it and put manure on it. If it bears fruit next year, well and good; but if not, you can cut it down.” Christ is that gardener. Every new year is a gift of patience from God, and also a call to bear fruit.

Paul, near the end of Romans, describes his work as “the priestly service of the gospel of God, so that the offering of the Gentiles may be acceptable, sanctified by the Holy Spirit.” He boasts only of what Christ has accomplished through him, in word and deed, from Jerusalem to Illyricum. As we close the year, we offer to God what he has done through us, and ask pardon for the fruit we failed to bear.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the last day of the year St Paul looks back on his ministry: I have reason to boast in Christ Jesus of my work for God. For I will not venture to speak of anything except what Christ has accomplished through me (Romans 15:17–18).

At the end of a year we look back too. What has Christ accomplished through us? Not what have I achieved, but what has God done through me? Paul's boasting is gratitude.

In the Gospel Jesus tells the parable of a fig tree that bore no fruit for three years. The owner says: cut it down. The gardener pleads: sir, let it alone for one more year, until I dig around it and put manure on it. If it bears fruit next year, well and good; if not, you can cut it down (Luke 13:6–9).

The new year is that one more year: a gift of God's patience. Christ, the gardener, pleads for us and works on our roots through trials, through his word and through the sacraments.

But the parable also carries urgency. God gives time so that we may bear fruit, not so that we may waste it. What fruit is God waiting for from me: reconciliation, prayer, generosity, purity, service?

Tonight let us do three things: thank God for all that Christ has done through us this year, ask pardon for our sins, and make one resolution for the new year. And let us begin the new year in the arms of the Child of Yaldo. Amen.

For children

Children, God gives you a whole new year to grow and do good things. Thank Him tonight and ask Him to bless next year!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, patient gardener, you have given us another year. Thank you for every grace of this year; forgive the fruit we did not bear; dig around our roots and make us fruitful in the year to come. Amen.
