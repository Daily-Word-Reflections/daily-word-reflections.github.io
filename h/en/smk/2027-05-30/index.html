Second Sunday after Pentecost

Word for the day

“Whoever does not take up the cross and follow me is not worthy of me. Those who find their life will lose it, and those who lose their life for my sake will find it.” Matthew 10:38–39

Reflection

On the morning of Pentecost Peter stands up and explains what the crowds are seeing: it is the fulfilment of Joel: “In the last days it will be, God declares, that I will pour out my Spirit upon all flesh, and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy… Then everyone who calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved.” The Spirit is not reserved to a few; he is poured out on young and old, men and women, all who call on the Lord.

St Paul tells us what this means for daily life: “There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has set you free from the law of sin and of death… If the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, he who raised Christ from the dead will give life to your mortal bodies also through his Spirit that dwells in you.” The Spirit who raised Jesus lives in us.

In the evening Gospel Jesus spends the night in prayer on the mountain, then chooses the Twelve and proclaims: “Blessed are you who are poor, for yours is the kingdom of God. Blessed are you who are hungry now, for you will be filled. Blessed are you who weep now, for you will laugh.” The Beatitudes are the portrait of a life led by the Spirit, often hidden, often poor, always blessed.

Leviticus gives the deepest command: “You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy.” Jesus shows the way: “Whoever does not take up the cross and follow me is not worthy of me… whoever gives even a cup of cold water to one of these little ones in the name of a disciple… none of these will lose their reward.” Holiness is the Cross carried with love, and the small cup of water given in Christ's name.

Homily outline

Theme: “Called after a night of prayer.”

Jesus spends the night in prayer, chooses the Twelve, and proclaims the Beatitudes. The Spirit poured out at Pentecost makes this way of life possible for all of us: chosen, blessed, and called to holiness.

1. Chosen after prayer

  • Jesus spends the whole night in prayer before choosing the apostles.
  • He calls each by name and names them apostles, “sent ones”.
  • As God was with David, so he is with those he calls.
“And David became greater and greater, for the LORD, the God of hosts, was with him.” 2 Samuel 5:10
“Now during those days he went out to the mountain to pray; and he spent the night in prayer to God. And when day came, he called his disciples and chose twelve of them, whom he also named apostles.” Luke 6:12–13

Apply: Before an important decision this week, spend extra time in prayer.

2. The happiness of the kingdom

  • Jesus calls the poor, the hungry and those who weep “blessed”.
  • Their hope is in the kingdom that God promised, a kingdom that will never be destroyed.
  • The Beatitudes turn the world's values upside down.
“For he is the living God, enduring forever. His kingdom shall never be destroyed, and his dominion has no end.” Daniel 6:26
“Blessed are you who are poor, for yours is the kingdom of God. Blessed are you who are hungry now, for you will be filled. Blessed are you who weep now, for you will laugh.” Luke 6:20–21

Apply: Thank God for one blessing hidden in a present difficulty.

3. Be holy: the cross and the cup of water

  • God's command to Israel: “You shall be holy, for I am holy.”
  • Holiness means taking up the cross and following Jesus.
  • It also means small kindnesses: even a cup of cold water given in his name.
“You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy.” Leviticus 19:2
“Whoever gives even a cup of cold water to one of these little ones in the name of a disciple—truly I tell you, none of these will lose their reward.” Matthew 10:42

Apply: Do one hidden act of kindness each day this week.

Prayer, poverty of spirit and humble love are the marks of those whom Christ calls.

Let the Spirit of Pentecost make us holy in great crosses and small kindnesses.

Take-home resolution: Read the Beatitudes (Luke 6:20–26) each morning this week.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, on the day of Pentecost Peter stood up and said: this is what the prophet Joel foretold: “I will pour out my Spirit upon all flesh… everyone who calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved.” Not only on priests and prophets, but on sons and daughters, old and young. On you.

And what does the Spirit do in us? St Paul says: “There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. The law of the Spirit of life has set you free.” The same Spirit who raised Jesus from the dead lives in us and will raise our mortal bodies. We are not slaves of sin; we are temples of the Spirit.

In the Gospel Jesus prays all night, chooses the Twelve, and then looks at his disciples and says: Blessed are you who are poor, blessed are you who are hungry, blessed are you who weep. This is the strange happiness of the Spirit: not the happiness of the rich and comfortable, but of those who trust God.

God says in Leviticus: “You shall be holy, for I am holy.” Holiness is not only for saints in pictures. Jesus shows two ways: to take up the cross and follow him, and to give a cup of cold water to a little one in his name. Big crosses and small kindnesses. Let us do one hidden act of kindness each day this week. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus once prayed all night long! And in the morning he chose twelve friends to be his apostles.

Then he said: “Happy are the poor, happy are the hungry, happy are those who cry, because God will take care of them.”

Jesus also said that even giving a cup of water in his name is precious. What kind thing can you do today?

Prayer

Spirit of life, poured out on all flesh, set us free from sin and death and make us holy as the Father is holy. Teach us the joy of the Beatitudes, give us strength to carry the cross, and let no cup of water given in Christ's name be forgotten. Amen.
