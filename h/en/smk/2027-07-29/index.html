Readings

  • Gospel Luke 10:38–42 · Martha welcomes Jesus into her home, and Mary sits at his feet

Word for the day

“Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken away from her.” Luke 10:42

Reflection

The letter to the Hebrews ends with plain, practical love, and the first words are the oldest rule of the household: “Let mutual love continue. Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it.” The writer is thinking of Abraham and Sarah at the oaks of Mamre, who ran out to three travellers in the noon heat, brought water and bread and a calf, and found that they had received God's own messengers. The letter goes on: remember those in prison as if you were in prison with them; let marriage be honoured by all; be content with what you have, “for he has said, ‘I will never leave you or forsake you.’” Then comes the confident cry of the one who has nothing and fears nothing: “The Lord is my helper; I will not be afraid. What can anyone do to me?” A home where love is steady, where the stranger is welcome and the poor are not forgotten, is a small Church. It is such a home that the Gospel puts before us today.

“Now as they went on their way, he entered a certain village, where a woman named Martha welcomed him into her home.” Luke says that Martha was the host, the one who opened the door, and we should not forget that before we hear her scolded. The Church has sometimes been unfair to Martha. She did what the Letter to the Hebrews asks: she welcomed a traveller, and she wanted the best for him. Her fault was not in serving; it was in being “worried and distracted by many things.” Her sister Mary “sat at the Lord's feet and listened to what he was saying,” the place of a disciple at the feet of a teacher; Saul of Tarsus was brought up “at the feet of Gamaliel”. That Jesus accepts a woman in the disciple's place is itself a quiet revolution. When Martha asks him to send Mary to help, he does not rebuke her harshly; he calls her name twice, as one calls someone dear: “Martha, Martha, you are worried and distracted by many things; there is need of only one thing. Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken away from her.”

John fills in the rest of the picture. He names the village, Bethany, “the village of Mary and her sister Martha,” about two miles from Jerusalem, and he tells us in a single sentence how the Lord regarded this house: “Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus.” When Lazarus died, it was Martha who ran to meet the Lord on the road, and it was to Martha that he spoke the words that every funeral still repeats: “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live, and everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?” And Martha answered with a confession as great as Peter's: “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.” Then Mary came and fell at his feet, Jesus began to weep, and Lazarus was called out of the tomb. At a supper in the same house some days later Martha was serving again, without complaint now, and Mary anointed his feet with costly perfume until “the house was filled with the fragrance.” The sisters who seem so different are two halves of one discipleship. Mary of Bethany should not be confused with Mary Magdalene, whom the Church remembered on 22 July.

The Church needs both houses of Bethany. Without Martha there would be no table, no school, no hospital, no one to open the door in the monsoon night; without Mary the table would have nothing to serve, for the one thing needed is to know who is sitting at it. Archbishop Mar Ivanios founded Bethany Ashram in 1919, and its name recalls the house at Bethany, a place where welcome and prayer belong together. In our own homes the lesson is easy to apply. In Kerala no visitor comes to the door without being offered water at least, and in the rains a traveller sheltering on the veranda is a guest of God. But when the guest is Jesus himself, hospitality begins with listening. At the Holy Qurbono the two halves of Bethany meet: first we sit at his feet to hear the Word, and then we are brought to his table, where he serves us himself with his own Body and Blood.

Homily outline

Theme: “Mary has chosen the better part.”

On Thursday of the tenth week after Pentecost, with Martha, Mary and Lazarus of Bethany: the welcome of a home, the one thing needed, and the friends whom Jesus loved.

1. The welcome of a home

  • Do not neglect hospitality to strangers.
  • Abraham ran out to meet three travellers.
  • Martha opened the door.
“He said, “My lord, if I find favor with you, do not pass by your servant.” Genesis 18:3
“for I was hungry and you gave me food, I was thirsty and you gave me something to drink, I was a stranger and you welcomed me,” Matthew 25:35

Apply: Invite one person who is alone to your home this week.

2. The one thing needed

  • Mary at the Lord's feet.
  • Martha called by name, with love.
  • Worried and distracted by many things.
“One thing I asked of the Lord, that will I seek after: to live in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the Lord, and to inquire in his temple.” Psalm 27:4
“Whatever your task, put yourselves into it, as done for the Lord and not for your masters,” Colossians 3:23

Apply: Give five minutes each morning this week to listening to the Gospel, before any task.

3. The friends Jesus loved

  • Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus.
  • Martha's confession: the Messiah, the Son of God.
  • Mary's tears and perfume; Lazarus called out of the tomb.
“he will swallow up death forever. Then the Lord God will wipe away the tears from all faces,” Isaiah 25:8
“Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.” John 11:27

Apply: Say the confession of Martha slowly today: Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God.

4. Bethany today

  • Work and prayer belong together.
  • Bethany Ashram, founded by Mar Ivanios in 1919.
  • At the Holy Qurbono the Lord serves us himself.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“Mary took a pound of costly perfume made of pure nard, anointed Jesus' feet, and wiped them with her hair. The house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume.” John 12:3

Apply: Serve one household task today without grumbling, and pray as you do it.

Martha and Mary are two halves of one discipleship: the welcome that serves and the listening that loves.

Jesus loved the house at Bethany, and he comes to our homes and to his table to serve us himself.

Take-home resolution: Welcome the stranger, sit at the Lord's feet, and serve without grumbling.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Letter to the Hebrews tells us: Let mutual love continue. Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it (Hebrews 13:1–2). Abraham ran out to three travellers at noon, and found that they were messengers of God. A home where love is steady and the stranger is welcome is a small Church.

In the Gospel a woman named Martha welcomed Jesus into her home. Let us not be unfair to her. She was the host. Her trouble was that she was worried and distracted by many things, while her sister Mary sat at the Lord's feet and listened. Jesus called her name twice, with great love: Martha, Martha. There is need of only one thing, and Mary has chosen the better part (Luke 10:41–42).

John tells us that Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus. When Lazarus died, Martha ran to meet the Lord and made her great confession: I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God (John 11:27). Mary fell at his feet; Jesus wept; Lazarus came out of the tomb. Martha serves, Mary anoints: the two are halves of one discipleship. Mary of Bethany is not Mary Magdalene.

So let us open our homes, and then sit at his feet. Let the day begin with five minutes of listening, and let our service be done without grumbling. At the Holy Qurbono he serves us himself. Amen.

For children

Children, do you like it when your friends come to your house? Jesus had friends called Martha, Mary and Lazarus, and he loved to visit them. Martha cooked, and Mary sat beside Jesus to listen to him. Jesus loved them both, but he wanted Martha to know that listening to him is the most important thing. Today, when someone comes to your home, say hello with a happy face. And before you do your homework, sit quietly for a moment and tell Jesus, “Welcome, Lord. I am listening.”

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, friend of Martha, Mary and Lazarus, you were welcomed into their home at Bethany and you wept at their grief. Come into our homes. Make us welcoming to every stranger, attentive to your word, and faithful in service. Teach us the one thing that is needed, that we may sit at your feet and rise to serve, and bring us at last to the house where there is no more death. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
