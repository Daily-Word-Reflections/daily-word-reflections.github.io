Word for the day

“And he said to them, “Follow me, and I will make you fish for people.”” Matthew 4:19

Reflection

Today's Epistle comes from the most painful pages of St Paul's letters, where he has to defend his own ministry before the people he had loved and served. Rival preachers had come to Corinth, boasting of their gifts, and some of them were being paid for their teaching. They looked down on Paul because he had worked with his hands (Acts 18:3) and had asked nothing from the Corinthians. Paul answers with a sharp irony: “Did I commit a sin by humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I proclaimed God's good news to you without payment?” He had accepted help from other churches, “I robbed other churches by accepting support from them in order to serve you,” and he lists the friends from Macedonia who supplied his needs, the Philippians who had always helped him (Philippians 4:15–16). So, he says, “I did not burden anyone,” and “I will continue to refrain from burdening you in any way.” We must read him rightly. Paul does not teach that the Church should never support her ministers; elsewhere he says plainly that the Lord commanded those who proclaim the gospel to get their living by the gospel (1 Corinthians 9:14). He is saying that he gave up this right at Corinth so that no one could say he preached for money. And he ends with the reason of the heart: “And why? Because I do not love you? God knows I do!”

Then Paul's tone turns grave: “For such boasters are false apostles, deceitful workers, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ. And no wonder! Even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. So it is not strange if his ministers also disguise themselves as ministers of righteousness. Their end will match their deeds.” The danger he names is not crude evil but a polished counterfeit, a message that sounds beautiful and a preacher who looks holy, but who seeks his own glory or gain. How does a simple believer tell the difference? The Lord himself gave the test: “You will know them by their fruits” (Matthew 7:16). St John adds: “do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God” (1 John 4:1). The Church has always taught us to ask whether a teaching agrees with the faith handed down from the Apostles, which the bishops, in communion with the successor of Peter, are charged to guard, and whether the preacher bears the fruit of humility, love and service. This is not a call to suspicion of others; it is a call to be wise, for the simple believe everything, but the clever consider their steps (Proverbs 14:15). Paul himself is the pattern of the true apostle: he gives, he does not take; he works, he does not boast.

The Gospel shows us what the true call looks like at its beginning. “As he walked by the Sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon, who is called Peter, and Andrew his brother, casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen. And he said to them, ‘Follow me, and I will make you fish for people.’ Immediately they left their nets and followed him.” Then two more: “James son of Zebedee and his brother John, in the boat with their father Zebedee, mending their nets, and he called them. Immediately they left the boat and their father, and followed him.” These men had no theology degree, no fine speech, no fame. They had boats, nets and a father, and they had a call. Anyone who has watched the fishermen of Kerala mend their nets on the beach after the monsoon knows how much a net means to a family. They left what they had in order to follow him, and he promised them no income, only a new kind of work: to draw people toward God. The false apostles take; the true Apostles leave. Yesterday the Panchangam remembered St Benedict, the monk of Italy who began his Rule with the single word “Listen” and taught his monks to put nothing before the love of Christ (Rule, Prologue and chapter 4, in substance). He too heard “Follow me” and left what he had. The same call goes out to each of us today, in the plain places where we work and mend our nets.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Gospel today takes us to the shore of the lake. Jesus is walking, and he sees two brothers casting a net, and he says: Follow me, and I will make you fish for people (Matthew 4:19). They were not scholars or leaders. They were working people. And the Gospel says: Immediately they left their nets and followed him (Matthew 4:20). Then it happens again with James and John, who were mending their nets in the boat with their father.

Think of what they left: the boat, the nets, the income, the father who needed them. They could not see where this would lead. They only knew who was calling. And that is the heart of every vocation: not a clear plan, but a clear voice. Jesus calls each of us in the middle of our ordinary work. The question is what we are ready to leave behind: a habit, a grudge, a fear, the love of money.

In the Epistle, St Paul shows us what it is to follow Jesus to the end. He worked with his own hands and asked nothing from the Corinthians so that nobody could say he preached for gain (2 Corinthians 11:9). He serves, he does not take. But he warns us as well: Even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light (2 Corinthians 11:14). Not everything that shines is of God. The Lord gives us a simple test: you will know them by their fruits (Matthew 7:16): humility, love and service.

Yesterday the Panchangam remembered St Benedict, who began his Rule with the word Listen. Let us listen today to the voice that says: Follow me. Let us leave one net at his feet, and let us serve without asking for a reward. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what Jesus said to the fishermen? He said, Follow me! And they left their nets and boats right away, and went with him. They did not know everything about where they were going, but they loved Jesus and trusted him. Jesus calls you too. He does not call you to leave your family; he calls you to be kind, to pray, to help others and to listen to him. And St Paul tells us to serve people without asking for anything back, just like a good fisherman who shares his fish. This week, do one kind thing for someone without telling anyone, and say: Jesus, I want to follow you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you walked beside the Sea of Galilee and called fishermen to follow you, and they left their nets at once. Call us also, and give us hearts that can leave what holds us back. Make us servants like Paul, a burden to no one, glad to give and not to take. Keep us from the disguises of light and from every preaching that seeks its own glory, and keep your Church in the faith of the Apostles. By the prayers of St Benedict and all your holy servants, teach us to listen and to put nothing before your love. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
