Aneede Sunday · Commemoration of the Departed Faithful

31 January 2027 · Syro-Malankara Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Evening Gospel Matthew 25:31–46 · The judgement of the nations
  • Old Testament Genesis 49:29–33 · Jacob is gathered to his people
  • Old Testament 2 Maccabees 12:38–45 · Prayer for the dead
  • Old Testament Sirach 41:1–4 · Do not fear death's decree
  • Old Testament Isaiah 38:9–22 · Hezekiah's song
  • Catholic Epistle 2 Peter 3:8–13 · New heavens and a new earth
  • Epistle of St Paul 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 · We will be with the Lord for ever
  • Gospel John 5:19–29 · The dead will hear the voice of the Son of God

Word for the day

“Very truly, I tell you, anyone who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and does not come under judgment, but has passed from death to life.” John 5:24

Reflection

Last Sunday we remembered the departed priests; today, Aneede Sunday, we remember all the faithful departed: our parents and grandparents, brothers and sisters, children and friends. The Church does not forget them. Before we enter the Great Fast, we gather them in prayer, because Lent leads to Easter, the victory of Christ over death.

Jesus says: “The hour is coming, and is now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live… all who are in their graves will hear his voice and will come out.” St Paul writes to the Thessalonians so “that you may not grieve as others do who have no hope.” Christian grief is real, but it is grief with hope: “so we will be with the Lord forever.”

The reading from 2 Maccabees shows that God's people prayed for the dead even before Christ. Judas Maccabeus took up a collection for a sin offering for the fallen, “for if he were not expecting that those who had fallen would rise again, it would have been superfluous and foolish to pray for the dead”. From the beginning the Church has prayed for the departed and offered the Holy Qurbono for them, that they may be purified and enter the joy of God.

The evening Gospel reminds us how we will be judged: “I was hungry and you gave me food… just as you did it to one of the least of these who are members of my family, you did it to me.” The best way to honour our dead is to pray for them and to live in such a way that, when our turn comes, we too may hear: “Come, you that are blessed by my Father.”

Homily outline

Theme: “From death to life.”

On Aneede Sunday we remember all our departed: parents, grandparents, friends. We do not come as people without hope. We come to pray for them and to learn from them how to live.

1. In Christ, death is a passage to life

  • Jacob ended his blessing, breathed his last and was “gathered to his people”.
  • Jesus promises: whoever hears his word and believes has already passed from death to life.
  • Our departed are not lost; they are on the way home, and we pray that they reach it.
“When Jacob ended his charge to his sons, he drew up his feet into the bed, breathed his last, and was gathered to his people.” Genesis 49:33
“Very truly, I tell you, anyone who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and does not come under judgment, but has passed from death to life.” John 5:24

Apply: Name your departed loved ones in prayer each day this week.

2. We grieve, but with hope

  • Hezekiah, facing death, turned to God and found mercy.
  • St Paul does not forbid grief; he forbids grief without hope.
  • The Church prays for the dead, that they may be purified and see God; this is an act of love (CCC 1032).
“Surely it was for my welfare that I had great bitterness; but you have held back my life from the pit of destruction, for you have cast all my sins behind your back.” Isaiah 38:17
“We do not want you to be uninformed … about those who have died, so that you may not grieve as others do who have no hope. For since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so, through Jesus, God will bring with him those who have died.” 1 Thessalonians 4:13–14

Apply: Have a Holy Qurbono offered for your departed, and visit the cemetery with your family.

3. We will be judged by love

  • At the last judgment the King asks about the hungry, the stranger, the sick and the prisoner.
  • Proverbs says kindness to the poor is a loan to the Lord.
  • The best way to honour our dead is to live the works of mercy they taught us.
“Whoever is kind to the poor lends to the LORD, and will be repaid in full.” Proverbs 19:17
“Truly I tell you, just as you did it to one of the least of these who are members of my family, you did it to me.” Matthew 25:40

Apply: Do one work of mercy this week in memory of a departed loved one.

The dead hear the voice of the Son of God; those who have done good will rise to life.

Let us pray for our departed with faith and live with charity, so that one day we may meet them in the Father's house.

Take-home resolution: Pray “Eternal rest grant to them, O Lord” for the departed after each meal this week.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, last Sunday we remembered our departed priests. Today, Aneede Sunday, we remember all our faithful departed. Let us take a moment of silence and bring to mind those we have lost: a father, a mother, a husband, a wife, a child, a friend.

St Paul writes: we do not want you to grieve “as others do who have no hope”. He does not say “do not grieve”. Jesus himself wept at the tomb of his friend. But our grief has hope, because Christ is risen. Jesus says: “All who are in their graves will hear his voice and will come out.” One day our dead will hear that voice.

Why do we pray for the dead? Our reading from Maccabees says: if Judas had not believed in the resurrection, it would have been foolish to pray for the fallen. The Church has always prayed and offered the Holy Qurbono for the departed, that they may be purified and enter fully into God's joy. St Monica asked her son Augustine only one thing before she died: “Remember me at the altar of the Lord.”

And the evening Gospel reminds us how we ourselves will be judged: “I was hungry and you gave me food… as you did it to one of the least of these, you did it to me.” Next Sunday the Great Fast begins. Let us enter it remembering that life is short, that love is what remains, and that we too are walking towards the Lord. Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord. Amen.

For children

Children, do you have a grandpa or grandma who has gone to God? Today we remember all the people we love who have died.

We feel sad, but we also have hope, because Jesus rose from the dead and he will bring them to new life.

Tonight, pray for them by name. Let's say: “Jesus, keep them happy with you in heaven!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you are the resurrection and the life. Remember all our departed brothers and sisters, our parents, relatives, friends and benefactors, and those whom no one remembers. Forgive their sins, give them eternal rest, and let perpetual light shine upon them. Amen.
