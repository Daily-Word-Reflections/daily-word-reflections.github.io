Word for the day

“There were also many lepers in Israel in the time of the prophet Elisha, and none of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian.” Luke 4:27

Reflection

Today the Panchangam remembers Elisha the Prophet, and the Gospel names him. Elisha was a farmer's son, ploughing with twelve yoke of oxen, when Elijah passed by and threw his mantle over him; he left the plough, killed the oxen, fed the people with the meat and followed (1 Kings 19:19–21). When Elijah was about to be taken up, Elisha asked for one thing: “Please let me inherit a double share of your spirit.” The Books of Kings then tell of a ministry full of mercy: water made sweet, a poor widow's oil that flowed until every borrowed jar was full, a dead child given back to his mother at Shunem, a hundred people fed with twenty loaves of barley and some left over. Even after his death, a dead man who touched his bones came to life. The Fathers saw in Elisha a figure of Christ, who also fed the crowds, raised the dead and gave his Spirit in full measure to his disciples. Many Eastern Churches keep his memory on this day, and so do we, two days before the Apostles' Fast begins.

In the Gospel Jesus has just read from Isaiah in the synagogue of Nazareth and announced that the Scripture is fulfilled. His neighbours admire him, and then begin to doubt: is this not Joseph's son? Jesus answers with two stories from their own Scriptures: “But the truth is, there were many widows in Israel in the time of Elijah, when the heaven was shut up three years and six months, and there was a severe famine over all the land; yet Elijah was sent to none of them except to a widow at Zarephath in Sidon. There were also many lepers in Israel in the time of the prophet Elisha, and none of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian.” A Gentile widow and a Syrian army commander: God's mercy had always reached beyond the borders people drew around it. Origen, preaching on this passage, saw in Naaman a figure of the nations who would be cleansed through the true Elisha, our Lord, in the waters of Baptism (Homilies on Luke 33, in substance). Jesus is not insulting his own people; he is reminding them, as a prophet of Israel, of what Israel's own prophets had already shown. But the truth stings: “When they heard this, all in the synagogue were filled with rage.” They try to throw him from the cliff. “But he passed through the midst of them and went on his way.” No anger can stop the mission of mercy. It will go on, through Galilee to Jerusalem, and through the apostles to the ends of the earth, and to Kerala.

St Paul tells us today how that mercy takes hold of a life: “Therefore, do not let sin exercise dominion in your mortal bodies, to make you obey their passions. No longer present your members to sin as instruments of wickedness, but present yourselves to God as those who have been brought from death to life, and present your members to God as instruments of righteousness. For sin will have no dominion over you, since you are not under law but under grace.” Naaman went down into the Jordan proud and came up clean; we went down into the waters of Baptism and came up from death to life. St Ambrose taught the newly baptised that Naaman was healed not by the water of the Jordan alone but by the word of God and grace working in it, and that the same is true of the font (On the Mysteries 3–4, in substance). But the old habits of the body and the tongue still pull at us; the Catechism calls this the struggle left to us after Baptism, a struggle in which grace gives us the victory (Catechism 1264). On Wednesday the Apostles' Fast begins. It is a time to take back our members from sin and to offer them to God: eyes that look with purity, a tongue that blesses, hands that give, a stomach that learns to say no, feet that go to those in need. Like Elisha, let us ask for a double share of the Spirit, and like Naaman, let us humbly go down into the water of repentance.

Homily outline

Theme: “Present your members to God as instruments of righteousness.”

On Monday of the fourth week after Pentecost, the memory of Elisha the Prophet, two days before the Apostles' Fast: a double share of the Spirit, mercy beyond borders, and bodies offered to God.

1. Elisha: a double share of the Spirit

  • From the plough to the mantle.
  • A ministry of mercy: oil, bread, life.
  • A figure of Christ.
“Please let me inherit a double share of your spirit.” 2 Kings 2:9
“The prayer of the righteous is powerful and effective.” James 5:16

Apply: Ask the Holy Spirit each morning of the Apostles' Fast for the grace you most need.

2. Naaman the Syrian: mercy beyond borders

  • A widow of Sidon and a Syrian soldier.
  • The anger at Nazareth.
  • He passed through the midst of them.
“his flesh was restored like the flesh of a young boy, and he was clean.” 2 Kings 5:14
“I truly understand that God shows no partiality, but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him.” Acts 10:34–35

Apply: Do one kindness this week for someone of another community, language or religion.

3. Instruments of righteousness

  • Brought from death to life in Baptism.
  • Sin will have no dominion: grace, not law.
  • The Apostles' Fast: giving back our members to God.
“Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable to you, O Lord, my rock and my redeemer.” Psalm 19:14
“present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship.” Romans 12:1

Apply: Write down the one offering you will make during the Apostles' Fast and place it before the image of Christ at home.

Elisha's spirit, Naaman's cleansing and Paul's call all point to Christ, whose mercy reaches every nation and whose grace releases us from the rule of sin.

The Apostles' Fast is the time to offer our members to God as instruments of righteousness.

Take-home resolution: Ask for a double share of the Spirit, cross one border in kindness, and choose your offering for the Apostles' Fast.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Panchangam remembers Elisha the Prophet. He was ploughing his field when Elijah threw his mantle over him, and he left everything to follow. Before Elijah was taken up, Elisha asked: Please let me inherit a double share of your spirit (2 Kings 2:9). God gave it, and Elisha's life was full of mercy: oil for a poor widow, a child raised to life, bread for the hungry, healing for a foreign soldier.

In the Gospel Jesus reminds the people of Nazareth: there were also many lepers in Israel in the time of the prophet Elisha, and none of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian (Luke 4:27). God's mercy reached beyond the borders people expected. The people were filled with rage, but he passed through the midst of them and went on his way (Luke 4:30). Nothing can stop the mercy of God.

St Paul shows us what that mercy does in us: present yourselves to God as those who have been brought from death to life, and present your members to God as instruments of righteousness (Romans 6:13). Like Naaman in the Jordan, we went down into the water of Baptism and came up clean. Now our eyes, our tongues, our hands and our feet belong to God.

On Wednesday the Apostles' Fast begins. Let us decide now what we will offer: a meal, a habit, our time, our care for a stranger. Let us ask, like Elisha, for a double share of the Spirit, and let us offer our bodies to God as instruments of his righteousness. Amen.

For children

Children, today we remember Elisha, a prophet who helped many people with God's power. A soldier called Naaman was very sick, and a little girl in his house told his wife about Elisha. Naaman went to the river Jordan, washed seven times, and was healed! Jesus said God loves people from every country. On Wednesday our Church begins the Apostles' Fast. You can give something to God too: maybe kind words, sharing your snacks, or saying your prayers without being told.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you are the prophet greater than Elijah and Elisha; your mercy reached the widow of Zarephath and Naaman the Syrian, and it has reached us. In Baptism you brought us from death to life: do not let sin rule in our mortal bodies, but make our members instruments of righteousness. As the Apostles' Fast draws near, give us, as you gave Elisha, a double share of your Spirit, and the humility of Naaman to go down into the waters of repentance. When anger or prejudice rises against your word, pass through the midst of it and carry your mercy on its way. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
