Word for the day

“But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you,” Matthew 5:44

Reflection

Today's first reading is painful. At the feast for Isaac, Sarah sees Ishmael, Hagar's son, playing with her son and demands: “Cast out this slave woman with her son; for the son of this slave woman shall not inherit along with my son Isaac.” Scripture is honest: “The matter was very distressing to Abraham on account of his son.” Ishmael was his child too. God tells Abraham that the line of promise will run through Isaac, but he does not forget the other boy: “As for the son of the slave woman, I will make a nation of him also, because he is your offspring.” Abraham rises early, puts bread and a skin of water on Hagar's shoulder, and sends her into the wilderness of Beer-sheba. This is a family torn apart by jealousy and fear of losing an inheritance, the kind of quarrel that still divides homes over land and property. Yet God's care reaches beyond the walls of Abraham's tent. The boy who is sent away is not sent away from God.

Paul gives the Corinthians a principle that would have helped Abraham's household: “Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up.” The Christians of Corinth knew that idols are nothing and that “there is no God but one,” and some were using that knowledge to look down on weaker believers. Paul corrects them: “anyone who loves God is known by him.” What matters is not how much I know but whether I build others up. St Augustine summed up the whole Christian life in a short line: love, and do what you will; for if the root within is love, nothing but good can grow from it (in substance). Jesus takes love to its furthest point: “You have heard that it was said, ‘You shall love your neighbor and hate your enemy.’ But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you, so that you may be children of your Father in heaven; for he makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good.” The Father does not ration his sunshine according to merit. In this Great Fast we are asked to imitate that generosity: to pray for those who wrong us, and to greet those whom we would rather avoid. “Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect.”

Full homily

Dear friends, in the first reading a family is torn apart. Sarah says: cast out this slave woman with her son. The matter was very distressing to Abraham on account of his son (Genesis 21:10–11). Jealousy and fear of losing an inheritance still divide many families.

But God does not forget Ishmael: as for the son of the slave woman, I will make a nation of him also, because he is your offspring (Genesis 21:13). God's care reaches the one who is sent away.

Paul tells us: knowledge puffs up, but love builds up; anyone who loves God is known by him (1 Corinthians 8:1, 3). Knowing many things is not enough; we must build others up.

And Jesus says: love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you, so that you may be children of your Father in heaven; for he makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good (Matthew 5:44–45). In this Fast, let us pray for one person we find hard to love. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus asks us to do something very brave: to love even people who are not nice to us, and to pray for them. God makes the sun shine on everyone, good and bad. Tonight, can you pray for someone who was unkind to you? That makes you look like God's own child.

Prayer

Heavenly Father, you make your sun rise on the evil and on the good and send rain on the righteous and the unrighteous. You did not forget the boy who was sent into the wilderness. Heal the jealousy and quarrels that divide our families. Keep us from the knowledge that puffs up, and fill us with the love that builds up. Give us grace in this Great Fast to love our enemies and pray for those who persecute us, so that we may be your children and grow perfect as you are perfect. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
