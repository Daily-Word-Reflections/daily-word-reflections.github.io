Word for the day

“I have compassion for the crowd, because they have been with me now for three days and have nothing to eat;” Matthew 15:32

Reflection

After the drama of Moriah, Genesis seems to change the subject: “Now after these things it was told Abraham, ‘Milcah also has borne children, to your brother Nahor.’” A list of names follows, and among them one short sentence: “Bethuel became the father of Rebekah.” It is easy to skip, but it is the hinge of the next chapter. While Abraham was climbing the mountain with Isaac, God was already preparing, far away in Haran, the girl who would one day be Isaac's wife and the mother of Jacob. God's promises do not move forward only through visions and angels; they move through births, families and ordinary news. St Paul says that “when the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman”; the long genealogies of the Bible are the patient road by which that fullness came. No family is too ordinary to be part of God's plan.

The Gospel shows the same God, now in the flesh, caring for ordinary needs. “I have compassion for the crowd, because they have been with me now for three days and have nothing to eat; and I do not want to send them away hungry, for they might faint on the way.” The disciples see only the desert: “Where are we to get enough bread in the desert to feed so great a crowd?” Jesus asks what they have, “Seven, and a few small fish,” and then does what he will do at the Last Supper: “he took the seven loaves and the fish; and after giving thanks he broke them and gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds.” Many Fathers noted that this second feeding took place among people from the Gentile side of the lake, a sign that the Bread of Life is for all nations. Notice the order: Christ gives to the disciples, and the disciples give to the people. That is the ministry of the priest at the Holy Qurbono, and it is why the Letter to Timothy says: “Let the elders who rule well be considered worthy of double honor, especially those who labor in preaching and teaching.” The same passage asks for justice (“Never accept any accusation against an elder except on the evidence of two or three witnesses”) and for great care in choosing them: “Do not ordain anyone hastily.” St Ignatius of Antioch urged the faithful to respect their presbyters as the council of God and the college of the apostles (in substance). To honour our priests is to honour the Lord who feeds us through their hands; to pray for them is to share in their work.

Full homily

Dear friends, after Moriah Genesis gives us a list of names, and in it one small sentence: Bethuel became the father of Rebekah (Genesis 22:23). While Abraham was on the mountain, God was already preparing Isaac's future wife. God works through ordinary families.

In the Gospel Jesus says: I have compassion for the crowd, because they have been with me now for three days and have nothing to eat (Matthew 15:32). The disciples have only seven loaves and a few small fish.

Jesus took the seven loaves, gave thanks, broke them and gave them to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowds (Matthew 15:36). This is what happens at every Holy Qurbono: Christ gives, and the priest hands on what he has received.

So Paul writes: let the elders who rule well be considered worthy of double honor, especially those who labor in preaching and teaching (1 Timothy 5:17). Let us thank God for our priests, pray for them, and share our bread with the hungry. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus saw that the people were hungry and he felt sorry for them. With only seven loaves he fed thousands of people! At church the priest gives us Jesus, the Bread of Life. Tonight, say a prayer for your parish priest, and share something you like with someone else.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you had compassion on the hungry crowd and fed them with seven loaves, giving thanks, breaking and giving through your disciples. Bless the priests who give us your word and your Body and Blood; keep them holy, guard them from injustice, and send good workers into your harvest. As you prepared Rebekah's family long before she was needed, prepare our families for your plan. Amen.
