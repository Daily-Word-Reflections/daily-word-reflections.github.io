Word for the day

“The kingdom of God is as if someone would scatter seed on the ground, and would sleep and rise night and day, and the seed would sprout and grow, he does not know how.” Mark 4:26–27

Reflection

Paul prays that the Ephesians may have “the eyes of your heart enlightened” to know the hope of their calling, the riches of their inheritance and the immeasurable power of God, the same power that raised Christ from the dead and seated him above every power. Christ is head over all things “for the church, which is his body.” Faith is not only knowing about God; it is seeing with the heart.

In Mark's short parable a farmer sows, sleeps and rises, and the seed grows “he does not know how”: first the stalk, then the head, then the full grain. God's kingdom grows quietly, by God's power, often unseen. St John the Almsgiver, Patriarch of Alexandria, sowed generously among the poor and trusted God with the harvest.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul prays that the eyes of the Ephesians' hearts may be enlightened, so that they may know the hope of their calling, the riches of their inheritance and the immeasurable greatness of God's power, the power that raised Christ from the dead (Ephesians 1:18–20). That power is at work in the Church today.

In the Gospel Jesus speaks of the seed that grows by itself: someone scatters seed on the ground, sleeps and rises night and day, and the seed sprouts and grows, he does not know how (Mark 4:26–27). The growth of the Kingdom is not in our control; we sow, God gives the growth.

Today the Church remembers St John the Almsgiver, Patriarch of Alexandria, who called the poor my masters. Whoever is kind to the poor lends to the Lord. Today sow one small seed of mercy; God will give the growth. Amen.

For children

Children, a little seed grows into a big plant even when we do not see it. God will make big even a small good deed you do today!

Prayer

Lord of the harvest, the seed of your word grows by your power. Enlighten the eyes of our hearts, make us patient sowers, and generous like St John the Almsgiver. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Paul prays that the eyes of the heart may be opened to see God's power at work in the risen Christ and in his Church. Mark's short parable shows the same power working in silence: the seed grows by itself, night and day, while the farmer sleeps. The Church grows by God's power, not by our anxiety.

Ephesians 1:15–23 · The eyes of your heart enlightened

Setting.

After the great blessing of 1:3–14, Paul gives thanks and prays for his readers. In Greek the prayer runs on as one long sentence. It moves from the readers' faith and love, to hope, inheritance and power, and ends with Christ raised, seated in heaven and given as head to the Church.

Structure.

  • vv. 15–16: thanksgiving for their faith and love.
  • vv. 17–19: the prayer: a spirit of wisdom and revelation, and the eyes of the heart enlightened to know hope, inheritance and power.
  • vv. 20–23: that power shown in Christ: raised, seated above every power, head over all things for the Church, his body.

Key words.

  • ophthalmoi tēs kardias, eyes of the heart: In the Bible the heart is the centre of the person: thought, will and love. Faith gives the heart eyes.
  • plērōma, fullness: The Church is “the fullness of him who fills all in all”: she is filled by Christ, and his fullness dwells in her for the world.

Verse by verse.

“so that, with the eyes of your heart enlightened, you may know what is the hope to which he has called you,” (1:18)
“And he has put all things under his feet and has made him the head over all things for the church,” (1:22)

An echo of Psalm 8:6. The risen Christ is Lord of all creation, and he exercises his lordship for the good of the Church.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that Christ is the head of the Body, and that the Church lives from him, in him and for him (CCC 792–795, in substance). The early Church called baptism itself “enlightenment”, because it opens the eyes of the heart (CCC 1216).

Mark 4:26–29 · The seed grows by itself

Setting.

This short parable is found only in Mark. It stands in the chapter of parables by the sea (Mark 4), after the sower and the lamp and before the mustard seed. Jesus' ministry looked small and met opposition; the parable says that God's Kingdom is already growing, though no one sees how.

Structure.

  • v. 26: a man scatters seed.
  • v. 27: he sleeps and rises; the seed grows, he does not know how.
  • v. 28: the earth produces of itself: stalk, head, full grain.
  • v. 29: when the grain is ripe, the sickle and the harvest.

Key words.

  • automatē, of itself: The source of our word “automatic”. The growth comes from the seed and the soil, that is, from God, not from the farmer's effort.
  • drepanon, sickle: The harvest image comes from Joel 3:13: “Put in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe.” Harvest is a biblical picture of the end of the age.

Verse by verse.

“and would sleep and rise night and day, and the seed would sprout and grow, he does not know how.” (4:27)

The farmer is not lazy; he has sown. But he cannot make the seed grow. This frees us from the anxiety of thinking that everything depends on us.

“The earth produces of itself, first the stalk, then the head, then the full grain in the head.” (4:28)

Growth has stages. Patience belongs to faith.

How the Church reads it.

The Second Vatican Council compares the Kingdom to a seed: the word of the Lord, sown in the field, grows by its own power until the harvest (Lumen Gentium 5, in substance). The Church sows and trusts; the growth is God's.

For study and teaching

  • What would I see differently if the “eyes of my heart” were enlightened?
  • Where must I sow and then trust, instead of trying to force the growth?
