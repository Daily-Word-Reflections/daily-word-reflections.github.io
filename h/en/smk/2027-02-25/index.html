Thursday of the third week of the Great Fast · Blessed Rani Maria

25 February 2027 · Syro-Malankara Rite

  • Epistle 2 Corinthians 4:7–15 · Treasure in clay jars
  • Gospel Matthew 10:16–25 · Sheep in the midst of wolves

Word for the day

“But we have this treasure in clay jars, so that it may be made clear that this extraordinary power belongs to God and does not come from us.” 2 Corinthians 4:7

Reflection

Genesis tells the end of Sodom in a few heavy lines: “the Lord rained on Sodom and Gomorrah sulfur and fire from the Lord out of heaven.” Lot reaches Zoar as the sun rises. “But Lot’s wife, behind him, looked back, and she became a pillar of salt.” She was already outside the city, already saved, yet her heart had stayed behind. Jesus would later say only three words about her: “Remember Lot’s wife.” Then the reading turns to Abraham, who goes early in the morning to the place where he had pleaded with God and sees the smoke rising. And Scripture adds the reason Lot was spared: “God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow.” Lot was saved because someone had prayed for him. Our intercession for others is never wasted.

Today we remember Blessed Rani Maria Vattalil, a Franciscan Clarist sister born at Pulluvazhy in Kerala, who served the poor in Madhya Pradesh and helped landless families to free themselves from moneylenders. On 25 February 1995 she was stabbed to death on a bus near Indore by a man hired by those who resented her work. Paul's words describe her: “we have this treasure in clay jars… We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed.” Jesus had warned: “I am sending you out like sheep into the midst of wolves.” What followed her death is the fruit of the Gospel: her family forgave the killer; her sister, Sr Selmy Paul, visited him in prison and tied a rakhi on his wrist as a brother; he repented and later came to her beatification at Indore in 2017. Tertullian said that the blood of Christians is seed (in substance). Rani Maria's blood bore the seed of forgiveness.

Homily outline

Theme: “We have this treasure in clay jars.”

On the memorial of Blessed Rani Maria: the heart that looked back, the prayer that saved, and the treasure that shines through weakness.

1. Remember Lot's wife

  • Already outside the city.
  • A heart left behind.
  • A pillar of salt.
“He is mindful of his covenant forever, of the word that he commanded, for a thousand generations,” Psalm 105:8
“Remember Lot’s wife.” Luke 17:32

Apply: Remove this week one occasion that keeps drawing your heart back to sin.

2. Treasure in clay jars

  • Afflicted, but not crushed.
  • Carrying the death of Jesus.
  • So that his life may be visible.
“Can I not do with you, O house of Israel, just as this potter has done? says the Lord. Just like the clay in the potter’s hand, so are you in my hand, O house of Israel.” Jeremiah 18:6
“but he said to me, “My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness.”” 2 Corinthians 12:9

Apply: Offer to God this week the weakness you are most ashamed of, and ask him to shine through it.

3. Sheep among wolves

  • Rani Maria among the poor.
  • Struck down on a bus near Indore.
  • A killer forgiven and changed.
“Even though you intended to do harm to me, God intended it for good, in order to preserve a numerous people, as he is doing today.” Genesis 50:20
“Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.” Romans 12:21

Apply: Pray daily this week for someone who has harmed you, and do them one good turn if you can.

God remembers those for whom we pray.

In clay jars like Rani Maria, his power overcomes evil with forgiveness.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day of the Fast for one person who has hurt you.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Lord rained fire on Sodom. Lot's wife, behind him, looked back, and she became a pillar of salt (Genesis 19:26). She was already outside, but her heart had stayed behind. Jesus says: remember Lot's wife (Luke 17:32).

Yet the reading ends with mercy: God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow (Genesis 19:29). Lot was saved because Abraham had prayed. Our prayer for others is never wasted.

Today we remember Blessed Rani Maria, a sister from Kerala who served the poor in Madhya Pradesh and was killed on 25 February 1995. Paul could have been speaking of her: we have this treasure in clay jars... struck down, but not destroyed (2 Corinthians 4:7, 9).

Jesus warned: I am sending you out like sheep into the midst of wolves (Matthew 10:16). Rani Maria went, and her family forgave her killer, who repented. Let us ask her prayers for the courage to serve and the grace to forgive. Amen.

For children

Children, Sister Rani Maria was a brave nun from Kerala. She went far away to help poor families. A man hurt her and she died. But her family forgave him, and he became sorry and good. Jesus' love is stronger than hate. Can you forgive someone today?

Prayer

Lord God, you remembered Abraham and rescued Lot from the overthrow. Keep our hearts from looking back to what you have saved us from. You placed the treasure of your Gospel in the clay jar of Blessed Rani Maria and gave her strength to serve the poor and to die for you. Through her prayers, give us courage among wolves, and give our families the grace to forgive as hers forgave. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
