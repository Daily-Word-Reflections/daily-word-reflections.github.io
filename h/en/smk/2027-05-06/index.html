Word for the day

“While he was blessing them, he withdrew from them and was carried up into heaven.” Luke 24:51

Reflection

Forty days after his Resurrection, Jesus leads his disciples out to Bethany, “and, lifting up his hands, he blessed them. While he was blessing them, he withdrew from them and was carried up into heaven.” The last earthly sight of Jesus is of hands raised in blessing. He goes, but he does not stop blessing his Church.

The Ascension is not Jesus leaving us; it is our humanity entering heaven. The Son of God, who took our flesh, now takes it to the right hand of the Father. St Mark says: “he was taken up into heaven and sat down at the right hand of God.” Where the Head has gone, the body hopes to follow. Our true home is with him.

The angels ask the disciples: “Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking up toward heaven? This Jesus… will come in the same way as you saw him go.” The Ascension is not a time for gazing at the sky but for mission: “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation… And they went out and proclaimed the good news everywhere, while the Lord worked with them.”

As Elijah was taken up and Elisha received his mantle and spirit, so Christ ascends and pours out his Spirit on his Church. Jeremiah's promise will be fulfilled at Pentecost: “I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts.” Like the disciples, who “returned to Jerusalem with great joy and were continually in the temple blessing God”, we now enter ten days of prayer for the Spirit.

Homily outline

Theme: “He was carried up into heaven, blessing them.”

Forty days after his Resurrection the Lord ascends. It is not a farewell of absence but of glory: our human nature, in Christ, enters heaven, and the Church is sent to the ends of the earth.

1. He ascends while blessing

  • Elijah was taken up in a whirlwind; Elisha received his spirit.
  • Jesus lifts up his hands and blesses the disciples; while blessing them he is carried up.
  • His last gesture on earth is blessing: his hands remain raised over the Church.
“As they continued walking and talking, a chariot of fire and horses of fire separated the two of them, and Elijah ascended in a whirlwind into heaven.” 2 Kings 2:11
“Then he led them out as far as Bethany, and, lifting up his hands, he blessed them. While he was blessing them, he withdrew from them and was carried up into heaven.” Luke 24:50–51

Apply: Receive the final blessing at every Qurbono as Christ's own blessing.

2. Seated at the right hand of the Father

  • The Psalm foretold: “Sit at my right hand.”
  • Christ, true God and true man, takes our humanity into the glory of the Father.
  • There he intercedes for us as our High Priest; where the Head has gone, the members hope to follow (CCC 661–667).
“The LORD says to my lord, “Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies your footstool.”” Psalm 110:1
“So then the Lord Jesus, after he had spoken to them, was taken up into heaven and sat down at the right hand of God.” Mark 16:19

Apply: Lift your heart to heaven each day: “Lord, draw me to where you are.”

3. Why stand looking up? Be witnesses

  • The prophets saw that the spirit of Elijah rested on Elisha.
  • The angels ask: “Why do you stand looking up toward heaven?” He will come again.
  • Meanwhile, with the power of the Spirit, we are witnesses to the ends of the earth.
“When the company of prophets who were at Jericho saw him at a distance, they declared, “The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha.”” 2 Kings 2:15
“But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.” Acts 1:8

Apply: Begin the novena to the Holy Spirit with Mary, from tomorrow until Pentecost.

The Ascension is the feast of our hope: our humanity is in heaven with Christ.

Let us return like the apostles “with great joy”, praying with Mary for the Spirit.

Take-home resolution: Pray the novena to the Holy Spirit from the Ascension to Pentecost.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, forty days after Easter Jesus leads his disciples out to Bethany. He lifts up his hands and blesses them, and while he is blessing them he is carried up into heaven. The last picture the disciples have of Jesus is of hands raised in blessing. He has never lowered them.

The Ascension does not mean that Jesus has gone far away. It means that our human nature, which he took from Mary, now sits at the right hand of the Father. Where he, our Head, has gone, we, his body, hope to follow. Heaven is our home.

But the angels say: “Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking up toward heaven?” The Ascension is not a time for gazing at the sky; it is a time for mission. Jesus said: “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news.” And they went out and preached everywhere, and the Lord worked with them.

Elijah was taken up, and Elisha received his mantle and his spirit. Jesus ascends, and he will send his Spirit upon us at Pentecost. Like the apostles with Mary, let us spend these ten days in prayer: Come, Holy Spirit. And at every Qurbono, when we hear “Lift up your hearts,” let us truly lift them up to him. Amen.

Homily option 2 · He ascended and gave gifts

Saint Paul reads the Ascension through Psalm 68: the King goes up in triumph and gives gifts to his people. Those gifts build up the Church.

1. Captivity taken captive

  • The King goes up in triumph.
  • Sin and death defeated.
  • The victor shares the spoils.
📖 Psalm 68:17–18
📖 Ephesians 4:7–10

Apply: Thank Christ today for his victory over your sins.

2. Gifts for building the body

  • Apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors, teachers.
  • Bezalel filled with the spirit to build the tabernacle.
  • Every gift for the common good.
📖 Exodus 31:1–5
📖 Ephesians 4:11–12

Apply: Ask yourself what gift God has given you, and offer it to your parish.

3. Growing up into the Head

  • No longer children tossed about.
  • Speaking the truth in love.
  • The whole body growing together.
📖 Hosea 11:3–4
📖 Ephesians 4:13–16

Apply: Learn one teaching of the faith more deeply this month.

The ascended Christ is not absent: he pours out gifts to build up his body.

We grow toward him, our Head, by using those gifts in love.

Take-home resolution: In these days before Pentecost I will discover and offer my gift for building up the Church.

Dear brothers and sisters, on this feast of Suloko Saint Paul helps us to see the Ascension in a new way. He quotes an old psalm of victory: when he ascended on high he made captivity itself a captive; he gave gifts to his people (Ephesians 4:8; Psalm 68:18). The picture is of a king returning from battle, leading his defeated enemies behind him and sharing the spoils with his people.

Christ has fought the great battle. On the Cross and in the Resurrection he defeated sin and death. Today he goes up to the Father as victor. But he does not keep the victory for himself. He gives gifts. Paul names them: the gifts he gave were that some would be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, some pastors and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ (Ephesians 4:11–12).

In the book of Exodus, when the tabernacle was to be built, God said of the craftsman Bezalel: I have filled him with divine spirit, with ability, intelligence, and knowledge in every kind of craft (Exodus 31:3). God gave skills so that his dwelling could be built. So too the ascended Christ gives gifts so that his Church may be built: bishops and priests, catechists and singers, parents and teachers, those who care for the sick and those who pray in hidden ways.

What is the purpose of all these gifts? That we may grow. Paul says we must no longer be children, tossed to and fro and blown about by every wind of doctrine, but speaking the truth in love, we must grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ (Ephesians 4:14–15). Hosea shows God teaching Israel to walk, holding them by the arms (Hosea 11:3). The ascended Lord still holds us and teaches us to walk.

In these ten days before Pentecost, let us ask: what gift has the Lord given me, and am I using it for his body? Let us offer it to our parish. Let us learn our faith more deeply, so that we are not tossed about. And at every Holy Qurbono let us lift up our hearts to Christ our Head, who has gone up to give gifts to his people. Amen.

Homily option 3 · Our High Priest in the true sanctuary

The Ascension is not only a departure. Christ enters heaven itself as our High Priest, carrying our names before the Father and writing his law on our hearts.

1. Into heaven itself

  • Not a sanctuary made by hands.
  • He appears before God for us.
  • Our human nature at the Father's right hand.
📖 Leviticus 16:12–15
📖 Hebrews 9:24

Apply: At the Holy Qurbono, when the priest calls us to lift up our minds and hearts on high, lift them to Christ.

2. He always lives to intercede

  • Aaron carried the names of Israel on his heart.
  • Christ carries ours.
  • Never alone in prayer.
📖 Exodus 28:29–30
📖 Hebrews 7:24–25

Apply: Bring the names of those you love to Christ in prayer each day.

3. The law written on hearts

  • The tablets placed in the ark.
  • The new covenant promised.
  • The Spirit who writes within.
📖 Jeremiah 31:31–34
📖 2 Corinthians 3:2–3

Apply: Pray each day of the ten days: Holy Spirit, write God's law on my heart.

The ascended Christ is our High Priest in heaven, interceding for us always.

From there he sends the Spirit to write his law on our hearts.

Take-home resolution: In these days before Pentecost I will pray each day for those I love, trusting that Christ intercedes for them, and ask the Spirit to write his law in my heart.

Dear friends, the Ascension can sound like a farewell. But the Letter to the Hebrews shows us that it is an entrance. Once a year the high priest of Israel passed through the curtain into the Holy of Holies with the blood of the offering (Leviticus 16:15). Today Christ, our High Priest, enters a greater sanctuary: Christ did not enter a sanctuary made by human hands, a mere copy of the true one, but he entered into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf (Hebrews 9:24).

On our behalf. That is the heart of today's feast. Our Lord takes our human nature, the body he received from Mary, the body that bore the wounds of the Cross, into the presence of the Father. Where our Head has gone, we his members hope to follow.

And what does he do there? When Aaron went into the holy place, he carried on his breast the names of the twelve tribes: so Aaron shall bear the names of the sons of Israel on his heart (Exodus 28:29). Christ carries our names on his heart. He always lives to make intercession for them (Hebrews 7:25). When we pray, we never pray alone. The ascended Lord is already praying for us.

One of today's readings tells how Moses placed the tablets of the law in the ark (Deuteronomy 10:5). Another gives God's promise through Jeremiah: I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts (Jeremiah 31:33). From heaven the ascended Christ will send his Spirit to do exactly this. Saint Paul says that we are a letter written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts (2 Corinthians 3:3).

So in these ten days of waiting, let us pray each day for those we love, trusting that Christ carries their names before the Father. Let us ask: Holy Spirit, write God's law on my heart. And at every Holy Qurbono, when we are called to lift up our minds and hearts on high, let us lift them to where our High Priest is. Amen.

For children

Children, today Jesus went up to heaven! He took his friends to a hill, raised his hands and blessed them, and then went up into the sky until a cloud hid him.

Jesus is now with God the Father, but he still blesses us and loves us. And he promised to send the Holy Spirit to help us.

For the next ten days, let's pray every day: “Come, Holy Spirit!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, ascended into heaven, you raised your hands to bless your disciples and took our humanity to the Father's right hand. Bless us from heaven, lift up our hearts to you, send us to proclaim your Gospel, and pour out your Spirit on your Church. Amen.
