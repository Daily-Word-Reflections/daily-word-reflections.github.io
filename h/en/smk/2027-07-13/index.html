Word for the day

“If we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord; so then, whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's.” Romans 14:8

Reflection

The Epistle gives us one of the shortest and fullest summaries of the Christian life: “We do not live to ourselves, and we do not die to ourselves. If we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord; so then, whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's.” Paul is writing to a church in which believers were judging one another over food and holy days. He answers not with a rule but with a belonging: we are the Lord's, and so is the brother or sister whom we are tempted to despise. “For to this end Christ died and lived again, so that he might be Lord of both the dead and the living. Why do you pass judgment on your brother or sister? Or you, why do you despise your brother or sister? For we will all stand before the judgment seat of God.” Then he quotes the prophet, “As I live, says the Lord, every knee shall bow to me, and every tongue shall give praise to God,” and concludes, “So then, each of us will be accountable to God.” The Church teaches that each person comes before the Lord at the moment of death and that our life is the time given for accepting his grace (Catechism 1021–1022, in substance). So today's reading is not a threat but a freedom: if we belong to the Lord, we need not be anxious about the judgments of others, and we have no right to pass them on others.

The Gospel shows how the Lord himself looks at people. “Then Jesus went about all the cities and villages, teaching in their synagogues, and proclaiming the good news of the kingdom, and curing every disease and every sickness. When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd.” St John Chrysostom, speaking of this verse, notes that the Lord is moved not by the crowd's strength but by its need, and that his compassion is the mark of the true shepherd (Homilies on Matthew, in substance). Then comes the saying that has sent so many to their vocation: “The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few; therefore ask the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers into his harvest.” St Gregory the Great, preaching on the same words in Luke's Gospel, said that we cannot speak of them without sorrow, for there are many who would listen to good things, but few to speak them (Homilies on the Gospels 17, in substance). The remedy Jesus gives is first of all prayer: ask the Lord of the harvest. And then, in the next chapter, the disciples who prayed are the ones sent out.

The Panchangam today remembers Mar Sheelas, St Silas, one of those laborers. We meet him in the Acts of the Apostles when the Church of Jerusalem resolved, after the first great council, to send two men to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas: “Judas called Barsabbas, and Silas, leaders among the brothers.” They carried the letter that welcomed the Gentiles without burdening them, and they “were themselves prophets, said much to encourage and strengthen the believers.” After the quarrel between Paul and Barnabas, “Paul chose Silas and set out, the believers commending him to the grace of the Lord.” At Philippi the two were beaten and thrown into prison, though both were Roman citizens, and at midnight, with their feet fastened in the stocks, “Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them.” That night the jailer asked, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”, and they answered, “Believe on the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household.” He is usually identified with Silvanus, the form of the name found in the letters: Paul writes with “Silvanus and Timothy” to Thessalonica, and Peter says, “Through Silvanus, whom I consider a faithful brother, I have written this short letter to encourage you.” He served both Paul and Peter and carried the Church's peace from Jerusalem to the nations. Little else is known for certain, and later traditions about his end are not firm. Today we also remember Most Rev. Antony Mar Silvanos on his name day, and we pray that he may be a faithful brother and a servant of the Church's unity. Lord of the harvest, send out laborers.

Homily outline

Theme: “We are the Lord's.”

On Tuesday of the eighth week after Pentecost, with Mar Sheelas (St Silas): belonging to the Lord, the compassion of the shepherd, and the laborers for the harvest.

1. We are the Lord's

  • Living and dying to the Lord.
  • The brother and sister are his too.
  • No one has the right to despise another.
“Know that the Lord is God. It is he that made us, and we are his; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.” Psalm 100:3
“If we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord; so then, whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's.” Romans 14:8

Apply: Say each morning, “I am the Lord's,” and pray for someone you were tempted to judge.

2. Mar Sheelas, a faithful brother

  • The messenger of peace from Jerusalem to Antioch.
  • Praying and singing hymns in the prison at Philippi.
  • Companion of Paul and, as Silvanus, of Peter.
“Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil.” Ecclesiastes 4:9
“Through Silvanus, whom I consider a faithful brother, I have written this short letter to encourage you” 1 Peter 5:12

Apply: Be a carrier of peace this week: bring a kind word between two people who are not on speaking terms.

3. Ask the Lord of the harvest

  • The crowds like sheep without a shepherd.
  • The harvest is plentiful, the laborers few.
  • First pray, then be sent.
“Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” And I said, “Here am I; send me!”” Isaiah 6:8
“therefore ask the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers into his harvest.” Matthew 9:38

Apply: Pray for vocations from your parish, and answer “Here am I” to one small task in the Church.

We belong to the Lord in life and in death, and so does every brother and sister we are tempted to judge.

The Lord of the harvest looks at the crowds with compassion and asks us to pray, and then he sends us, as he sent Mar Sheelas.

Take-home resolution: Live this day as the Lord's, judge no one, and pray for laborers.

Full homily

Dear friends, today St Paul gives us in a few words the whole of the Christian life: We do not live to ourselves, and we do not die to ourselves. If we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord; so then, whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's (Romans 14:7–8). Everything that follows grows from this belonging. If we are the Lord's, then so is our brother, and so is our sister. That is why he asks: Why do you pass judgment on your brother or sister? (Romans 14:10).

The Gospel shows how the Lord looks at people. When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd (Matthew 9:36). And then he says to his disciples: The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few; therefore ask the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers into his harvest (Matthew 9:37–38). Notice: he does not first say, go; he says, pray. And those who pray are then the ones he sends.

Today the Panchangam remembers Mar Sheelas, St Silas. He was one of those laborers. The Church at Jerusalem sent him to Antioch with a letter of peace, and he strengthened the believers there. Then he went with Paul, was beaten and put in prison at Philippi, and at midnight he was singing hymns to God (Acts 16:25). Later he served Peter too. He carried the Church's peace and he sang in prison. We pray today also for Most Rev. Antony Mar Silvanos on his name day.

So let us live this day as the Lord's. Let us not judge. Let us pray for laborers for the harvest, and let us ask what work he is asking of us. Amen.

For children

Children, today we remember St Silas, who was a helper of St Paul. Once Silas and Paul were put in prison for talking about Jesus. At midnight, in the dark, they did not cry; they sang hymns to God! And the other prisoners listened. Then the doors of the prison opened and the jailer and his family became Christians. Jesus says that many people are waiting to hear about him, and he needs helpers. We can be his helpers too, by being kind, by praying, and by singing and speaking about Jesus. This week, sing a song of praise at home, and ask Jesus to send more helpers to his work.

Prayer

Lord of the harvest, we are yours in life and in death, and so are all our brothers and sisters. Keep us from judging and despising one another, and give us the compassion with which you looked at the crowds. Through the prayers of Mar Sheelas, who carried the Church's peace and sang your praise in prison, send out laborers into your harvest, and make us ready to be among them. Bless Most Rev. Antony Mar Silvanos on his name day and all our bishops, that they may be faithful brothers and servants of unity. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
