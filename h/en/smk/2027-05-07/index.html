Word for the day

“May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.” Galatians 6:14

Reflection

Yesterday the Church watched the Lord go up into heaven. Today, on the first day of the ten days of waiting, the Panchangam turns our eyes back to the wood on which he died: it keeps the Finding of the Cross of our Lord. This is not a step backwards. The one who ascended is the Crucified; he carries the marks of the nails into the glory of the Father, and the Cross is the road by which he went up. Our Malankara tradition calls the Cross the Sleeba, and we never pray without it: we sign ourselves at the beginning and the end of every prayer, the priest blesses with the hand cross, and the bare cross on our altars, without a figure, proclaims that the Crucified is risen. What can be said with confidence about the finding is this. In the reign of the emperor Constantine, the place of Golgotha and the tomb had been uncovered in Jerusalem and a great church built over them. St Cyril of Jerusalem, preaching there around 350, could already say that the wood of the Cross had been distributed in fragments over the whole world (Catechetical Lectures 4.10; 13.4, in substance). By the end of that century St Ambrose of Milan told how St Helena, Constantine's mother, had searched Golgotha and found the Cross (On the Death of Theodosius 43–46, in substance). The details belong to tradition; the heart of the feast is certain. The Cross is not lost. It was found, and it is found again whenever a Christian chooses it.

St Paul ends his letter to the Galatians in his own handwriting: “See what large letters I make when I am writing in my own hand!” He has argued at length with those who wanted the Gentile converts to be circumcised, and now he gathers everything into one sentence: “May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.” To boast is to rest your worth on something. People boast of family, money, education, the size of their house or their church. Paul rests his whole worth on the Cross, where the Son of God “loved me and gave himself for me” (Galatians 2:20). Then he adds: “For neither circumcision nor uncircumcision is anything; but a new creation is everything!” The Cross makes new creatures. And he closes with a line that only a man who had been beaten and stoned could write: “I carry the marks of Jesus branded on my body.” St John Chrysostom, commenting here, said that what seems a reproach to the world is for Paul the cause of countless blessings, and so he glories in it (Commentary on Galatians 6, in substance). The Cross is the place where the world's values are turned upside down: weakness becomes strength, shame becomes glory, death becomes life.

The Gospel gives the feast its picture. “The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field, which someone found and hid; then in his joy he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field.” The finder does not sell everything grudgingly; he does it “in his joy.” So it is with the Cross. To the world it looks like loss; to the one who has found it, it is the treasure that makes everything else worth selling. The merchant who finds “one pearl of great value” does the same. On this First Friday many of our parishes keep the devotion to the Sacred Heart: the Heart of Jesus opened on the Cross, from which “at once blood and water came out” (John 19:34), the Church's sacraments of Baptism and the Holy Qurbono. The Catechism says that the Heart of Jesus, pierced by our sins and for our salvation, is the sign and symbol of the love with which he loves us (CCC 478). Make the sign of the Cross today slowly and with attention. Go to confession if you can, and take part in the Holy Qurbono. And in these ten days, as we wait for the Spirit, ask for the grace to boast of nothing but the Cross, and to find in it the hidden treasure of your life.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Yesterday we celebrated Suloko, the Ascension. Today the Panchangam keeps the Finding of the Cross of our Lord. The one who has gone up to the Father is the Crucified; the Cross was the road of his glory. Very early, by the time of St Cyril of Jerusalem around 350, pieces of the wood of the Cross were honoured throughout the world, and tradition tells that St Helena found it on Golgotha.

St Paul writes in large letters with his own hand: may I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world (Galatians 6:14). What matters is not outward marks but a new creation. Paul carried the marks of Jesus on his body; he was not ashamed of them.

In the Gospel Jesus says that the kingdom is like a treasure hidden in a field. A man finds it and in his joy sells everything to buy the field (Matthew 13:44). The Cross is that treasure. The world sees loss; the believer sees the love of God, and gladly gives up everything for it.

Today is also First Friday. We honour the Heart of Jesus, opened on the Cross, from which blood and water flowed. Let us make the sign of the Cross with love, go to confession, receive the Holy Qurbono, and in these days of waiting ask the Spirit to make us boast only of the Cross. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Do you like stories about hidden treasure? Jesus told one: a man found treasure buried in a field and was so happy that he sold everything to buy that field. Today we remember the day the Cross of Jesus was found. The Cross is our treasure, because it shows how much Jesus loves us. Every time you make the sign of the Cross, do it slowly and say in your heart: “Jesus, thank you for loving me!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen and ascended, you carried the marks of your Cross into the glory of the Father. On this day of the Finding of your Cross we thank you for the holy wood on which you redeemed the world. Let us boast of nothing but your Cross; crucify the world to us and us to the world, and make us a new creation. Show us the hidden treasure and give us the joy to sell all for it. From your pierced Heart pour out on us the blood and water of your mercy, and as we wait for the Holy Spirit, keep us under the sign of the Cross. You live and reign for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Our Church keeps today the feast of the Finding of the Cross, and it is also the First Friday of May. The Epistle gives us the last lines of Galatians, written in Paul's own hand: “May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ.” The Gospel gives us the man who finds a treasure hidden in a field and the merchant who finds one pearl of great value, and both sell everything for what they have found. Paul is such a man. He found in the Cross the treasure for which he let everything else go. The key words are boast, treasure and new creation.

Galatians 6:11–18 · May I never boast of anything except the cross

Setting.

Paul wrote to the churches of Galatia, in the interior of what is now Turkey, when some teachers were urging his Gentile converts to be circumcised and keep the Mosaic law in order to belong fully to God's people. The whole letter answers them: we are made right with God through faith in Christ, and the Spirit has been given to us.

Paul usually dictated his letters to a secretary and then added a few lines himself (compare 1 Corinthians 16:21; 2 Thessalonians 3:17). Here the secretary lays down the pen and Paul takes it up. In a few sentences he gathers the whole letter together: not the mark of circumcision, but the Cross; not the old order, but a new creation.

Structure.

  • v. 11: Paul writes in his own hand, in large letters.
  • vv. 12–13: the motives of those who press circumcision on the Galatians.
  • v. 14: Paul's one boast: the cross of the Lord.
  • v. 15: what counts is a new creation.
  • v. 16: peace and mercy on those who keep this rule.
  • vv. 17–18: the marks of Jesus on his body; the closing blessing.

Key words.

  • kauchasthai, to boast: To glory in something, to rest one's confidence and pride on it. Paul uses the word often. The opponents boast in the flesh of the circumcised (v. 13); Paul boasts in the Cross, the very thing the world thought shameful (1 Corinthians 1:23).
  • kanōn, rule: A measuring rod, a standard. From it comes the Church's word “canon”. The rule Paul gives is v. 15: what matters is not circumcision or uncircumcision, but the new creation God makes in Christ.
  • stigmata, marks: The brand that marked a slave or a soldier as belonging to his master. Paul means the scars of the beatings and stonings he suffered for Christ (2 Corinthians 11:23–25). They mark him as Christ's own.

Verse by verse.

“See what large letters I make when I am writing in my own hand!” (6:11)

Large letters were a way of underlining. Some have guessed that Paul's eyesight was poor (compare 4:15), but the plain point is emphasis: read this carefully.

“May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.” (6:14)

The Cross is not only something that happened to Jesus. It has changed Paul's relation to everything. The world's ways of ranking people, by race, rite or status, no longer have a hold on him.

“For neither circumcision nor uncircumcision is anything; but a new creation is everything!” (6:15)

Paul has said the same in 5:6: what counts is “faith working through love”. The new creation is what God does in the baptized (3:27).

“peace be upon them, and mercy, and upon the Israel of God.” (6:16)

Scholars discuss whether “the Israel of God” means the whole Church of Jews and Gentiles in Christ, or the people of Israel, on whom Paul also prays God's mercy. Either way, Paul's blessing does not cancel God's love for Israel, as he will teach plainly in Romans 11:29.

“for I carry the marks of Jesus branded on my body.” (6:17)

The opponents wanted a mark in the flesh. Paul has one already: the wounds of the Lord's servant. The Church later used the same word for the wounds of Christ seen in some saints, such as St Francis of Assisi.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that the Cross is the one sacrifice of Christ, the one mediator, and that he calls his disciples to take up their cross and follow him (CCC 618). It also teaches that Baptism makes the believer “a new creature”, an adopted child of God (CCC 1265). The Church's love for the Cross is not love of suffering for its own sake. It is love for the One who loved us and gave himself for us (2:20). The Roman liturgy opens the Mass of the Lord's Supper with words taken from verse 14, and the whole Church, East and West, sings of the Cross as the tree of life.

Matthew 13:44–52 · The treasure and the pearl

Setting.

Chapter 13 is the third of the five great discourses in Matthew, the chapter of parables. Jesus first teaches the crowds from a boat; then he leaves them and goes into the house, where he explains the parables to his disciples (13:36). Today's parables are spoken to the disciples alone. The treasure, the pearl, the net and the master of the household are found only in Matthew.

In a land often crossed by armies, people sometimes buried their valuables in the ground, and some died without digging them up. A farm labourer might strike such a hoard with his plough. Pearls came from far away and were among the most costly things in the ancient world.

Structure.

  • v. 44: the treasure hidden in a field.
  • vv. 45–46: the merchant and the one pearl.
  • vv. 47–48: the net that gathers every kind of fish.
  • vv. 49–50: the meaning of the net: the separation at the end of the age.
  • vv. 51–52: “Have you understood?”; the scribe who brings out what is new and what is old.

Key words.

  • thēsauros, treasure: The word of v. 44 returns in v. 52: the master of the household brings things “out of his treasure”. The disciple who has found the treasure of the Kingdom becomes himself a storehouse for others.
  • margaritēs, pearl: In the ancient world a single fine pearl could be worth a fortune. The merchant is a man who knows values. When he sees the one pearl, he knows that everything else he owns is worth less.
  • mathēteutheis, trained, made a disciple: Literally, “discipled”. It is the same verb as the Lord's last command in Matthew: “make disciples of all nations” (28:19). A true scribe of the Kingdom is first of all a disciple.

Verse by verse.

“then in his joy he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field.” (13:44)

The word to notice is joy. He does not sell with regret, counting his losses. What he has found makes everything else easy to give up.

“on finding one pearl of great value, he went and sold all that he had and bought it.” (13:46)

The first man stumbles on his treasure; the merchant has been searching. God's Kingdom is found both ways: by surprise, and by a long search.

“a net that was thrown into the sea and caught fish of every kind;” (13:47)

The net does not choose while it is in the water. The Church gathers all kinds of people, and the sorting belongs to God at the end, not to us now.

“brings out of his treasure what is new and what is old.” (13:52)

The new comes first. The disciple does not throw away the Law and the Prophets; he reads them in the light of Christ, who fulfils them (5:17).

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism uses these parables to show that the Kingdom asks for a radical choice: to gain it, one must give everything (CCC 546). St Irenaeus taught that the treasure hidden in the field is Christ, hidden in the Scriptures and revealed when they are opened (in substance). The Second Vatican Council quotes St Augustine's saying that the New Testament is hidden in the Old and the Old made plain in the New (Dei Verbum 16), which is the treasure of the scribe in v. 52. The Catechism quotes the same words, “the furnace of fire”, from the nearby parable of the weeds when it speaks of the reality of hell (CCC 1034), always as a call to conversion now.

Questions people ask

Was it honest for the man to hide the treasure and buy the field?

The parable does not ask us to judge his business ethics. Jesus often takes a story from ordinary life, even a doubtful one, to make one point (compare the dishonest manager in Luke 16:8). The point here is the joy that makes a person give everything for the Kingdom.

Was the Cross really found?

The tradition that St Helena, mother of the emperor Constantine, sought the Cross in Jerusalem around AD 326 is told by St Ambrose before the end of that century. Already about AD 350 St Cyril of Jerusalem told his catechumens that pieces of the wood of the Cross had spread through the whole world. The details of the story grew with time. The Church's feast does not rest on proving any one relic. It honours the Cross of the Lord, the sign of our salvation.

Does “the world has been crucified to me” mean that Christians must despise the world?

No. God made the world good and loves it (John 3:16). Paul means the world as a system of values that ranks people by status and flesh. That world has lost its hold on him. A Christian loves creation and people more, not less, when he no longer needs the world's approval.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

Our Church honours the holy Cross at many points of the year, above all on the feast of the Cross in September, and today on the feast of its finding. The Syriac tradition loves to call the Cross the tree of life, set again in the midst of the garden. In the Holy Qurbono the priest blesses the people with the hand cross, and we sign ourselves with it at the beginning and end of prayer. Today is also the First Friday, when many families come to Confession and the Holy Qurbono in honour of the Heart of Jesus, pierced on the Cross (John 19:34). The two belong together: the Cross is the treasure, and the pierced Heart shows why it was given.

For study and teaching

  • What do I really boast in: my family name, my work, my achievements, or the Cross of the Lord?
  • The man sold everything “in his joy”. What would I find easy to give up if I truly saw the treasure of the Kingdom?
  • Paul carried the marks of Jesus on his body. What marks has faithfulness to Christ left on my life?
  • How can our family make the sign of the Cross with more attention and love this week?
