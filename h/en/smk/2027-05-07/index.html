Word for the day

“May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.” Galatians 6:14

Reflection

Yesterday the Church watched the Lord go up into heaven. Today, on the first day of the ten days of waiting, the Panchangam turns our eyes back to the wood on which he died: it keeps the Finding of the Cross of our Lord. This is not a step backwards. The one who ascended is the Crucified; he carries the marks of the nails into the glory of the Father, and the Cross is the road by which he went up. Our Malankara tradition calls the Cross the Sleeba, and we never pray without it: we sign ourselves at the beginning and the end of every prayer, the priest blesses with the hand cross, and the bare cross on our altars, without a figure, proclaims that the Crucified is risen. What can be said with confidence about the finding is this. In the reign of the emperor Constantine, the place of Golgotha and the tomb had been uncovered in Jerusalem and a great church built over them. St Cyril of Jerusalem, preaching there around 350, could already say that the wood of the Cross had been distributed in fragments over the whole world (Catechetical Lectures 4.10; 13.4, in substance). By the end of that century St Ambrose of Milan told how St Helena, Constantine's mother, had searched Golgotha and found the Cross (On the Death of Theodosius 43–46, in substance). The details belong to tradition; the heart of the feast is certain. The Cross is not lost. It was found, and it is found again whenever a Christian chooses it.

St Paul ends his letter to the Galatians in his own handwriting: “See what large letters I make when I am writing in my own hand!” He has argued at length with those who wanted the Gentile converts to be circumcised, and now he gathers everything into one sentence: “May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.” To boast is to rest your worth on something. People boast of family, money, education, the size of their house or their church. Paul rests his whole worth on the Cross, where the Son of God “loved me and gave himself for me” (Galatians 2:20). Then he adds: “For neither circumcision nor uncircumcision is anything; but a new creation is everything!” The Cross makes new creatures. And he closes with a line that only a man who had been beaten and stoned could write: “I carry the marks of Jesus branded on my body.” St John Chrysostom, commenting here, said that what seems a reproach to the world is for Paul the cause of countless blessings, and so he glories in it (Commentary on Galatians 6, in substance). The Cross is the place where the world's values are turned upside down: weakness becomes strength, shame becomes glory, death becomes life.

The Gospel gives the feast its picture. “The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field, which someone found and hid; then in his joy he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field.” The finder does not sell everything grudgingly; he does it “in his joy.” So it is with the Cross. To the world it looks like loss; to the one who has found it, it is the treasure that makes everything else worth selling. The merchant who finds “one pearl of great value” does the same. On this First Friday many of our parishes keep the devotion to the Sacred Heart: the Heart of Jesus opened on the Cross, from which “at once blood and water came out” (John 19:34), the Church's sacraments of Baptism and the Holy Qurbono. The Catechism says that the Heart of Jesus, pierced by our sins and for our salvation, is the sign and symbol of the love with which he loves us (CCC 478). Make the sign of the Cross today slowly and with attention. Go to confession if you can, and take part in the Holy Qurbono. And in these ten days, as we wait for the Spirit, ask for the grace to boast of nothing but the Cross, and to find in it the hidden treasure of your life.

Homily outline

Theme: “May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ.”

On the Finding of the Cross and First Friday, the day after Suloko: boasting only in the Cross, the treasure hidden in a field, and the pierced Heart of Jesus.

1. Boasting only in the Cross

  • In Paul's own large letters.
  • The world crucified to me.
  • A new creation is everything.
“Make a poisonous serpent, and set it on a pole; and everyone who is bitten shall look at it and live.” Numbers 21:8
“For the message about the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God.” 1 Corinthians 1:18

Apply: Make the sign of the Cross openly before meals this week, even in public.

2. A treasure hidden in a field

  • The Cross found in Jerusalem.
  • Selling all in joy.
  • The pearl of great value.
“I will give you the treasures of darkness and riches hidden in secret places,” Isaiah 45:3
“More than that, I regard everything as loss because of the surpassing value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord.” Philippians 3:8

Apply: Name one thing you hold too tightly, and give part of it up this week for Christ.

3. The pierced Heart

  • First Friday.
  • Blood and water.
  • The marks of Jesus on our bodies.
“when they look on the one whom they have pierced, they shall mourn for him, as one mourns for an only child,” Zechariah 12:10
“Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.” John 19:34

Apply: Go to confession before Pentecost, and offer one suffering you carry to the Heart of Jesus.

The ascended Lord is the Crucified; the Cross is the road of his glory and of ours.

Whoever finds the Cross finds the treasure for which everything else may be gladly sold.

Take-home resolution: Sign yourself with the Cross attentively, boast of nothing but Christ crucified, and bring your heart to his pierced Heart in these days of waiting.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Yesterday we celebrated Suloko, the Ascension. Today the Panchangam keeps the Finding of the Cross of our Lord. The one who has gone up to the Father is the Crucified; the Cross was the road of his glory. Very early, by the time of St Cyril of Jerusalem around 350, pieces of the wood of the Cross were honoured throughout the world, and tradition tells that St Helena found it on Golgotha.

St Paul writes in large letters with his own hand: may I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world (Galatians 6:14). What matters is not outward marks but a new creation. Paul carried the marks of Jesus on his body; he was not ashamed of them.

In the Gospel Jesus says that the kingdom is like a treasure hidden in a field. A man finds it and in his joy sells everything to buy the field (Matthew 13:44). The Cross is that treasure. The world sees loss; the believer sees the love of God, and gladly gives up everything for it.

Today is also First Friday. We honour the Heart of Jesus, opened on the Cross, from which blood and water flowed. Let us make the sign of the Cross with love, go to confession, receive the Holy Qurbono, and in these days of waiting ask the Spirit to make us boast only of the Cross. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Do you like stories about hidden treasure? Jesus told one: a man found treasure buried in a field and was so happy that he sold everything to buy that field. Today we remember the day the Cross of Jesus was found. The Cross is our treasure, because it shows how much Jesus loves us. Every time you make the sign of the Cross, do it slowly and say in your heart: “Jesus, thank you for loving me!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen and ascended, you carried the marks of your Cross into the glory of the Father. On this day of the Finding of your Cross we thank you for the holy wood on which you redeemed the world. Let us boast of nothing but your Cross; crucify the world to us and us to the world, and make us a new creation. Show us the hidden treasure and give us the joy to sell all for it. From your pierced Heart pour out on us the blood and water of your mercy, and as we wait for the Holy Spirit, keep us under the sign of the Cross. You live and reign for ever. Amen.
