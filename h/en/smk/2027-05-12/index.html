Word for the day

“for it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure.” Philippians 2:13

Reflection

The Panchangam joins two bishops of the fourth century today, one from the Greek world and one from the Syriac. Mar Yaqob of Nisibis was the first bishop of that frontier city between the Roman and Persian empires, in the south-east of today's Turkey. He sat among the bishops of the Council of Nicaea in 325, where the Church confessed that the Son is “of one being with the Father.” Our Church calls him Malpan, teacher, and Syriac tradition remembers him as the teacher of St Ephrem, who grew up in Nisibis and sang its bishops in his hymns. Theodoret of Cyrrhus tells that when the Persian king laid siege to the city, the bishop's prayers were its true wall (Religious History 1); whatever the details of that story, the people of Nisibis never forgot a shepherd who stood with them in danger. Mar Epiphanios was born in Palestine, became a monk, and in 367 was chosen bishop of Salamis in Cyprus, where he served for more than thirty-five years. He was a man of vast learning and fierce zeal for the true faith. His great book, the Panarion, or “medicine chest,” gathered the errors of his time and set beside each one the remedy of the apostolic faith. Among those he answered were some who denied that the Mother of God remained a virgin, and others who offered sacrifice to her as to a goddess. To both he gave the Church's balanced answer: “Let Mary be held in honour, but let the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit be worshipped; let no one worship Mary” (Panarion 79.7, in substance). Honour for the Mother, worship for God alone: that is still our faith.

St Paul's words to the Philippians describe the life of such shepherds and of every believer. “Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure.” There is no contest here between God's grace and our effort. God works, and therefore we work; his grace gives both the desire and the strength. Then Paul asks for a certain kind of life: “Do all things without murmuring and arguing, so that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, in which you shine like stars in the world.” A star does not argue with the darkness; it simply shines. And Paul adds a line that sounds like the voice of a martyr: “even if I am being poured out as a libation over the sacrifice and the offering of your faith, I am glad and rejoice with all of you.” The bishops we remember today poured out their lives in that way, in study, in teaching, in the defence of their people.

The Gospel brings us back to these days of waiting. Jesus says to the crowds: “When you see a cloud rising in the west, you immediately say, ‘It is going to rain’; and so it happens... You hypocrites! You know how to interpret the appearance of earth and sky, but why do you not know how to interpret the present time?” In Kerala we know the signs of the monsoon well; in mid-May every farmer watches the western sky. The Church watches too. The Lord has ascended; the promised Spirit is near. This is a present time to be read rightly. And Jesus says what a wise person does when trouble is coming: “when you go with your accuser before a magistrate, on the way make an effort to settle the case.” We are all on the way. Before the day of the Lord, before Pentecost, before the next Holy Qurbono, there may be someone with whom we must make peace. Jesus asks us to settle it now, on the road, while there is still time. The Spirit comes most fully into hearts that have forgiven.

Homily outline

Theme: “It is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure.”

On the memory of Mar Epiphanios of Cyprus and Mar Yaqob of Nisibis, in the days before Pentecost: grace at work in us, lives that shine, and peace made on the way.

1. God at work in you

  • Work out your salvation with fear and trembling.
  • Grace gives the will and the work.
  • No contest between God and us.
“I will put my spirit within you, and make you follow my statutes and be careful to observe my ordinances.” Ezekiel 36:27
“I am confident of this, that the one who began a good work among you will bring it to completion by the day of Jesus Christ.” Philippians 1:6

Apply: Each night before sleep, thank God for one good thing he did through you that day.

2. Shining like stars in the world

  • Without murmuring and arguing.
  • Two bishops who guarded the faith of Nicaea.
  • Honour for Mary, worship for God alone.
“Those who are wise shall shine like the brightness of the sky, and those who lead many to righteousness, like the stars forever and ever.” Daniel 12:3
“You are the light of the world. A city built on a hill cannot be hid.” Matthew 5:14

Apply: Go three days without complaining, and offer each held-back complaint as a prayer.

3. Read the present time; settle it on the way

  • Watching the western sky in May.
  • The Spirit is near.
  • Peace before the gift.
“Seek the Lord while he may be found, call upon him while he is near;” Isaiah 55:6
“leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift.” Matthew 5:24

Apply: Before Pentecost, make one call or visit to someone with whom you need to make peace.

The shepherds of Nisibis and Salamis guarded the faith with zeal; we guard it by shining, not by murmuring.

The Spirit is near like the monsoon; hearts that have made peace on the way will receive him fully.

Take-home resolution: Trust God at work in you, stop murmuring, and make peace on the way before Pentecost.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! In these days between the Ascension and Pentecost, the Church remembers two bishops who lived more than sixteen hundred years ago: Mar Yaqob of Nisibis and Mar Epiphanios of Cyprus. Mar Yaqob was one of the bishops at the great Council of Nicaea, which gave us the words we still say in the Creed: that the Son is of one being with the Father. Mar Epiphanios spent his life defending that same faith. One thing he taught is very useful for us today. Some people in his time denied the honour of the Mother of God; others went too far and worshipped her like a goddess. Epiphanios said: honour Mary, but worship only the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. That is exactly what the Church still teaches.

St Paul gives us today a beautiful sentence: “work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure.” Sometimes we think that either God does everything, or we must do everything. Paul says: God is at work in you, so you work too. Every good desire you have is already his gift. Every good deed is his grace and your effort together.

Then Paul asks us to do everything “without murmuring and arguing,” so that we may “shine like stars in the world.” Think how much murmuring there is in our homes and workplaces: about the weather, the prices, the neighbours, the parish. A star does not complain about the darkness. It shines. In the Gospel, Jesus says that people know how to read the clouds and the wind, but do not know how to read the present time. In May we all watch for the monsoon. Jesus asks us to watch for something greater: the coming of the Holy Spirit.

And Jesus gives one practical command. If you are on the way to court with your accuser, settle the case on the way. We are all on the way. Is there someone I have not spoken to for a long time? Someone I have hurt? Before Pentecost, let us make peace. The Spirit loves to come into hearts that have forgiven. Then at this altar, when we receive the Body and Blood of the Lord, we shall be ready to shine.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! In May, when the sky gets dark over the hills and the wind starts to blow, what do we say? “The rain is coming!” Jesus says that people are very good at watching the clouds. But he wants us to watch for something even more wonderful. Right now the Church is waiting for the Holy Spirit, who will come at Pentecost on Sunday. How can we get ready? St Paul says: do everything without grumbling. And Jesus says: make peace with anyone you have quarrelled with. So this week, if you have fought with a brother, a sister or a friend, say sorry first. Then your heart will be ready for the Holy Spirit, like a field ready for the rain.

Prayer

Lord God, you are at work in us, giving us both the will and the strength to do your good pleasure. We thank you for Mar Yaqob of Nisibis and Mar Epiphanios of Cyprus, shepherds who guarded the faith of Nicaea and poured out their lives for your people. Teach us to honour the Mother of your Son as they did, and to worship you alone, Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Help us to read the signs of this present time, and to make peace on the way with all whom we have wronged, so that our hearts may be ready for the coming of your Spirit. Make us shine like stars in the world, holding fast to the word of life. Through Christ our Lord, to whom with you and the Holy Spirit be glory for ever. Amen.
