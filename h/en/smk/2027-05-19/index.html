Word for the day

“For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes.” 1 Corinthians 11:26

Reflection

Three days after Pentecost our Church keeps a memory that belongs to her very heart: the memory of Mar Yaqob Shleeha offering the Holy Qurbono for the first time. The Panchangam does not say which James is meant, but our Syriac tradition has always looked to St James of Jerusalem, called by St Paul “James the Lord's brother,” that is, a close kinsman of Jesus. The risen Lord appeared to him (1 Corinthians 15:7); he led the Church of Jerusalem and spoke decisively at the council of the apostles (Acts 15:13–21); ancient writers remember him as the first bishop of that city and tell of a man of such prayer that his knees grew hard as a camel's (Hegesippus, in Eusebius, Church History 2.23, in substance). He was put to death in Jerusalem about the year 62. To him the tradition ascribes the Anaphora of St James, the great Eucharistic prayer of the Church of Jerusalem, which became the pattern of the West Syriac Qurbono that we celebrate. We cannot date the morning when he first stood at the altar. But we know what the memory means: from the first days after Pentecost, the Church that had received the Spirit gathered for “the breaking of bread and the prayers” (Acts 2:42), doing what the Lord had commanded.

The Epistle gives us the oldest written account of that command. Writing to Corinth some twenty years after the Resurrection, St Paul says: “For I received from the Lord what I also handed on to you, that the Lord Jesus on the night when he was betrayed took a loaf of bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and said, ‘This is my body that is for you. Do this in remembrance of me.’ In the same way he took the cup also, after supper, saying, ‘This cup is the new covenant in my blood. Do this, as often as you drink it, in remembrance of me.’” Received and handed on: that is how the Qurbono reached us, from the upper room to Jerusalem, from Jerusalem to Antioch, from Antioch to the shores of Kerala, from one generation of priests and faithful to the next. In the Gospel St Luke lets us hear the longing in the Lord's voice: “I have eagerly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer.” The Supper is not something the disciples arranged for Jesus; it is something he longed to give them. “This is my body, which is given for you.” “This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.”

St Cyril of Jerusalem, teaching the newly baptised in the very city of St James, told them not to regard the bread and wine as plain bread and wine, for they are the Body and Blood of Christ according to the Lord's own word; even if the senses suggest otherwise, let faith make you sure (Mystagogical Catecheses 4.6, in substance). This is the faith of the Catholic Church: in the Holy Qurbono the one sacrifice of the Cross is made present, and Christ, true God and true man, is truly, really and substantially present under the forms of bread and wine. “Remembrance” here is not a fond memory of something absent; it is the living memorial in which the Lord's sacrifice becomes present to us. And it looks forward too: “For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes.” Today let us thank God for every priest who has stood at the altar for us, from St James to the young priest of our own parish. Let us come to the Qurbono not as spectators but as those whom the Lord eagerly desired to feed. And if we have grown careless, arriving late, leaving early, receiving without preparation, let this memory of the first Qurbono make our next one feel like the first again.

Homily outline

Theme: “Do this in remembrance of me.”

On the memory of Mar Yaqob Shleeha's first offering of the Holy Qurbono: what was received and handed on, the Lord's longing, and the memorial that waits for his coming.

1. Received and handed on

  • The oldest account of the Supper.
  • From Jerusalem to our altar.
  • The Anaphora of St James.
“This day shall be a day of remembrance for you.” Exodus 12:14
“They devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers.” Acts 2:42

Apply: Learn by heart one response of the Holy Qurbono that you usually only mumble, and say it with meaning next time.

2. Eagerly desired

  • The Lord longs to give himself.
  • Body given, Blood poured out.
  • Truly present, not a symbol only.
“I will lift up the cup of salvation and call on the name of the Lord” Psalm 116:13
“I am the living bread that came down from heaven.” John 6:51

Apply: Make a short visit to the church this week and spend ten minutes before the Lord in the tabernacle.

3. Until he comes

  • A living memorial.
  • One sacrifice made present.
  • Waiting for his coming.
“For from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations, and in every place incense is offered to my name, and a pure offering” Malachi 1:11
“Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!” Revelation 22:20

Apply: After your next Qurbono, do one concrete act of love the same day, so that your life proclaims what you received.

What Mar Yaqob offered in Jerusalem, the Church still offers: the one sacrifice of Christ, received and handed on.

The Lord who eagerly desired to eat the Passover with his disciples still longs to feed us, until he comes.

Take-home resolution: Come to the Holy Qurbono prepared and hungry, thank God for our priests, and let your life proclaim the Lord's death and Resurrection.

Full homily

Dear friends, today our Church remembers Mar Yaqob Shleeha offering the Holy Qurbono for the first time. Our tradition honours St James of Jerusalem, the Lord's brother, the first bishop of that city, to whom the Anaphora of St James is ascribed, the source of the Qurbono we celebrate. After Pentecost the first Christians devoted themselves to the breaking of bread and the prayers (Acts 2:42).

St Paul tells us how the Qurbono came to us: For I received from the Lord what I also handed on to you (1 Corinthians 11:23). On the night when he was betrayed, the Lord took bread and said, This is my body that is for you. Do this in remembrance of me (1 Corinthians 11:24). From the upper room, through St James and the apostles, through the centuries, this gift has been handed on to our own altar.

In the Gospel we hear the longing of Jesus: I have eagerly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer (Luke 22:15). He gives the bread and says, This is my body, which is given for you; he gives the cup, the new covenant in my blood (Luke 22:19–20). What we receive is not a symbol only. It is truly his Body and his Blood, his one sacrifice made present.

For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes (1 Corinthians 11:26). Let us thank God today for every priest who has offered the Qurbono for us. Let us come early, prepare our hearts, and receive the Lord as one who longs to feed us. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what the Holy Qurbono is? It is the greatest gift Jesus gave us. At the Last Supper he said: This is my body, this is my blood, do this in memory of me. Today we remember St James, who offered the Qurbono in Jerusalem long, long ago. He prayed so much that his knees became hard! Next time you go to the Qurbono, remember: Jesus really comes to us, and he is very happy to see you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, on the night you were betrayed you took bread and the cup and gave them to your apostles as your Body and Blood, and you commanded, Do this in remembrance of me. We thank you for Mar Yaqob Shleeha and for all who have offered the Holy Qurbono from the first days of your Church until now. As you eagerly desired to eat the Passover with your disciples, give us a hunger for your holy Mysteries. Make us worthy to receive them with faith and love, to proclaim your death and Resurrection, and to wait for your coming in glory. With your Father and your Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
