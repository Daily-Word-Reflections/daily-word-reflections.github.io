Word for the day

“So deeply do we care for you that we are determined to share with you not only the gospel of God but also our own selves, because you have become very dear to us.” 1 Thessalonians 2:8

Reflection

Thessalonica was one of Paul's shortest missions. He had arrived there bruised from Philippi, preached for a few weeks, and was forced to leave by night. Now, writing back to the young church, he remembers how he had lived among them, and the letter becomes a portrait of a pastor's heart. “You yourselves know, brothers and sisters, that our coming to you was not in vain, but though we had already suffered and been shamefully mistreated at Philippi, as you know, we had courage in our God to declare to you the gospel of God in spite of great opposition.” The courage did not come from Paul's temperament; it came “in our God.” And his motive was clean: “we speak, not to please mortals, but to please God who tests our hearts.” He had not used flattery, had not looked for money, had not sought praise. Every preacher, every catechism teacher, every parent who speaks of God at home can examine himself by these lines. Am I trying to be liked, or trying to be faithful? God tests the heart, and the heart is where the Gospel either rings true or sounds hollow.

Then Paul uses two images that are surprising from a man so often pictured as stern. “But we were gentle among you, like a nurse tenderly caring for her own children.” And a few lines later: “we dealt with each one of you like a father with his children, urging and encouraging you and pleading that you lead a life worthy of God.” Mother and father together: tenderness and firmness, care and challenge. At the centre is the verse that sums up all ministry: “So deeply do we care for you that we are determined to share with you not only the gospel of God but also our own selves, because you have become very dear to us.” A message alone is not enough. The Gospel travels from person to person through shared lives, through time given, meals eaten together, nights of work so as not to be a burden. Paul again mentions his manual labour: “we worked night and day, so that we might not burden any of you while we proclaimed to you the gospel of God.” St Gregory the Great wrote that the pastor must be near to each one in compassion and raised above all in contemplation, so that he can carry the weaknesses of others in his own heart (Pastoral Rule II.5, in substance).

In the Gospel the argument in the temple grows sharp. Some of Jesus' hearers say, “Are we not right in saying that you are a Samaritan and have a demon?” When John speaks of “the Jews” in passages like this he means those among the leaders and hearers who opposed Jesus in that hour, not the Jewish people as a whole, of whom Jesus himself, his Mother and all the apostles were members; the Church firmly rejects any blame of the Jewish people for these quarrels (Nostra Aetate 4; CCC 597). Jesus does not answer insult with insult. He says simply, “I do not have a demon; but I honor my Father,” and then he makes one of the great promises of the fourth Gospel: “Very truly, I tell you, whoever keeps my word will never see death.” In the season of Qyomtho we hear it with Easter ears. The one who says it has already passed through death and come out the other side. To keep his word is to be joined to his risen life, so that bodily death, when it comes, is no longer the end but a door. Keeping his word begins in small things: the word of forgiveness I must speak, the word of truth I must not bend. Like Paul at Thessalonica, we keep it best when we share it with our whole life.

Homily outline

Theme: “Not only the gospel of God but also our own selves.”

On Tuesday of the fifth week after Qyomtho: the pure motive of the preacher, the gentleness of a nurse and the firmness of a father, and the word that conquers death.

1. To please God who tests our hearts

  • Courage in our God.
  • No flattery, no greed.
  • The heart behind the message.
“for the Lord does not see as mortals see; they look on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart.” 1 Samuel 16:7
“Am I now seeking human approval, or God’s approval? Or am I trying to please people? If I were still pleasing people, I would not be a servant of Christ.” Galatians 1:10

Apply: Before you speak about God or give advice this week, ask silently: am I seeking to please God or to be admired?

2. Gentle like a nurse, firm like a father

  • Tenderness in ministry.
  • Urging and encouraging.
  • Sharing our own selves.
“He will feed his flock like a shepherd; he will gather the lambs in his arms, and carry them in his bosom, and gently lead the mother sheep.” Isaiah 40:11
“No one has greater love than this, to lay down one’s life for one’s friends.” John 15:13

Apply: Give one person an hour of undivided attention this week, without looking at your phone.

3. Whoever keeps my word will never see death

  • Insult answered with truth.
  • The promise of the Risen One.
  • Keeping his word in small things.
“I have set before you life and death, blessings and curses. Choose life so that you and your descendants may live,” Deuteronomy 30:19
“I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live,” John 11:25

Apply: Choose one word of Jesus and practise it deliberately for one whole day.

The Gospel passes from life to life when its messengers give themselves.

Christ, risen from death, gives life to all who keep his word.

Take-home resolution: Seek to please God, share yourself generously, answer insults with truth, and keep one word of Jesus each day.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! St Paul remembers his short time in Thessalonica. He had been mistreated at Philippi, yet he says: we had courage in our God to declare to you the gospel of God (1 Thessalonians 2:2). And his motive was pure: not to please mortals, but to please God who tests our hearts (2:4).

Then he uses two tender pictures. We were gentle among you, like a nurse tenderly caring for her own children. And: we dealt with each one of you like a father with his children (2:7, 11). He adds: we were determined to share with you not only the gospel of God but also our own selves (2:8). The Gospel is passed on not only by words but by lives given.

In the Gospel some of Jesus' hearers insult him: you are a Samaritan and have a demon. Jesus does not insult them back. He says: I honor my Father. And he gives a great promise: whoever keeps my word will never see death (John 8:51). The one who says this has already risen from the dead.

So let us keep his word, and let us share it as Paul did, with our time and our love. Parents, teachers, priests: be gentle and firm, and give yourselves. And let us all remember: in Christ, death is not the end. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Who takes care of you when you are sick? Maybe your mother or your grandmother sits beside you and gives you medicine and soup. St Paul says he loved the people of Thessalonica like that, as gently as a nurse with little children. He gave them his time and his whole heart. Jesus says that if we keep his word, we will live for ever with him. Let us keep Jesus' words and share his love gently with everyone.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, you promised that whoever keeps your word will never see death. Give us courage in God to speak your Gospel, and purity of heart to seek only to please the Father who tests our hearts. Make our priests, teachers and parents gentle as a nurse and firm as a father, ready to share not only your word but their own selves. Keep us in your word, that we may share your risen life for ever. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, one God, for ever. Amen.
