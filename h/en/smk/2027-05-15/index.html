Word for the day

“Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and obey it!” Luke 11:28

Reflection

On the last day before Pentecost, with the Church gathered in prayer as the apostles were gathered with Mary, the Panchangam turns our eyes to the Mother of God, and it does so in the language of the fields. Today is the commemoration of the Holy Mother of God “for the ears of grain.” In the old farming year of the Syriac lands the wheat was coming to the ear in May, and the Church brought the coming harvest to the Mother of the Lord and asked her prayers. (The Syriac calendars know other such days in the farmer's year, for the seed that is sown and for the vines; our Panchangam keeps today's.) This is not a pious decoration on the calendar. It springs from the heart of our faith. The Fathers of the Syriac Church loved to call Mary the field that was never sown, yet brought forth the Sheaf of blessing: the Virgin earth from which sprang the Bread of life. St Ephrem sings of her as the land that gave us the Wheat which feeds the world (Hymns on the Nativity, in substance). Jesus himself spoke of the grain: “unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.” He was born in Bethlehem, whose name means “house of bread”; he gives himself as bread at every Holy Qurbono. When we remember the ears of grain in May, we remember the Mother who gave the world its true Bread.

The Gospel might seem at first to turn away from Mary. A woman in the crowd cries out, “Blessed is the womb that bore you and the breasts that nursed you!” And Jesus answers, “Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and obey it!” But the Lord is not lessening his Mother; he is telling us where her true greatness lies. St Augustine says that Mary was more blessed in receiving the faith of Christ than in conceiving the flesh of Christ (On Holy Virginity 3, in substance). She was the first to hear the word and keep it: “Here am I, the servant of the Lord; let it be with me according to your word.” She “treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart.” Elizabeth had already said of her, “blessed is she who believed.” The Second Vatican Council reads this very Gospel so: when Jesus proclaimed blessed those who hear and keep the word of God, Mary was faithfully doing just that (Lumen Gentium 58). So the woman in the crowd was right, and Jesus made her blessing deeper. Every one of us can share in the blessedness of the Mother, not by bearing Christ in the flesh, but by bearing his word in a believing heart. And tonight, on the eve of Pentecost, we wait with her, who “aided the beginnings of the Church by her prayers.”

St Paul's words to the Corinthians speak the farmer's language too. He is gathering a collection for the poor Christians of Jerusalem, and he writes: “the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully. Each of you must give as you have made up your mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.” Then he praises the Lord of every harvest: “He who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed for sowing and increase the harvest of your righteousness.” The Panchangam also remembers today Mar Pachomios, St Pachomius of Egypt, who lived in the fourth century. As a young conscript held in a prison in Thebes, he was astonished when strangers came at night with bread and water for the prisoners. They were Christians. He promised God that if he were released he would serve others in the same way. He was baptised, learned the life of prayer from an old hermit, and gathered the first great communities of monks who lived, prayed, worked the land and shared everything in common. A cheerful gift of bread made a saint, and the saint made thousands of brothers. We pray today too for Most Rev. Mathews Mar Pachomios, a bishop of our Church, on his name day, that he may sow bountifully and reap a harvest of righteousness among God's people. Tomorrow the great Harvest begins: the Spirit will come down, and the apostles will gather in the first sheaves of the Church.

Theme: “Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and obey it!”

On the commemoration of the Holy Mother of God for the ears of grain and the memory of Mar Pachomios, on the eve of Pentecost: the field that bore the Bread, the blessedness of hearing and keeping, and the cheerful giver.

1. The field that bore the Bread

  • The ears of grain in May.
  • The Virgin earth and the Bread of life.
  • Bethlehem, the house of bread.
“The Lord will give what is good, and our land will yield its increase.” Psalm 85:12
“Very truly, I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.” John 12:24

Apply: Thank God for your daily bread at each meal today, and pray a Hail Mary for farmers.

2. Blessed rather: hearing and keeping

  • The woman in the crowd.
  • Mary, the first to hear and keep.
  • Waiting with her for the Spirit.
“I treasure your word in my heart, so that I may not sin against you.” Psalm 119:11
“But Mary treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart.” Luke 2:19

Apply: Choose one verse from tomorrow's Pentecost readings and keep it in your heart all week.

3. God loves a cheerful giver

  • Sowing bountifully.
  • Bread given in a prison at night.
  • The harvest of righteousness.
“May those who sow in tears reap with shouts of joy.” Psalm 126:5
“So let us not grow weary in doing what is right, for we will reap at harvest time, if we do not give up.” Galatians 6:9

Apply: Give food or money to someone in need before tomorrow's Holy Qurbono, gladly and without counting.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Tomorrow is Pentecost. Today, the last day of waiting, our Church turns to the Mother of God, and she does it in a very homely way: the Panchangam calls today the commemoration of the Holy Mother of God “for the ears of grain.” In May the wheat in the old Syriac lands was coming to the ear, and the Church brought the harvest to Mary and asked her prayers. Why Mary? Because the Fathers called her the field that was never sown, from which came the Bread of life. Jesus was born in Bethlehem, which means “house of bread,” and he feeds us with his own Body at every Holy Qurbono.

In the Gospel a woman praises Jesus' Mother: “Blessed is the womb that bore you!” And Jesus says, “Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and obey it!” Is Jesus putting his Mother down? Not at all. He is telling us the secret of her greatness. Mary was blessed because she heard God's word and said yes to it: “let it be with me according to your word.” She kept the words of God in her heart and pondered them. So we too can share her blessedness, if we hear God's word and keep it.

St Paul gives us the farmer's lesson: “the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully... for God loves a cheerful giver.” Today we also remember Mar Pachomios, St Pachomius of Egypt. As a young soldier in prison, he was given bread by strangers in the night. They were Christians. That one act of kindness made him a Christian, and later he founded great communities of monks who prayed, worked and shared everything. We also pray today for Most Rev. Mathews Mar Pachomios, whose name day it is.

So, on the eve of Pentecost, let us do three things. Let us thank God for the harvest and pray for our farmers. Let us give something today, cheerfully, to someone in need. And let us wait with Mary, as the apostles did, for the Holy Spirit. Let us say tonight, with the Mother of God, “Here am I; let it be with me according to your word.” Then tomorrow the Spirit will find a field ready for his harvest.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Do you know where bread comes from? From wheat, a plant that grows in fields. In May the wheat grows little heads full of grain, called ears. Today our Church thanks God for the grain, and remembers Mother Mary. Why? Because Mary gave us Jesus, who said, “I am the bread of life.” Jesus also said that the people who are really happy are those who listen to God's word and do it. Mary did that perfectly. Tomorrow is Pentecost, the birthday of the Church! Tonight, say a prayer with your family: “Mother Mary, help us to listen to Jesus. Come, Holy Spirit!”

Prayer

Lord God, Father of all good gifts, you supply seed to the sower and bread for food, and from the Virgin Mary, the field that was never sown, you gave us your Son, the Bread of life. On this commemoration of the Holy Mother of God for the ears of grain, we bring you the fields of our land and the work of all who farm them; bless the coming harvest and let no one go hungry. Make us like the Mother of your Son, who heard your word and kept it, and teach us to sow bountifully and give cheerfully, as Mar Pachomios learned from the strangers who brought him bread. Bless Most Rev. Mathews Mar Pachomios on his name day, and keep him a faithful shepherd. As we wait with Mary for the coming of your Spirit, gather us into the harvest of your Kingdom. Through Christ our Lord, who with you and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns for ever. Amen.
