Word for the day

“When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth” John 16:13

Reflection

St Paul's letters are full of names we pass over quickly, and today the Church stops at one of them. Epaphroditus was a member of the little church at Philippi, sent by them to Paul in prison with a gift and with orders to stay and help him. In prison he fell ill, so ill that he nearly died, and when he recovered he was homesick and worried that his people were anxious about him. So Paul sends him home with a letter, and with a testimonial that any of us would treasure: “Still, I think it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother and co-worker and fellow soldier, your messenger and minister to my need.” Brother, co-worker, fellow soldier: three titles of friendship, labour and struggle. And the word translated “messenger” is the Greek word apostolos: the Philippians had sent him, and he was their apostle to Paul. The Church in her season of Pentecost remembers that the apostolic mission does not belong only to the Twelve; it is carried on by thousands of people whose names never reach a history book.

Paul asks the Philippians for something important: “Welcome him then in the Lord with all joy, and honor such people, because he came close to death for the work of Christ, risking his life to make up for those services that you could not give me.” Honour such people. Every parish is held up by an Epaphroditus or two: the catechism teacher who has given thirty Sunday mornings a year for twenty years, the sacristan who opens the church before dawn, the woman who sits with the sick when no one else will go, the young people who carry chairs and clean the hall. In these first weeks of June, when the schools of Kerala reopen in the monsoon rain, we might think also of teachers, bus drivers and cooks in school kitchens who will spend themselves for our children all year. And we may think of the many nurses from our parishes who serve in hospitals far from home, like Epaphroditus, sometimes at real risk to their health. Paul also says something tender: “God had mercy on him, and not only on him but on me also, so that I would not have one sorrow after another.” The great apostle does not pretend to be beyond grief; he thanks God for sparing his friend. St John Chrysostom remarks that Paul is not ashamed to show that he was anxious and would have mourned, because love for the brethren is no weakness in a saint (Homilies on Philippians 9, in substance).

The Gospel takes us back to the Last Supper: “I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now. When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth.” Jesus is a patient teacher. He does not pour everything into the disciples at once; he knows what they can carry. The Spirit continues his teaching through the whole life of the Church, leading her deeper into the one truth that Jesus is, not adding a new Gospel but opening up the one Gospel: “he will take what is mine and declare it to you.” This is what happens in the councils of the Church, in the prayer of the saints, in the slow growth of a believer's understanding over a lifetime. It happens to each of us too. There are things in our faith we could not bear at fifteen and understand at fifty; there are crosses whose meaning we will see only later. Like a good teacher at the start of a school year, the Spirit begins where we are. Our part is to stay in the classroom: to keep coming to the Holy Qurbono, where the word is read and the Bread of Life is given, and to let him guide us, one step at a time, into all the truth.

Homily outline

Theme: “When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth.”

On Thursday of the third week after Pentecost: Epaphroditus, the co-worker who nearly died for Christ, the honour due to hidden servants, and the patient teaching of the Spirit.

1. Brother, co-worker, fellow soldier

  • A messenger from Philippi.
  • An apostle with a small “a”.
  • The mission carried by unnamed hands.
“Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in your power to do it.” Proverbs 3:27
“esteem them very highly in love because of their work.” 1 Thessalonians 5:13

Apply: Thank, by name and in person, one hidden worker of your parish this Sunday.

2. God had mercy on him

  • Sickness in the service of Christ.
  • Paul is not ashamed of his anxiety.
  • Mercy spares us one sorrow after another.
“The Lord sustains them on their sickbed; in their illness you heal all their infirmities.” Psalm 41:3
“the Father of mercies and the God of all consolation, who consoles us in all our affliction” 2 Corinthians 1:3–4

Apply: Visit or call one person who is sick, especially a health worker who has fallen ill.

3. Into all the truth

  • “You cannot bear them now.”
  • The Spirit takes what is Christ's.
  • Staying in the Spirit's school.
“All your children shall be taught by the Lord, and great shall be the prosperity of your children.” Isaiah 54:13
“But the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you everything” John 14:26

Apply: Pray to the Holy Spirit each day this week before you or your children begin study or work.

The apostolic mission is carried by many Epaphroditus figures, and the Church must honour them.

Take-home resolution: Honour those who serve in hidden ways, and stay faithfully in the school of the Holy Spirit.

Full homily

Dear friends, today St Paul introduces us to a man most of us hardly know: Epaphroditus. Paul calls him my brother and co-worker and fellow soldier, your messenger and minister to my need (Philippians 2:25). The Philippians had sent him to help Paul in prison, and he became so ill that he nearly died.

Paul says: Welcome him then in the Lord with all joy, and honor such people (Philippians 2:29). Every parish is held up by people like Epaphroditus, catechists, sacristans, those who care for the sick, those who clean and arrange. This month the schools reopen, and many teachers and staff will work hard for our children. Let us honour such people, and thank them.

In the Gospel Jesus says: I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now. When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth (John 16:12–13). The Holy Spirit is a patient teacher. He teaches the Church through the centuries, and he teaches each of us through our lives, step by step.

So let us stay in his school. Let us keep coming to the Holy Qurbono, keep reading the word, keep praying, even when we do not understand everything. And let us be co-workers like Epaphroditus, giving ourselves for the work of Christ without asking for honour. Amen.

For children

Children, schools are opening again! Your teachers will help you learn new things, a little at a time. Jesus said that the Holy Spirit is like a teacher too: he helps us understand God a little more every day. Today we also heard about Epaphroditus, a helper of St Paul who worked so hard that he became sick. St Paul said: honour people like him. Who helps you every day? Your parents, your teachers, your bus driver? Say thank you to them this week!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you sent your apostles and their co-workers to carry the Gospel, and you send your Spirit of truth to guide your Church into all the truth. We thank you for every Epaphroditus among us, for those who serve without being noticed, for teachers and catechists, for nurses and carers, for those who risk their health for others. Have mercy on them in sickness, as you had mercy on your servant, and spare them one sorrow after another. Teach us patiently, as we are able to bear it, and lead us one step at a time into the fullness of your truth. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
