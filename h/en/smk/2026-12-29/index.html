Readings

  • Epistle Romans 11:25–32 · God's mercy to all
  • Gospel Matthew 15:1–9 · Honouring God with lips, not hearts

Word for the day

“For God has imprisoned all in disobedience so that he may be merciful to all.” Romans 11:32

Reflection

Paul unveils a mystery: a hardening has come upon part of Israel until the full number of the Gentiles has come in, “and so all Israel will be saved.” God has not rejected his first people: “the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable.” In the end everything is mercy: “God has imprisoned all in disobedience so that he may be merciful to all.” The child born in Bethlehem is the Messiah of Israel and the Saviour of the nations; no one is outside the reach of that mercy.

The Pharisees accuse Jesus' disciples of breaking the tradition of the elders by not washing their hands. Jesus answers that they themselves break God's commandment for the sake of their tradition, for example by using a religious vow to avoid supporting their parents. He quotes Isaiah: “This people honors me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me.” In the joy of Christmas, God asks for our hearts, not only our customs.

Homily outline

Theme: “Merciful to all.”

Irrevocable calling, mercy for all, hearts not only lips.

1. An everlasting love

  • God loves his people with an everlasting love.
  • His gifts and calling are irrevocable.
“I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore I have continued my faithfulness to you.” Jeremiah 31:3
“for the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable.” Romans 11:29

Apply: Pray for the Jewish people and for Christian unity.

2. Merciful and gracious

  • The Lord is slow to anger and full of love.
  • God shows mercy to all.
“The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love.” Psalm 103:8
“For God has imprisoned all in disobedience so that he may be merciful to all.” Romans 11:32

Apply: Forgive one person this Christmas.

3. Hearts far away

  • Isaiah: lips near, hearts far.
  • Jesus repeats Isaiah's warning.
“The Lord said: Because these people draw near with their mouths and honor me with their lips, while their hearts are far from me,” Isaiah 29:13
“This people honors me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me; in vain do they worship me, teaching human precepts as doctrines.” Matthew 15:8–9

Apply: Slow down and mean each word at your next prayer.

God's mercy reaches everyone.

Let our hearts be near him.

Take-home resolution: Do one concrete act of care for your parents or elders this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul unveils a mystery: God has not rejected His first people, for the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable. In the end, God has imprisoned all in disobedience so that He may be merciful to all (Romans 11:29, 32).

God's last word is mercy. Our failures do not cancel His call; His faithfulness is greater than our unfaithfulness.

In the Gospel Jesus criticises those who honour God with their lips while their hearts are far from Him (Matthew 15:8). Religion can become only external customs, while the heart remains unchanged.

As Christmas continues, let us ask: do I honour God only with my lips, with songs and decorations, or also with my heart? Let our celebrations come from hearts touched by His mercy. Amen.

For children

Children, God loves to forgive. When you say sorry to God, He forgives you completely!

Prayer

Merciful Father, your gifts and your call are irrevocable, and your mercy embraces all. Turn our hearts, not only our lips, to you, and let our Christmas customs express true love for you and for our neighbours. Amen.
