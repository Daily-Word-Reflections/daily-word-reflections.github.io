Word for the day

“And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” John 17:3

Reflection

The Gospel today takes us into the upper room on the night before the Passion. Jesus has finished his long farewell to the disciples, and now he turns to the Father: “After Jesus had spoken these words, he looked up to heaven and said, ‘Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you.’” The hour is the hour of the Cross. Jesus calls it the hour of glory, because on the Cross the love of the Father and the Son will be shown to the whole world. Then he tells us what all his work has been for: “And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” Eternal life is not only life that goes on for ever after death. It begins now, and its heart is knowing God: not knowing about him, as we know facts from a book, but knowing him as a child knows its father and a friend knows a friend. St Irenaeus put it in words the Church has never forgotten: the glory of God is a human being fully alive, and the life of a human being is the vision of God (Against Heresies IV, 20, 7, in substance). Every Holy Qurbono, every prayer of the Shehimo, every page of the Gospel read in faith is a step deeper into that knowing.

Jesus ends: “I glorified you on earth by finishing the work that you gave me to do. So now, Father, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had in your presence before the world existed.” The Son who speaks is not a creature who has earned a reward. He shared the Father's glory before the world began; he is God from God, light from light. Now, having taken our flesh and finished his work, he asks that this flesh, our human nature, be taken into that glory. This is what we celebrated at Suloko and at Pentecost: the risen Lord in glory at the Father's side, and the Spirit sent so that we may share his life. St Paul's prayer for the Thessalonians shows what that life looks like when it takes root in a community: “We must always give thanks to God for you, brothers and sisters, as is right, because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing.” Knowing God and loving one another grow together. A parish where faith is growing will be a parish where the love of its members is growing too.

Paul adds that this growth is taking place under pressure: “Therefore we ourselves boast of you among the churches of God for your steadfastness and faith during all your persecutions and the afflictions that you are enduring.” He speaks of God's justice, that God will one day give “relief to the afflicted as well as to us, when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels.” Christians do not take revenge; they leave judgement to God and pray for those who wrong them, as Jesus did on the Cross. Today is the last day of May, the month our people dedicate to the Mother of God, and the Latin Church keeps the feast of the Visitation. No one knew Jesus more deeply than his holy Mother. She carried him in her womb to the hill country, and Elizabeth greeted her: “blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” As the month of Mary closes, ask her to teach you what the Gospel calls eternal life: to know the Father and the Son whom he sent, to believe his word as she did, and to carry Christ to others in haste and in love.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul gives thanks for the Christians of Thessalonica, because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing (2 Thessalonians 1:3). They are suffering persecution, and still they are growing. Faith and love grow together.

In the Gospel Jesus looks up to heaven on the night before he dies and prays: Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you (John 17:1). The hour of the Cross is the hour of glory, because there the love of God is shown to all.

Then Jesus tells us what eternal life is: and this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent (John 17:3). Eternal life begins now. It is not only knowing about God, but knowing him, as a child knows a father. We come to know him in prayer, in the Gospel and above all in the Holy Qurbono.

Today the month of May ends, the month of the Mother of God, and the Latin Church keeps the feast of the Visitation. No one knew Jesus better than his holy Mother. Let us ask her to help us know her Son, to grow in faith and in love, and to carry him to others as she carried him to Elizabeth. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know the difference between knowing about someone and knowing them? You may know about the Prime Minister, but you know your mother! Jesus said that eternal life is knowing God, really knowing him like a friend and a Father. How do we get to know God? By talking to him every day, by listening to his stories in the Bible, and by meeting Jesus in the Holy Qurbono. Today is the last day of Mary's month. Ask Mother Mary to help you know Jesus better.

Prayer

Father, the only true God, you sent your Son Jesus Christ, and in him you have given us eternal life: to know you and the one whom you have sent. As he glorified you on earth by finishing your work, let us glorify you by our faith and our love. Make our faith grow abundantly and our love for one another increase, even in affliction. With the holy Mother of God, who believed your word and carried your Son in haste to others, let us know your Son more deeply and bring him to those we meet. Through Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
