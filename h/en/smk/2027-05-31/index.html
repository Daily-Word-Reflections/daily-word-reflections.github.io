Word for the day

“And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” John 17:3

Reflection

The Gospel today takes us into the upper room on the night before the Passion. Jesus has finished his long farewell to the disciples, and now he turns to the Father: “After Jesus had spoken these words, he looked up to heaven and said, ‘Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you.’” The hour is the hour of the Cross. Jesus calls it the hour of glory, because on the Cross the love of the Father and the Son will be shown to the whole world. Then he tells us what all his work has been for: “And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” Eternal life is not only life that goes on for ever after death. It begins now, and its heart is knowing God: not knowing about him, as we know facts from a book, but knowing him as a child knows its father and a friend knows a friend. St Irenaeus put it in words the Church has never forgotten: the glory of God is a human being fully alive, and the life of a human being is the vision of God (Against Heresies IV, 20, 7, in substance). Every Holy Qurbono, every prayer of the Shehimo, every page of the Gospel read in faith is a step deeper into that knowing.

Jesus ends: “I glorified you on earth by finishing the work that you gave me to do. So now, Father, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had in your presence before the world existed.” The Son who speaks is not a creature who has earned a reward. He shared the Father's glory before the world began; he is God from God, light from light. Now, having taken our flesh and finished his work, he asks that this flesh, our human nature, be taken into that glory. This is what we celebrated at Suloko and at Pentecost: the risen Lord in glory at the Father's side, and the Spirit sent so that we may share his life. St Paul's prayer for the Thessalonians shows what that life looks like when it takes root in a community: “We must always give thanks to God for you, brothers and sisters, as is right, because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing.” Knowing God and loving one another grow together. A parish where faith is growing will be a parish where the love of its members is growing too.

Paul adds that this growth is taking place under pressure: “Therefore we ourselves boast of you among the churches of God for your steadfastness and faith during all your persecutions and the afflictions that you are enduring.” He speaks of God's justice, that God will one day give “relief to the afflicted as well as to us, when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels.” Christians do not take revenge; they leave judgement to God and pray for those who wrong them, as Jesus did on the Cross. Today is the last day of May, the month our people dedicate to the Mother of God, and the Latin Church keeps the feast of the Visitation. No one knew Jesus more deeply than his holy Mother. She carried him in her womb to the hill country, and Elizabeth greeted her: “blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” As the month of Mary closes, ask her to teach you what the Gospel calls eternal life: to know the Father and the Son whom he sent, to believe his word as she did, and to carry Christ to others in haste and in love.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul gives thanks for the Christians of Thessalonica, because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing (2 Thessalonians 1:3). They are suffering persecution, and still they are growing. Faith and love grow together.

In the Gospel Jesus looks up to heaven on the night before he dies and prays: Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you (John 17:1). The hour of the Cross is the hour of glory, because there the love of God is shown to all.

Then Jesus tells us what eternal life is: and this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent (John 17:3). Eternal life begins now. It is not only knowing about God, but knowing him, as a child knows a father. We come to know him in prayer, in the Gospel and above all in the Holy Qurbono.

Today the month of May ends, the month of the Mother of God, and the Latin Church keeps the feast of the Visitation. No one knew Jesus better than his holy Mother. Let us ask her to help us know her Son, to grow in faith and in love, and to carry him to others as she carried him to Elizabeth. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know the difference between knowing about someone and knowing them? You may know about the Prime Minister, but you know your mother! Jesus said that eternal life is knowing God, really knowing him like a friend and a Father. How do we get to know God? By talking to him every day, by listening to his stories in the Bible, and by meeting Jesus in the Holy Qurbono. Today is the last day of Mary's month. Ask Mother Mary to help you know Jesus better.

Prayer

Father, the only true God, you sent your Son Jesus Christ, and in him you have given us eternal life: to know you and the one whom you have sent. As he glorified you on earth by finishing your work, let us glorify you by our faith and our love. Make our faith grow abundantly and our love for one another increase, even in affliction. With the holy Mother of God, who believed your word and carried your Son in haste to others, let us know your Son more deeply and bring him to those we meet. Through Jesus Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Paul gives thanks because the faith of the Thessalonians is growing and their love for one another increasing, even under persecution. In the Gospel Jesus lifts his eyes to heaven at the Last Supper and begins his great prayer: “Father, the hour has come.” He tells us what eternal life is: to know the Father, the only true God, and the one whom he has sent. The key words are growing and knowing.

2 Thessalonians 1:3–7 · Your faith is growing abundantly

Setting.

2 Thessalonians names Paul, Silvanus and Timothy as its senders. Many scholars think it was written later by a disciple in Paul's name, because its tone and its teaching about the Day of the Lord differ from 1 Thessalonians; others hold that Paul wrote it soon after the first letter, around AD 51. The Church receives it as inspired Scripture.

The church of Thessalonica was still suffering persecution. Some members were also confused about the Lord's coming, thinking that the Day of the Lord had already come (2:2). The letter opens, as Paul's letters usually do, with thanksgiving, and here the thanksgiving is also encouragement.

Structure.

  • v. 3: thanksgiving for growing faith and increasing love.
  • v. 4: Paul boasts of their steadfastness among the churches.
  • vv. 5–7: their sufferings are a sign of God's just judgment; relief will come when the Lord Jesus is revealed.

Key words.

  • hyperauxanei, is growing abundantly: A rare compound verb, found only here in the New Testament: “grows beyond measure”. Faith is alive. It is meant to grow, like a plant that has taken root.
  • hypomonē, steadfastness: Not passive endurance but staying power: remaining under a weight without giving way. Paul names it together with faith, as the fruit that persecution has not destroyed.
  • apokalypsis, revelation: The Lord's coming is described as an unveiling. Christ is already Lord; at the end his glory, now hidden, will be seen by all.

Verse by verse.

“because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing.” (1:3)

In 1 Thessalonians Paul had prayed that their love would increase (1 Thessalonians 3:12). Now he sees the prayer answered.

“Therefore we ourselves boast of you among the churches of God for your steadfastness and faith during all your persecutions and the afflictions that you are enduring.” (1:4)

Paul tells other churches about them. The witness of one suffering community strengthens the whole Church.

“This is evidence of the righteous judgment of God, and is intended to make you worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you are also suffering.” (1:5)

Their suffering is not a sign that God has abandoned them. It is a sign that they belong to his kingdom, which the world resists.

“and to give relief to the afflicted as well as to us, when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven” (1:7)

Justice belongs to God, not to us. Paul elsewhere tells believers never to avenge themselves but to leave room for God (Romans 12:19). In the POC, verses 6 and 7 are arranged in a different order.

How the Church reads it.

The Church reads this passage as a word of hope for those who suffer for the faith. The Catechism teaches that on the last day Christ will come in glory to bring about the definitive triumph of good over evil, and that the Last Judgment will reveal that God's justice triumphs over all the injustices committed by his creatures (CCC 681, 1040). We pray for Christians who suffer in many lands, and for those who wrong them, leaving all judgment to the Lord.

John 17:1–5 · This is eternal life

Setting.

John 17 is the long prayer of Jesus at the end of the Last Supper, just before he goes out to the garden where he will be arrested (18:1). Since the sixteenth century it has often been called the “priestly prayer”, because in it Jesus consecrates himself and offers himself to the Father for his disciples (17:19).

Throughout John's Gospel “the hour” has been coming: at Cana it “has not yet come” (2:4); at the feast his enemies could not arrest him, “because his hour had not yet come” (7:30). In 12:23 Jesus says, “The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.” Now, in prayer, he hands that hour to the Father.

Structure.

  • v. 1: the hour has come: glorify your Son, that the Son may glorify you.
  • v. 2: the Son's authority, to give eternal life.
  • v. 3: what eternal life is.
  • v. 4: the work finished.
  • v. 5: the glory he had with the Father before the world existed.

Key words.

  • hōra, hour: Not sixty minutes, but the decisive time set by the Father: the hour of the cross, the resurrection and the return to the Father, seen as one.
  • doxazō, glorify: In the Old Testament God's glory is his presence made visible. In John, the glory of Jesus is revealed above all on the cross, where his love goes to the end (13:1).
  • ginōskōsin, that they may know: The verb is in a form that speaks of continuing knowledge. In the Bible to know is not only to have information but to be in a living relationship, as husband and wife know each other.

Verse by verse.

“After Jesus had spoken these words, he looked up to heaven and said, “Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you,” (17:1)

Jesus prays with his eyes lifted up, as he did at the tomb of Lazarus (11:41). The first word is “Father”, the word on which the whole prayer rests.

“And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” (17:3)

Eternal life is not only endless time after death. It begins now, in knowing God. Many commentators think the wording here, with the full name “Jesus Christ”, reflects the evangelist's own confession of faith.

“I glorified you on earth by finishing the work that you gave me to do.” (17:4)

On the cross Jesus will say, “It is finished” (19:30). The work is the Father's will, carried out to the end.

“So now, Father, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had in your presence before the world existed.” (17:5)

The Son existed with the Father before creation, as the prologue says: “In the beginning was the Word” (1:1). The Church confesses this in the Creed: “God from God, Light from Light”.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism calls this the prayer of the hour of Jesus, rightly named his “priestly” prayer, which takes in the whole economy of creation and salvation, his death and resurrection (CCC 2746–2751). Verse 3 is often quoted in Christian teaching to show that the goal of our life is communion with God. In every Holy Qurbono we are drawn into this prayer, as the priest lifts the gifts and our hearts to the Father through the Son in the Holy Spirit.

Questions people ask

Does “the only true God” mean that Jesus is not God?

No. Jesus contrasts the one true God with the false gods of the nations, and in the same breath names himself as the one sent, whom to know is eternal life. Two verses later he speaks of the glory he had with the Father “before the world existed” (17:5). The Church has always read this prayer as the Son speaking to the Father in the unity of the one God (CCC 240–242).
