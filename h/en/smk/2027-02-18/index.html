Readings

  • Epistle 2 Corinthians 6:14–18 · We are the temple of the living God

Word for the day

“For we are the temple of the living God; as God said, “I will live in them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.” 2 Corinthians 6:16

Reflection

“The Lord appeared to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre, as he sat at the entrance of his tent in the heat of the day.” Three men stand near him, and the old man runs: “he ran from the tent entrance to meet them, and bowed down to the ground… Let a little water be brought, and wash your feet, and rest yourselves under the tree. Let me bring a little bread.” He promises a little and gives a feast: cakes from three measures of choice flour, a calf “tender and good,” curds and milk, and he himself stands by them under the tree while they eat. The Fathers read this scene with wonder. St Ambrose says that Abraham saw three and worshipped one (in substance), and the Church has seen in the three guests at Mamre a foreshadowing of the Holy Trinity, painted in the famous icon of the three angels at table. The God who will one day be revealed as Father, Son and Holy Spirit came first as a guest to an old man's tent.

Paul turns the picture around: now it is God who makes his home in us. “We are the temple of the living God; as God said, ‘I will live in them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.’” A temple cannot be shared with idols, and so he warns: “Do not be mismatched with unbelievers… what fellowship is there between light and darkness?” He is not telling us to despise anyone, but not to yoke our hearts to what is opposed to Christ. And in the Gospel God is the host: a king gives a wedding banquet for his son, and when the invited guests make light of it, he fills the hall from the streets, “both good and bad.” Yet one guest has no wedding robe. St Gregory the Great says that the wedding garment is charity: we may be inside the Church and still lack love (in substance). On this Thursday, the day of the Lord's Supper, we prepare the robe of love for the banquet of the altar.

Homily outline

Theme: “We are the temple of the living God.”

God as guest at Mamre, God dwelling in his temple, God as host at the wedding feast.

1. The guests at Mamre

  • He ran to meet them.
  • A little bread, a great feast.
  • Three seen, one worshipped.
“He said, “My lord, if I find favor with you, do not pass by your servant.” Genesis 18:3
“Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it.” Hebrews 13:2

Apply: Share a meal this week with someone who is alone or a stranger.

2. The temple of the living God

  • God lives and walks among us.
  • No fellowship with idols.
  • Sons and daughters of the Lord Almighty.
“And I will walk among you, and will be your God, and you shall be my people.” Leviticus 26:12
“Do you not know that you are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit dwells in you?” 1 Corinthians 3:16

Apply: Name one idol in your life and give it up for the rest of the Fast.

3. The wedding robe

  • Those invited made light of it.
  • Good and bad gathered in.
  • The robe of charity.
“I will greatly rejoice in the Lord, my whole being shall exult in my God; for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation, he has covered me with the robe of righteousness,” Isaiah 61:10
“Above all, clothe yourselves with love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony.” Colossians 3:14

Apply: Be reconciled with one person before your next Holy Qurbono.

The God who came as a guest now dwells in us as his temple.

He invites all to his feast and clothes us in love.

Take-home resolution: Welcome every guest this Fast as if the Lord himself had come to your door.

Full homily

Dear friends, on a hot afternoon Abraham saw three men near his tent. He ran to meet them, bowed down, and said: my lord, if I find favor with you, do not pass by your servant (Genesis 18:2–3). He promised a little bread and gave a feast.

The Fathers saw in these three guests a sign of the Holy Trinity. St Ambrose says Abraham saw three and worshipped one (in substance). The God of glory came as a guest to an old man's tent.

Now God makes his home in us: we are the temple of the living God; I will live in them and walk among them (2 Corinthians 6:16). A temple must be kept free of idols.

And God invites us to his Son's wedding banquet, good and bad alike. But he asks: friend, how did you get in here without a wedding robe? (Matthew 22:12). That robe is charity. On this Thursday, let us prepare it for the banquet of the altar. Amen.

For children

Children, Abraham had three visitors, and he ran to welcome them! He gave them water to wash their feet and cooked them a big meal. When a visitor comes to your house, you can be like Abraham: say welcome, offer a chair and a glass of water.

Prayer

Lord God, you visited Abraham at Mamre as a guest, and he ran to welcome you. Make us generous to every guest and stranger. Dwell in us as in your temple, and keep our hearts free from every idol. You invite us to the wedding feast of your Son; clothe us in the robe of charity, so that we may not be found unworthy at your table. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
