Word for the day

“For it is the God who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,” who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” 2 Corinthians 4:6

Reflection

Paul describes his ministry as pure light: “we do not proclaim ourselves; we proclaim Jesus Christ as Lord and ourselves as your slaves for Jesus' sake.” The same God who said at creation, “Let light shine out of darkness”, has shone in our hearts “to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” In Denho we contemplate that face: the glory of God is not far away but shines in a human face.

In the synagogue a man with a withered hand is placed before Jesus as a test: is it lawful to cure on the sabbath? Jesus answers: you would lift your sheep out of a pit; “How much more valuable is a human being than a sheep! So it is lawful to do good on the sabbath.” He says, “Stretch out your hand,” and it is restored. Today we remember Mar Thevodosios the Abbot, who built monasteries with houses for the sick, the old and the poor beside them: prayer and works of mercy in one place. We pray for Bishop Yoohanon Mar Theodosius on his name day.

Homily outline

Theme: “It is lawful to do good on the sabbath.”

The light of God's glory in Christ's face, shining through works of mercy.

1. Light out of darkness

  • Creation's first word.
  • A new creation in our hearts.
  • The face of Jesus Christ.

“For it is the God who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,” who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” 2 Corinthians 4:6

Apply: Spend a few minutes before an icon of Christ today.

2. Not ourselves but Jesus

  • Ministers are servants.
  • Christ is Lord.
  • Boast only in the Lord.
“Thus says the LORD: Do not let the wise boast in their wisdom, do not let the mighty boast in their might, do not let the wealthy boast in their wealth;” Jeremiah 9:23
“For we do not proclaim ourselves; we proclaim Jesus Christ as Lord and ourselves as your slaves for Jesus' sake.” 2 Corinthians 4:5

Apply: Speak of Christ rather than yourself in one conversation.

3. Do good on the sabbath

  • The withered hand.
  • A human being more than a sheep.
  • Stretch out your hand.
“He has told you, O mortal, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?” Micah 6:8
“How much more valuable is a human being than a sheep! So it is lawful to do good on the sabbath.”” Matthew 12:12

Apply: Do one work of mercy this Sunday.

God's light shines in Christ's face and through our deeds.

Prayer and mercy belong together, as Mar Thevodosios showed.

Take-home resolution: Make next Sunday a day of worship and mercy.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul says: we do not proclaim ourselves; we proclaim Jesus Christ as Lord and ourselves as your slaves for Jesus' sake (2 Corinthians 4:5). The minister is a window, not a picture.

The same God who said at creation, let light shine out of darkness, has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ (2 Corinthians 4:6). In Denho we gaze at that face.

In the Gospel, they watch to see if Jesus will heal on the sabbath. He asks: which of you would not lift a sheep out of a pit? How much more valuable is a human being than a sheep! So it is lawful to do good on the sabbath (Matthew 12:12). He heals the withered hand.

Mar Thevodosios the Abbot built houses for the sick, the old and the poor beside his monastery. Prayer and mercy belong together. Let our Sundays be days of both. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus healed a man's hand on God's day. Sunday is a good day to help someone! Whom will you help this Sunday?

Prayer

God of light, you said “Let light shine out of darkness” and shone in our hearts in the face of Jesus. Let us proclaim him, not ourselves, and stretch out our hands to do good. Through the prayers of Mar Thevodosios, unite prayer and mercy in our lives. Amen.
