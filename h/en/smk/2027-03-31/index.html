Word for the day

“For mortals it is impossible, but not for God; for God all things are possible.” Mark 10:27

Reflection

On this Easter Wednesday the Gospel tells of a man who “ran up and knelt before” Jesus and asked: “Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?” He has kept the commandments since his youth, and Mark adds one of the tenderest lines in the Gospels: “Jesus, looking at him, loved him.” Out of that love comes the call: “You lack one thing; go, sell what you own, and give the money to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; then come, follow me.” But “he was shocked and went away grieving, for he had many possessions.” Clement of Alexandria, in his little book on this passage, taught that what the Lord asks us to leave is above all the grip of wealth on the soul, the passions that make us its slaves, so that riches become a servant of love and not a master (in substance). The disciples are astonished: “Then who can be saved?” And Jesus answers: “For mortals it is impossible, but not for God; for God all things are possible.” In Easter week this sentence shines with new light. Four days ago the most impossible thing of all happened: a man who had died on a cross and been laid in a tomb rose bodily from the dead. The God who did that can also loosen the grip of possessions on a human heart.

St Paul's charge to Timothy fits the same road: “I am giving you these instructions, Timothy, my child... so that by following them you may fight the good fight, having faith and a good conscience.” Faith and conscience go together like the two oars of a boat. “By rejecting conscience, certain persons have suffered shipwreck in the faith; among them are Hymenaeus and Alexander.” Paul's severe discipline, handing them over “to Satan,” has a healing purpose: “so that they may learn not to blaspheme.” The Church corrects in order to bring back, never simply to cast out. The Second Vatican Council calls conscience the most secret core and sanctuary of a person, where one is alone with God (Gaudium et Spes 16). The rich man's conscience told him the truth when Jesus spoke; his possessions told him to walk away. Easter gives us the strength to listen to the first voice. The risen Lord, who says “Peace be with you” to his frightened disciples, also gives us his Spirit to keep our consciences clear and our faith afloat.

Hevoro week continues: keep it as Easter. Go to the Holy Qurbono if you can, and keep using the Easter greeting. This week is also a good moment to make an Easter gift of what you have, as the rich man was invited to do: give something you value to someone poor, not only something you would have thrown away. Each night before sleeping, a family can make a short examination of conscience together: where did we follow Jesus today, and where did we walk away? Then entrust everything to the God for whom all things are possible. On Sunday, New Sunday, the Church will hear how the risen Lord came through closed doors to Thomas; prepare to meet him there with your own questions.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today a man runs to Jesus, kneels and asks: good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life? (Mark 10:17). And Mark tells us: Jesus, looking at him, loved him (Mark 10:21).

Out of that love comes a hard call: go, sell what you own, and give the money to the poor... then come, follow me. The man went away grieving, for he had many possessions (Mark 10:21–22). Possessions are good, but they can take hold of the heart.

The disciples ask: then who can be saved? Jesus answers: for mortals it is impossible, but not for God; for God all things are possible (Mark 10:26–27). Four days ago we celebrated the most impossible thing of all: the Lord rose bodily from the dead. The God who did that can set our hearts free.

St Paul tells Timothy to fight the good fight, having faith and a good conscience (1 Timothy 1:18–19). This Easter week, let us give something we value to the poor, examine our conscience each night, and trust the God for whom nothing is impossible. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! A man asked Jesus how to go to heaven. Jesus looked at him with love and said: share what you have with the poor and follow me. The man went away sad because he did not want to share. But you can be different! Jesus looks at you with love too. What can you share this week with someone who has less?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you looked on the rich man and loved him, and you look on us with the same love. Loosen the grip of our possessions on our hearts, and give us courage to follow you. Keep our consciences clear and our faith whole, so that we may fight the good fight to the end. You rose from the dead, for whom all things are possible: do in us what we cannot do alone. You live and reign for ever. Amen.
