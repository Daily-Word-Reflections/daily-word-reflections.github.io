Readings

Word for the day

“He said, “Lord, I believe.” And he worshiped him.” John 9:38

Reflection

Today we hear the last lines of St Paul's Letter to the Ephesians, and they are not about doctrine but about a friend. “So that you also may know how I am and what I am doing, Tychicus will tell you everything. He is a dear brother and a faithful minister in the Lord. I am sending him to you for this very purpose, to let you know how we are, and to encourage your hearts.” Tychicus is one of those names we pass over quickly. He carried Paul's letters over sea and road, he brought news from the prison, and he sat with the believers to strengthen them. He was not one of the Twelve, and no great church is named after him, yet the Church would not have grown without people like him. The Apostles' Fast honours the Apostles, and with them all the quiet helpers of the gospel: the catechist who teaches on Sunday mornings, the sacristan who opens the church in the rain, the friend who visits the sick. Paul's blessing reaches all of them: “Peace be to the whole community, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Grace be with all who have an undying love for our Lord Jesus Christ.”

The Gospel brings us to the end of the long story of the man born blind. Jesus had made mud, anointed his eyes and sent him to wash in the pool of Siloam, and he came back seeing. Instead of rejoicing, his questioners argued, his parents grew afraid, and in the end he was driven out. Then comes a beautiful line: “Jesus heard that they had driven him out, and when he found him, he said, ‘Do you believe in the Son of Man?’” The one whom others pushed out, Jesus goes looking for. The man answers with an open heart: “And who is he, sir? Tell me, so that I may believe in him.” Jesus said to him, “You have seen him, and the one speaking with you is he.” And the man's eyes, which had first seen the light of day, now see the Lord: “Lord, I believe.” And he worshiped him. Through the chapter his faith has grown step by step: he first spoke of “the man called Jesus”, then called him a prophet, then said he must be from God, and now he kneels. St Augustine saw in this man a picture of the catechumen: anointed, sent to the water, washed, and given sight, so that he could worship (Tractates on John 44, in substance). The early Church called Baptism illumination, as St Justin Martyr records (First Apology 61), and every baptized Christian is that man, given eyes to see the Lord.

The last verses turn the light on another kind of blindness. “I came into this world for judgment so that those who do not see may see, and those who do see may become blind.” Some of the Pharisees near him heard this and said to him, “Surely we are not blind, are we?” Jesus said to them, “If you were blind, you would not have sin. But now that you say, ‘We see,’ your sin remains.” This is not a judgement on a whole people; the evangelist is showing a danger that lives in every religious heart, including ours. The person who knows he is blind can be healed; the person who insists that he sees everything clearly shuts the door on the doctor. A fast is a good time to admit what we cannot see: our own faults, the needs of others at our own table, the hand of God in a hard year. Tomorrow we keep the birth of John the Baptist, who would one day say of Jesus that he must increase. To say “Lord, I believe”, and to kneel, is the beginning of that decrease, and the beginning of true sight.

Homily outline

Theme: “Lord, I believe.”

On Wednesday of the fifth week after Pentecost, in the Apostles' Fast: Tychicus the faithful minister, the man born blind who comes to worship, and the blindness that says “We see”.

1. “Who is he, sir?”

  • Driven out by others, found by Jesus.
  • A seeker's honest question.
  • Faithful helpers like Tychicus who bring the word.
““Come,” my heart says, “seek his face!” Your face, Lord, do I seek.” Psalm 27:8
“So faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ.” Romans 10:17

Apply: Bring the word of God to one person who is searching, as Tychicus brought Paul's letter.

2. “Lord, I believe”

  • From “the man called Jesus” to worship.
  • Baptism as illumination.
  • Faith that kneels.
“O come, let us worship and bow down, let us kneel before the Lord, our Maker!” Psalm 95:6
“Thomas answered him, “My Lord and my God!”” John 20:28

Apply: Kneel in silence for a few minutes before the tabernacle this week and renew your act of faith.

3. “Now that you say, ‘We see’”

  • The danger of the religious heart.
  • The doctor cannot heal one who will not be seen.
  • The fast as a time to admit our blindness.
“Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped;” Isaiah 35:5
“If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.” 1 John 1:8

Apply: Make a good confession before the feast of Sts Peter and Paul.

Jesus seeks out the one who is driven out, and leads him from sight to faith and from faith to worship.

Only the one who admits his blindness can be given sight.

Take-home resolution: Admit what you cannot see, kneel before the Lord, and say: Lord, I believe.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul ends his Letter to the Ephesians by sending a friend: Tychicus, a dear brother and a faithful minister in the Lord (Ephesians 6:21). Paul sends him to let you know how we are, and to encourage your hearts (Ephesians 6:22). The Church grows through the great Apostles, and also through quiet helpers like Tychicus. Every parish has them, and we thank God for them.

In the Gospel, the man born blind has been healed, but instead of rejoicing with him, people argue and throw him out. Then Jesus heard that they had driven him out, and when he found him, he said, Do you believe in the Son of Man? (John 9:35). The man answers: Tell me, so that I may believe in him (John 9:36). And when Jesus shows himself, he said, Lord, I believe. And he worshiped him (John 9:38).

Then Jesus speaks of another blindness. Some said: Surely we are not blind, are we? (John 9:40). And Jesus answers that if they admitted their blindness, they could be healed; but now that you say, We see, your sin remains (John 9:41). The most dangerous blindness is the one that refuses to admit it is blind.

In this Apostles' Fast, let us ask the Lord to show us what we do not see, and let us go to confession. Let us look for those who are left out, as Jesus did. And let us say with our whole heart, and with our knees: Lord, I believe. Amen.

For children

Children, close your eyes for a moment. Now open them. What do you see? The man in today's Gospel could not see anything from the day he was born. Jesus healed him, and then Jesus came to find him again. When the man understood who Jesus was, he said: Lord, I believe! and he knelt down. When you come into the church and see the tabernacle, you can kneel and whisper the same words: Jesus, I believe in you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, light of the world, you went looking for the man whom others had driven out, and you opened the eyes of his heart to know you. Find us when we are pushed aside, and lead us from faith to worship. Take from us the pride that says “We see” and keeps us blind. Bless all the quiet ministers of your gospel, like Tychicus, who carry your word and encourage hearts. Grant us an undying love for you, and peace to our whole community. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
