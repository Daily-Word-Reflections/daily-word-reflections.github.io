Word for the day

“And one does not presume to take this honor, but takes it only when called by God, just as Aaron was.” Hebrews 5:4

Reflection

Today is the first day of July, the Syriac month of Tammuz, and the Panchangam begins it by remembering a priest: Aaron, the brother of Moses and the first High Priest of Israel. He did not push himself forward. The Epistle says so plainly: “And one does not presume to take this honor, but takes it only when called by God, just as Aaron was.” At Sinai the Lord said to Moses: “Then bring near to you your brother Aaron, and his sons with him, from among the Israelites, to serve me as priests.” The calling came first; the vestments, the oil and the altar followed. And the vestments themselves carried a lesson. On the shoulders of the High Priest were two stones engraved with the names of the sons of Israel: “Aaron shall bear their names before the Lord on his two shoulders for remembrance.” On the breastpiece over his heart were the same names, “for a continual remembrance before the Lord.” A priest in the Bible is never a man standing alone. He carries a whole people on his shoulders and on his heart.

Then the Epistle says something that may surprise us. The high priest “is able to deal gently with the ignorant and wayward, since he himself is subject to weakness; and because of this he must offer sacrifice for his own sins as well as for those of the people.” Aaron's own story shows it. While Moses was on the mountain, the people gathered around Aaron and asked him to make gods for them, and he fashioned a golden calf (Exodus 32:1–4). When Moses questioned him, Aaron answered with excuses (Exodus 32:21–24). The Bible does not hide the failing of its first High Priest. Yet the same Aaron, when a plague broke out in the camp, took his censer at the word of Moses and ran into the middle of the assembly, and the text says: “He stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stopped.” A sinner standing between death and life with incense in his hand: that is the picture of a priest. God himself confirmed the choice when the leaders of the tribes laid their staffs before him, and “the staff of Aaron for the house of Levi had sprouted. It put forth buds, produced blossoms, and bore ripe almonds.” At the end, on Mount Hor, Moses placed Aaron's vestments on his son Eleazar, so that the priesthood passed on (Numbers 20:25–28).

All of this was a shadow, and the Letter to the Hebrews shows where the light falls. Aaron had to offer sacrifice for his own sins before he could offer it for others. Jesus Christ, our great High Priest, “has been tested as we are, yet without sin,” so that we may “approach the throne of grace with boldness, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need” (Hebrews 4:15–16). The sacrifices of the old covenant had to be repeated again and again; of Christ the letter says that he “has no need to offer sacrifices day after day, first for his own sins, and then for those of the people; this he did once for all when he offered himself” (Hebrews 7:27). The Church therefore does not think of her priests as men who add something to the one sacrifice of the Cross. They serve in the name and in the person of Christ, so that the one offering made once for all is made present on the altar for every generation (Catechism 1545, in substance). We thank God for our bishops, priests and deacons, and we ask ourselves how often we pray for them by name.

St Luke begins his Gospel inside this very priesthood. “In the days of King Herod of Judea, there was a priest named Zechariah, who belonged to the priestly order of Abijah. His wife was a descendant of Aaron, and her name was Elizabeth.” Aaron's line, after more than a thousand years, has reached the threshold of the New Testament. Zechariah and Elizabeth were righteous, advanced in years and without a child. Zechariah “was chosen by lot, according to the custom of the priesthood, to enter the sanctuary of the Lord and offer incense.” Then Luke adds a sentence that is easy to miss: “Now at the time of the incense offering, the whole assembly of the people was praying outside.” The priest is inside, the people are outside, and the smoke of the incense rises between them, as the psalmist had prayed: “Let my prayer be counted as incense before you” (Psalm 141:2). It is at that hour of prayer, in that meeting of priest and people, that the angel appears and the new age begins. The same shape is in every Holy Qurbono: the priest at the altar, the people in prayer, the incense rising, and heaven opening above them. As the month begins, let us pray that our priests may be faithful to the call by which God took them from among us, and let us not stay outside the church even when we sit inside it, but pray with them.

Homily outline

Theme: “And one does not presume to take this honor, but takes it only when called by God, just as Aaron was.”

On Thursday of the sixth week after Pentecost, the memory of Aaron the High Priest: the call of God, the priest who knows weakness, the one High Priest, and the prayer that rises like incense.

1. Called by God

  • Aaron was brought near by the Lord.
  • No one takes the honour for himself.
  • The names of the people on his shoulders and heart.
“Then bring near to you your brother Aaron, and his sons with him, from among the Israelites, to serve me as priests” Exodus 28:1
“You did not choose me but I chose you.” John 15:16

Apply: Thank God for the call by which one priest was taken from among us, and pray for him by name this week.

2. A priest who knows weakness

  • The golden calf and the excuses.
  • The censer between the dead and the living.
  • Jesus, tested yet without sin.
“He stood between the dead and the living; and the plague was stopped.” Numbers 16:48
“For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but we have one who in every respect has been tested as we are, yet without sin.” Hebrews 4:15

Apply: When you see the failing of a priest or of anyone who serves, pray for him before you judge him.

3. Prayer that rises like incense

  • Zechariah inside, the people outside.
  • The whole assembly was praying.
  • The prayers of the saints in golden bowls.
“Let my prayer be counted as incense before you, and the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.” Psalm 141:2
“each holding a harp and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints.” Revelation 5:8

Apply: At the next Holy Qurbono, when the incense rises, offer to God the names of those you carry in prayer.

God calls his priests from among the people, and he works through them in spite of their weakness, because the one High Priest is Jesus Christ.

The prayer of the people rises with the priest's offering; no one in the church should stay outside.

Take-home resolution: Pray for your priests by name, pray with them at the altar, and let your prayer rise like incense.

Full homily

Dear friends, today is the first day of July, and the Panchangam begins the month by remembering Aaron the High Priest, the brother of Moses. The Letter to the Hebrews says that no one takes the honour of the priesthood for himself: one takes it only when called by God, just as Aaron was (Hebrews 5:4). God brought Aaron near and gave him vestments with the names of the twelve tribes on his shoulders and over his heart, so that he would never forget the people he served.

Aaron was not perfect. The Epistle says that a high priest is subject to weakness and must offer sacrifice for his own sins as well as for those of the people (Hebrews 5:2–3). Aaron made the golden calf. Yet when the plague came he took his censer and ran into the middle of the assembly, and he stood between the dead and the living, and the plague was stopped (Numbers 16:48). God works through weak people who stand for the others.

Jesus Christ is the great High Priest who was tested as we are, yet without sin (Hebrews 4:15). He offered himself once for all (Hebrews 7:27). Every priest who serves at our altar serves in his name. In the Gospel Zechariah, a priest of Aaron's line, offers incense, and the whole assembly of the people is praying outside (Luke 1:10). The priest and the people pray together.

So today let us pray for our bishops, priests and deacons by name, and let us pray with them at every Holy Qurbono, so that our prayer rises like incense. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what a priest is? A priest is a person whom God chooses to be a special helper for his people. A long time ago Aaron, the brother of Moses, was the first High Priest. He wore a beautiful breastpiece with twelve stones, and on every stone there was a name, so that he carried all the families of Israel on his heart. Today our priests also carry us on their hearts. Say a little prayer for your parish priest today, and when you see him, say thank you!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, great High Priest, tested as we are yet without sin, you offered yourself once for all and you ever live to make intercession for us. We thank you for Aaron, whom you called from among his people to stand before you with their names on his heart, and we ask that you keep our bishops, priests and deacons faithful to the call by which you took them from among us. Make them gentle with the ignorant and the wayward, since they too are subject to weakness. Let our prayer rise before you like incense, and let none of your people stay outside when your holy mysteries are celebrated. At the beginning of this new month bless our homes and our parish, and bring us at last to the sanctuary of heaven, where you live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
