Word for the day

“Then God said to him in the dream, “Yes, I know that you did this in the integrity of your heart; furthermore it was I who kept you from sinning against me. Therefore I did not let you touch her.” Genesis 20:6

Reflection

Abraham, the friend of God who had pleaded so boldly for Sodom, now behaves like a frightened man. Living as a foreigner in Gerar, he says of Sarah, “She is my sister,” and King Abimelech takes her. It is a half-truth told out of fear, and it puts at risk the very promise God has just renewed: that Sarah herself will bear Isaac. God steps in, not to Abraham, but to the pagan king in a dream: “You are about to die because of the woman whom you have taken; for she is a married woman.” Abimelech protests his innocence, and God agrees: “Yes, I know that you did this in the integrity of your heart; furthermore it was I who kept you from sinning against me.” Two things stand out. The foreign king has a more honest conscience in this story than the father of believers. And God does not discard his flawed servant: “return the man’s wife; for he is a prophet, and he will pray for you and you shall live.” God guards his promise even when his friend fails.

Paul says that Abimelech's kind of honesty is no accident: “When Gentiles, who do not possess the law, do instinctively what the law requires… They show that what the law requires is written on their hearts, to which their own conscience also bears witness.” And “it is not the hearers of the law who are righteous in God’s sight, but the doers.” Abraham knew God's word, yet in fear he hid the truth. Jesus asks for the opposite courage: “everyone who acknowledges me before others, the Son of Man also will acknowledge before the angels of God.” Fear makes us disown what we belong to, as Abraham disowned his wife. But Jesus promises help: “the Holy Spirit will teach you at that very hour what you ought to say.” He also warns that “whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven.” St Augustine taught that this sin is the hardness of heart that refuses to the end the forgiveness the Spirit offers (in substance). No one who fears having committed it has committed it; the door of mercy is open to all who knock.

Homily outline

Theme: “It was I who kept you from sinning against me.”

Abraham's fearful half-truth, the honest conscience of a stranger, and the courage to acknowledge Christ.

1. “She is my sister”

  • Fear in a foreign land.
  • A half-truth that endangers the promise.
  • God guards Sarah.
“Lying lips are an abomination to the Lord, but those who act faithfully are his delight.” Proverbs 12:22
“Do not lie to one another, seeing that you have stripped off the old self with its practices” Colossians 3:9

Apply: Examine this week where you use half-truths, and confess them.

2. Written on their hearts

  • The integrity of Abimelech.
  • Doers, not hearers only.
  • Conscience bears witness.
“I the Lord test the mind and search the heart, to give to all according to their ways, according to the fruit of their doings.” Jeremiah 17:10
“whenever our hearts condemn us; for God is greater than our hearts, and he knows everything.” 1 John 3:20

Apply: Thank God tonight for one time he kept you from sin without your knowing.

3. Acknowledge me

  • Before others.
  • The Spirit teaches what to say.
  • The door of mercy is open.
“You are my witnesses, says the Lord, and my servant whom I have chosen,” Isaiah 43:10
“because if you confess with your lips that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.” Romans 10:9

Apply: Make one public sign of your faith this week without shame.

God guards his promise even when his friends fail.

The Spirit gives truthful lips and brave hearts to those who ask.

Take-home resolution: Tell the whole truth and acknowledge Christ openly for the rest of the Fast.

Full homily

Dear friends, Abraham, the friend of God, became afraid in Gerar and said of Sarah: she is my sister (Genesis 20:2). A half-truth told in fear put God's promise at risk.

But God guarded his promise. He spoke to King Abimelech in a dream: yes, I know that you did this in the integrity of your heart; furthermore it was I who kept you from sinning against me (Genesis 20:6). And he still called Abraham a prophet who would pray for the king. God does not discard his weak servants.

Paul says the law is written on the hearts of Gentiles too, and their conscience bears witness. It is not the hearers of the law who are righteous in God's sight, but the doers (Romans 2:13, 15).

Jesus asks for courage: everyone who acknowledges me before others, the Son of Man also will acknowledge before the angels of God. And he promises: the Holy Spirit will teach you at that very hour what you ought to say (Luke 12:8, 12). As the third week of the Fast ends, let us ask the Spirit for truthful lips and brave hearts. Amen.

For children

Children, even Abraham was sometimes afraid and did not tell the whole truth. But God still loved him and helped him. Jesus wants us to be brave and tell the truth, and not be ashamed of him. The Holy Spirit will help you. Try saying a prayer before lunch at school this week!

Prayer

Lord God, you guarded Sarah and your promise when Abraham failed through fear, and you kept Abimelech from sin. Thank you for the times you have kept us from sinning against you without our knowing. Write your law ever more deeply on our hearts, make us doers of your word, and give us courage to acknowledge your Son before others. Holy Spirit, teach us what to say. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
