Word for the day

“Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you. I do not give to you as the world gives. Do not let your hearts be troubled, and do not let them be afraid.” John 14:27

Reflection

Yesterday, on the Third Sunday after Pentecost, we heard Jesus send out the Twelve two by two, and say “I am the bread of life.” Today, at the beginning of a new week, the readings turn to the inner life of the community he sends: how it prays, how its members bear themselves, and what gift holds it together. St Paul writes to Timothy: “I desire, then, that in every place the men should pray, lifting up holy hands without anger or argument.” Lifting up the hands was the ancient posture of prayer, and it is still the priest's posture at the altar in our Holy Qurbono, as he stands facing the east with the people behind him. But Paul adds a condition: the hands must be holy, and the heart must be without anger and quarrelling. A man who has just shouted at his wife, a member of a parish committee who has come straight from an argument, cannot lift up holy hands until he has made peace. Jesus said the same: leave your gift at the altar and first be reconciled to your brother. That is why, before the great prayer of offering, the peace is passed from the altar to the people, hand to hand, in every Holy Qurbono.

Paul then turns to the women of the community, asking them to dress “modestly and decently in suitable clothing, not with their hair braided, or with gold, pearls, or expensive clothes, but with good works, as is proper for women who profess reverence for God.” In a Kerala of gold-laden weddings and competitive display, the word is not out of date, and it is addressed to men as much as to women: what makes a Christian beautiful is not what one wears but what one does. The verses that follow are among the hardest in the New Testament for modern ears: “Let a woman learn in silence with full submission. I permit no woman to teach or to have authority over a man.” The Church reads them within the whole of Scripture and her living tradition. Paul is writing about a particular community in Ephesus, troubled by false teachers who had found a hearing in some households (1 Timothy 1:3–7; 2 Timothy 3:6–7), and about order in its public worship. He is not denying what Scripture says from its first page: “So God created humankind in his image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them.” Man and woman are equal in dignity (Catechism 369). The Church keeps the apostolic order by which the sacred ministry of priests, the preaching of the homily at the Qurbono among them, is entrusted to ordained men; and she has always rejoiced in women who teach the faith: mothers and grandmothers, catechists, religious sisters, and women Doctors of the Church such as St Catherine of Siena, St Teresa of Avila and St Thérèse of Lisieux. Paul's last line, that the woman “will be saved through childbearing”, was read by some ancient interpreters as pointing to the one Childbirth through which salvation came to all: the Son born of the Virgin Mary, the new Eve.

The Gospel gives the key to reading every hard page: the Holy Spirit. “I have said these things to you while I am still with you. But the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you everything, and remind you of all that I have said to you.” The Spirit poured out at Pentecost is the Church's teacher and memory: he guides her to understand the Scriptures as a whole, without tearing out one verse against another. And the gift the Spirit brings is peace: “Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you. I do not give to you as the world gives. Do not let your hearts be troubled, and do not let them be afraid.” The world gives peace as a truce between enemies, a quiet that lasts until the next quarrel. Christ gives peace as a share in his own life with the Father, a peace that holds even in trouble. St Augustine said that Christ leaves us peace in this world, so that we may overcome the enemy, and will give us his own peace in the world to come, where there will be no enemy left (Tractates on John 77, in substance). This Monday morning, before work and school, let us lift up holy hands, let go of yesterday's anger, and receive the peace that the world cannot give.

Homily outline

Theme: “Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you.”

On Monday of the third week after Pentecost: holy hands lifted without anger, a Christian beauty made of good works, and the Advocate who teaches us and brings the peace of Christ.

1. Holy hands without anger

  • The ancient posture of prayer.
  • No prayer with a quarrel in our hands.
  • The peace passed at the Holy Qurbono.
“Let my prayer be counted as incense before you, and the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.” Psalm 141:2
“Do not worry about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God.” Philippians 4:6

Apply: Make it a family rule this week: no night prayer until every quarrel of the day has been made up.

2. Adorned with good works

  • Beauty in deeds, not display.
  • Man and woman equal in dignity.
  • The women who teach us the faith.
“Charm is deceitful, and beauty is vain, but a woman who fears the Lord is to be praised.” Proverbs 31:30
“rather, let your adornment be the inner self with the lasting beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which is very precious in God's sight.” 1 Peter 3:4

Apply: Thank, in words or in writing, one woman who taught you the faith: a mother, a grandmother, a catechist or a sister.

3. The Advocate and the peace of Christ

  • The Spirit teaches and reminds.
  • Scripture read as a whole, in the Church.
  • A peace the world cannot give.
“the Lord lift up his countenance upon you, and give you peace.” Numbers 6:26
“When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth;” John 16:13

Apply: Before reading a hard passage of Scripture, pray: Come, Holy Spirit, teach me; and read it with the Church's help.

Prayer needs holy hands and a heart without anger; Christian beauty is made of good works.

The Holy Spirit teaches the Church to read God's word as a whole, and Christ gives the peace the world cannot give.

Take-home resolution: Make peace before you pray, let the Spirit teach you, and receive the peace of Christ without fear.

Full homily

Dear friends, today St Paul tells us how to pray: I desire, then, that in every place the men should pray, lifting up holy hands without anger or argument (1 Timothy 2:8). Our hands must be holy and our hearts must be at peace. We cannot shout at our family and then lift up our hands to God as if nothing had happened. That is why, in every Holy Qurbono, we give one another the peace before the great prayer of offering.

Paul also asks Christians not to show off with gold and expensive clothes, but to be adorned with good works. That is a word for all of us, in a time of costly weddings and display. Some of Paul's words to the women of Ephesus are hard to hear today. The Church reads them in the light of all Scripture, which teaches that God created man and woman equal in dignity, in his own image. And the Church has always honoured the women who teach the faith: our mothers, our catechists, our sisters, and the women saints.

In the Gospel Jesus promises: the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you everything, and remind you of all that I have said to you (John 14:26). The Holy Spirit helps the Church understand God's word. And Jesus gives a gift: Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you. I do not give to you as the world gives (John 14:27).

So this week, let us lift up holy hands, let go of anger, make peace before we pray, and let the Holy Spirit teach us. And let us not be afraid, because the peace of Christ is stronger than any trouble. Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever said your prayers while you were still angry with your brother or sister? It is hard to pray then, isn't it? St Paul says we should lift up our hands to God without anger. So first, say sorry and make peace. Jesus gives us a special gift: his peace. It is not just being quiet; it is a happy, calm feeling inside, because we know Jesus loves us. Tonight, before you pray, give a hug to anyone you were angry with today.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, before you returned to the Father you left us your peace and promised the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, to teach us everything and remind us of all you said. Take away from our hearts anger and quarrelling, so that we may lift up holy hands in prayer. Clothe us, men and women alike, not with the display of the world but with good works. Let your Spirit lead your Church into all the truth, and let your peace keep our hearts from fear. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
