Word for the day

“Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.” 1 Thessalonians 5:16–18

Reflection

Paul ends his first letter with a burst of short commands, like a spiritual rule of life: respect those who labour among you; be at peace; admonish the idle, encourage the faint-hearted, help the weak; do not repay evil for evil. “Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.” Do not quench the Spirit; test everything; hold fast to what is good.

Jesus speaks with startling seriousness about sin: better to lose a hand, a foot or an eye than to be thrown into the fire. He does not mean self-mutilation, but radical decisiveness: cut off whatever leads you or a little one into sin. Then a gentle image: “Salt is good… Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another.” Salt preserves and gives flavour; in the Old Testament every offering was seasoned with “the salt of the covenant”. Our fast is a season to become salt again.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul gives the Thessalonians a list of short commands: respect those who labour among you; be at peace among yourselves; encourage the faint-hearted, help the weak, be patient with all of them. Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you (1 Thessalonians 5:12–18).

Rejoice, pray, give thanks: three practices that can transform a life. Not rejoice when things go well, but always. Not pray when in trouble, but without ceasing. Not give thanks for good things only, but in all circumstances.

Paul adds: do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise the words of prophets, but test everything; hold fast to what is good; abstain from every form of evil (1 Thessalonians 5:19–22).

In the Gospel Jesus speaks strongly: if any of you put a stumbling block before one of these little ones who believe in me, it would be better for you if a great millstone were hung around your neck and you were thrown into the sea (Mark 9:42). Then he says: if your hand causes you to stumble, cut it off (Mark 9:43). These are images of radical seriousness about sin.

Jesus ends: salt is good. Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another (Mark 9:50). Salt gives taste and preserves from decay. A Christian gives flavour to the world and preserves it from corruption.

During this fast, let us remove one stumbling block: a bad habit, a source of temptation, an unhealthy relationship. And let us practise Paul's three words each day: rejoice, pray, give thanks. Amen.

For children

Children, St Paul says: be happy, pray always, and say thank you to God. Try all three today!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, teach us to rejoice always, pray without ceasing and give thanks in all things. Give us courage to cut away what leads us into sin, and make us salt that brings peace. Amen.
