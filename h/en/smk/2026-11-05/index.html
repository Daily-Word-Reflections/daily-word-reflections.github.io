Word for the day

“Now, discipline always seems painful rather than pleasant at the time, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.” Hebrews 12:11

Reflection

Hebrews speaks to Christians tired of struggling. Their trials are not a sign that God has abandoned them but that he treats them as sons and daughters: “the Lord disciplines those whom he loves.” Every good parent corrects a child; God trains us “for our good, in order that we may share his holiness.” What hurts now bears fruit later: the peaceful fruit of righteousness.

In the Gospel Jesus reveals his own sonship. The Son does nothing on his own, “but only what he sees the Father doing.” The Father loves the Son and shows him everything; he gives him power to give life and to judge, “so that all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father.” Our sonship is a share in his: like him, we learn to watch the Father and do what he does.

Homily outline

Theme: “The Lord disciplines those whom he loves.”

Sons and daughters in the Son.

1. Discipline is love

  • Proverbs already called correction a father's love.
  • Its fruit comes later: peace and righteousness.
“My child, do not despise the LORD's discipline or be weary of his reproof, for the LORD reproves the one he loves, as a father the son in whom he delights.” Proverbs 3:11–12
“Now, discipline always seems painful rather than pleasant at the time, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.” Hebrews 12:11

Apply: Accept one correction today without excuses.

2. The beloved Son

  • The psalm announces the Son of God.
  • Jesus does only what he sees the Father doing.
“I will tell of the decree of the LORD: He said to me, “You are my son; today I have begotten you.”” Psalm 2:7
“Very truly, I tell you, the Son can do nothing on his own, but only what he sees the Father doing; for whatever the Father does, the Son does likewise.” John 5:19

Apply: Ask before each decision: what would the Father do?

3. Honour the Son

  • God shares his glory with no idol.
  • He shares it with the Son, one with him.
“I am the LORD, that is my name; my glory I give to no other,” Isaiah 42:8
“so that all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. Anyone who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him.” John 5:23

Apply: Bow your head at the name of Jesus today.

Trials are the Father's training.

In the Son we learn to be children.

Take-home resolution: Thank God each night for one hard thing that shaped you.

Full homily

Dear friends, Hebrews speaks to Christians tired of struggling. Their trials are not a sign that God has abandoned them but that he treats them as sons and daughters: the Lord disciplines those whom he loves (Hebrews 12:6). Discipline is painful at the time, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness.

In the Gospel Jesus reveals his relationship with the Father: the Son can do nothing on his own, but only what he sees the Father doing; the Father loves the Son. All should honour the Son just as they honour the Father (John 5:19–23).

The Son lived in complete dependence on the Father, and we are called to live the same way. Place one trial that tires you into the Father's hands now: what do you want to teach me through this? Amen.

For children

Children, good parents sometimes correct us because they love us. Today accept their advice with a smile!

Prayer

Father, you train us as your children. Help us to accept your discipline with trust, to watch what you do as your Son did, and to honour him in all things. Amen.
