Word for the day

“Only, live your life in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ” Philippians 1:27

Reflection

Yesterday the Church kept Pentecost, and today the Panchangam opens a new page with a new heading: the season of Pentecost. For ten days after the Ascension the Church waited, as the apostles waited in the upper room with the Mother of Jesus; in those days our Panchangam even asks that weddings not be celebrated. Now the waiting is over. The Spirit has come, and a Church that has received the Spirit cannot stay indoors. The first weekday of the new season gives us two readings that answer one question: what does a life filled with the Spirit look like on an ordinary Monday? St Paul answers from prison, writing to the Christians of Philippi: “Only, live your life in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ, so that, whether I come and see you or am absent and hear about you, I will know that you are standing firm in one spirit, striving side by side with one mind for the faith of the gospel, and are in no way intimidated by your opponents.” Philippi was a Roman colony, proud of its citizenship, and the verb Paul uses for “live your life” is the verb for living as a citizen. He is saying: you belong to a city whose king is Christ; let your conduct show your true citizenship.

In the Gospel Jesus sends out the Twelve for the first time, and his instructions are startling. “Go nowhere among the Gentiles, and enter no town of the Samaritans, but go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.” This is not a narrowing of God's love. God keeps faith with the people he chose first, and the good news goes to them first; after the Resurrection the same Lord will send the same apostles to “all nations.” Then comes their task: “As you go, proclaim the good news, ‘The kingdom of heaven has come near.’ Cure the sick, raise the dead, cleanse the lepers, cast out demons. You received without payment; give without payment.” And their luggage: “Take no gold, or silver, or copper in your belts, no bag for your journey, or two tunics, or sandals, or a staff.” St John Chrysostom comments that the Lord stripped them of every resource so that the power that worked through them would plainly be his, and so that their poverty itself would preach (Homilies on Matthew 32, in substance). They are to enter a house with a greeting of peace, and if the house is worthy, their peace will rest upon it. Even our Holy Qurbono keeps this pattern: again and again the priest turns to the people and gives them the peace, and the Church goes out from the altar carrying it.

Paul adds a word that we would rather not hear: “For he has graciously granted you the privilege not only of believing in Christ, but of suffering for him as well.” A Spirit-filled life is not a life without opponents; it is a life that is not frightened by them. The Spirit who came at Pentecost did not remove the apostles from danger; he took away their fear. So the new season begins with a simple examination. Am I living as a citizen of Christ's kingdom at my desk, in my kitchen, on my phone? Am I standing side by side with my parish, or standing apart and criticising? What have I received without payment: faith, Baptism, the Holy Qurbono, a family that taught me to pray? And to whom am I giving it without payment? The gifts of the Spirit are not trophies for the cupboard. They are given to be handed on, at no cost, to the next person who needs them.

Homily outline

Theme: “You received without payment; give without payment.”

On the first Monday of the season of Pentecost: citizens worthy of the gospel, standing side by side, giving as we have received.

1. Live worthy of the gospel

  • Citizens of Christ's city.
  • Conduct that shows whose we are.
  • The Spirit in ordinary days.
“He has told you, O mortal, what is good; and what does the Lord require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?” Micah 6:8
“I therefore, the prisoner in the Lord, beg you to lead a life worthy of the calling to which you have been called” Ephesians 4:1

Apply: Name one place, at work, at home or online, where your conduct does not yet match your Baptism, and change one thing there this week.

2. Side by side and unafraid

  • One spirit, one mind.
  • Not intimidated by opponents.
  • The gift of suffering for Christ.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“Now the whole group of those who believed were of one heart and soul” Acts 4:32

Apply: Make peace this week with one person in your parish or family with whom you have been at odds.

3. Received as gift, given as gift

  • Sent out with empty belts.
  • Proclaim, heal, cleanse.
  • Peace to every house.
“Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” And I said, “Here am I; send me!”” Isaiah 6:8
“It is more blessed to give than to receive.” Acts 20:35

Apply: Offer one skill or one hour each week to someone who cannot pay for it.

The Spirit who came at Pentecost sends the Church out, not with wealth, but with gifts to be given away.

A life worthy of the gospel is a life that gives what it has received, side by side with others and without fear.

Take-home resolution: Live as a citizen of Christ's kingdom, stand with your brothers and sisters, and give without payment what you received without payment.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we kept Pentecost, and today a new season begins in our Church: the season of Pentecost. The days of waiting are over. The Spirit has come, and now the Church is sent out. St Paul tells us how to live in this season: only, live your life in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ (Philippians 1:27).

Philippi was proud of being a Roman city. Paul tells its Christians that their true citizenship is in Christ's kingdom. He wants to hear that they are standing firm in one spirit, striving side by side with one mind for the faith of the gospel, and are in no way intimidated by your opponents (Philippians 1:27–28). Side by side, not each one alone; firm, not frightened.

In the Gospel Jesus sends the Twelve: proclaim the good news, cure the sick, cleanse the lepers. You received without payment; give without payment (Matthew 10:8). He sends them with nothing in their belts, so that everyone can see that the power is his and not theirs. They are to bring peace into every house they enter.

This is our task in the new season. Everything we have from God (faith, Baptism, the Holy Qurbono, the prayer our parents taught us) came to us as a gift. Let us give it away as a gift: in a visit, a lesson, a kind word, a hand held out. The Spirit is not given to be kept but to be shared. Amen.

For children

Children, yesterday was Pentecost, the birthday of the Church, when the Holy Spirit came like fire. Today Jesus sends his friends out. He told them: you received without payment; give without payment. That means: God gave you many good things for nothing, your family, your food, your faith. Now you can give for nothing too! Can you help someone today without asking for anything back?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, yesterday you poured out your Holy Spirit on your Church, and today you send us out as you sent the Twelve. Make our lives worthy of your gospel, so that all who see us may know whose kingdom we belong to. Keep us standing firm in one spirit and striving side by side for the faith, unafraid of those who oppose us. What we have received from you without payment, let us give without payment, and let your peace rest on every house we enter. With your Father and your Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
