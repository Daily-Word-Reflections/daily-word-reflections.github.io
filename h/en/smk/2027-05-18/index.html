Word for the day

“Because he himself was tested by what he suffered, he is able to help those who are being tested.” Hebrews 2:18

Reflection

Two short readings today tell one story from two sides: how far God was willing to come to find us. The Letter to the Hebrews begins with a plain fact about us: “the children share flesh and blood.” We are bodies that get tired, fall ill, grow old and die. Then it tells us what the Son of God did about it: “he himself likewise shared the same things, so that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil.” He did not rescue us from a distance, like a ship that throws a rope and sails on. He came on board. He took a body from the Virgin Mary, the same flesh and blood as ours, so that death, the one thing we all share, could become the place where he met the enemy and defeated him. “For it is clear that he did not come to help angels, but the descendants of Abraham.” God's mercy is not for some other, cleaner kind of creature. It is for us, the children of Abraham's faith, with our weak bodies and our anxious hearts.

From this Hebrews draws a conclusion that is full of comfort: “Therefore he had to become like his brothers and sisters in every respect, so that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest in the service of God, to make a sacrifice of atonement for the sins of the people. Because he himself was tested by what he suffered, he is able to help those who are being tested.” Our High Priest is not a stranger to temptation, hunger, betrayal or grief. When we bring him our struggle, we are not explaining something he cannot imagine; we are speaking to someone who has been there. In the Gospel the same mercy wears an everyday face. “Or what woman having ten silver coins, if she loses one of them, does not light a lamp, sweep the house, and search carefully until she finds it?” A silver coin of that kind was roughly a day's wage, and for a poor household one coin mattered. The house is dark, the floor is earth, and the coin is somewhere in the dust. She does not shrug and say she still has nine. She lights a lamp, she sweeps, she searches until she finds it.

St Gregory the Great read this parable as a picture of the Incarnation: the woman is the Wisdom of God, and the lamp she lights is light in a clay vessel, the divinity of the Son shining in our flesh; with it she sweeps the house of our conscience until the lost image of God is found again (Homilies on the Gospels 34, in substance). The coin, after all, bears an image, and we were made in God's image. That image can be buried in dust, but it is never erased. The parable ends in a party: “When she has found it, she calls together her friends and neighbors, saying, ‘Rejoice with me, for I have found the coin that I had lost.’ Just so, I tell you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” In this season of Pentecost, the Spirit is the lamp and the broom in the hands of the Church. Is there someone you have written off as lost, a relative, a former friend, a young person who has stopped coming to church? Do not say there are still nine. Light a lamp: pray. Sweep: make the first move. Search carefully: keep looking until the coin is found. And if you are the lost coin today, know that you are being searched for, and come to the sacrament of Confession, where heaven's joy begins.

Homily outline

Theme: “He is able to help those who are being tested.”

On Tuesday after the Sunday of Pentecost: the Son who shared our flesh and blood, the merciful High Priest, and the woman who searches until she finds.

1. He shared our flesh and blood

  • Not rescue from a distance.
  • Death met from the inside.
  • Mercy for the children of Abraham.
“It was no messenger or angel but his presence that saved them; in his love and in his pity he redeemed them” Isaiah 63:9
“And the Word became flesh and lived among us, and we have seen his glory” John 1:14

Apply: When you feel your weakness this week, tired, ill or afraid, say quietly: Lord, you know this from the inside.

2. A merciful and faithful high priest

  • Like us in every respect.
  • Tested by what he suffered.
  • Able to help us now.
“As a father has compassion for his children, so the Lord has compassion for those who fear him.” Psalm 103:13
“For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but we have one who in every respect has been tested as we are, yet without sin.” Hebrews 4:15

Apply: Go to Confession before the end of this month, and bring the temptation that troubles you most.

3. Light a lamp, sweep, search

  • One coin out of ten.
  • The image in the dust.
  • Joy in the presence of the angels.
“Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.” Psalm 119:105
“For the Son of Man came to seek out and to save the lost.” Luke 19:10

Apply: Pray by name each day this week for one person who has drifted away, and find one simple way to show that you care.

The Son of God came all the way into our flesh, our trials and our death, to find us.

Found by him, we become his lamp, searching for others until heaven rejoices.

Take-home resolution: Bring your trials to the merciful High Priest, and do not rest content while one coin is still lost.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Letter to the Hebrews tells us how far God came to find us. Since the children share flesh and blood, he himself likewise shared the same things, so that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death (Hebrews 2:14). The Son did not save us from a distance. He took our body, so that he could meet death from the inside and defeat it.

Therefore he had to become like his brothers and sisters in every respect, so that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest (Hebrews 2:17). Because he himself was tested by what he suffered, he is able to help those who are being tested (Hebrews 2:18). When we pray in temptation, we speak to one who has been there.

In the Gospel Jesus gives us a homely picture of this mercy. A woman loses one of her ten silver coins. She lights a lamp, sweeps the house, and searches carefully until she finds it (Luke 15:8). Then she calls her friends and neighbours to rejoice. Just so, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents (Luke 15:10).

St Gregory the Great saw in the lamp the divinity of Christ shining in our flesh. The Spirit now puts that lamp in our hands. Let us search for those who are lost: pray for them, make the first move, keep looking. And if we ourselves are the lost coin, let us come to Confession and let heaven rejoice. Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever lost something precious, a toy or a pencil box, and looked everywhere for it? Jesus told a story about a woman who lost one coin. She lit a lamp, swept the whole house, and looked under everything until she found it. Then she was so happy that she called all her friends! Jesus says God looks for us like that when we go away from him, and heaven is full of joy when we come back.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you shared our flesh and blood and through your death you destroyed the power of the one who held us in fear. You became like us in every respect, a merciful and faithful High Priest, tested as we are tested. Help us in our hour of trial. Like the woman who lit a lamp and swept her house, you search for every one of us who is lost. By your Holy Spirit make us lamps in your hands, to seek out those who have strayed and to rejoice with heaven over every sinner who returns. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
