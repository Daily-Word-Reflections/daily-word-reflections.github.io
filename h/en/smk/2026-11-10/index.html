Word for the day

“So the last will be first, and the first will be last.” Matthew 20:16

Reflection

Paul's words to Christian slaves sound strange to us, but their core is clear: whatever our station, we serve so that “the name of God and the teaching may not be blasphemed”, and we serve a believing master “all the more” because he is a brother. Work done well and honestly is a witness to Christ in every office, field and kitchen.

In the parable the owner goes out at dawn, at nine, at noon, at three and at five to call workers into his vineyard, and pays the last the same as the first. Those who worked all day grumble. “Are you envious because I am generous?” God's call comes at every hour, and his reward is not wages but himself. Samuel heard the call as a boy; Leo the Great served as Pope in hard times. Each answered when called.

Homily outline

Theme: “The last will be first.”

The generous Lord of the vineyard.

1. Called at every hour

  • Seek the Lord while he may be found.
  • The owner calls workers even at the last hour.
“Seek the LORD while he may be found, call upon him while he is near;” Isaiah 55:6
“and he said to them, “You also go into the vineyard, and I will pay you whatever is right.”” Matthew 20:4

Apply: Answer one call of God you have been postponing.

2. God is good to all

  • His compassion is over all he has made.
  • Envy closes our eyes to grace.
“The LORD is good to all, and his compassion is over all that he has made.” Psalm 145:9
“Am I not allowed to do what I choose with what belongs to me? Or are you envious because I am generous?” Matthew 20:15

Apply: Thank God for a blessing given to someone else.

3. Speak, Lord

  • Samuel answered as a boy.
  • In God's kingdom the last become first.
“Now the LORD came and stood there, calling as before, “Samuel! Samuel!” And Samuel said, “Speak, for your servant is listening.”” 1 Samuel 3:10
“So the last will be first, and the first will be last.” Matthew 20:16

Apply: Pray Samuel's words before you sleep tonight.

God's call and God's reward are both grace.

Let us answer gladly.

Take-home resolution: Offer your work each morning this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul's words to slaves may sound strange to us, but their core is clear: whatever our station, we serve so that the name of God and the teaching may not be blasphemed (1 Timothy 6:1). Our work is our witness.

In the parable of the labourers in the vineyard, the landowner calls workers early in the morning, at nine, at noon, at three, and even at five o'clock, and in the end gives all the same wage. Are you envious because I am generous, he asks (Matthew 20:15). God calls at every hour, and his generosity is greater than our calculations.

Today the Church remembers the prophet Samuel and Pope St Leo the Great. The boy Samuel said: speak, Lord, for your servant is listening (1 Samuel 3:10). At every age and at every hour, let this be our prayer. Amen.

For children

Children, the boy Samuel said to God: speak, I am listening. Say the same prayer tonight before you sleep.

Prayer

Lord of the vineyard, you call us at every hour and pay beyond our deserving. Free us from envy, make our work an offering, and give us Samuel's listening heart. Amen.
