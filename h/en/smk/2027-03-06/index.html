Word for the day

“I must proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God to the other cities also; for I was sent for this purpose.” Luke 4:43

Reflection

The first reading closes the week of Abraham's story with a scene of peace. Abimelech, the same king Abraham once feared, comes to him with the commander of his army and says: “God is with you in all that you do.” A pagan ruler recognizes God's presence in Abraham's life, and asks for a covenant of loyalty. There is still a dispute: Abimelech's servants have seized a well. Wells meant life in that dry land, and quarrels over water were bitter. Abraham does not fight; he sets apart seven ewe lambs as a witness that he dug the well, and the two men swear an oath. The place is named Beer-sheba, the well of the oath or the well of seven. Then, in a land that was not yet his, Abraham “planted a tamarisk tree in Beer-sheba, and called there on the name of the Lord, the Everlasting God.” A tree takes years to give shade; planting it is an act of hope. Abraham, still “an alien many days in the land of the Philistines,” lives as a guest who trusts that the Everlasting God will keep his promise.

Paul's words to Timothy are especially fitting in the Great Fast. Some false teachers, he says, “forbid marriage and demand abstinence from foods, which God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and know the truth. For everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected, provided it is received with thanksgiving; for it is sanctified by God’s word and by prayer.” Our fasting is not a rejection of God's good creation. We do not abstain from meat, fish or eggs because they are evil, but because we want to hunger more for God, to share with the poor, and to master our desires. St Basil the Great said that the true fast is to be a stranger to evil: to restrain the tongue, to put away anger, to stop lying and slander; fasting from food without fasting from sin is not enough (in substance). And when the Fast ends, we will receive God's gifts again with thanksgiving. In the Gospel, the crowds want to keep Jesus for themselves, but he says: “I must proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God to the other cities also; for I was sent for this purpose.” Our Lenten prayer is not meant to make us comfortable in one place. It sends us out, like Jesus, to bring the good news further, to the next village, the next house, the next person who has not heard.

Theme: “I must proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God to the other cities also.”

Peace at the well of Beer-sheba, the true meaning of fasting, and Jesus sent to the other cities.

  • God is with you in all that you do.
  • Seven ewe lambs as witness.
  • A tree planted in hope.

“but those who drink of the water that I will give them will never be thirsty. The water that I will give will become in them a spring of water gushing up to eternal life.” John 4:14

Apply: Take one step this week to settle a dispute peacefully.

2. Received with thanksgiving

  • Creation is good.
  • We fast to hunger for God.
  • Sanctified by word and prayer.

“give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.” 1 Thessalonians 5:18

“Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” Mark 16:15

Apply: Invite one person this week to pray with you or to come to the Holy Qurbono.

Take-home resolution: Fast with a thankful heart, make peace where you can, and share the good news with one person before Easter.

Full homily

Dear friends, Abimelech came to Abraham and said: God is with you in all that you do (Genesis 21:22). When a dispute arose over a well, Abraham did not fight; he made a covenant of peace at Beer-sheba.

Then Abraham planted a tamarisk tree in Beer-sheba, and called there on the name of the Lord, the Everlasting God (Genesis 21:33). A tree is an act of hope. Abraham lived as a guest, trusting God's promise.

Paul reminds us that everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected, provided it is received with thanksgiving (1 Timothy 4:4). We do not fast because food is evil. We fast to hunger for God, to master ourselves, and to share with the poor.

And Jesus says: I must proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God to the other cities also; for I was sent for this purpose (Luke 4:43). Let our fasting send us out to share the good news. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus did not stay in one town. He said: I must tell the good news to other towns too! Jesus wants everyone in the world to know God loves them. You can help him: tell one friend about Jesus, or invite someone to come to church with you.

Prayer

Everlasting God, Abraham called on your name under the tamarisk tree at Beer-sheba, a guest in a land that was not yet his. Make us peacemakers who settle quarrels without violence, and pilgrims who plant seeds of hope. Teach us to fast not out of contempt for your good creation but out of hunger for you, and to receive all your gifts with thanksgiving. Send us, like your Son, to bring the good news to the other cities also. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
