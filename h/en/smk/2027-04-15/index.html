Word for the day

“One who has become a priest, not through a legal requirement concerning physical descent, but through the power of an indestructible life.” Hebrews 7:16

Reflection

Christ is risen! In these weeks of Qyomtho our Church reads the Letter to the Hebrews almost every weekday, and today it asks a bold question. Israel had a priesthood given by God himself: the sons of Aaron, from the tribe of Levi, served at the altar, and “the people received the law under this priesthood.” Why, then, did the Scriptures speak of “another priest arising according to the order of Melchizedek”? Jesus was not a Levite; “it is evident that our Lord was descended from Judah.” His priesthood does not rest on a family tree. He became a priest “not through a legal requirement concerning physical descent, but through the power of an indestructible life.” This is an Easter sentence. The life that the Cross could not end and the tomb could not hold is the very foundation of his priesthood. The Letter does not despise the priesthood of Aaron; that priesthood was God's gift and a preparation, and the Church sees in it a figure of what Christ fulfils (Catechism 1539–1545). St John Chrysostom, preaching on this chapter, observes that a priest who dies needs a successor, but the priest who lives for ever needs none (in substance).

The Gospel finds Jesus on the road, “teaching as he made his way to Jerusalem.” Someone asks, “Lord, will only a few be saved?” Jesus does not give a number. He turns the question from curiosity to decision: “Strive to enter through the narrow door.” A day comes when the owner of the house has shut the door, and some will knock and say, “We ate and drank with you, and you taught in our streets.” Having been near him is not yet the same as having entered by him. The warning is not for one people; it is for every believer who thinks that knowing the Lord's name or sitting at his table is a guarantee without a changed life, and so it speaks first to us who receive the Holy Qurbono. Christian writers have said that the door is narrow only for the proud; for the humble it is wide enough. Then comes the great hope of the passage: “people will come from east and west, from north and south, and will eat in the kingdom of God.” We are among those who came from the east. The faith reached our shores through the preaching of the apostle Mar Thoma, as our tradition holds, and now we too are called to the banquet.

How do we live this today? The priest who lives for ever is the priest of every Holy Qurbono. When the celebrant stands at the altar of our church, it is Christ, risen and alive, who offers and who gives himself; “he always lives to make intercession” for us (Hebrews 7:25). The “better hope, through which we approach God” is not an idea but a person. So come to the Holy Qurbono this Easter season with the readiness of one who enters a door, not only of one who watches from the street. Pray today for the priests of our eparchy and for those preparing for the priesthood, that they may serve as men who belong to the eternal priest. And choose the narrow door in small things: one honest word, one act of forgiveness, one confession made before the coming Sunday. The door is narrow because it is the shape of Christ himself, and through him the way is open to all.

Homily outline

Theme: “Through the power of an indestructible life.”

On this Easter weekday: the priest who cannot die, the narrow door, and the banquet that gathers east and west.

1. A priest who cannot die

  • Not the order of Aaron, but of Melchizedek.
  • Not physical descent, but an indestructible life.
  • He always lives to intercede for us.
“And King Melchizedek of Salem brought out bread and wine; he was priest of God Most High.” Genesis 14:18
“Consequently he is able for all time to save those who approach God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them.” Hebrews 7:25

Apply: At the Holy Qurbono this week, entrust one person you love to the intercession of Christ the High Priest.

2. The narrow door

  • “Will only a few be saved?”
  • “We ate and drank with you”: nearness is not yet entrance.
  • Narrow for the proud, wide for the humble.
“This is the gate of the Lord; the righteous shall enter through it.” Psalm 118:20
“I am the gate. Whoever enters by me will be saved, and will come in and go out and find pasture.” John 10:9

Apply: Do one humble act today that your pride has been resisting: an apology, a request for help, a confession.

3. From east and west

  • The table of the kingdom is wide.
  • We are among those who came from the east.
  • The last may be first.
“For from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations, and in every place incense is offered to my name, and a pure offering.” Malachi 1:11
“I tell you, many will come from east and west and will eat with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven.” Matthew 8:11

Apply: Invite one person who has stopped coming to church to the Holy Qurbono with you this Sunday.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today the Letter to the Hebrews tells us that Jesus is not a priest because of the family he was born into. He became a priest through the power of an indestructible life (Hebrews 7:16).

That is the Easter mystery. The life that death could not destroy is the root of his priesthood. A priest who dies needs someone to take his place; our high priest lives for ever and always intercedes for us.

In the Gospel someone asks, “Lord, will only a few be saved?” Jesus answers: strive to enter through the narrow door (Luke 13:24). Some will say, “We ate and drank with you,” and still stand outside. Being near the Lord is not yet the same as entering by him. The door is narrow for the proud and wide for the humble.

And yet the promise is wide: people will come from east and west and eat in the kingdom of God. We have come from the east. Let us enter by the door who is Christ: in humble confession, in faithful coming to the Holy Qurbono, in daily love. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is alive, and he is our priest for ever! Someone asked him how many people would go to heaven. Jesus said: try hard to go in through the narrow door. To pass through a low, narrow door you have to bend down. That is what humble people do. Can you do one humble thing today, like saying sorry first or letting someone else go first?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, you became our high priest through the power of an indestructible life. You live for ever to make intercession for us. Bring us through the narrow door of humility, that we may not only eat and drink in your presence but truly belong to you. Gather your children from east and west, from north and south, to the table of your kingdom, and keep our priests faithful to you. You live and reign for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Today Hebrews names the source of Christ's priesthood: not family descent but “the power of an indestructible life”, the life of the Risen One. Luke shows Jesus on his way to Jerusalem, where that life will pass through death, and someone asks him how many will be saved. He answers with a call: strive to enter through the narrow door. The key words are indestructible, approach and strive.

Hebrews 7:11–19 · The power of an indestructible life

Setting.

In 7:1–10 the writer has shown that Melchizedek, who blessed Abraham and received a tenth from him, stands above the priesthood of Levi. Now he draws the conclusion. Under the law of Moses the priests came from the tribe of Levi and the family of Aaron. Jesus was of the tribe of Judah. If he is a priest, his priesthood must rest on something other than descent.

The writer speaks with deep respect of Israel's worship. His point is that God himself, in Psalm 110:4, had promised another priest, so the old order was always pointing beyond itself.

Structure.

  • vv. 11–12: if the Levitical priesthood had brought perfection, no other priest would be needed.
  • vv. 13–14: our Lord came from Judah.
  • vv. 15–17: a priest by the power of an indestructible life.
  • vv. 18–19: a better hope, through which we approach God.

Key words.

  • akatalytos, indestructible: Literally “that cannot be dissolved”. The life of the risen Christ is beyond the reach of death. This is the ground of his priesthood: “he always lives to make intercession” (7:25).
  • athetēsis, abrogation, setting aside: A legal word for cancelling an arrangement. Hebrews applies it to the rule that only descendants of Aaron could be priests, now that Christ has come.
  • engizomen, we approach, we draw near: The verb of worship. The whole purpose of priesthood is to let people come near to God, and in Christ this happens fully.

Verse by verse.

“For it is evident that our Lord was descended from Judah,” (7:14)

Hebrews takes for granted what the Gospels tell: Jesus was of the house of David, of the tribe of Judah (Matthew 1:1–3; Luke 1:27).

“but through the power of an indestructible life.” (7:16)

Aaron's priesthood passed from father to son because every priest died. Christ's priesthood passes to no successor, because he lives for ever. Priests of the Church act in his person, not as his successors.

“(for the law made nothing perfect); there is, on the other hand, the introduction of a better hope, through which we approach God.” (7:19)

The law was holy and good (Romans 7:12), but it could not by itself bring people to the goal. The “better hope” is Christ himself, the one through whom we draw near.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that the Old Law is holy, spiritual and good, yet still imperfect, a first stage on the way to the Kingdom (CCC 1963). It also says that Christ is the one true Priest and that the ministerial priesthood makes his one priesthood present, quoting St Thomas Aquinas: only Christ is the true priest, the others being his ministers (CCC 1545). When a priest of our Church stands at the altar, it is Christ, alive for ever, who offers.

Luke 13:22–30 · Strive to enter through the narrow door

Setting.

Since 9:51 Luke has shown Jesus making his way to Jerusalem. Verse 22 reminds the reader of this journey: everything Jesus teaches along the road is said on the way to the Cross.

The question “will only a few be saved?” was discussed by teachers of the time. Jesus does not give a number. He turns a question about others into a call to the one who asked.

Structure.

  • vv. 22–23: the journey and the question.
  • v. 24: strive to enter through the narrow door.
  • vv. 25–27: the shut door and the knocking from outside.
  • vv. 28–30: the banquet of the kingdom, with guests from every direction.

Key words.

  • agōnizesthe, strive: From the word for an athletic contest, the root of our word “agony”. Entering the kingdom takes the whole of a person.
  • thyra stenē, narrow door: A door, not a road: there is a moment to go in. Jesus speaks of himself as the door elsewhere (John 10:9).
  • ouk oida pothen este, I do not know where you come from: The householder does not deny having seen them. He denies any real belonging, any bond of life with them.

Verse by verse.

“Lord, will only a few be saved?” (13:23)

A question of curiosity. Jesus does not answer the question of how many; he answers the question of how.

“Strive to enter through the narrow door;” (13:24)

The door is open now. The effort asked is not to earn entry but to go in while the door stands open, by grace.

“We ate and drank with you, and you taught in our streets.” (13:26)

Being near Jesus is not the same as following him. Christians who eat at his table every Sunday hear this as a word to themselves.

“Then people will come from east and west, from north and south, and will eat in the kingdom of God.” (13:29)

The prophets foresaw the nations gathering at God's feast (Isaiah 25:6). The table is wide, and Abraham, Isaac and Jacob are already at it.

How the Church reads it.

This warning is addressed to those who heard Jesus in person, and through them to every disciple; it is not a judgement on any people. The Catechism takes up the Lord's call to “enter by the narrow gate” (Matthew 7:13–14) in its teaching on the last things, as an urgent call to conversion and responsibility (CCC 1036). The good news is in verse 29: the door is narrow, but the hall is full.

For study and teaching

  • Christ's priesthood rests on “an indestructible life”. How does the Resurrection change the way I pray at the Holy Qurbono?
  • Do I ask questions about others' salvation more readily than I strive for my own? What would striving look like for me this week?
