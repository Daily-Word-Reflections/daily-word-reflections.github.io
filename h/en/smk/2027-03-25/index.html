Word for the day

“Then he took a loaf of bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it to them, saying, “This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.”” Luke 22:19

Reflection

On the night before he suffered, Jesus gathered his apostles in the upper room. “I have eagerly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer.” At that table he took bread, gave thanks, broke it and said: “This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.” And after supper he took the cup: “This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.” On this night the Holy Qurbono was born.

Exodus tells of the first Passover: a lamb without blemish, its blood on the doorposts, eaten in haste by a people ready to leave slavery. “When I see the blood, I will pass over you.” Jesus is the true Passover Lamb. His blood marks us, his Body feeds us for the journey from slavery to freedom, from death to life.

At the same table Jesus gave his apostles a lesson in greatness: “Who is greater, the one who is at the table or the one who serves? Is it not the one at the table? But I am among you as one who serves.” In our tradition the washing of feet makes this visible. The Eucharist and service cannot be separated: the One who gives his Body washes our feet.

St Paul hands on what he received: “For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes.” He also warns against receiving unworthily. Pesaha is the night to thank God for the Holy Qurbono and the priesthood, to examine our hearts, and to stay awake with Jesus as he goes out to Gethsemane.

Homily outline

Theme: “This is my body, given for you.”

On this holy night Jesus eats the Passover with his disciples, gives us the Holy Qurbono and the priesthood, and washes their feet. Everything we are as a Church flows from this Upper Room.

1. The new Passover Lamb

  • In Egypt the blood of the lamb on the doorposts saved the people from death.
  • Jesus takes bread and the cup: his body given, his blood poured out, the new covenant.
  • He is the Lamb whose blood marks us in Baptism and feeds us in the Qurbono.
“The blood shall be a sign for you on the houses where you live: when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague shall destroy you.” Exodus 12:13
“Then he took a loaf of bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and gave it to them, saying, “This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.” And he did the same with the cup after supper, saying, “This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.”” Luke 22:19–20

Apply: Receive Holy Communion tonight with deep faith that it is truly the Lord.

2. A feast that anticipates heaven

  • Isaiah foresaw a feast for all peoples on God's mountain.
  • St John hears: “Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb.”
  • Every Qurbono is a foretaste of that eternal banquet, and Jesus made his apostles priests to celebrate it.
“On this mountain the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a feast of rich food, a feast of well-aged wines.” Isaiah 25:6
“And the angel said to me, “Write this: Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb.”” Revelation 19:9

Apply: Pray tonight for your priests, and for vocations to the priesthood.

3. Love that kneels and washes

  • Abraham welcomed strangers: “Let a little water be brought, and wash your feet.”
  • Jesus, Lord and Teacher, kneels and washes his disciples' feet.
  • The Eucharist and service cannot be separated: whoever receives the Lord must serve the brethren.
“Let a little water be brought, and wash your feet, and rest yourselves under the tree.” Genesis 18:4
“So if I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have set you an example, that you also should do as I have done to you.” John 13:14–15

Apply: Do one humble service at home tonight or tomorrow, especially for someone you find difficult.

Jesus loved his own to the end. In the bread and the cup he gives himself; in the basin and towel he shows how to love.

Let us receive him with faith, adore him with gratitude, and serve one another with humility.

Take-home resolution: Stay with the Lord in prayer for an hour tonight, as he asked his disciples.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, tonight we enter the upper room. Jesus says: “I have eagerly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer.” He knew what was coming: betrayal, arrest, the Cross. And on that night he gave us his greatest gift.

“He took bread, gave thanks, broke it and gave it to them, saying: This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.” And the cup: “This cup is the new covenant in my blood.” The lamb of the first Passover saved Israel from death; Jesus, the true Lamb, gives himself to save the whole world. Every Holy Qurbono since that night is this same gift.

And at the same table Jesus taught them how to be great: “I am among you as one who serves.” He knelt and washed their feet. We cannot receive the Body of Christ and refuse to serve the body of Christ in our brothers and sisters. The altar and the towel belong together.

Tonight, at home, families will break the Pesaha appam. Let it be a real family communion: forgive one another, remember those far away, thank God for the Qurbono and for our priests. And after supper, do not leave Jesus alone. Stay with him, as he goes out to pray in Gethsemane. Amen.

For children

Children, tonight is Pesaha. The night before Jesus died, he had a special supper with his friends. He took bread and said: “This is my body.” He took the cup and said: “This is my blood.” That was the first Holy Qurbana!

Then Jesus knelt down and washed his friends' feet, like a servant. He wanted to teach them to love and help one another.

Tonight at home, help someone without being asked. Let's say: “Thank you, Jesus, for giving us yourself!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus, on the night you were betrayed you gave us your Body and Blood and washed your disciples' feet. Thank you for the Holy Qurbono and for the priesthood. Make us worthy to receive you, humble to serve one another, and faithful to stay with you in your agony. Amen.
