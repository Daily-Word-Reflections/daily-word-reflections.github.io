Word for the day

“To them God chose to make known how great among the Gentiles are the riches of the glory of this mystery, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.” Colossians 1:27

Reflection

Abraham's servant sets out with ten camels and choice gifts and travels to Aram-naharaim, to the city of Nahor. “He made the camels kneel down outside the city by the well of water; it was toward evening, the time when women go out to draw water.” There, before doing anything else, he prays. It is the first prayer for guidance recorded in the Bible: “O Lord, God of my master Abraham, please grant me success today and show steadfast love to my master Abraham.” Then he asks for a sign, and the sign he asks for is wise. He does not ask for the most beautiful girl or the richest family. He asks for a heart: the girl who, when a tired stranger asks for a drink, will say, “Drink, and I will water your camels.” Ten thirsty camels after a desert journey would need a great deal of water drawn by hand. Only a generous, hard-working and kind young woman would offer that. The servant knows that what makes a good wife and mother in Israel is not wealth but a heart like God's own, quick to serve. Before a great decision he kneels his camels, stands by the spring and prays.

Paul shows a different kind of service, the service of suffering: “I am now rejoicing in my sufferings for your sake, and in my flesh I am completing what is lacking in Christ’s afflictions for the sake of his body, that is, the church.” This does not mean that the Cross of Christ was insufficient; his one sacrifice redeemed the whole world. It means that Christ, the Head, wills to share his saving work with his Body. St Augustine taught that the whole Christ is Head and members, and that what Christ suffered in his own flesh is filled up in the sufferings of his members until the end of time (in substance). That is why the Lord said to Saul, “why do you persecute me?” And the heart of it all is “Christ in you, the hope of glory.” In the Gospel Jesus tells his disciples a second time: “The Son of Man is to be betrayed into human hands, and they will kill him, and three days after being killed, he will rise again.” Mark adds, sadly: “they did not understand what he was saying and were afraid to ask him.” Holy Week is near. Let us not be afraid to ask him what his Cross means for our lives.

Full homily

Dear friends, Abraham's servant comes to a well after a long journey. Before he does anything else, he makes his camels kneel and he prays: O Lord, God of my master Abraham, please grant me success today (Genesis 24:12).

He asks God for a sign: a girl who will say, Drink, and I will water your camels (Genesis 24:14). He is not looking for beauty or wealth. He is looking for a generous heart. That is the kind of heart God looks for in us too.

Paul says something surprising: I am now rejoicing in my sufferings for your sake (Colossians 1:24). Christ's Cross saved the world, but he lets us share in it. When we offer our pain with love, it becomes a gift for the whole Church. And the secret is this: Christ in you, the hope of glory (Colossians 1:27).

Jesus tells his disciples again that he will be killed and will rise. But they did not understand what he was saying and were afraid to ask him (Mark 9:32). Holy Week is coming. Let us not be afraid to ask Jesus what his Cross means for us. He will answer those who ask. Amen.

For children

Children, the servant made his camels kneel down and then he prayed. Before a test, before a game, before you say sorry to someone, you can stop and pray: Jesus, help me today. And try to be like the girl at the well: do a little more than you are asked.

Prayer

Lord God of Abraham, your servant prayed at the well and you heard him before he had finished speaking. Teach us to pray before we choose, and give us generous hearts that serve beyond what is asked. Unite our sufferings with the afflictions of your Son for the sake of his Body, the Church. Let Christ dwell in us, our hope of glory, and do not let us be afraid to ask him the meaning of his Cross. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
