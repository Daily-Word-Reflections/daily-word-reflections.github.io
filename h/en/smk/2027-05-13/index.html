Word for the day

“We have this hope, a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul” Hebrews 6:19

Reflection

One week ago, on Suloko, we watched the Lord go up from the Mount of Olives; three days from now, at Pentecost, we shall celebrate the coming of the Spirit. Today's Gospel stands exactly between those two feasts. It is the last and greatest day of the feast of Tabernacles in Jerusalem. Each morning of that feast the priests carried water from the pool of Siloam and poured it out at the altar, praying for the rains of the coming year. And on that day, the Gospel says, “while Jesus was standing there, he cried out, ‘Let anyone who is thirsty come to me, and let the one who believes in me drink. As the scripture has said, “Out of the believer's heart shall flow rivers of living water.”’” Then St John explains: “Now he said this about the Spirit, which believers in him were to receive; for as yet there was no Spirit, because Jesus was not yet glorified.” This last line describes the very days we are living in the calendar. Jesus has now been glorified: he has died, risen and ascended to the Father. The rivers are about to flow. St Augustine says that we come to this fountain not with our feet but with our love: we come by desiring, by believing, by thirsting (Tractates on John 32, in substance). These ten days are given to the Church to make her thirst.

Today our Church remembers a Syriac teacher who knew this thirst: Mar Ishaq Malpan of Antioch, St Isaac of Antioch. He lived in the fifth century, and Syriac tradition remembers him as a priest and a poet who wrote long metrical homilies, memre, to be sung and recited in church. His poems speak of prayer and watchfulness, of repentance and the fear of God, of the struggle of the monk and the hope of the sinner. Scholars note that the writings of more than one Syriac Isaac have been gathered under his name, so the details of his life are uncertain; what is certain is that the Syriac Churches have treasured his verse as a school of prayer. That is why our Church gives him the title Malpan, teacher. He belongs to the great company of those who, after St Ephrem, taught the faith not only in prose but in song, so that the people could carry the teaching of the Church home on their lips. Our own Shehimo and the hymns of the Holy Qurbono are the fruit of that school.

The Letter to the Hebrews gives us the word that holds these waiting days together: hope. “When God made a promise to Abraham, because he had no one greater by whom to swear, he swore by himself.” Abraham, “having patiently endured, obtained the promise.” God bound himself by promise and by oath, “so that through two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible that God would prove false, we who have taken refuge might be strongly encouraged to seize the hope set before us.” Then comes one of the most beautiful images in the Bible: “We have this hope, a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul, a hope that enters the inner shrine behind the curtain, where Jesus, a forerunner on our behalf, has entered, having become a high priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek.” An anchor is thrown out of sight, below the waves, and holds the ship steady in the storm. Our anchor is thrown upward, into heaven, where the ascended Christ has gone before us. In the Holy Qurbono, when the curtain of the sanctuary opens, we glimpse that inner shrine. The forerunner is there, interceding for us, and from there he will send the Spirit like rivers of water on the thirsty land.

Homily outline

Theme: “We have this hope, a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul.”

On the memory of Mar Ishaq Malpan of Antioch, three days before Pentecost: the promise sworn by God, hope anchored behind the curtain, and the rivers of living water.

1. God has sworn by himself

  • The promise to Abraham.
  • Patient endurance obtains the promise.
  • Two unchangeable things.
“God is not a human being, that he should lie, or a mortal, that he should change his mind.” Numbers 23:19
“For in him every one of God's promises is a “Yes.”” 2 Corinthians 1:20

Apply: Choose one promise of God from Scripture and pray it every day until Pentecost.

2. An anchor behind the curtain

  • Hope thrown upward, not downward.
  • Jesus our forerunner and high priest.
  • The sanctuary curtain at the Holy Qurbono.
“For God alone my soul waits in silence, for my hope is from him.” Psalm 62:5
“and hope does not disappoint us, because God's love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit that has been given to us.” Romans 5:5

Apply: When the sanctuary curtain opens at the next Holy Qurbono, offer to Christ the storm you are passing through.

3. Rivers of living water

  • The great day of the feast.
  • As yet there was no Spirit.
  • A school of prayer in song.
“For I will pour water on the thirsty land, and streams on the dry ground; I will pour my spirit upon your descendants, and my blessing on your offspring.” Isaiah 44:3
“And let everyone who is thirsty come. Let anyone who wishes take the water of life as a gift.” Revelation 22:17

Apply: Sing or recite one hymn of the Holy Qurbono slowly at home tonight as a prayer for the Spirit.

God's promise cannot fail, and our hope is anchored where Christ has already gone.

The glorified Lord is about to pour out the Spirit; these days are given to make us thirsty.

Take-home resolution: Anchor your hope in Christ, come to him thirsty, and pray in song as Mar Ishaq taught.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! In today's Gospel Jesus stands up in the Temple on the last great day of the feast of Tabernacles and cries out with a loud voice: “Let anyone who is thirsty come to me, and let the one who believes in me drink.” Every day of that feast the priests poured water at the altar and prayed for rain. And Jesus says: I am the source. St John tells us what he meant: “he said this about the Spirit,” who would be given once Jesus was glorified.

Now Jesus has been glorified. He has suffered, died and risen, and a week ago, on Suloko, we celebrated his Ascension. In three days, at Pentecost, the Spirit will be poured out. So these days are days of thirst. A person who is not thirsty does not look for water. Let us ask the Lord to make us thirsty for him: thirsty for prayer, for his word, for the Holy Qurbono, for holiness. Mar Ishaq Malpan of Antioch, whom we remember today, wrote long poems to help the faithful pray. He knew that the soul must learn to thirst.

The Letter to the Hebrews gives us something for the journey: hope. God made a promise to Abraham and swore by his own name, because there was no one greater. Abraham waited patiently and received what was promised. Then the letter says: “We have this hope, a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul.” Think of a boat in a storm. If its anchor holds, it will not be thrown onto the rocks. Our anchor is not thrown down into the sea but up into heaven, “behind the curtain,” where Jesus has gone before us as our forerunner and high priest.

So whatever storm you are in today, illness, money troubles, worry for your children, hold on to the anchor. Christ is at the Father's right hand, praying for you. And when the curtain of the sanctuary opens in the Holy Qurbono, remember that our hope has already entered there. Let us come to this altar thirsty, and the Lord will give us to drink.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! When you have been playing outside on a hot day, what do you want most? A glass of cold water! Jesus said, “Let anyone who is thirsty come to me.” He was talking about a different kind of thirst, a thirst in our hearts for God, for love, for goodness. And he promised to give us the Holy Spirit, like a river of water flowing inside us. This Sunday is Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit came. Today, every time you drink water, say a tiny prayer: “Jesus, fill me with your Spirit.” Then you will be ready for Sunday!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, our forerunner, you have entered the inner shrine behind the curtain, and there you live for ever to intercede for us. Make our hope a sure and steadfast anchor, fixed in you and in the promise of the Father that cannot fail. You cried out, “Let anyone who is thirsty come to me”: we come thirsty in these days of waiting. Pour out upon us the Holy Spirit like rivers of living water, as you promised. By the prayers of your holy Mother, who waited in the upper room with your apostles, and of Mar Ishaq Malpan, who taught your Church to pray in song, keep us watchful, repentant and full of hope. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory for ever. Amen.
