Word for the day

“He called the crowd with his disciples, and said to them, “If any want to become my followers, let them deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me.” Mark 8:34

Reflection

Yesterday we left Abraham and Isaac walking up the mountain together. Today the story reaches its summit. “Abraham built an altar there and laid the wood in order. He bound his son Isaac, and laid him on the altar, on top of the wood.” At the last moment the angel calls: “Do not lay your hand on the boy or do anything to him; for now I know that you fear God, since you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me.” Abraham sees a ram caught in a thicket and offers it “instead of his son,” and he names the place “The Lord will provide.” God then swears by himself: “by your offspring shall all the nations of the earth gain blessing for themselves, because you have obeyed my voice.” St Paul hears the echo of this chapter when he writes: “He who did not withhold his own Son, but gave him up for all of us, will he not with him also give us everything else?” Isaac was spared; the Son of God was not. Tradition places Moriah where the Temple later stood, near the hill where Christ was crucified, so that the mountain of the test became the mountain of the gift.

In the Gospel Jesus speaks openly of that gift: “the Son of Man must undergo great suffering, and be rejected… and be killed, and after three days rise again.” Peter, who had just confessed him as the Messiah, takes him aside to rebuke him, and Jesus answers: “Get behind me, Satan! For you are setting your mind not on divine things but on human things.” Then he speaks to all: “If any want to become my followers, let them deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me.” Today the Church remembers forty soldiers who did exactly that. At Sebaste in Armenia, around the year 320, under the emperor Licinius, forty Christian soldiers refused to sacrifice to idols. They were left naked through a winter night on a frozen lake, with a warm bath-house on the shore for anyone who would deny Christ. One gave up and ran to the bath; a guard, seeing the faith of the others, took off his own clothes and joined them, so that forty still stood on the ice. St Basil the Great, preaching on their feast, imagined them praying: the winter is bitter, but paradise is sweet; the frost is painful, but the rest that follows is blessed (in substance). St Paul gives the meaning of such suffering: “I am now rejoicing in my sufferings for your sake, and in my flesh I am completing what is lacking in Christ's afflictions for the sake of his body, that is, the church.” Nothing is lacking in Christ's sacrifice; what remains is for his members to share in it, so that his love reaches every place and age.

Homily outline

Theme: “Let them deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me.”

The ram in Isaac's place, the Son the Father did not withhold, and the Forty Martyrs who would not turn back.

Apply: Thank the Father tonight for one gift that came to you through the Cross.

  • Peter's human thinking.
  • Deny yourself.
  • Losing life to save it.
“When you pass through the waters, I will be with you; and through the rivers, they shall not overwhelm you; when you walk through fire you shall not be burned, and the flame shall not consume you.” Isaiah 43:2
“For this slight momentary affliction is preparing us for an eternal weight of glory beyond all measure,” 2 Corinthians 4:17

Apply: Accept one daily discomfort this week without complaint, for Christ.

  • Sebaste, around 320.

  • The guard who took the empty place.
“Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his faithful ones.” Psalm 116:15

God provided the Lamb on the mountain; Christ invites us to share his Cross.

The martyrs show that no comfort is worth more than fidelity to Christ.

Take-home resolution: Carry one hidden cross daily for the rest of the Fast, offered for the persecuted Church.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the mountain Abraham bound Isaac and laid him on the wood. Then the angel called: do not lay your hand on the boy; for now I know that you fear God, since you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me (Genesis 22:12). A ram was offered in his place, and the place was named: The Lord will provide.

St Paul tells us what God provided: he who did not withhold his own Son, but gave him up for all of us (Romans 8:32). Isaac came down the mountain alive; Jesus went up Calvary and gave his life, and on the third day he rose.

When Jesus spoke of his Passion, Peter tried to stop him, and Jesus said: you are setting your mind not on divine things but on human things. If any want to become my followers, let them deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me (Mark 8:33–34).

Today we remember the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste, who stood through a winter night on a frozen lake rather than deny Christ. When one fell away, a guard took his place. Paul says: I am now rejoicing in my sufferings for your sake (Colossians 1:24). May their prayers help us carry our cross to the end of this Fast. Amen.

For children

Children, God stopped Abraham and gave a ram instead of Isaac. But God the Father gave us his own Son, Jesus, because he loves us so much. Today we remember forty brave soldiers who stood all night on ice because they loved Jesus. When something is hard this week, say: Jesus, I carry this with you.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, the Father did not withhold you from us but gave you up for us all, and on the mountain of the Lord he provided the Lamb. You call us to deny ourselves, take up our cross and follow you. By the prayers of the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste, who stood firm through the frozen night, give us courage not to turn back toward easy comfort, and let our small sufferings serve your body, the Church. Amen.
