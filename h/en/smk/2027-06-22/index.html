Word for the day

“for God loves a cheerful giver.” 2 Corinthians 9:7

Reflection

We are in the middle of the Apostles' Fast, which began last Wednesday and runs to the feast of Sts Peter and Paul. The Church has always kept three companions together in a time of fasting: prayer, fasting and almsgiving. Fasting without prayer becomes a diet; fasting without almsgiving can turn into a private achievement. Today's readings speak of the third companion. St Paul is gathering a collection among the Gentile churches for the poor believers in Jerusalem, and he does not ask the Corinthians to give out of pity or shame. He reaches for a farmer's picture: “The point is this: the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully.” In these weeks of the monsoon, the paddy fields of Kuttanad and the hill farms of the high ranges are being sown. No farmer throws a single handful on an acre and hopes for a full harvest. Seed that leaves the hand seems lost for a time in the wet earth; in truth it has only gone ahead to become the harvest.

Then Paul says something about the heart of the giver: “Each of you must give as you have made up your mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.” God does not need our money. What he wants is a heart that has become like his own, a heart that gives because it is glad to give. And Paul is not afraid of running dry: “And God is able to provide you with every blessing in abundance, so that by always having enough of everything, you may share abundantly in every good work.” The one who gives seed to the sower and bread for food will also keep the generous person supplied, so that the giving can go on. St John Chrysostom, preaching on this chapter, pointed out that Paul calls almsgiving a sowing so that no one should think of it as a loss: the seed seems thrown away, but it returns as a harvest, and the harvest is greater than the seed (Homilies on Second Corinthians, in substance). In the fast we give up a meal or a pleasure; the money and the time we save are meant for someone who has less.

The Gospel goes to the root: “Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust consume and where thieves break in and steal; but store up for yourselves treasures in heaven.” In the time of Jesus wealth was kept in fine clothes, which moths could eat, and in coins and metal, which rust could spoil, or which a thief could dig through a mud wall to take. Our wealth is kept in bank accounts and gold in a locker, but the question has not changed: what will last? Then comes the sentence that searches every heart: “For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” St Augustine told his people that the poor are like porters who carry our goods ahead of us to heaven: what we give them is not lost but sent on to the place where we hope to live for ever (Sermon 60, in substance). The Apostles whose fast we keep left their nets and boats and found a treasure that no moth could touch. Let us ask, during these days of the fast, where our treasure really is, and let one generous act move it a little closer to heaven.

Homily outline

Theme: “The one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully.”

On Tuesday of the fifth week after Pentecost, in the Apostles' Fast: almsgiving as sowing, the cheerful giver, and the treasure that lasts.

1. Sowing bountifully

  • Paul's collection for Jerusalem.
  • Seed given is not lost.
  • The harvest is greater than the seed.
“Whoever is kind to the poor lends to the Lord, and will be repaid in full.” Proverbs 19:17
“give, and it will be given to you. A good measure, pressed down, shaken together, running over, will be put into your lap” Luke 6:38

Apply: Put aside what you save by fasting this week and give it before the feast of the Apostles.

2. God loves a cheerful giver

  • Not reluctantly, not under compulsion.
  • A heart like God's own.
  • The God who supplies seed to the sower.
“Give liberally and be ungrudging when you do so, for on this account the Lord your God will bless you in all your work and in all that you undertake.” Deuteronomy 15:10
“remembering the words of the Lord Jesus, for he himself said, ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’” Acts 20:35

Apply: The next time you give, give a little more than you planned, and give it with a smile.

3. Where your treasure is

  • Moth, rust and thieves.
  • The poor as porters to heaven.
  • The Apostles who left their nets.
“if riches increase, do not set your heart on them.” Psalm 62:10
“Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth,” Colossians 3:2

Apply: Name one thing you hold on to too tightly and loosen your grip on it this week.

Almsgiving is not loss but sowing: what we give to God and to the poor goes ahead of us as a harvest.

The heart follows the treasure; let the fast move our treasure towards heaven.

Take-home resolution: Sow bountifully, give cheerfully, and keep your treasure where moth and rust cannot reach.

Full homily

Dear friends, we are keeping the Apostles' Fast, and the Church reminds us that fasting has two companions: prayer and almsgiving. Today St Paul speaks of giving, and he uses the picture of a farmer: the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully (2 Corinthians 9:6). In these rainy weeks our farmers are sowing. Nobody sows a handful and hopes to fill a granary.

Paul goes on: each of you must give as you have made up your mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:7). God does not ask for a gift squeezed out of us with a sigh. He looks for a heart that gives gladly, because it has learned from him. And he promises that the giver will not run dry: God is able to provide you with every blessing in abundance (2 Corinthians 9:8).

Jesus goes deeper still. Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust consume and where thieves break in and steal (Matthew 6:19). Store them up in heaven. And he tells us why it matters so much: for where your treasure is, there your heart will be also (Matthew 6:21). What we hold on to holds on to us.

So in these days of the fast, let us put aside what we save, a meal, a treat, a little spending, and give it with a smile to someone who needs it. That is seed sown for a harvest, and treasure sent ahead to heaven. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what a farmer does when the rain comes? He takes his seeds and throws them into the wet field. The seeds seem to disappear, but after some weeks green plants come up, and later there is a big harvest! St Paul says that when we share, it is like sowing seeds. What we give is not lost; it grows. And God loves people who share with a happy face. This week, share something with a smile: a snack, a pencil, a toy, or your time.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you became poor for our sake so that by your poverty we might become rich. In this Apostles' Fast teach us to sow bountifully and to give with a glad heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion. Take from us the fear that makes us hold on to what moth and rust will consume, and set our hearts on the treasure that is kept in heaven. Bless the hands that give and the hands that receive, and supply us with all we need, so that we may share abundantly in every good work. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
