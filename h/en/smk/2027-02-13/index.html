Word for the day

“Everyone then who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock.” Matthew 7:24

Reflection

Abram is ninety-nine when God appears again: “I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless.” God changes his name: “No longer shall your name be Abram, but your name shall be Abraham; for I have made you the ancestor of a multitude of nations.” The covenant is “an everlasting covenant, to be God to you and to your offspring after you.” A new name means a new life lived in God's sight. The Letter to the Hebrews tells us how God shapes that life: “the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword… it is able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart.” And Jesus, closing the Sermon on the Mount, says that hearing is not enough: “Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father.” The wise build on rock by doing what they hear; the foolish build on sand, “and great was its fall!”

On this first Saturday of the Great Fast our Church remembers Mar Aprem, St Ephrem the Syrian. Born at Nisibis about 306, he served as a deacon there and later at Edessa, where he died in 373. He taught the faith through hymns sung by choirs, and our liturgy is full of his voice; he is called the Harp of the Spirit, and Pope Benedict XV declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1920. The prayer that bears his name is prayed throughout Lent in many Churches: O Lord and Master of my life, take from me the spirit of sloth, despair, lust of power and idle talk; give me the spirit of chastity, humility, patience and love. With him we remember Mar Theodore, a soldier who chose to die rather than deny Christ in the persecution of the early fourth century, and the Prophet Haggai, who told a people living in comfortable houses to rebuild the ruined house of God. Ephrem in the word, Theodore in his blood, Haggai in rebuilding: each built on rock.

Full homily

Dear friends, God says to Abram: I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless. And he gives him a new name, Abraham, father of a multitude (Genesis 17:1, 5). The Fast is a time to walk again in God's sight.

God shapes us by his word: the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword; it is able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart (Hebrews 4:12).

But Jesus warns: not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven. Everyone who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock (Matthew 7:21, 24).

Today we remember Mar Aprem, who built on the word with his hymns; Mar Theodore, who built on it with his blood; and the Prophet Haggai, who called the people to rebuild God's house. Let us pray the Prayer of St Ephrem each day of this Fast and build on rock. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus said: build your house on the rock, not on the sand! When we listen to Jesus and do what he says, we are strong like a house on a rock. St Ephrem wrote beautiful songs about Jesus. Can you sing a hymn to Jesus today?

Prayer

Lord God Almighty, you called Abram to walk before you and gave him a new name. Let your living word search our hearts in this Fast. Through the prayers of Mar Aprem, the Harp of the Spirit, of Mar Theodore the martyr and of the Prophet Haggai, take from us the spirit of sloth, despair, lust of power and idle talk, and give us the spirit of chastity, humility, patience and love, so that we may build our lives on the rock of your word. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

The week's Genesis readings end with a new name and an everlasting covenant: “walk before me, and be blameless” (17:1). The epistle says what shapes such a life: “the word of God is living and active” (Hebrews 4:12). Jesus, closing the Sermon on the Mount, says that hearing that word is not enough; the house stands on rock only if we do it. It is a good day to remember Mar Aprem, who sang that word, Mar Theodore, who died for it, and the prophet Haggai, who called God's people to build.

Genesis 17:1–8 · Walk before me

Setting.

Abram was eighty-six when Ishmael was born (16:16) and is now ninety-nine (17:1). Thirteen silent years lie between the chapters. This covenant scene repeats and deepens chapter 15. Many scholars think that it comes from a later priestly strand of tradition. It goes on in vv. 9–14 to give the sign of circumcision. Read as it stands, it shows God coming back to an old man to renew all that he promised.

Structure.

  • vv. 1–2: God names himself and asks for a whole life: walk before me.
  • v. 3: Abram falls on his face.
  • vv. 4–6: the new name: ancestor of a multitude of nations.
  • vv. 7–8: an everlasting covenant, and the land.

Key words.

  • ’ēl šadday, God Almighty: An ancient divine name, used especially in the patriarchal stories. Its original meaning is uncertain. Some connect it with a word for mountain. The Greek and Latin tradition understood it as the All-powerful.
  • tāmîm, blameless: Whole, complete, undivided. The same word describes an animal without blemish fit for sacrifice (Exodus 12:5). Chapter 16 shows that Abram had not lived without fault; God does not cast him off, but calls him to an undivided life.
  • ’ab hămôn gôyim, ancestor of a multitude of nations: The Hebrew plays on the sound of Abraham and ab hămôn. The new name is a promise that an old, nearly childless man has to carry about in public.

Verse by verse.

“I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless.” (17:1)

God's self-naming comes before the demand. To walk before someone means to live in the sight of the one we serve, as a servant before his lord. Hagar has just called God the one who sees; Abram is told to live in that sight.

“No longer shall your name be Abram, but your name shall be Abraham; for I have made you the ancestor of a multitude of nations.” (17:5)

“I have made”: the verb is in the past tense although Abraham has only one son. For God, what he has promised is as good as done.

“for an everlasting covenant, to be God to you and to your offspring after you.” (17:7)

This is the heart of every covenant in Scripture: “to be God to you”. It is the gift of himself, even more than land or descendants.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism says that God gave Abram the name that means “the father of a multitude of nations” (CCC 59). Paul calls him “the ancestor of all who believe without being circumcised” (Romans 4:11). The Second Vatican Council says that all who believe in Christ, Abraham's children according to faith, are included in his call, and it reminds us that the Church draws nourishment from the root of Israel, onto which we have been grafted (Nostra Aetate 4). Baptism also gives a new name and a new life before God.

Matthew 7:21–28 · The house built on rock

Setting.

These are the last words of the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5–7). A sermon that began with the Beatitudes ends with a judgment and a storm. Matthew's Jesus has given his teaching, and now he says what is to be done with it. Verse 28 is the formula that closes each of the five great discourses of this Gospel: “Now when Jesus had finished saying these things”.

Structure.

  • v. 21: not those who say but those who do.
  • vv. 22–23: great deeds without relationship: “I never knew you.”
  • vv. 24–25: the wise man and the rock.
  • vv. 26–27: the foolish man and the sand.
  • v. 28: the crowds are astounded.

Key words.

  • thelēma, will: The same word as in the Lord's Prayer: “Your will be done” (6:10). In Matthew, the one who does the Father's will belongs to Jesus' true family (12:50).
  • anomia, lawlessness (NRSV: evildoers): The people in v. 23 claim to have worked miracles, and yet Jesus calls what they did a breaking of the law. Charisms without obedience and charity are worth nothing (compare 1 Corinthians 13:1–3).
  • phronimos / mōros, wise / foolish: Both men build, and both hear the words. The storm comes on both. The only difference is doing.

Verse by verse.

“Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father in heaven.” (7:21)

Calling him Lord is right and necessary; Jesus does not reject it. He rejects it when it stands alone. “Lord, Lord” is the prayer of the bridesmaids who come too late (25:11).

“I never knew you; go away from me, you evildoers.” (7:23)

To be known by Jesus means a bond, not information. Much religious activity can go on without it.

“Everyone then who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock.” (7:24)

Jesus says “these words of mine”, placing his own teaching where Israel placed the word of the Lord. No one is promised a life with no storm.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism quotes this saying when it teaches on “Thy will be done”: by prayer we can discern God's will and obtain the endurance to do it, and one enters the kingdom not by speaking words but by doing the Father's will (CCC 2826). St James says the same in plain words: “be doers of the word, and not merely hearers” (James 1:22). A Fast that does not lead to doing is only a form of saying “Lord, Lord”.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

Our Church today remembers Mar Aprem, St Ephrem the Syrian. Born at Nisibis about 306, he served as a deacon there and later at Edessa, where he died in 373. He taught the faith through hymns sung by choirs; he is called the Harp of the Spirit, and Pope Benedict XV declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1920. He wrote of the word of God that we are like thirsty people at a spring: we take what we can and much more remains, and we should give thanks for what we received and not grieve over what we could not hold (in substance, Commentary on the Diatessaron). With him we remember Mar Theodore, a soldier who chose to die rather than deny Christ, and the prophet Haggai, who asked a people back from exile: “Is it a time for you yourselves to live in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?” (Haggai 1:4). One sang, one bled, one called for building, and each did what he heard.

  • If a storm came today, what would my house be found standing on? What word of Jesus have I heard many times and not yet done?
