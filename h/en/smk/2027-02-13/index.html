Saturday of the first week of the Great Fast · Mar Aprem and Mar Theodore; Prophet Haggai

Readings

Word for the day

“Everyone then who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock.” Matthew 7:24

Reflection

Abram is ninety-nine when God appears again: “I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless.” God changes his name: “No longer shall your name be Abram, but your name shall be Abraham; for I have made you the ancestor of a multitude of nations.” The covenant is “an everlasting covenant, to be God to you and to your offspring after you.” A new name means a new life lived in God's sight. The Letter to the Hebrews tells us how God shapes that life: “the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword… it is able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart.” And Jesus, closing the Sermon on the Mount, says that hearing is not enough: “Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father.” The wise build on rock by doing what they hear; the foolish build on sand, “and great was its fall!”

On this first Saturday of the Great Fast our Church remembers Mar Aprem, St Ephrem the Syrian. Born at Nisibis about 306, he served as a deacon there and later at Edessa, where he died in 373. He taught the faith through hymns sung by choirs, and our liturgy is full of his voice; he is called the Harp of the Spirit, and Pope Benedict XV declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1920. The prayer that bears his name is prayed throughout Lent in many Churches: O Lord and Master of my life, take from me the spirit of sloth, despair, lust of power and idle talk; give me the spirit of chastity, humility, patience and love. With him we remember Mar Theodore, a soldier who chose to die rather than deny Christ in the persecution of the early fourth century, and the Prophet Haggai, who told a people living in comfortable houses to rebuild the ruined house of God. Ephrem in the word, Theodore in his blood, Haggai in rebuilding: each built on rock.

Homily outline

Theme: “Hears these words of mine and acts on them.”

At the end of the first week of the Fast: Abraham's new name, the living word, and the saints who built on rock.

1. Walk before me

  • God Almighty appears.
  • A new name, a new life.
  • An everlasting covenant.
“When Abram was ninety-nine years old, the Lord appeared to Abram, and said to him, “I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless.” Genesis 17:1
“Hoping against hope, he believed that he would become “the father of many nations,” according to what was said, “So numerous shall your descendants be.”” Romans 4:18

Apply: Begin each day of next week with a short act of placing yourself before God.

2. Living and active

  • Sharper than a two-edged sword.
  • Nothing hidden from him.
  • Our great high priest.
“so shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty, but it shall accomplish that which I purpose, and succeed in the thing for which I sent it.” Isaiah 55:11
“Indeed, the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing until it divides soul from spirit, joints from marrow; it is able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart.” Hebrews 4:12

Apply: Pray the Prayer of St Ephrem every day of the Fast.

3. Built on rock

  • Not “Lord, Lord” but doing.
  • Rain, floods and winds.
  • Ephrem, Theodore and Haggai.
“Is it a time for you yourselves to live in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?” Haggai 1:4
“Everyone then who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock.” Matthew 7:24

Apply: Choose one word of Jesus heard this week and put it into practice.

God gives a new name to those who walk before him.

The saints built on the rock of his word by doing it.

Take-home resolution: Pray the Prayer of St Ephrem each day and act on one word of the Gospel each week of the Fast.

Full homily

Dear friends, God says to Abram: I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless. And he gives him a new name, Abraham, father of a multitude (Genesis 17:1, 5). The Fast is a time to walk again in God's sight.

God shapes us by his word: the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword; it is able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart (Hebrews 4:12).

But Jesus warns: not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven. Everyone who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock (Matthew 7:21, 24).

Today we remember Mar Aprem, who built on the word with his hymns; Mar Theodore, who built on it with his blood; and the Prophet Haggai, who called the people to rebuild God's house. Let us pray the Prayer of St Ephrem each day of this Fast and build on rock. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus said: build your house on the rock, not on the sand! When we listen to Jesus and do what he says, we are strong like a house on a rock. St Ephrem wrote beautiful songs about Jesus. Can you sing a hymn to Jesus today?

Prayer

Lord God Almighty, you called Abram to walk before you and gave him a new name. Let your living word search our hearts in this Fast. Through the prayers of Mar Aprem, the Harp of the Spirit, of Mar Theodore the martyr and of the Prophet Haggai, take from us the spirit of sloth, despair, lust of power and idle talk, and give us the spirit of chastity, humility, patience and love, so that we may build our lives on the rock of your word. Amen.
