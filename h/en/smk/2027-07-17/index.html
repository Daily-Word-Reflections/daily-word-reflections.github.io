Word for the day

“For we are the aroma of Christ to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing;” 2 Corinthians 2:15

Reflection

Paul writes today from a worried heart. He has come to Troas to proclaim the good news, and the Lord has opened a door for him, yet he cannot rest because his brother Titus is not there with news from Corinth. So he leaves the open door and goes on to Macedonia. Then, almost in the same breath, the worry turns into thanksgiving: “But thanks be to God, who in Christ always leads us in triumphal procession, and through us spreads in every place the fragrance that comes from knowing him.” The picture is of a victory procession in which incense is carried, so that the scent reaches the crowds long before they see the one who is passing. Paul does not claim to be the victor. He is part of the procession, and the fragrance is Christ's. Like every scent, it cannot choose whom it reaches: “For we are the aroma of Christ to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing; to the one a fragrance from death to death, to the other a fragrance from life to life.” The same gospel is life to one who welcomes it and a sharp reproach to one who closes the door. Paul himself trembles at this: “Who is sufficient for these things?”

He answers by describing the kind of messenger God can use: “For we are not peddlers of God's word like so many; but in Christ we speak as persons of sincerity, as persons sent from God and standing in his presence.” A peddler waters down his goods to please the buyer. A person sent from God speaks as one who stands before God. In the Kerala monsoon, when rain has soaked the house and the cupboards smell of damp, an old household remedy is to burn a little frankincense or sambrani on a few embers. The smell fills the rooms, clings to clothes and stays in the hair. We carry with us the fragrance of whoever we have spent time with. The disciple who has stood in the presence of Christ in prayer and in the Holy Qurbono carries something of him into the market, the office and the kitchen without having to say a word.

The Gospel shows us the Lord himself in the synagogue of Nazareth, “as was his custom,” on the sabbath. The scroll of Isaiah is given to him, and he finds the place: “The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to let the oppressed go free, to proclaim the year of the Lord's favor.” The very word Christ means the Anointed One. The Spirit rests on him not for his own comfort but for the sake of the poor, the captive, the blind and the oppressed. Luke tells us that he then says: “Today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing.” The good news is not an idea about the future; it begins to happen when he opens his mouth. Paul's fragrance and the Lord's anointing are one thing seen from two sides: Christ is anointed, and the fragrance of that anointing is spread through those who belong to him.

Today the Panchangam also remembers St Alexius of Rome. The old Life of Alexius, whose historical details cannot be confirmed and which is best read as a story told by the Church to praise a way of life, tells of the only son of a wealthy Roman family who left his home and bride, lived for years as a beggar in a far-off city, and finally returned unrecognised to his father's house, where he lived out his days among the poor at the door. Whatever the details, the Church has loved this story because it makes Christ's own path visible: he who was rich became poor for our sake. Alexius's name is also borne today by Most Rev. Yoohanon Mar Alexios, whose name day the Panchangam keeps, and the Church remembers with thanks the anniversary of the episcopal ordination of Most Rev. Thomas Mar Koorilos in 1997. A bishop is anointed so that he may be a person “sent from God and standing in his presence.” We pray that these pastors, and all who serve us, may spread the fragrance of Christ.

Full homily

Dear friends, today St Paul says something beautiful: thanks be to God, who in Christ always leads us in triumph, and through us spreads in every place the fragrance that comes from knowing him (2 Corinthians 2:14). The picture is of a procession in which incense is carried, so that its scent reaches people before they even see the one who is passing. Paul is not the victor; Christ is. But Paul and all of us who belong to Christ carry his fragrance.

In these monsoon days, many of our homes use a little incense to drive away the damp smell. The smell stays in our clothes and hair for hours. Paul says it is like this with the gospel: those who have been with Christ carry something of him wherever they go. He adds: we are not peddlers of God's word like so many, but in Christ we speak as persons of sincerity, as persons sent from God and standing in his presence (2 Corinthians 2:17). Let us not water down the gospel to please people.

In the Gospel, Jesus stands up in the synagogue of Nazareth and reads from Isaiah: The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor (Luke 4:18). The Anointed One is anointed for the poor, the captive, the blind and the oppressed. That is the fragrance we are to spread. Today the Church remembers St Alexius of Rome, who, according to the old story, left his riches to live among the poor, and she prays for Thomas Mar Koorilos on the anniversary of his episcopal ordination and for Yoohanon Mar Alexios on his name day.

Let us ask the Lord to make us people who carry the fragrance of Christ: in our kindness to the poor, in our honesty, in the way we pray and the way we speak. Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever smelled incense in the church, or a sweet flower in the garden? You can smell it even if you cannot see it! St Paul says that people who stay close to Jesus carry something sweet with them, the love of Jesus. When you are kind to your friends, when you share with someone poor, when you say sorry, people can tell that you belong to Jesus. Today, try to carry the sweet smell of Jesus to everyone you meet.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, anointed by the Father with the Holy Spirit to bring good news to the poor, we thank you that in you we are always led in triumph. Let the fragrance of your knowledge spread through us in every place, in our homes, our work and our parish. Keep us sincere, as persons sent from God and standing in his presence, and make us gentle with the poor, the captive and the oppressed. Through the prayers of St Alexius, who for your sake became poor, and of all your saints, bless our bishops and priests, and bless Thomas Mar Koorilos and Yoohanon Mar Alexios whom we remember today. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
