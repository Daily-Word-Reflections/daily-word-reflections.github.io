Word for the day

“The good person brings good things out of a good treasure, and the evil person brings evil things out of an evil treasure.” Matthew 12:35

Reflection

Paul asks the Corinthians, who were busy testing him, to turn the test around: “Examine yourselves to see whether you are living in the faith. Test yourselves. Do you not realize that Jesus Christ is in you?” And he states a principle for every Christian: “we cannot do anything against the truth, but only for the truth.” Saturday, before the Lord's Day, is a good time for an examination of conscience.

In the Gospel Jesus says: “the tree is known by its fruit.” What we say reveals what we are: “out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” And he warns: “on the day of judgment you will have to give an account for every careless word you utter.” Today the Syriac tradition remembers Mar Jacob Aphrahat, the Persian Sage of the fourth century, the earliest Syriac Father whose writings survive. In his Demonstrations he spoke of faith as a house built on the rock of Christ, and taught that the heart is the true temple where Christ dwells. We remember too Sts Hyacintha and Martina. Let us examine our hearts, so that our words may come from a good treasure.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul says: examine yourselves to see whether you are living in the faith. Do you not realize that Jesus Christ is in you? (2 Corinthians 13:5). On the eve of the Lord's Day, it is good to look into our hearts.

Jesus says: the tree is known by its fruit. The good person brings good things out of a good treasure (Matthew 12:33, 35). Our words reveal our hearts.

He warns that we will give an account for every careless word (Matthew 12:36). Words can heal or wound.

Today we remember Mar Jacob Aphrahat, the Persian Sage, who taught that the heart is the temple where Christ dwells. Let us examine our hearts tonight, and fill them with good treasure. Amen.

For children

Children, a good tree gives good fruit, and a good heart gives kind words! Let us say only kind words today.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you dwell in our hearts. Search us and know us; make the tree good so that its fruit may be good. Through the prayers of Mar Jacob Aphrahat and Sts Hyacintha and Martina, fill our hearts with good treasure and our mouths with words of truth and kindness. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

On this Saturday before the Lord's Day, both readings ask for a look within. Paul turns the Corinthians' testing of him back on themselves: examine yourselves. Jesus says that a tree is known by its fruit and that the mouth speaks from what fills the heart. Mar Aphrahat, the Persian Sage remembered today, taught that faith is a house that good works must build.

2 Corinthians 13:5–8 · Test yourselves

Setting.

Chapters 10–13 are the sharpest part of the letter. Rival teachers, whom Paul calls “super-apostles” (11:5; 12:11), had been questioning his authority, and some in Corinth wanted proof that Christ was speaking through him (13:3). Paul is about to come for a third visit (13:1). These verses come near the end of his defence, and he turns it into a pastoral appeal.

Structure.

  • v. 5: examine yourselves; Christ is in you.
  • v. 6: Paul's hope that they will see that he has not failed.
  • v. 7: his prayer: that they do right, even if he seems to fail.
  • v. 8: the principle: nothing against the truth.

Key words.

  • dokimazō / adokimos, to test / failing the test: The word for testing metal or coin to see whether it is genuine. A coin that fails is adokimos. The Corinthians have been testing Paul; he asks what the test would show of them. In Greek, the word “yourselves” comes first and is repeated, so that the stress falls on it.
  • alētheia, truth: For Paul this is not only a correct statement. It is the Gospel itself and what agrees with it.

Verse by verse.

“Examine yourselves to see whether you are living in the faith. Test yourselves. Do you not realize that Jesus Christ is in you?” (13:5)

The examination is not a search only for what is wrong. Its first discovery is that Christ is present. Paul speaks in the same way of himself: “it is Christ who lives in me” (Galatians 2:20).

“But we pray to God that you may not do anything wrong” (13:7)

Here is the heart of a pastor. He prays that they will do what is right even if it makes him look like a failure, for it is their good he wants, not his own reputation.

“For we cannot do anything against the truth, but only for the truth.” (13:8)

Even when he is defending himself, Paul will not win by bending the truth. In a quarrel, this is the hardest rule to keep.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that the sacrament of Penance should be prepared for by an examination of conscience made in the light of the word of God (CCC 1454). St Paul uses the same word to the Corinthians about coming to the Eucharist: “Examine yourselves, and only then eat of the bread and drink of the cup” (1 Corinthians 11:28; CCC 1385). A Saturday evening is a good hour for it, before the Holy Qurbono of the Lord's Day.

Matthew 12:33–37 · From the abundance of the heart

Setting.

Jesus has just healed a man who was blind and mute, and some Pharisees say that he drives out demons by the ruler of the demons (12:22–24). He answers them and warns about blasphemy against the Holy Spirit (12:31–32). Today's words follow directly, so they first concern these particular opponents and what they said about the work of the Spirit. The Pharisees were not all of one mind, and many scholars think that Matthew's sharp tone also reflects the disputes of his own day. The teaching itself is for every disciple.

Structure.

  • v. 33: the tree and its fruit.
  • vv. 34–35: the heart and the mouth, the treasure and what comes from it.
  • vv. 36–37: the account to be given for words.

Key words.

  • perisseuma, abundance: What overflows. Speech is what spills over when the heart is full. Under pressure, a person's words show what was stored.
  • thēsauros, treasure, storehouse: The word can mean both what is stored and the place of storing. See 6:21: “where your treasure is, there your heart will be also”.
  • rhēma argon, careless word: Argos means idle or not working. It is a word that does no good work, spoken without thought.

Verse by verse.

“for the tree is known by its fruit.” (12:33)

Those who called his good work evil are told to look at the fruit: a man has been healed. The same test applies to us (7:16).

“You brood of vipers! How can you speak good things, when you are evil? For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” (12:34)

John the Baptist used the same harsh words (3:7). They are a prophet's warning to those who should have known better, not a description of a people.

“I tell you, on the day of judgment you will have to give an account for every careless word you utter;” (12:36)

Words are not small things. James says the same at length (James 3:2–10).

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism says that the heart is the seat of moral personality, and that sin has its root there, as charity also does (CCC 2517, 1853). It warns against rash judgment, detraction and calumny as sins of the tongue against the truth and against a neighbour's good name (CCC 2477). Changing the words alone will not do. The Lord asks for a change in the tree.

Questions people ask

Must I really answer for every careless word? Who could survive that?

Jesus is not trying to frighten people into silence. He is saying that words matter because they show the heart, and that God takes us seriously. The same Lord forgives those who come to him, and the sacrament of Penance is there for the sins of the tongue as for others. James says that all of us make many mistakes (3:2). The right answer to this saying is not fear but an examination of conscience, and a heart more often filled with good.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

The Syriac tradition remembers today Mar Jacob Aphrahat, the Persian Sage, who lived in the Persian Empire in the fourth century and is the earliest Syriac Father whose writings have come down to us. His Demonstrations open with one on faith, which he compares to a house built on the rock who is Christ, and he says that the house must be raised with works such as pure fasting, pure prayer, love and almsgiving (in substance). The tree and its fruit is his teaching too.

For study and teaching

  • Paul says the first thing to look for is that Christ is in me. How would this change the way I make my examination of conscience tonight?
  • Thinking back over this week's words, what do they tell me about what is filling my heart?
