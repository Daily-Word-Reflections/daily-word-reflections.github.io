Word for the day

“Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish.” Matthew 15:28

Reflection

A Canaanite woman, a foreigner and a pagan, comes shouting after Jesus: “Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David; my daughter is tormented by a demon.” At first Jesus does not answer her. The disciples want her sent away. Jesus says he was sent to the lost sheep of Israel. But she kneels and says: “Lord, help me.” And when Jesus speaks of the children's bread, she replies: “Yes, Lord, yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table.”

This is one of the most moving prayers in the Gospel. She does not argue about her rights; she does not give up; she accepts being called small, and still trusts. Jesus is amazed: “Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish.” Her daughter is healed from that hour. Jesus' silence was not a refusal; it was a school of persevering faith.

Isaiah had prophesied that foreigners who hold fast to the covenant would be brought to God's holy mountain, “for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples.” The Canaanite mother is the first fruit of that promise. The bread meant for the children is enough for all; in the desert God “rained bread from heaven” for the whole people (Exodus 16:4).

St Paul cries out in Romans: “Wretched man that I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death? Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!” The mother's cry and Paul's cry are the same: we cannot save ourselves, but Christ can. Halfway through the Fast, the Church sets up the Cross in the middle of the church: the answer to every cry for help.

Homily outline

Theme: “Woman, great is your faith!”

A foreign mother cries out for her daughter. Jesus is silent; the disciples want her sent away; she is answered with a hard word. Yet she persists, and Jesus praises her great faith. Her story teaches us how to pray when God seems silent.

1. A mother's cry

  • She is a Canaanite, an outsider, yet she calls Jesus “Lord, Son of David”.
  • She brings her daughter's suffering as her own: “Have mercy on me.”
  • Isaiah's prayer is hers and ours: “O LORD, be gracious to us; we wait for you.”
“O LORD, be gracious to us; we wait for you. Be our arm every morning, our salvation in the time of trouble.” Isaiah 33:2
“Just then a Canaanite woman from that region came out and started shouting, “Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David; my daughter is tormented by a demon.”” Matthew 15:22

Apply: Pray this week for children in trouble: sick, addicted, lost or far from God.

2. Faith tested by silence

  • “He did not answer her at all.” God's silence is not his absence.
  • The Psalmist also cried: “How long, O LORD?”
  • She does not leave; she kneels: “Lord, help me.” Persistent, humble prayer grows deeper through testing.
“How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?” Psalm 13:1
“But he did not answer her at all … But she came and knelt before him, saying, “Lord, help me.”” Matthew 15:23, 25

Apply: Do not stop praying for the intention you have carried longest.

3. Great faith and bread for all

  • She accepts the hard word humbly and turns it into hope: even the dogs eat the crumbs.
  • Jesus praises her: “Woman, great is your faith!” and her daughter is healed.
  • Isaiah foresaw it: foreigners gathered into God's house of prayer for all peoples.
“These I will bring to my holy mountain, and make them joyful in my house of prayer … for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples.” Isaiah 56:7
“She said, “Yes, Lord, yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table.” Then Jesus answered her, “Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish.”” Matthew 15:27–28

Apply: Welcome a stranger or someone from another community in your parish or workplace.

The Canaanite woman shows us humble, persistent, confident faith.

Let us keep praying for those we love, and never doubt that the Lord's table has bread for all.

Take-home resolution: Pray a decade of the Rosary each day this week for a child or young person in difficulty.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today a mother comes to Jesus. She is not one of his people; she is a Canaanite, a foreigner. Her daughter is suffering terribly. She cries out: “Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David!” And Jesus does not answer her a word.

Have you ever felt that? You pray and pray, and heaven seems silent. The disciples even want her sent away. Jesus says he was sent first to Israel. But she does not give up. She kneels and says three simple words: “Lord, help me.” And when Jesus speaks of the children's bread, she answers with amazing humility: “Yes, Lord, yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table.”

Jesus is amazed: “Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish.” His silence was not a refusal. It was a school of faith, and she passed with honours. Isaiah had said that God's house would be a house of prayer for all peoples. This foreign mother shows us that no one is outside God's mercy.

This Wednesday we reach the middle of the Fast, and the Cross is set up in our church. Look at it when you feel God is silent. St Paul cries: “Who will rescue me? Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!” Mothers and fathers, keep praying for your children. Don't give up. Say with her: “Lord, help me.” Amen.

For children

Children, a mother came to Jesus because her daughter was very sick. She asked and asked: “Jesus, please help!” At first Jesus did not answer.

But she kept trusting and asking. Jesus said: “Your faith is great!” And her daughter was healed.

When you pray and nothing seems to happen, don't stop! Jesus is listening. Let's say: “Jesus, help me!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on us. Give us the humble, persevering faith of the Canaanite mother; hear our prayers for our children and families; and make your Church a house of prayer for all peoples. Amen.
