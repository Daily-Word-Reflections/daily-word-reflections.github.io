Word for the day

“No one has greater love than this, to lay down one’s life for one’s friends.” John 15:13

Reflection

The story of the field at Hebron ends with a legal formula repeated twice: “So the field of Ephron in Machpelah… the field with the cave that was in it and all the trees that were in the field… passed to Abraham as a possession.” Then, simply: “After this, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah facing Mamre (that is, Hebron) in the land of Canaan.” The first part of the promised land that Abraham owns is a tomb. Later Abraham himself will be laid there, and Isaac and Rebekah, and Jacob and Leah. It looks like a sad ending, but it is an act of hope. To bury your dead in the land of promise is to say: God will keep his word, even beyond death. The Letter to the Hebrews says of the patriarchs: “they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God.” Christian cemeteries carry the same faith; we call them places of sleep, because we await the resurrection of the body.

St Paul asks: “Then what becomes of boasting? It is excluded. By what law? By that of works? No, but by the law of faith. For we hold that a person is justified by faith apart from works prescribed by the law.” Abraham did not earn the promise; he received it and trusted it, even to the grave. Paul means that no one is made righteous by the observances of the Mosaic law, and the Church teaches that justification is God's free gift, received in faith and baptism, which then bears fruit in love; so no one can boast. And Paul adds at once: “Is he not the God of Gentiles also? Yes… since God is one,” and “Do we then overthrow the law by this faith? By no means! On the contrary, we uphold the law.” The law is fulfilled in the commandment Jesus gives in the Gospel: “This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you. No one has greater love than this, to lay down one’s life for one’s friends.” He calls us friends, not servants, and he reminds us whose initiative it was: “You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last.” St Augustine commented that Christ did not choose us because we were good; he chose us so that we might become good (in substance). Today we give thanks for four shepherds of our Church, Vincent Mar Paulos, Thomas Mar Antonios, Samuel Mar Irenios and Philipose Mar Stephanos, ordained bishops on this day in 2010: chosen and appointed to bear fruit that will last. Tomorrow, on Samio Sunday, the Lord opens the eyes of the blind.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the field and the cave of Machpelah passed to Abraham, and Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah (Genesis 23:19). His first possession in the promised land was a grave. To bury his dead there was an act of hope.

Paul asks: then what becomes of boasting? It is excluded. For we hold that a person is justified by faith apart from works prescribed by the law (Romans 3:27–28). Abraham received the promise as a gift; so do we. And yet faith does not overthrow the law: we uphold the law (Romans 3:31).

Jesus sums up the law: this is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you. No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends (John 15:12–13). He calls us friends.

And he says: you did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last (John 15:16). Today we thank God for our four bishops ordained on this day in 2010, and we ask that each of us may bear fruit that lasts. Tomorrow the Lord opens the eyes of the blind; may he open ours. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says: I call you my friends, and I chose you! Isn't that wonderful? Jesus loved us so much that he gave his life for us on the Cross. Today we pray for four of our bishops whom Jesus chose. This week, be a good friend to someone who is lonely.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you laid down your life for your friends and called us friends, not servants. You chose us, not because we were good, but so that we might become good and bear fruit that will last. Exclude all boasting from our hearts, keep us in the faith of Abraham who buried his dead in hope, and bless our bishops Vincent Mar Paulos, Thomas Mar Antonios, Samuel Mar Irenios and Philipose Mar Stephanos, whom you chose and appointed for your people. Amen.
