Word for the day

“In the days of his flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to the one who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverent submission.” Hebrews 5:7

Reflection

Christ is risen! The Letter to the Hebrews today takes us back to the night before the Passion, and then forward to the Resurrection. “In the days of his flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to the one who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverent submission.” We think of Gethsemane, where the Lord prayed, “Abba, Father, for you all things are possible; remove this cup from me; yet, not what I want, but what you want” (Mark 14:36). And Hebrews says: he was heard. Not by being spared the Cross, but by being brought through death into the glory of the Resurrection. The Father's answer to the tears of Gethsemane was the empty tomb. This is a deep comfort for everyone who prays and seems not to be answered. The Son himself prayed with tears; he knows what it is. And the Father hears, often in a way we cannot see until later.

“Although he was a Son, he learned obedience through what he suffered; and having been made perfect, he became the source of eternal salvation for all who obey him, having been designated by God a high priest according to the order of Melchizedek.” Christ did not make himself a priest: “Christ did not glorify himself in becoming a high priest, but was appointed by the one who said to him, ‘You are my Son, today I have begotten you.’” His priesthood is not like the old priesthood that passed from father to son and ended in death. Like Melchizedek, who brought out bread and wine (Genesis 14:18), Christ is “a priest forever.” Risen and ascended, he stands before the Father for us now. Every time the priest stands at the altar in the Holy Qurbono, he acts in the person of this one eternal High Priest, who offers himself and makes intercession for us (Catechism 1544–1548). Our Church has always loved this mystery: the Lord who is both the priest and the offering. Today the Panchangam also remembers Mar Seraphion, whose memory our calendar keeps among the saints; with all the saints he now shares in the prayer of the great High Priest.

The Gospel shows how the Son's prayer became ours. “He was praying in a certain place, and after he had finished, one of his disciples said to him, ‘Lord, teach us to pray, as John taught his disciples.’” The disciples watched Jesus pray and wanted what he had. He gave them the words we still say: “Father, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come. Give us each day our daily bread. And forgive us our sins, for we ourselves forgive everyone indebted to us. And do not bring us to the time of trial.” To call God “Father” is possible only because the Son shares his own relationship with us; Tertullian called this prayer a summary of the whole Gospel (in substance). In our Church we say the Lord's Prayer at every hour of prayer and before Holy Communion in the Holy Qurbono. Pray it slowly today, at least once, thinking about each line. Ask yourself whom you need to forgive, and what “daily bread” you are anxious about. Then trust the Father, who heard the cries of his Son.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Hebrews says: in the days of his flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to the one who was able to save him from death, and he was heard (Hebrews 5:7).

He was not spared the Cross, but he was heard: the Father brought him through death into the Resurrection. When we pray with tears, we are not alone. The Son has prayed this way before us.

Christ did not make himself a priest. The Father said to him, You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek. Risen and alive, he offers himself for us at every Holy Qurbono and always lives to intercede for us.

The disciples said, Lord, teach us to pray, and Jesus gave them the Our Father. Let us pray it slowly today, forgive those who have hurt us, and trust the Father who heard his Son. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! One day the disciples saw Jesus praying and said, Lord, teach us to pray. So Jesus taught them the Our Father. Do you know it by heart? Tonight, say it very slowly with your family, and think about each word. Jesus is teaching you to talk to God as your own Father!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, in the days of your flesh you prayed with loud cries and tears, and the Father heard you and raised you from the dead. You are our High Priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek, living always to make intercession for us. Teach us to pray as you prayed: to call God our Father, to seek his kingdom, to forgive as we are forgiven, and to trust him in every trial. By the prayers of Mar Seraphion and all the saints, keep us faithful. You live and reign for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Both readings today are about prayer, and both go back to the source. Hebrews shows the Son himself praying “with loud cries and tears” and being heard, so that he became our High Priest. Luke shows the disciples watching Jesus pray and asking him to teach them, and he gives them the Our Father. The prayer of the Son becomes the prayer of his brothers and sisters. The key words are heard, priest and Father.

Hebrews 5:5–10 · He was heard because of his reverent submission

Setting.

The Letter to the Hebrews calls itself a “word of exhortation” (13:22). It reads like a long sermon to Christians who were growing tired and tempted to drift away. The writer does not give his name. Our Syriac tradition, with much of the ancient Church, counted it among the letters of St Paul, and many scholars today think it came from someone in his circle. The Church receives it as inspired Scripture in either case.

In 5:1–4 the writer has described what any high priest must be: taken from among people, gentle with the ignorant, and called by God, not self-appointed. Today's verses show how Christ fulfils each point.

Structure.

  • vv. 5–6: Christ did not appoint himself: two psalms show the Father's call (Psalm 2:7; Psalm 110:4).
  • vv. 7–8: his prayer and his obedience in suffering.
  • vv. 9–10: made perfect, he becomes the source of salvation and is named High Priest.

Key words.

  • eulabeia, reverent submission: Reverence before God, the awe of one who trusts and yields. The Son was heard not because he escaped death but because he handed himself over to the Father with complete trust.
  • teleiōtheis, having been made perfect: In Hebrews this does not mean that Christ was once morally imperfect. It means brought to the goal, completed. The same family of words was used for the consecration of priests. Through his Passion and Resurrection the Son reaches the fullness of his work as Priest.
  • Melchisedek, Melchizedek: The king of Salem and “priest of God Most High” who brought out bread and wine to Abraham (Genesis 14:18). Psalm 110:4 names a priesthood “according to the order of Melchizedek”, and Hebrews applies it to Christ.

Verse by verse.

“So also Christ did not glorify himself in becoming a high priest,” (5:5)

Every true priesthood is received, not taken. This is true of the Son, and it is true of those who share his priesthood in the Church.

“Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to the one who was able to save him from death,” (5:7)

The words recall Gethsemane, where Jesus prayed, “Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me” (Luke 22:42). Hebrews does not hide the human anguish of the Son.

“and he was heard because of his reverent submission.” (5:7)

He was not spared the Cross. He was heard in a deeper way: the Father brought him through death and raised him. In this Easter season we read the verse in the light of the empty tomb.

“he became the source of eternal salvation for all who obey him,” (5:9)

He learned obedience by suffering, and those who obey him receive salvation from him. The obedience of the Head becomes the way of the members.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism sees in the agony of Gethsemane the Son's acceptance of the cup from the Father (CCC 612), and it teaches that the Father answered the cry of his Son beyond all hope by raising him from the dead (CCC 2606). It also says that the Christian tradition sees Melchizedek as a figure of the priesthood of Christ (CCC 1544). St Cyprian already joined the bread and wine of Melchizedek to the Eucharist (Letter 63, in substance). Every Holy Qurbono is offered through this one High Priest.

Luke 11:1–4 · Lord, teach us to pray

Setting.

Luke more than any other evangelist shows Jesus at prayer: at his baptism (3:21), before choosing the Twelve (6:12), before asking “Who do you say that I am?” (9:18), and at the Transfiguration (9:28–29). Here the disciples watch him pray and want to learn. John the Baptist had taught his disciples a way of prayer, and they ask for one of their own.

Luke's form of the Lord's Prayer is shorter than Matthew's (Matthew 6:9–13). The prayer the Church uses in the liturgy follows Matthew.

Structure.

  • v. 1: Jesus at prayer; the request.
  • v. 2: the address and two petitions for God's name and kingdom.
  • vv. 3–4: three petitions for our needs: bread, forgiveness, protection in trial.

Key words.

  • Pater, Father: Behind the Greek is very likely the Aramaic Abba, the word Jesus used (Mark 14:36). He shares with his disciples his own way of speaking to God.
  • epiousios, daily: A rare Greek word, hardly found outside the Lord's Prayer. It may mean “for today”, “for the coming day” or “needed for life”. The Church hears in it both ordinary bread and the Bread of Life.
  • peirasmos, time of trial: Testing that could lead a person away from God. We ask the Father not to let us enter into a trial we cannot bear, and not to let us give way in it.

Verse by verse.

“Lord, teach us to pray, as John taught his disciples.” (11:1)

Prayer is learned. The disciples did not think it came by itself, and they went to the right teacher.

“Father, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come.” (11:2)

We begin with God and not with ourselves. To “hallow” the name is to let God be known as holy, in our words and in our lives.

“And forgive us our sins, for we ourselves forgive everyone indebted to us.” (11:4)

Luke speaks of sins in the first half and debts in the second. The forgiveness we ask and the forgiveness we give belong together.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism notes that Luke gives five petitions and Matthew seven, and that the liturgy of the Church kept Matthew's text (CCC 2759). It quotes Tertullian, who called the Lord's Prayer a summary of the whole Gospel (CCC 2761). On the word “daily” it notes that the bread asked for is also the Bread of Life, the Body of Christ (CCC 2837).

Questions people ask

If Jesus was heard, why did he still die?

Hebrews says he prayed “to the one who was able to save him from death”. The Father did save him from death, not by keeping him from dying but by raising him on the third day. That is the answer our own prayers also share in: God does not always remove the cross, but he never leaves us in the grave.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

The Panchangam remembers Mar Seraphion today. In every hour of our daily prayer and before Communion in the Holy Qurbono we say the prayer the Lord taught, and we say it as sons and daughters who have learned it from the Son.
