Word for the day

“Everyone therefore who acknowledges me before others, I also will acknowledge before my Father in heaven.” Matthew 10:32

Reflection

Christ is risen! On the first Friday after Qyomtho the Church does not return to the Friday of the Cross alone; she keeps the memory of all the confessors. In the Syriac tradition a mawdyono, a confessor, is one who confessed Christ openly before others and bore the cost: prison, exile, torture, and for many, death. The Panchangam calls this day the Friday of All the Confessors (Sakala Mawdyanmar). It is fitting that they are remembered in the white week. On Good Friday the Lord went down into death; on this Friday we honor those who followed him there and now share his victory. The Book of Revelation sees them “robed in white” before the throne, having “washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.” Hevoro, the white week of the newly baptized, is also the week of the white robes of the witnesses. The Letter to the Hebrews names some of the heroes of faith of the old covenant and then lists those whose names it does not give: “Others suffered mocking and flogging, and even chains and imprisonment. They were stoned to death, they were sawn in two, they were killed by the sword.” Then comes a sentence that every age of persecution has repeated with gratitude: they were people “of whom the world was not worthy.”

Why did they not deny Christ? Today's Gospel gives the Lord's own reasons. First, nothing stays hidden: “What I say to you in the dark, tell in the light; and what you hear whispered, proclaim from the housetops.” Second, the worst that persecutors can do has a limit: “Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul.” Third, we are known and loved in every detail: “Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? Yet not one of them will fall to the ground apart from your Father. And even the hairs of your head are all counted. So do not be afraid; you are of more value than many sparrows.” And finally, a promise: “Everyone therefore who acknowledges me before others, I also will acknowledge before my Father in heaven.” Tertullian told the rulers of his time that the blood of Christians is seed: the more they were mown down, the more they grew (in substance). Hebrews ends with a mystery: those heroes “did not receive what was promised, since God had provided something better so that they would not, apart from us, be made perfect.” The confessors of every age wait to be made perfect together with us, in the one Body of the risen Christ.

How do we keep the Friday of All the Confessors? Remember the confessors of our own land: St Thomas the Apostle, who according to the tradition of our Churches gave his life for Christ in India; and the Christians of our own time in India and elsewhere who have lost homes, health or life rather than deny the Lord. Pray today for all who suffer for the faith, and if you can, give to a fund that helps persecuted Christians. Ask the Virgin Mary, whom the Church invokes as Queen of Confessors, for courage. Then look at your own day: where am I silent about Christ out of fear of laughter or of losing favor? A word at the office, a sign of the cross before a meal in a restaurant, a Sunday kept holy against pressure: these are small confessions, and the Lord who counts the hairs of our head does not forget them. Come to the Holy Qurbono, where the risen Lord whom the confessors acknowledged gives himself to us.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! On this first Friday after Qyomtho our Church remembers all the confessors: those who confessed Christ before others and paid for it with prison, exile or death. The Letter to the Hebrews calls them people of whom the world was not worthy (Hebrews 11:38).

Why did they stand firm? The Lord tells us in the Gospel. Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul. Not a sparrow falls without your Father; even the hairs of your head are counted. So do not be afraid; you are of more value than many sparrows (Matthew 10:31).

And he gives a promise: Everyone who acknowledges me before others, I also will acknowledge before my Father in heaven (Matthew 10:32). In this white week we see the confessors, with Revelation, robed in white, their robes washed in the blood of the Lamb.

Let us pray today for Christians who suffer for the faith, ask the Virgin Mary, Queen of Confessors, for courage, and make our own small confessions: a sign of the cross, a word for Christ, a Sunday kept holy. The risen Lord does not forget them. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Today we remember all the brave Christians who said “I belong to Jesus,” even when people hurt them for it. Jesus said: do not be afraid! God looks after every little sparrow, and he even knows how many hairs are on your head. Can you be brave for Jesus today, maybe by making the sign of the cross before you eat?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen and glorious, you promised to acknowledge before your Father everyone who acknowledges you before others. We thank you for all the confessors who held fast to you in chains, in exile and in death, of whom the world was not worthy. Through their prayers strengthen the Christians who suffer for your name today. Deliver us from the fear that keeps us silent, and teach us that we are of more value than many sparrows. With the Virgin Mary, Queen of Confessors, and all your witnesses, we praise you with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

The Friday of Hevoro week is kept as the commemoration of all the Confessors, the faithful who bore witness to Christ before others, many of them to the point of death. Hebrews runs out of time to name such people: judges, kings and prophets, and countless unnamed men and women “of whom the world was not worthy”. In the Gospel Jesus tells his disciples three times not to be afraid, because the Father counts even the hairs of their heads, and he promises to acknowledge before the Father everyone who acknowledges him. The Church reads these together in Easter week because the resurrection is what makes such courage reasonable.

Hebrews 11:32–40 · Of whom the world was not worthy

Setting.

Chapter 11 is a long roll of honour that begins with a definition: “faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen” (11:1). Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Moses and others follow, each introduced with the words “by faith”. Our reading is the end of the list, where the author speeds up because the names are too many.

The sermon was written for Christians who had already suffered public abuse, prison and the loss of their property (10:32–34), and who were growing tired. The heroes of Israel are set before them as companions in the same race (12:1).

Structure.

  • v. 32: names without stories: Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel and the prophets.
  • vv. 33–35a: what faith achieved: victories, rescues, the dead given back.
  • vv. 35b–38: what faith endured: torture, prison, stoning, the sword, exile.
  • vv. 39–40: none of them received what was promised, so that they might be made perfect together with us.

Key words.

  • dia pisteōs, through faith: The phrase sums up the whole list. Faith here is trust in God's promise that holds on when the promise is not yet seen.
  • etympanisthēsan, they were tortured: The verb comes from the word for a drum, and seems to mean a beating or torture on a frame. 2 Maccabees 6:19 and 6:28 use the related noun for the instrument to which the old man Eleazar went of his own accord rather than betray the law.
  • teleiōthōsin, be made perfect: A favourite word of Hebrews: to be brought to completion, to reach the goal. Christ himself was made “perfect through sufferings” (2:10), and through him the saints of the old covenant reach the goal together with us.

Verse by verse.

“Women received their dead by resurrection.” (11:35)

The widow of Zarephath through Elijah (1 Kings 17:17–24) and the Shunammite woman through Elisha (2 Kings 4:18–37). Those were returns to this life, which would end again.

“Others were tortured, refusing to accept release, in order to obtain a better resurrection.” (11:35)

The “better resurrection” is the one that never ends. The author is thinking of the martyrs of 2 Maccabees 6–7, the old man Eleazar and the mother with her seven sons, who died hoping that the King of the universe would raise them “to an everlasting renewal of life” (2 Maccabees 7:9).

“they were sawn in two,” (11:37)

Scripture does not tell this story. An ancient Jewish tradition, preserved in the writing known as the Ascension of Isaiah, held that the prophet Isaiah was sawn in two under King Manasseh, and many think that the author has this in mind.

“of whom the world was not worthy.” (11:38)

A reversal. The world judged these people unfit and drove them out to deserts and caves. God's judgement is that the world was unfit for them.

“since God had provided something better so that they would not, apart from us, be made perfect.” (11:40)

The saints of Israel are not left behind. Their hope and ours are fulfilled together, in Christ, in one family.

How the Church reads it.

The Church honours the holy men and women of the Old Testament as our fathers and mothers in faith; the Catechism calls Abraham “the father of all who believe” (CCC 146). It also teaches that martyrdom is the supreme witness given to the truth of the faith (CCC 2473). The mother and her seven sons of 2 Maccabees are honoured in the calendars of many Churches, East and West, a sign that the Church knows this cloud of witnesses to be one.

Matthew 10:26–33 · Do not be afraid; acknowledge me

Setting.

Matthew 10 is the second of the five great discourses in Matthew's Gospel, the one in which Jesus sends out the Twelve. He tells them plainly that they will be handed over to councils and brought before governors and kings because of him (10:17–18), and that a disciple is not above his teacher (10:24). Our verses come right after, and three times they say “do not fear” (vv. 26, 28, 31).

Structure.

  • vv. 26–27: what is hidden will be revealed; proclaim it from the housetops.
  • v. 28: fear God, not those who can kill only the body.
  • vv. 29–31: the sparrows and the hairs of your head: you are of more value.
  • vv. 32–33: acknowledging and denying.

Key words.

  • homologeō, acknowledge, confess: Literally “to say the same thing”, to agree openly and publicly. It is the word behind the Church's “confession” of faith, and a confessor is one who has made that confession under trial. Matthew's Greek says, in a Semitic turn of phrase, “confess in me”.
  • assarion, penny: A small Roman copper coin, a sixteenth of a denarius, when a denarius was a labourer's daily wage. Two sparrows cost that much. In Luke five sparrows are sold for two pennies (Luke 12:6), and preachers like to say that the fifth was thrown in for nothing, yet God does not forget it.
  • geenna, hell: The Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, remembered for the evil once done there (Jeremiah 7:31). In Jesus' words it stands for final ruin, the loss of body and soul together.

Verse by verse.

“What I say to you in the dark, tell in the light; and what you hear whispered, proclaim from the housetops.” (10:27)

The flat roofs of a village were where news was called out. The quiet teaching given to a few is meant for the whole world.

“rather fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell.” (10:28)

This is God, not the devil. Fear of God here is not terror but reverence, taking God seriously, and it drives out every lesser fear.

“And even the hairs of your head are all counted.” (10:30)

A care that reaches to what no one else would bother to count.

“Everyone therefore who acknowledges me before others, I also will acknowledge before my Father in heaven;” (10:32)

The word “therefore” ties the promise to the sparrows: because the Father cares, the disciple can speak. Jesus will speak for us as we have spoken for him.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism cites this saying when it teaches that those who belong to Christ through faith and Baptism must confess their baptismal faith before others (CCC 14). Peter, who denied the Lord three times, was restored by the risen Christ and later gave his life for him (John 21:15–19). For anyone who returns, denial is not the last word.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

Our West Syriac tradition fills the days of Hevoro week with the joy of the Resurrection, and on this Friday it remembers all the Confessors, the men and women who confessed Christ before others by their life and often by their death. Many of their names are known only to God. The Malankara Church keeps this day in thanksgiving for them, from the first centuries to our own time, and in prayer for all who suffer for their faith in many lands today, and for those who make them suffer.

For study and teaching

  • Where am I tempted to keep my faith “in the dark”, at work, among friends or online? What would it mean to acknowledge Christ there, simply and kindly?
  • Which confessor of the faith, from the Scriptures, the saints or my own family, gives me courage? What did that person fear less than I do?
