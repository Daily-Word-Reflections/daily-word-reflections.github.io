Friday of the first week after Pentecost

Readings

  • Epistle 1 Thessalonians 3:1–5 · Timothy sent to strengthen and encourage you, so that no one would be shaken
  • Gospel Mark 12:41–44 · The poor widow put in everything she had, all she had to live on

Word for the day

“she out of her poverty has put in everything she had, all she had to live on.” Mark 12:44

Reflection

St Paul had founded the church in Thessalonica in a hurry. Within weeks trouble broke out, and he had to leave the city by night, leaving behind a handful of new believers who had only just been baptized. He worried about them constantly. In today's Epistle he tells them what he did: “Therefore when we could bear it no longer, we decided to be left alone in Athens; and we sent Timothy, our brother and co-worker for God in proclaiming the gospel of Christ, to strengthen and encourage you for the sake of your faith, so that no one would be shaken by these persecutions.” Paul gives up the company he needed so that they might have the help they needed. He sends his best helper. This is what a pastor's heart looks like, and it is what the Church still does: a bishop sends a priest to a distant parish, a parish sends catechists to the children, a family sends a son or daughter to serve the Church in a far place. Paul is honest about the cost of the Christian life: “Indeed, you yourselves know that this is what we are destined for. In fact, when we were with you, we told you beforehand that we were to suffer persecution; so it turned out, as you know.” He never sold the gospel as an easy road. On this Friday, the day our Church keeps in memory of the Cross, we remember that every disciple walks behind a crucified Lord.

Paul's fear is not that they will suffer, but that they will give up: “For this reason, when I could bear it no longer, I sent to find out about your faith; I was afraid that somehow the tempter had tempted you and that our labor had been in vain.” The tempter rarely needs to make us deny Christ in one dramatic moment. More often he works by tiredness and discouragement: the long illness, the unemployment, the mocking at work, the slow cooling of prayer. Against this the Church does what Paul did. She sends people to strengthen one another. Every one of us can be a Timothy to someone: a phone call to a family going through trouble, a visit to a young person who has stopped coming to the Qurbono, an hour with a neighbour who has lost heart. Hebrews says: “let us consider how to provoke one another to love and good deeds.” Encouragement is not a small thing. It can keep a faith alive.

The Gospel moves from Paul's anxious love to a quiet scene in the temple. Jesus “sat down opposite the treasury, and watched the crowd putting money into the treasury. Many rich people put in large sums. A poor widow came and put in two small copper coins, which are worth a penny.” No one else notices her. But Jesus calls his disciples and says: “Truly I tell you, this poor widow has put in more than all those who are contributing to the treasury. For all of them have contributed out of their abundance; but she out of her poverty has put in everything she had, all she had to live on.” God does not measure the gift by its size but by what it costs and by the trust it shows. The widow gave her whole living, and in doing so she placed herself in God's hands. St Cyprian of Carthage, urging his people to give to the poor, held up this widow to the wealthy as a reproach and an example: if she, poor and alone, gave all she had, what excuse have those who have plenty (On Works and Almsgiving 15, in substance)? The Catechism sees in her the poverty of heart that Jesus asks of his disciples (CCC 2544). And on this Friday we see in her a picture of the Lord himself, who a few days later in the same city gave not two coins but his whole life on the Cross. When we bring our offering to the altar, whether it is large or small, let it be a sign that we give ourselves.

Homily outline

Theme: “She out of her poverty has put in everything she had, all she had to live on.”

On Friday of the first week after Pentecost, the day of the Cross: Timothy sent to strengthen the shaken, and the widow who gave her whole living.

1. Sent to strengthen and encourage

  • Paul gives up his best helper.
  • A pastor's anxious love.
  • Every Christian can be a Timothy.
“Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble knees.” Isaiah 35:3
“And let us consider how to provoke one another to love and good deeds” Hebrews 10:24

Apply: Write or call one person this week who needs encouragement in faith.

2. So that no one would be shaken

  • The honest cost of discipleship.
  • The tempter of discouragement.
  • Faith kept alive by companionship.
“He alone is my rock and my salvation, my fortress; I shall not be shaken.” Psalm 62:6
“Indeed, all who want to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted.” 2 Timothy 3:12

Apply: Pray each day this week for Christians who suffer for their faith, naming one country where they are persecuted.

3. Everything she had

  • Two small copper coins.
  • God measures by cost and trust.
  • The Lord who gave his whole life on the Cross.
“For all things come from you, and of your own have we given you.” 1 Chronicles 29:14
“Each of you must give as you have made up your mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.” 2 Corinthians 9:7

Apply: Set aside your offering for the poor first, before other spending, as a sign that God comes first.

The Church strengthens the shaken by sending its members to one another.

The widow's two coins point to the Cross, where the Lord gave everything.

Take-home resolution: Encourage someone who is shaken, and give God not your leftovers but yourself.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul could not stop worrying about the new Christians in Thessalonica. So he sent Timothy, our brother and co-worker for God in proclaiming the gospel of Christ, to strengthen and encourage you for the sake of your faith, so that no one would be shaken by these persecutions (1 Thessalonians 3:2–3).

Paul is afraid of one thing: that somehow the tempter had tempted you and that our labor had been in vain (1 Thessalonians 3:5). The tempter often works through tiredness and discouragement. And the Church answers by sending people to strengthen one another. Each of us can be a Timothy for someone this week.

In the Gospel Jesus watches people giving money in the temple. Many rich people put in large sums. A poor widow came and put in two small copper coins (Mark 12:41–42). And Jesus says: for all of them have contributed out of their abundance; but she out of her poverty has put in everything she had, all she had to live on (Mark 12:44). God sees what no one else sees.

Today is Friday, the day of the Cross. On the Cross Jesus gave not two coins but his whole life. When we bring our offering to the Holy Qurbono, let us bring ourselves with it. And let us go out to encourage someone who is shaken. Amen.

For children

Children, one day Jesus sat in the temple and watched people putting money in the offering box. Rich people put in lots of money, clink, clink, clink! Then a poor lady put in just two tiny coins. Jesus said: she gave the most of all! Why? Because she gave everything she had. God does not count how big our gift is. He looks at how much love is in it. What can you give to God with all your love?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, on the Cross you gave your whole life for us, holding nothing back. Teach us to give as the poor widow gave, not from what we do not need but from what we have, trusting in the Father's care. Make us like Timothy, sent to strengthen and encourage those who are shaken by trials. Guard all who suffer for your name from the tempter's discouragement, and let our offering at the altar be the offering of our whole selves. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
