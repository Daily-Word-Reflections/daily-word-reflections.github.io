Word for the day

“So, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God.” 1 Corinthians 10:31

Reflection

St Paul has been answering a question about meat that had been offered to idols, and at the end he lifts the whole discussion to a height: “So, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God.” Nothing is too small for this. The meal, the work, the journey, the hours of the day that no one sees can be offered to God and made into praise. Paul then adds a rule for the common life: “Give no offense to Jews or to Greeks or to the church of God, just as I try to please everyone in everything I do, not seeking my own advantage, but that of many, so that they may be saved.” He seeks the good of others, in the hope that they will be saved. And the model is not himself but the Lord: “Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ.” An imitator is not a copy but a pupil, one who has watched someone close to Christ and has learned how to walk.

The Gospel is harder. Large crowds are travelling with Jesus, and he turns and speaks to them without softening anything: “Whoever comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters, yes, and even life itself, cannot be my disciple. Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” The word “hate” is the strong speech of the East, which the Lord himself explains elsewhere: “Whoever loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me” (Matthew 10:37). He asks for the first place, not for the destruction of the family; the love of father and mother is a commandment, but no love may stand in the way of Christ. Then he gives two small stories: the builder who “does not first sit down and estimate the cost” and is laughed at when he cannot finish the tower, and the king who counts his ten thousand against twenty thousand and sends for the terms of peace. The Lord is not driving the crowd away; he wants them to choose with open eyes. Following him is neither a mood nor a bargain. “None of you can become my disciple if you do not give up all your possessions.”

Both readings come alive in St Ignatius of Loyola. Íñigo López was born in 1491 in the Basque country of Spain, the youngest of a large noble family, and trained to be a soldier. In 1521 a cannonball broke his leg at the siege of Pamplona. During the long weeks of recovery he asked for adventure stories, and in the castle there were only a life of Christ and the lives of the saints. He noticed that when he dreamed of fame he felt empty afterwards, but when he thought of doing for God what the saints had done he stayed content. That was the beginning of his conversion. At Manresa he prayed and wrote the notes that became the Spiritual Exercises, he went as a pilgrim to Jerusalem, and in Paris he gathered companions, among them Francis Xavier and Peter Faber. On the hill of Montmartre in 1534 they made their vows together. Pope Paul III approved the Society of Jesus in 1540, and Ignatius, elected its first Superior General, spent his last years in Rome writing letters and its constitutions. He died on 31 July 1556 and was canonised in 1622 together with Francis Xavier. His motto, taken from today's Epistle, is Ad maiorem Dei gloriam: to the greater glory of God.

The Exercises begin with the sentence that sums up what Paul says: human beings are created to praise, reverence and serve God, and by this means to save their souls. Everything else is for the sake of that end. Ignatius taught people to find God in all things, to look back each evening on the day in a short prayer called the examen, and to weigh in prayer the choices of their lives; this is how he put into practice Jesus' call to estimate the cost before building. His friend Xavier reached Goa in 1542 and travelled the coast of India. He went among the fishing villages in the south, including the shore of Travancore, and he is known to have prayed at Mylapore, where the tradition of the Thomas Christians places the tomb of the Apostle Thomas, so the first Jesuit of India honoured the same Apostle whom we remembered on 3 July. Many of our own children and grandchildren have studied in schools and colleges that Jesuits founded in Kerala. The lesson of Ignatius is for every one of us, in every state of life: ask what the Lord wants, count the cost, and then give the work, the meal and the ordinary day to the greater glory of God.

Homily outline

Theme: “Do everything for the glory of God.”

On Saturday of the tenth week after Pentecost, with St Ignatius of Loyola: the ordinary day given to God, the cost of discipleship, and the soldier who found a greater cause.

1. Everything for the glory of God

  • Eating, drinking, working: nothing is too small.
  • Seeking the good of many, that they may be saved.
  • The motto: to the greater glory of God.
“Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory, for the sake of your steadfast love and your faithfulness.” Psalm 115:1
“And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.” Colossians 3:17

Apply: Before each meal and each task today say: To the greater glory of God.

2. Count the cost

  • The builder and the king.
  • Hate father and mother: the first place for Christ.
  • Following him with open eyes.
“By wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established;” Proverbs 24:3
“For which of you, intending to build a tower, does not first sit down and estimate the cost, to see whether he has enough to complete it?” Luke 14:28

Apply: Think of one good work you have begun and ask the Lord for what you need to finish it.

3. St Ignatius of Loyola

  • Wounded at Pamplona in 1521.
  • The lives of Christ and the saints.
  • The Spiritual Exercises and the Society of Jesus.
“What shall I return to the Lord for all his bounty to me?” Psalm 116:12
“For what will it profit them if they gain the whole world but forfeit their life? Or what will they give in return for their life?” Matthew 16:26

Apply: Make the examen tonight: five minutes of looking back over the day with thanks.

4. Imitators of Christ

  • Carry the cross and follow.
  • Francis Xavier on the coast of India.
  • Every state of life is called.
“Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” And I said, “Here am I; send me!”” Isaiah 6:8
“Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ.” 1 Corinthians 11:1

Apply: Choose one good example of a believer you know and imitate it this week.

Paul asks us to do everything for the glory of God, and the Lord asks us to count the cost before we follow him.

St Ignatius gave his whole life to that greater glory, and sent Francis Xavier to the coast of India.

Take-home resolution: Do everything for the glory of God, count the cost, and carry the cross after the Lord.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul gives us a sentence that can fill every day: whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God (1 Corinthians 10:31). Nothing is too small, the meal, the work, the journey. And he adds: not seeking my own advantage, but that of many, so that they may be saved; be imitators of me, as I am of Christ (1 Corinthians 10:33–11:1).

In the Gospel Jesus speaks to large crowds, and he is not gentle. Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple (Luke 14:27). He asks us to count the cost, like a builder and a king. He wants the crowd to choose with open eyes. The strong word about hating father and mother means that no love may stand before Christ; elsewhere he says that whoever loves father or mother more than him is not worthy of him.

Today the Panchangam remembers St Ignatius of Loyola. He was a soldier, wounded at Pamplona in 1521. In bed he read about Jesus and the saints, and he gave his life to God. He wrote the Spiritual Exercises, gathered companions, among them Francis Xavier, and founded the Society of Jesus in 1540. He died in 1556. His motto is Ad maiorem Dei gloriam: to the greater glory of God. Xavier brought the Gospel to the coast of India.

So let us begin each task with the words, To the greater glory of God. Let us count the cost of our promises, and let us carry the cross after the Lord. Amen.

For children

Children, do you remember how to say your prayer before you eat? St Paul says that even eating and drinking can be done for the glory of God. St Ignatius, whom we remember today, was a soldier who got hurt and read about Jesus while he was in bed. He promised to do everything for the greater glory of God. Before you start your homework or your game, say very quietly, “For you, Jesus.” Every little thing becomes a gift.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you called St Ignatius from the battlefield to follow you, and you made him a servant of your greater glory. Teach us to do everything, eating and drinking and all our work, for the glory of God. Help us to count the cost and to carry the cross, that we may be your true disciples and imitators of you. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
