Word for the day

““What do you think of the Messiah? Whose son is he?” They said to him, “The son of David.”” Matthew 22:42

Reflection

Three days after Christmas the Church remembers David, the shepherd boy of Bethlehem who became king, poet and prophet. God promised him a house and a throne forever. Jesus, born in David's town, asks the Pharisees: “What do you think of the Messiah? Whose son is he?” They answer, “The son of David.” Then he quotes Psalm 110, where David calls the Messiah “my Lord”. How can he be both David's son and David's Lord? Only if he is the eternal Son of God who became David's descendant. The mystery of Christmas is hidden in that question.

Hebrews lists the heroes of faith, among them David, “who through faith conquered kingdoms, administered justice, obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions.” Others suffered mockery, chains and death. “Yet all these, though they were commended for their faith, did not receive what was promised”: they were waiting for Christ, and for us. The child of Bethlehem fulfils all their hopes.

David and the Messiah.

  • David calls the Messiah “my lord”.
  • Jesus is both son and Lord.

  • David faced Goliath in the Lord's name.

David's hope is fulfilled in Bethlehem.

Full homily

Dear friends, today our Church remembers David, king and prophet. The Letter to the Hebrews lists him among the heroes of faith: Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David and Samuel and the prophets, who through faith conquered kingdoms, administered justice, obtained promises (Hebrews 11:32–33).

Hebrews also remembers those who suffered: they were stoned, sawn in two, killed by the sword; they went about in skins of sheep and goats, destitute, persecuted, tormented, of whom the world was not worthy (Hebrews 11:37–38). Faith does not always win in this world; but it always wins with God.

David was a great sinner and a great penitent. He committed adultery and murder, yet when the prophet Nathan confronted him, he repented with all his heart. His psalm of repentance, Psalm 51, is prayed by the Church every day. God promised that the Messiah would come from his line.

In the Gospel Jesus asks the Pharisees: what do you think of the Messiah? Whose son is he? They answer: the son of David. Jesus asks: how is it then that David by the Spirit calls him Lord? (Matthew 22:42–43). The Messiah is David's son, but also David's Lord: truly man and truly God.

The Child of Yaldo is born in the city of David as son of David, yet he is David's Lord and ours. In him the promises to David are fulfilled.

Let us learn from David both his faith and his repentance. When we fall, let us not hide, but return to God with a contrite heart: have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love (Psalm 51:1). Amen.

For children

Children, David was a shepherd boy from Bethlehem who became a king. Jesus, born in Bethlehem too, is our King and Shepherd!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Son of David and David's Lord, you fulfil every promise made to the fathers. Give us David's faith and his love for prayer, and keep us faithful until we receive all you have promised. Amen.
