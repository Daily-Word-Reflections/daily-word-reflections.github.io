Word for the day

“This is the disciple who is testifying to these things and has written them, and we know that his testimony is true.” John 21:24

Reflection

Today the Panchangam remembers Mar Yohannan Shleeha, St John the Apostle, son of Zebedee and brother of Mar Yaqob, whom the Panchangam remembered on 30 April. He was a fisherman of Galilee, called with his brother from their father's boat; with Peter and James he saw the raising of Jairus' daughter, the Transfiguration and the agony in Gethsemane. The Church's tradition, from St Irenaeus of Lyons in the second century, recognises him as “the disciple whom Jesus loved,” who leaned on the Lord's breast at the Supper and later gave the Church the fourth Gospel while living at Ephesus (Against Heresies III.1.1, in substance). Many Eastern Churches keep his memory on this day in May. The Gospel shows him in the last scene by the lake after the Resurrection. Jesus has just asked Peter three times, “Do you love me?” and told him to feed his sheep. Then “Peter turned and saw the disciple whom Jesus loved following them.” Peter asks, “Lord, what about him?” and Jesus answers, “If it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? Follow me!” Each disciple has his own road. Peter will glorify God by a martyr's death; John will remain long, bearing witness. We are not to measure our call against another's. The only question that matters is the one Jesus puts to each of us: will you follow me?

The Gospel ends with John's own seal: “This is the disciple who is testifying to these things and has written them, and we know that his testimony is true.” The beloved disciple is above all a witness. He stood at the Cross when others had fled, he ran to the empty tomb and “saw and believed,” and he recognised the risen Lord on the shore before anyone else: “It is the Lord!” Love made him see. St Paul's words in the Epistle could stand over John's whole life: “Who will separate us from the love of Christ? Will hardship, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword?” And the answer: “No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us.” Nothing in all creation, “neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor rulers, nor things present, nor things to come,” can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord. St Jerome tells that when John was very old at Ephesus and could no longer walk, he was carried into the assembly and would say only, “Little children, love one another.” When asked why he always repeated it, he answered that it was the Lord's command, and if this alone were done, it would be enough (Commentary on Galatians 6:10, in substance). The apostle of love ended as he began, resting on the heart of Christ.

From the Cross Jesus entrusted his Mother to this disciple: “Here is your mother,” and “from that hour the disciple took her into his own home” (John 19:27). It is fitting, then, that today the Panchangam also remembers the founding of the Congregation of the Daughters of Mary, the Mary Makkal, on 8 May 1938 at Marthandam, in the time of the Servant of God Archbishop Mar Ivanios. Like John, the sisters took the Mother of the Lord into their home and their name. For nearly ninety years they have gone to the villages and to the poor in Kerala, Tamil Nadu and far beyond, in mission, teaching, nursing and the care of the forgotten, bearing quiet witness that nothing can separate us from the love of Christ. Thank God today for the sisters you know, pray for vocations to religious life in our Church, and ask Mar Yohannan to teach you his two lessons: to rest on the heart of Christ in prayer, and to take Mary, the Mother of God, into your home. As we wait in these days for the Holy Spirit, we wait as the apostles did, “constantly devoting themselves to prayer” with Mary the mother of Jesus (Acts 1:14).

Homily outline

Theme: “Who will separate us from the love of Christ?”

On the memory of Mar Yohannan Shleeha and the founding of the Daughters of Mary, in the days of waiting for the Spirit: the beloved disciple, love that nothing can defeat, and the Mother taken into his home.

1. The disciple whom Jesus loved

  • Resting on the heart of Christ.
  • “What is that to you? Follow me!”
  • A witness whose testimony is true.
“Set me as a seal upon your heart, as a seal upon your arm; for love is strong as death,” Song of Songs 8:6
“God is love, and those who abide in love abide in God, and God abides in them.” 1 John 4:16

Apply: Spend ten minutes in silent prayer this week simply resting in Christ's love, without asking for anything.

2. Nothing can separate us

  • Hardship, distress, persecution.
  • More than conquerors.
  • Neither death nor life.
“When you pass through the waters, I will be with you; and through the rivers, they shall not overwhelm you;” Isaiah 43:2
“I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one will snatch them out of my hand.” John 10:28

Apply: Write Romans 8:35 on a card and give it to someone who is suffering.

3. He took her into his own home

  • The Mother given from the Cross.
  • The Daughters of Mary, 1938.
  • Waiting for the Spirit with Mary.
“Where you go, I will go; where you lodge, I will lodge; your people shall be my people, and your God my God.” Ruth 1:16
“When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved standing beside her, he said to his mother, “Woman, here is your son.”” John 19:26

Apply: Pray a Marian prayer as a family each evening until Pentecost, and pray for religious vocations.

Mar Yohannan teaches us to rest on the heart of Christ and to love one another.

Like him and like the Daughters of Mary, we take the Mother of the Lord into our home and wait with her for the Spirit.

Take-home resolution: Follow Jesus without comparing, trust that nothing can separate you from his love, and welcome Mary into your home.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today the Panchangam remembers Mar Yohannan Shleeha, St John the Apostle, whom tradition knows as the disciple whom Jesus loved. He leaned on the Lord's breast at the Supper, he stood at the Cross, and he was the first to recognise the risen Lord on the shore.

In the Gospel Peter sees John and asks: Lord, what about him? Jesus answers: if it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? Follow me! (John 21:21–22). We are not to compare our road with another's. Each of us has one task: to follow Jesus.

St Paul asks: who will separate us from the love of Christ? Hardship, distress, persecution, famine, sword? No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us (Romans 8:35, 37). John lived this. In his old age, St Jerome tells us, he would say only: little children, love one another.

From the Cross Jesus gave his Mother to John, and John took her into his home. Today we also thank God for the Daughters of Mary, founded on this day in 1938, who serve the poor in the name of Mary. Let us take Mary into our homes, and with her wait in prayer for the Holy Spirit. Amen.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Today we remember St John. He was one of Jesus' best friends! At the Last Supper he sat right next to Jesus. On Good Friday, when many friends ran away, John stayed near the Cross with Mother Mary. Jesus said to him, “Here is your mother,” and John took care of her. When John was very old, he kept saying, “Little children, love one another.” Let us say it too, and do it!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, you loved Mar Yohannan Shleeha and let him rest on your heart; from the Cross you gave him your Mother, and after your Resurrection you called him to follow you. By his prayers make us witnesses whose testimony is true, convinced that nothing can separate us from your love. Bless the Daughters of Mary and all consecrated women, and give our Church many generous vocations. Teach us to take your Mother into our homes, and to wait with her in prayer for the Holy Spirit. You live and reign for ever. Amen.
