Word for the day

“Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean.” Luke 5:12

Reflection

The Sundays of the Great Fast in our tradition follow Christ the physician: the leper, the paralytic, the Canaanite woman's daughter, the bent woman, the man born blind. Lent is a season of healing. Today a man “covered with leprosy” falls on his face before Jesus and says: “Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean.” Jesus “stretched out his hand, touched him, and said, ‘I do choose. Be made clean.’ Immediately the leprosy left him.”

Leprosy in the Bible was more than a disease. The leper had to live outside the camp, cry “Unclean!”, and could touch no one. Jesus does what no one would do: he touches him. The Law said that touching a leper made you unclean; with Jesus, the opposite happens: his touch makes the leper clean. This is what Lent offers us: the touch of Christ on the parts of our life we hide from others.

Naaman, the proud Syrian general, was told by Elisha to wash seven times in the Jordan. At first he was angry: was this not too simple? But when he obeyed, “his flesh was restored like the flesh of a young boy, and he was clean.” Healing requires humility. The leper in the Gospel knelt; Naaman went down into the river. In Lent we kneel in confession and go down into the waters of repentance.

Isaiah prays: “O LORD, be gracious to us; we wait for you. Be our arm every morning, our salvation in the time of trouble.” Jesus himself, after healing the leper, “would withdraw to deserted places and pray.” Healing and prayer go together. Let us bring to Christ in this Fast whatever in us is unclean, and hear him say: “I do choose. Be made clean.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean.”

The Great Fast leads us through Sundays of healing. The first is the leper: a man shut out of community, who dares to come to Jesus. Leprosy is an image of sin, which isolates us from God and from one another.

1. Sin isolates

  • The Law made the leper live alone, outside the camp.
  • Sin does the same: it breaks our communion with God, family and Church.
  • The leper does not hide; he falls on his face before Jesus and asks.
“He shall remain unclean as long as he has the disease; he is unclean. He shall live alone; his dwelling shall be outside the camp.” Leviticus 13:46
“There was a man covered with leprosy. When he saw Jesus, he bowed with his face to the ground and begged him, “Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean.”” Luke 5:12

Apply: Name the sin that isolates you, and bring it humbly to the Lord.

2. Jesus touches the untouchable

  • No one touched lepers; Jesus stretches out his hand and touches him.
  • His answer is clear: “I do choose.” God wants our healing.
  • The Psalmist knows the connection: “heal me, for I have sinned against you.”
“As for me, I said, “O LORD, be gracious to me; heal me, for I have sinned against you.”” Psalm 41:4
“Then Jesus stretched out his hand, touched him, and said, “I do choose. Be made clean.” Immediately the leprosy left him.” Luke 5:13

Apply: Reach out this week to someone others avoid: the sick, the shamed, the lonely.

3. Healing through humble obedience

  • Naaman wanted a dramatic cure; he was told simply to wash in the Jordan.
  • When he obeyed, his flesh became like a child's.
  • Jesus sends the healed man to the priest: today Christ heals our sins through the priest in Confession.
“So he went down and immersed himself seven times in the Jordan, according to the word of the man of God; his flesh was restored like the flesh of a young boy, and he was clean.” 2 Kings 5:14
““Go,” he said, “and show yourself to the priest, and, as Moses commanded, make an offering for your cleansing, for a testimony to them.”” Luke 5:14

Apply: Make a good confession during this Great Fast.

What the leper said, we can say: “Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean.”

Christ chooses. Let us come to him, let him touch us, and go home clean.

Take-home resolution: Prepare an examination of conscience this week and go to confession.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, in our tradition every Sunday of the Great Fast shows us Christ the physician. Today a leper comes to him. In those days a leper lived outside the village, had to cry “Unclean!” when anyone came near, and could touch no one. This man kneels and says: “Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean.”

Then Jesus does something no one else would do. He stretches out his hand and touches him. “I do choose. Be made clean.” By the Law, touching a leper made a person unclean. But with Jesus the opposite happens: his touch makes the leper clean. That is what Lent offers each of us: the touch of Christ on the part of our life we hide from everyone.

Our first reading tells of Naaman, a proud Syrian general with leprosy. The prophet told him simply to wash seven times in the Jordan. He was offended: is that all? But when he humbled himself and obeyed, his flesh became like a child's. Healing needs humility. For us, the Jordan is the sacrament of Confession. It seems too simple. But it heals.

After healing the leper, Jesus withdrew to deserted places to pray. Let us make room for prayer in this Fast, make a good Confession, and reach out to the “lepers” of our time: the sick, the lonely, those everyone avoids. And let us hear Jesus say to us: “I do choose. Be made clean.” Amen.

For children

Children, long ago people with a skin disease called leprosy had to live alone. Nobody would touch them or play with them.

One day a man with leprosy came to Jesus and said: “If you want, you can heal me.” Jesus touched him and said: “I want to. Be healed!” And he was healed!

Is there someone at school whom nobody plays with? Be kind to them, like Jesus. Let's say: “Jesus, make me kind!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus, physician of souls and bodies, you touched the leper and made him clean. Stretch out your hand over us in this Fast: cleanse what is hidden, heal what is wounded, and give us the humility of Naaman and the faith of the leper. Amen.
