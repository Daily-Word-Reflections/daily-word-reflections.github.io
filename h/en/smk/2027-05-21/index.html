Word for the day

“I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.” Acts 3:6

Reflection

On the first Friday after Pentecost our Church keeps a day with a beautiful name: Golden Friday. It remembers the first miracle that the apostles worked in the name of Jesus after the Holy Spirit came upon them, the healing of the man lame from birth at the gate of the temple called the Beautiful Gate. The name is usually explained from St Peter's own words that day: “I have no silver or gold.” The Church has no gold to give, and yet the day is golden, because what Peter had was worth more than all the gold of the temple. Five days after Pentecost, the readings set before us three kinds of wealth: the power of the name of Jesus in Acts, the gold of love in St Paul, and the treasure in heaven of those who leave everything in the Gospel. And the Panchangam joins to them the memory of St Helena, a queen who had all the gold of an empire at her disposal and spent it for Christ. One with no gold heals; one with much gold gives. Both show that the only wealth that lasts is the wealth that is placed in the hands of Jesus.

The story in Acts is told with great care. “One day Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, at three o'clock in the afternoon.” The apostles, filled with the Spirit, still keep the hours of prayer; it is the ninth hour, the hour at which our own Shehimo still calls us to pray, the hour at which the Lord died on the Cross. “And a man lame from birth was being carried in. People would lay him daily at the gate of the temple called the Beautiful Gate so that he could ask for alms from those entering the temple.” He has never stood. He lives at the edge of the holy place, at the gate but never inside. “Peter looked intently at him, as did John, and said, ‘Look at us.’ And he fixed his attention on them, expecting to receive something from them.” He expects a coin. “But Peter said, ‘I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.’ And he took him by the right hand and raised him up; and immediately his feet and ankles were made strong.” St John Chrysostom remarks that the man asked for a few coins and received his feet, and with them the gift of walking into the house of God (Homilies on Acts 8, in substance). “Jumping up, he stood and began to walk, and he entered the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God.” The prophecy of Isaiah has come true before the eyes of Jerusalem: “then the lame shall leap like a deer.” The man who sat outside the gate now goes in. That is what the name of Jesus does.

St Paul tells us where the gold of the Church really lies. “If I speak in the tongues of mortals and of angels, but do not have love, I am a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. And if I have prophetic powers, and understand all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so as to remove mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing. If I give away all my possessions, and if I hand over my body so that I may boast, but do not have love, I gain nothing.” Even miracles, even generosity, even martyrdom, are empty without love. Peter's miracle was not a display of power; it was love that stopped, looked, touched and lifted. “Love is patient; love is kind; love is not envious or boastful or arrogant or rude. It does not insist on its own way; it is not irritable or resentful; it does not rejoice in wrongdoing, but rejoices in the truth.” St Augustine summed it up in a famous line: love, and do what you will; for if the root of love is within, nothing but good can grow from it (Homilies on the First Letter of John 7.8, in substance). The healing at the Beautiful Gate is the first gold of the Church after Pentecost, and it is the gold of love.

The Gospel brings the same question home. “Truly I tell you, it will be hard for a rich person to enter the kingdom of heaven. Again I tell you, it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for someone who is rich to enter the kingdom of God.” The disciples are astounded: “Then who can be saved?” Jesus looks at them: “For mortals it is impossible, but for God all things are possible.” Then Peter speaks, the same Peter who will one day say at the Beautiful Gate that he has no silver or gold: “Look, we have left everything and followed you. What then will we have?” Jesus promises a hundredfold and eternal life. Peter had left his boat and his nets; at the temple gate he discovered that he was richer than any merchant in Jerusalem. St Ambrose tells how the deacon St Lawrence, ordered to hand over the treasures of the Church, gathered the poor and said: these are the treasures of the Church (On the Duties of the Clergy 2.28, in substance). St Helena understood this too. Eusebius records that on her pilgrimage to the Holy Land, as an old woman, she built churches at Bethlehem and on the Mount of Olives and gave generously to the poor wherever she went (Life of Constantine 3.41–44, in substance). So Golden Friday asks each of us a direct question. What is my gold, and whose hands is it in? Perhaps I have no money to give; then give what you have: a hand to lift someone, a visit, a word spoken in the name of Jesus. Perhaps I have more than I need; then remember the camel and the needle, and let your wealth build up the house of God and lift up the poor. For God all things are possible, even a rich heart made poor enough to enter the kingdom.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear friends, today is Golden Friday, the first Friday after Pentecost. Our Church remembers the first miracle the apostles worked in the name of Jesus after receiving the Spirit. Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, at three o'clock in the afternoon (Acts 3:1), and at the Beautiful Gate lay a man lame from birth, asking for alms.

Peter looked intently at him and said, Look at us. The man expected a coin. But Peter said, I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk (Acts 3:6). He took him by the right hand and raised him up, and the man entered the temple, walking and leaping and praising God (Acts 3:8). The one who had sat outside the gate all his life now went in. That is the gold of the Church: the name of Jesus.

St Paul tells us that this gold is love. If I have all faith, so as to remove mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing (1 Corinthians 13:2). Love is patient; love is kind (1 Corinthians 13:4). Peter's miracle was love that stopped, looked, touched and lifted. Without love, even our good works are only a noisy gong.

In the Gospel Jesus warns that it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for someone who is rich to enter the kingdom of God; but for God all things are possible (Matthew 19:24, 26). Peter left everything, and found himself richer than anyone. St Helena, whom we also remember today, spent the riches of an empire on churches and on the poor. So let us ask: what is my gold, and whose hands is it in? Let us give what we have, in the name of Jesus. Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, today is Golden Friday, when our Church remembers the first miracle of the apostles after Pentecost. Saint Luke begins the story very simply: one day Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, at three o'clock in the afternoon (Acts 3:1). The Spirit had come down with wind and fire, but the apostles did not abandon the ordinary rhythm of prayer. They still went up at the fixed hour.

Prayer at fixed hours is very old. The prophet Daniel, even under threat of death, got down on his knees three times a day to pray to his God and praise him (Daniel 6:10). Our own Syriac tradition has kept the hours of prayer, morning and evening and through the day. The Holy Spirit does not make fixed prayer unnecessary. He fills it with life.

Notice also that Peter and John went together. Saint Luke says of the first Christians: day by day, as they spent much time together in the temple, they broke bread at home (Acts 2:46). The Psalm sings: I was glad when they said to me, let us go to the house of the Lord! (Psalm 122:1). Prayer is personal, but it is never private in the sense of being alone. We pray as a Church, and families pray together.

And see what happened at the gate. A man lame from birth had been carried there every day. He sat outside, at the door of the house of God, but he never went in. After Peter raised him up, he entered the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God (Acts 3:8). The Psalm says: I would rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God than live in the tents of wickedness (Psalm 84:10). The first fruit of the Spirit-filled Church was to bring someone from outside the gate to the inside.

So on this Golden Friday let us keep fixed times of prayer, morning and evening. Let us pray together as families. And let us think of someone who sits outside the gate, who once came to the Holy Qurbono and no longer does, and invite them to come in with us. That is the gold the Church has to give. Amen.

Dear friends, today's Gospel comes right after a sad scene. A rich young man had asked Jesus what he must do to have eternal life, and when Jesus told him to sell his possessions and follow him, he went away grieving, for he had many possessions. Then Jesus said: it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for someone who is rich to enter the kingdom of God (Matthew 19:24).

Peter, always quick to speak, asks the question that is in all our hearts: look, we have left everything and followed you. What then will we have? (Matthew 19:27). It is an honest question. Abraham too had left his country and his father's house at God's word (Genesis 12:1). Following Christ costs something. Every disciple has left something behind.

Jesus does not scold Peter. He makes a promise: everyone who has left houses or brothers or sisters or father or mother or children or fields, for my name's sake, will receive a hundredfold, and will inherit eternal life (Matthew 19:29). In Genesis we read that Isaac sowed seed in that land, and in the same year reaped a hundredfold (Genesis 26:12). What is the hundredfold? It is the new family Jesus gives us in the Church: brothers and sisters, fathers and mothers in faith, a home in every parish. And beyond all this, eternal life.

Then Jesus adds: many who are first will be last, and the last will be first (Matthew 19:30). Hannah sang the same truth: he raises up the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap (1 Samuel 2:8). On Golden Friday we remember that a poor fisherman, who had no silver or gold, raised a lame man in the name of Jesus. Peter, who had left his nets, had become richer than any king.

So let us ask: what is holding me back from following Christ fully? Let us give one thing up this week for his sake. Let us thank God for the family he has given us in the Church. And let us serve someone whom others overlook, because in God's kingdom the last will be first. Amen.

For children

Children, today is called Golden Friday! Do you know why? After the Holy Spirit came, Peter and John met a man who could not walk. He asked them for money. Peter said, I have no silver or gold, but in the name of Jesus, stand up and walk! And the man jumped up and danced into the temple, praising God! Peter had no gold, but he had Jesus, and that is better than all the gold in the world. What can you give someone today, even if you have no money? A smile, a helping hand, a prayer!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, after the coming of your Holy Spirit your apostles Peter and John healed the lame man at the Beautiful Gate, not with silver or gold but in your holy name. On this Golden Friday make us rich in what truly matters. Give us eyes to look intently at those who sit at the gate, hands to lift them up, and hearts full of the love without which we are nothing. Loosen our grip on what we own, so that like St Helena we may spend it for your glory and for the poor, and like your apostles leave everything to follow you. For with God all things are possible. With your Father and your Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
