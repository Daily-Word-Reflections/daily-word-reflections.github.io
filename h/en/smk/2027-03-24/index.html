Word for the day

“But as it is, he has appeared once for all at the end of the age to remove sin by the sacrifice of himself.” Hebrews 9:26

Reflection

Hasha Wednesday brings us to one of the saddest sentences in the Gospel: “Then one of the twelve, who was called Judas Iscariot, went to the chief priests and said, ‘What will you give me if I betray him to you?’ They paid him thirty pieces of silver. And from that moment he began to look for an opportunity to betray him.” From very early times Christians kept Wednesday as a day of fasting, and ancient Church orders explain it as the day on which the betrayal was agreed (in substance). Notice that the betrayer is “one of the twelve,” a friend who had eaten with Jesus, heard him and been sent by him. The danger to the Lord came from within his own circle; the danger to our faith comes most often from within our own hearts. Thirty shekels was the price set in the Law for a slave (Exodus 21:32), and Zechariah had spoken of a shepherd valued at “thirty shekels of silver.” Our Lord lets himself be priced like a slave so that slaves may be set free. The Church teaches that the Passion cannot be blamed on any people as a whole: all sinners were the authors of his suffering, and we too (Catechism 597–598). The question for us is not only what Judas did, but what price I set on Jesus when he costs me something.

Ezekiel's oracle sounds like the clash of iron: “A sword, a sword! Drawn for slaughter, polished to consume, to flash like lightning.” It is a word of judgment on the Ammonites and on everyone who lives by false visions and lies. Just before it the prophet says that the crown will be removed “until he comes whose right it is; to him I will give it,” and Christian readers have long heard in this the coming of Christ, the true King. Then comes the strange command: “Return it to its sheath!” In the garden, a few hours after Judas has made his bargain, Jesus will say to Peter: “Put your sword back into its sheath. Am I not to drink the cup that the Father has given me?” The sword of judgment does not fall on us; the Son takes the cup instead. The Letter to the Hebrews explains what he does with that cup. Christ “entered into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf.” He does not offer himself again and again like the high priest year by year: “he has appeared once for all at the end of the age to remove sin by the sacrifice of himself,” and “will appear a second time, not to deal with sin, but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him.” In every Holy Qurbono this one sacrifice is made present on the altar; it is not repeated, but it is truly offered for us (Catechism 1366–1367).

Tonight is the eve of Pesaha, and it helps a family to know what tomorrow holds. In the Holy Qurbono of Pesaha the Church remembers the night on which the Lord gave us his Body and Blood and instituted the priesthood. In the service of the washing of the feet the celebrant, kneeling, washes the feet of men chosen from the parish, as Jesus washed the feet of his disciples; follow the time your parish announces. At home, many St Thomas Christian families keep the Pesaha meal on Thursday night: the head of the family breaks the unleavened Pesaha bread, shares it with everyone in order of age, and the family prays together and reads the account of the Last Supper. Today, then, finish what remains: go to confession if you have not yet done so, keep the fast, help prepare the house and the Pesaha bread, and in the evening pray for Judas's opposite: a heart that, when it falls, runs back to Jesus as Peter did, and not away from him. In 2027 the Annunciation (25 March) falls on Pesaha; the Panchangam gives its readings too, so listen to how your eparchy keeps it.

Homily outline

Theme: “He has appeared once for all at the end of the age to remove sin by the sacrifice of himself.”

On Hasha Wednesday: the sword returned to its sheath, the friend who sold his Lord, and the one sacrifice that saves.

1. “Return it to its sheath”

  • A sword drawn for slaughter.
  • “Until he comes whose right it is.”
  • The Son drinks the cup.
“He makes wars cease to the end of the earth; he breaks the bow, and shatters the spear; he burns the shields with fire.” Psalm 46:9
“Put your sword back into its place; for all who take the sword will perish by the sword.” Matthew 26:52

Apply: Before Pesaha, lay down one harsh word or grudge you are keeping ready.

2. “What will you give me?”

  • One of the twelve.
  • The price of a slave.
  • Peter's tears, not Judas's despair.
“So they weighed out as my wages thirty shekels of silver.” Zechariah 11:12
“For godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation and brings no regret, but worldly grief produces death.” 2 Corinthians 7:10

Apply: Go to confession before Pesaha, and if you fall again, come back at once.

3. Once for all

  • Into heaven itself.
  • Not again and again.
  • Made present on our altar.
“But he was wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the punishment that made us whole, and by his bruises we are healed.” Isaiah 53:5
“And it is by God’s will that we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.” Hebrews 10:10

Apply: At tomorrow's Holy Qurbono, thank Christ in silence for his one sacrifice offered for you.

The Lord who was sold like a slave sets slaves free.

His one sacrifice, made present in the Holy Qurbono, is enough for every sin we bring him.

Take-home resolution: Choose Jesus over every price the world offers, and come to Pesaha reconciled and confessed.

Full homily

Dear friends, today's Gospel is short and sad. Judas, one of the twelve, said to the chief priests: what will you give me if I betray him to you? They paid him thirty pieces of silver (Matthew 26:15). The Church has long remembered this on Wednesday and kept it as a day of fasting.

Judas was not a stranger. He was a friend. The greatest danger to faith often comes from inside the heart. Peter also fell; but Peter wept and came back, while Judas lost hope. Our Lord wants every fall to end in a return.

Ezekiel speaks of a sword drawn for slaughter, and then God says: return it to its sheath (Ezekiel 21:30). In Gethsemane Jesus will tell Peter to put away his sword and will drink the cup himself (John 18:11). The judgment we deserved, he takes.

He has appeared once for all at the end of the age to remove sin by the sacrifice of himself (Hebrews 9:26). That one sacrifice is made present in every Holy Qurbono. Tomorrow is Pesaha. Today let us go to confession, put away our grudges, and choose Jesus over anything the world can pay. Amen.

For children

Children, today we hear about Judas, a friend of Jesus who sold him for thirty coins. That is very sad. But Peter also did something wrong: he said he did not know Jesus. Then Peter cried and said sorry, and Jesus forgave him. If you do something wrong, do not hide from Jesus. Run back to him like Peter! Tomorrow is Pesaha, the day Jesus gave us the Holy Qurbono.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you let yourself be priced like a slave so that we might be set free. You put away the sword of judgment and drank the cup the Father gave you. Keep us from ever selling you for anything this world can pay. When we fall, give us the tears of Peter and not the despair of Judas. Through your one sacrifice, made present on our altar, remove our sins and prepare us to keep your Pesaha with pure hearts. Amen.
