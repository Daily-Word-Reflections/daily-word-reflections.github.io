Word for the day

“He who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed for sowing and increase the harvest of your righteousness.” 2 Corinthians 9:10

Reflection

The Syriac Churches keep in January a commemoration of the Mother of God for the seeds: at the season of sowing, farmers brought their seed to church to be blessed, entrusting the year's harvest to the prayers of Mary. Paul's words fit perfectly: “the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully.” And: “He who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed.” Generosity is sowing; God gives the growth.

In the Gospel Mary receives the shepherds' story and “treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart.” Mary is the good soil where the Word is sown and bears fruit. Today we pray for farmers and for all who work the land, for a good harvest and fair prices, and for the Eparchy of Muvattupuzha on its anniversary. Let our hearts, like Mary's, receive the seed of God's word, and let our hands sow generously.

Full homily

Dear friends, today our tradition remembers the Mother of God for the seeds. At sowing time farmers brought their seed to be blessed and entrusted the harvest to Mary's prayers.

Paul says: the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully. God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:6–7). Generosity is like sowing.

And God is the true giver: he who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed (2 Corinthians 9:10). All growth is grace.

In the Gospel Mary treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart (Luke 2:19). She is the good soil where the Word was sown. Let us pray for our farmers and for Muvattupuzha eparchy, and let our hearts receive the Word as Mary did. Amen.

For children

Children, a tiny seed can become a big plant! Mary kept God's words in her heart like seeds. Let us keep Jesus' words in our hearts too.

Prayer

God who gives seed to the sower and bread for food, through the prayers of the Mother of God bless the seed of our farmers and the work of our hands. Make our hearts good soil for your word, as Mary's was, and make us cheerful givers. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Our calendar today remembers Mary, the Mother of God, for the seeds, at the time of sowing. Both readings speak of seed. Paul uses the farmer's work to teach generous giving: God gives the seed, and we sow it. In the Gospel the word about the Child is sown in the shepherds and in all who hear them, and in Mary it goes deep. The key words are sow and treasure.

2 Corinthians 9:6–11 · God supplies seed to the sower

Setting.

Chapters 8 and 9 of this letter are about a collection. Paul was gathering money among his Gentile churches for the poor Christians of Jerusalem (compare Romans 15:25–28; 1 Corinthians 16:1–4). It mattered to him as a sign that Jews and Gentiles in Christ are one family. He does not command the Corinthians. He reasons with them, and in these verses he turns to the farm.

Structure.

  • v. 6: the rule of the field: as you sow, so you reap.
  • v. 7: give from the heart, cheerfully.
  • vv. 8–9: God is able to provide, as Psalm 112 says of the just man.
  • vv. 10–11: God supplies the seed, and the harvest ends in thanksgiving.

Key words.

  • hilaros, cheerful: Our word “hilarious” comes from it, but here it means glad and willing. Paul is echoing the Greek text of Proverbs 22:8, which speaks of God blessing a cheerful giver.
  • epichorēgōn, he who supplies: The verb goes back to the rich citizen in a Greek city who paid for a chorus at the festival. So it came to mean supplying generously. Paul's picture is of God as the great sponsor of every harvest.
  • eulogia, blessing, bounty: In v. 6 “bountifully” is literally “with blessings”. The same word stands behind “bountiful gift” and “voluntary gift” in v. 5; the “blessing” of v. 8 is another Greek word, charis, grace. A gift to the poor is itself a blessing, for the one who gives and for the one who receives.

Verse by verse.

“the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully.” (9:6)

Every farmer knows that seed thrown on the field looks like a loss for a time. Paul is not promising riches to donors. In v. 10 the harvest he names is righteousness.

“God loves a cheerful giver.” (9:7)

God looks at the heart of the one who gives more than at the size of the gift.

“He who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed for sowing and increase the harvest of your righteousness.” (9:10)

The first half echoes Isaiah 55:10, where God's rain gives “seed to the sower and bread to the eater”. Even what we sow was first given to us.

How the Church reads it.

From the beginning Christians brought gifts for those in need along with the bread and wine for the Eucharist, and the Catechism cites Paul's collection in this connection (CCC 1351). What Paul asks of the Corinthians therefore stays close to the altar. In the Holy Qurbono we offer what the earth has given and human hands have made, and we are sent out to share.

Luke 2:15–19 · Mary treasured all these words

Setting.

The angels have announced the birth to shepherds in the fields near Bethlehem (2:8–14). David was once a shepherd in these same parts (1 Samuel 16:11). The Catechism sees meaning in the fact that simple shepherds were the first witnesses (CCC 525). The angels now leave, and the shepherds have only a word to go on.

Structure.

  • v. 15: the shepherds decide to go.
  • v. 16: they go in haste and find the family.
  • vv. 17–18: they tell what they were told, and all are amazed.
  • v. 19: Mary keeps it all in her heart.

Key words.

  • rhēma, word, thing: In vv. 15, 17 and 19 Luke uses the same Greek word. “This thing that has taken place” is literally “this word”. Like the Hebrew dābār, it means both a word and an event. In the Bible, what God says happens.
  • syntēreō, to treasure, to keep safe: To hold something carefully so that it is not lost. Luke says the same of Mary after the finding in the Temple (2:51, with a related verb).
  • symballō, to ponder: Literally “to throw together”: to set things side by side and compare them. Mary lays the angel's promise beside the manger and the shepherds' story, and she thinks them through.

Verse by verse.

““Let us go now to Bethlehem and see this thing that has taken place, which the Lord has made known to us.”” (2:15)

The shepherds have heard a word, and they go to see it. Faith starts with hearing and moves on to seeing.

“So they went with haste and found Mary and Joseph, and the child lying in the manger.” (2:16)

Luke used the same note of haste for Mary going to Elizabeth (1:39). The sign the angel gave them was a very ordinary one: a baby in a feeding trough.

“But Mary treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart.” (2:19)

“All who heard” were amazed (v. 18), but amazement soon fades. Mary does something else. She keeps the word and works at it. Luke does not say that she understood everything at once.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism says that Jesus learned to pray from his mother, who kept in her heart and meditated on all the great things done by the Almighty (CCC 2599). The Council of Ephesus (431) confessed Mary as Mother of God, because the Child in the manger is the eternal Son (CCC 466, 495). Our Syriac word for her is Yoldath Aloho, she who gave birth to God. The Word was sown in her heart before and after it took flesh in her womb, and she is the model of the good soil, those who “hold it fast in an honest and good heart” (Luke 8:15).

Questions people ask

Is it a superstition to bring seed to church or to ask Mary's prayers for a harvest?

No, if it is done with faith. Blessings of the fruits of the earth are among the Church's sacramentals (CCC 1667, 1671). We do not treat a blessing as a guarantee of a crop. We confess that the seed, the rain and the growth come from God, and we ask the Mother of God to pray with us. The Gospel adds that the farmer's own heart must also become good soil.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

Our calendar speaks today of the Mother of God “for the seeds”, a day that belongs to the sowing season. It lets us pray for farmers and all who work the land, for a good harvest and a fair price. Today also recalls the founding of the Eparchy of Muvattupuzha in 2003, a day to give thanks for those who sowed the faith there.

For study and teaching

  • What gift of mine do I give “reluctantly or under compulsion”? How could it become cheerful?
  • Mary set the events of her life side by side and pondered them. What in my life this year do I need to lay before God in that way?
