Word for the day

“Do not be afraid; for God has heard the voice of the boy where he is.” Genesis 21:17

Reflection

Yesterday Hagar and Ishmael were sent into the wilderness; today we follow them there. The water runs out. Hagar lays the boy under a bush and goes a bowshot away, “for she said, ‘Do not let me look on the death of the child.’ And as she sat opposite him, she lifted up her voice and wept.” It is one of the loneliest scenes in the Bible: a foreign slave woman, cast out, with a dying child, in a desert. And then: “God heard the voice of the boy; and the angel of God called to Hagar from heaven, and said to her, ‘What troubles you, Hagar? Do not be afraid; for God has heard the voice of the boy where he is.’” The name Ishmael means “God hears.” God hears him not after he is brought back to Abraham's tent, but “where he is,” under the bush in the desert. “Then God opened her eyes and she saw a well of water.” The well had been there all along; she needed God to open her eyes. In the Great Fast many of us feel that our water skin is empty. The Lord who heard Ishmael hears us where we are.

The Gospel shows us the Shepherd who hears and knows: “I am the good shepherd. I know my own and my own know me, just as the Father knows me and I know the Father. And I lay down my life for the sheep. I have other sheep that do not belong to this fold. I must bring them also, and they will listen to my voice. So there will be one flock, one shepherd.” Ishmael was outside the tent, but not outside God's care; Christ's heart reaches the sheep outside the fold, and he lays down his life to gather them. St Gregory the Great taught that “I know my own” means: I love them; and “my own know me” means: they obey me in love, for one who does not love does not yet truly know (in substance). Today our Church gives thanks for the anniversary of the enthronement of our Major Archbishop-Catholicos, Moran Mor Baselios Cardinal Cleemis, in 2007. We pray that he may be a shepherd after the heart of Christ, who knows his flock and seeks those far away. And Paul's farewell to the Corinthians becomes our prayer for all shepherds and for ourselves: “Keep alert, stand firm in your faith, be courageous, be strong. Let all that you do be done in love.” He ends with the ancient cry of the Church in Aramaic, which the NRSV renders: “Our Lord, come!”

Homily outline

Theme: “God has heard the voice of the boy where he is.”

Ishmael in the desert, Paul's farewell, and the Good Shepherd who gathers one flock; on the anniversary of our Catholicos' enthronement.

1. God hears where we are

  • The water skin is empty.
  • Hagar weeps a bowshot away.
  • God opens her eyes to the well.
“This poor soul cried, and was heard by the Lord, and was saved from every trouble.” Psalm 34:6
“Ask, and it will be given you; search, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened for you.” Matthew 7:7

Apply: Bring your driest place to God in prayer every day this week and ask him to open your eyes.

2. Stand firm, do all in love

  • Keep alert.
  • Be courageous, be strong.
  • Our Lord, come!
“I hereby command you: Be strong and courageous; do not be frightened or dismayed, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go.” Joshua 1:9
“Above all, clothe yourselves with love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony.” Colossians 3:14

Apply: Choose one duty you do grudgingly and do it this week with love.

3. One flock, one shepherd

  • I know my own.
  • I lay down my life.
  • Other sheep I have.
“I will set up over them one shepherd, my servant David, and he shall feed them: he shall feed them and be their shepherd.” Ezekiel 34:23
“There is one body and one Spirit, just as you were called to the one hope of your calling, one Lord, one faith, one baptism,” Ephesians 4:4–5

Apply: Pray daily for our Major Archbishop-Catholicos and for Christian unity.

God hears the cry of the outcast and opens eyes to hidden wells.

The Good Shepherd gathers his flock through shepherds who stand firm and do all in love.

Take-home resolution: Trust that God hears you where you are, and pray every day for the shepherds of the Church.

Full homily

Dear friends, Hagar and her son are in the wilderness, and the water is gone. She lays the boy under a bush and weeps (Genesis 21:15–16). It is one of the loneliest scenes in Scripture.

But God heard the voice of the boy; and the angel said: do not be afraid; for God has heard the voice of the boy where he is. Then God opened her eyes and she saw a well of water (Genesis 21:17, 19). God hears us where we are, not only when we are where we ought to be.

Jesus says: I am the good shepherd. I know my own and my own know me. I have other sheep that do not belong to this fold; so there will be one flock, one shepherd (John 10:14, 16). Today we pray for our Major Archbishop-Catholicos on the anniversary of his enthronement, that he may shepherd us with the heart of Christ.

And Paul tells us all: keep alert, stand firm in your faith, be courageous, be strong. Let all that you do be done in love (1 Corinthians 16:13–14). Amen.

For children

Children, a little boy named Ishmael was crying in the desert because he was thirsty. God heard him! And God showed his mother a well full of water. Ishmael's name means “God hears.” God hears you too, every time you pray, wherever you are. Today let us also pray for our Catholicos, the shepherd of our Church.

Prayer

God who hears, you heard the voice of the boy Ishmael under the bush in the wilderness, and you opened Hagar's eyes to see the well of water. Hear us where we are, in our dryness and our fears. Lord Jesus, Good Shepherd, you know your own and lay down your life for them; bring home the sheep that are far away, and make us one flock under one shepherd. Bless our Major Archbishop-Catholicos, Moran Mor Baselios Cardinal Cleemis, on the anniversary of his enthronement; keep him alert and firm in faith, courageous and strong, doing all things in love. Our Lord, come! Amen.
