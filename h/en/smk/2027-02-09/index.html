Word for the day

“I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” John 10:11

Reflection

Abram has believed, but he still asks: “O Lord God, how am I to know that I shall possess it?” God does not scold him. He asks for a sacrifice: a heifer, a goat, a ram, a turtledove and a pigeon. Abram cuts the animals in two and waits, and “when birds of prey came down on the carcasses, Abram drove them away.” Then darkness falls, and God speaks of the long road ahead: his descendants will be strangers and slaves for four hundred years, and then come out free. Faith is not the end of questions; it is staying beside the sacrifice and driving away whatever would snatch it. In the Fast our prayer, fasting and almsgiving are such an offering, and the birds of prey are many: distraction, discouragement, the voice that says it is useless.

Paul tells us why we can keep watch without fear: “There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has set you free from the law of sin and of death.” The Fast is not an attempt to earn God's love by our own strength; it is letting the Spirit who dwells in us set our minds “on the things of the Spirit”, which are “life and peace.” And in the Gospel Jesus shows whose sacrifice saves us: “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” Unlike the hired hand, he does not run when the wolf comes. He knows his own, and he will bring the other sheep too, “so there will be one flock, one shepherd.” St Gregory the Great says that the Good Shepherd first did what he taught: he laid down his life (in substance).

Homily outline

Theme: “The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.”

Abram keeps watch over the sacrifice; Christ lays down his life for us.

1. Driving away the birds of prey

  • How am I to know?
  • A covenant sacrifice.
  • Watching beside the offering.
“And when birds of prey came down on the carcasses, Abram drove them away.” Genesis 15:11
“And thus Abraham, having patiently endured, obtained the promise.” Hebrews 6:15

Apply: Name and drive away the distraction that most steals your prayer.

2. No condemnation

  • Set free by the Spirit of life.
  • The mind set on the Spirit.
  • The Spirit dwells in you.
“I will put my spirit within you, and make you follow my statutes and be careful to observe my ordinances.” Ezekiel 36:27
“There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.” Romans 8:1

Apply: Make a good confession during this first part of the Fast.

3. The good shepherd

  • He does not run from the wolf.
  • He knows his own.
  • One flock, one shepherd.
“I myself will be the shepherd of my sheep, and I will make them lie down, says the Lord God.” Ezekiel 34:15
“I am the good shepherd. I know my own and my own know me,” John 10:14

Apply: Pray daily in the Fast for one person who has left the flock.

Our Fast is an offering we guard with patient watching.

The Good Shepherd has already laid down his life for us.

Take-home resolution: Spend ten quiet minutes each day of the Fast with the day's Gospel.

Full homily

Dear friends, Abram believed God and yet asked: how am I to know? God answered with a covenant. Abram prepared the sacrifice, and when birds of prey came down on the carcasses, Abram drove them away (Genesis 15:8, 11).

Our Fast is an offering, and birds of prey come to snatch it: distraction, discouragement, the voice that says it is useless. Like Abram, we must keep watch.

But we do not keep watch in fear. There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1). The Spirit of life dwells in us.

And Jesus says: I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep (John 10:11). His sacrifice saves us; ours is only to stay close to him and listen to his voice. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is the Good Shepherd. He knows your name, he takes care of you, and he even gave his life for you. In the Fast, let us listen to his voice by reading the Bible.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Good Shepherd, you laid down your life for us and took away our condemnation. Pour your Spirit into our hearts, so that we may keep watch over the offering of this Fast and drive away all that would steal it. Gather all your sheep into one flock, and keep us close to your voice. Amen.
