Word for the day

“Son, your sins are forgiven.” Mark 2:5

Reflection

Jesus is teaching in a crowded house in Capernaum. Four men bring a paralysed friend on a mat, but they cannot get near. So they climb onto the roof, dig through it, and lower him down in front of Jesus. Mark says: “When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, ‘Son, your sins are forgiven.’” The faith of the friends opens the way for the sick man.

Everyone expected a healing of the legs. Jesus begins with the heart. The deepest paralysis is sin: it keeps us from walking towards God and towards others. The scribes object: “Who can forgive sins but God alone?” They are right; and that is exactly the point. Jesus then says: “So that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins… stand up, take your mat and go to your home.” The visible healing proves the invisible one.

Ezekiel warns of people who listen to the prophet's words like a lovely song and do nothing: “they hear what you say, but they will not do it.” Isaiah cries: “Ah, you who call evil good and good evil.” The paralysis of the soul often begins there: hearing without doing, calling sin by softer names. Lent is the time to stand up and walk.

St Paul gives the reason for our hope: “God proves his love for us in that while we still were sinners Christ died for us.” We do not have to cure ourselves first. Like the paralytic, we only need to be brought to Jesus, and to let others carry us when we cannot walk.

Homily outline

Theme: “When Jesus saw their faith.”

A paralysed man cannot reach Jesus by himself. Four friends carry him, break open a roof and lower him down. Jesus sees their faith, forgives the man's sins and raises him up.

1. Friends who carry us

  • Aaron and Hur held up the weary hands of Moses until the battle was won.
  • Four friends carry the paralytic and find a way through the roof.
  • The Church is a community where we carry one another to Jesus by prayer and help.
“Aaron and Hur held up his hands, one on one side, and the other on the other side; so his hands were steady until the sun set.” Exodus 17:12
“And when they could not bring him to Jesus because of the crowd, they removed the roof above him; and after having dug through it, they let down the mat on which the paralytic lay.” Mark 2:4

Apply: Carry one person to Jesus this week: pray for them by name and help them practically.

2. Forgiveness comes first

  • Jesus first says: “Son, your sins are forgiven.” The deepest paralysis is sin.
  • The Psalmist blesses God who forgives all iniquity and heals all diseases.
  • In the sacrament of Reconciliation Christ still speaks these words through his priest.
“Bless the LORD, O my soul, and do not forget all his benefits—who forgives all your iniquity, who heals all your diseases.” Psalm 103:2–3
“When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, “Son, your sins are forgiven.”” Mark 2:5

Apply: Thank God for the times he has forgiven you, and forgive someone who has hurt you.

3. Stand up and go home

  • God takes no pleasure in the death of the sinner, but wants him to turn and live.
  • Jesus shows his authority: “Stand up, take your mat and go to your home.”
  • The healed man walks out before all; forgiveness gives new life and new strength for daily duties.
“As I live, says the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from their ways and live.” Ezekiel 33:11
““I say to you, stand up, take your mat and go to your home.” And he stood up, and immediately took the mat and went out before all of them.” Mark 2:11–12

Apply: Take up one duty you have been avoiding at home, and do it with new heart.

Faith carried the man, mercy forgave him, power raised him.

Let us be friends who carry others, sinners who accept forgiveness, and disciples who rise and walk.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day this week for one person who is spiritually or physically “paralysed”.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, imagine the scene in Capernaum. Jesus is teaching in a house so crowded that no one can get to the door. Four men arrive carrying a friend who cannot walk. What do they do? They climb onto the roof, dig a hole, and lower their friend down right in front of Jesus. They would not give up.

St Mark says: “When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, ‘Son, your sins are forgiven.’” Their faith. Sometimes we cannot bring ourselves to God; others must carry us. And sometimes we are called to carry others: a son who has left the Church, a friend in addiction, a neighbour in despair. Our prayer can open the roof.

Everyone expected Jesus to heal the man's legs. He began with his sins. The deepest paralysis is not in our legs but in our hearts: sin keeps us from walking towards God and others. Only God can forgive sins, said the scribes. Exactly. And to show that he has that authority, Jesus told the man: “Stand up, take your mat and go home.”

Ezekiel warns of people who hear God's word like a beautiful song and then do nothing. Let us not be like that this Lent. Let us make a good Confession, so that we hear Jesus say: “Your sins are forgiven.” And let us become the four friends who carry others to him. Amen.

For children

Children, four friends wanted to bring their friend who could not walk to Jesus. But the house was full of people!

So they climbed onto the roof, made a hole, and lowered their friend down on a mat right in front of Jesus! Jesus forgave his sins and said: “Stand up and walk!” And he walked!

Good friends help each other come to Jesus. Let's say: “Jesus, help me to be a good friend!”

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you saw the faith of four friends and forgave the sins of the paralytic. Forgive our sins, heal the paralysis of our hearts, and make us friends who carry others to you. Raise us up, that we may walk in your ways. Amen.
