Readings

  • Epistle Philippians 1:15–20 · Christ is proclaimed, and in that I rejoice

Word for the day

“But blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear.” Matthew 13:16

Reflection

Paul is in chains, and some preachers are using his imprisonment to make themselves look better. His reaction is astonishing: “What does it matter? Just this, that Christ is proclaimed in every way, whether out of false motives or true; and in that I rejoice.” He is free of rivalry because his only concern is that Christ be exalted in his body, “whether by life or by death.”

The disciples ask why Jesus speaks in parables. Parables are an invitation: those who want to understand lean in and ask; those who do not, hear without hearing. Isaiah foresaw a people whose hearts had grown dull. “But blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear. Truly I tell you, many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see.” We are among those who now see; what we do with that gift is our responsibility.

Homily outline

Theme: “Blessed are your eyes.”

Joy that Christ is proclaimed; eyes that see.

1. Beautiful feet

  • Isaiah praises those who bring good news.
  • Paul rejoices whoever proclaims Christ.
“How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of the messenger who announces peace, who brings good news, who announces salvation,” Isaiah 52:7
“What does it matter? Just this, that Christ is proclaimed in every way, whether out of false motives or true; and in that I rejoice.” Philippians 1:18

Apply: Praise someone else's good work today.

2. Christ exalted in my body

  • The psalmist blesses God at all times.
  • Paul wants Christ exalted by life or death.
“I will bless the LORD at all times; his praise shall continually be in my mouth.” Psalm 34:1
“It is my eager expectation and hope that I will not be put to shame in any way, but that by my speaking with all boldness, Christ will be exalted now as always in my body, whether by life or by death.” Philippians 1:20

Apply: Offer your body and health to God today.

3. Eyes that see

  • Isaiah foresaw people who look but do not see.
  • Jesus blesses the eyes of disciples.
“Keep listening, but do not comprehend; keep looking, but do not understand.” Isaiah 6:9
“But blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear.” Matthew 13:16

Apply: Pray over the Sunday Gospel slowly.

Rejoice whenever Christ is proclaimed.

Use the gift of seeing.

Take-home resolution: Read one parable each day this week and ask Jesus its meaning.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul is in chains, and some preachers are using his imprisonment to make themselves look better. His reaction is astonishing: what does it matter? Just this, that Christ is proclaimed in every way, and in that I rejoice. My hope is that Christ will be exalted now as always in my body, whether by life or by death (Philippians 1:18–20).

In the Gospel Jesus tells his disciples: blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear. Many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see, but did not see it (Matthew 13:16–17).

We have been given the blessing the prophets longed for: to know Christ. That is greater than our own reputation. Today let us rejoice whenever Christ is proclaimed, even through others, and give thanks instead of being jealous. Amen.

For children

Children, when someone else does well, be happy instead of jealous. Praise a friend today!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, free us from rivalry, so that we rejoice whenever you are proclaimed. Open our eyes to see and our ears to hear, and be exalted in our bodies, in life and in death. Amen.
