Word for the day

“In him you also, when you had heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and had believed in him, were marked with the seal of the promised Holy Spirit” Ephesians 1:13

Reflection

Today the Panchangam names two brothers from far away: Sts Cyril and Methodius, the apostles of the Slav peoples. They were born in Thessalonica in the ninth century, sons of a Greek family who knew the Slavonic speech of the villages around the city. Constantine, the younger, was a scholar so gifted that he was called “the Philosopher”; Methodius, the elder, had been a governor before he became a monk. In 863 the prince of Great Moravia, in the lands of today's Czech Republic and Slovakia, asked the Emperor in Constantinople for teachers who could explain the faith to his people in their own tongue. The brothers were sent. Before they left, Constantine made an alphabet for a language that had never been written, the script now called Glagolitic, and began to translate the Gospels. The first words he wrote, according to the old account of his life, were the opening of St John: “In the beginning was the Word.” Then came the Psalms, the readings and the prayers of the Liturgy, so that the Slavs heard the Holy Mysteries celebrated in words they understood.

Not everyone was pleased. Some clergy held that God should be praised only in Hebrew, Greek and Latin, the three languages written over the Cross. The brothers went to Rome, and Pope Adrian II received them with honour and approved the Slavonic books for the Liturgy. There, in 869, Constantine fell ill; he took the monastic name Cyril and died, still young. Methodius returned as archbishop and laboured for sixteen more years, through opposition and even imprisonment, until he died in 885. The Eastern Churches call them “equal to the apostles,” and in 1980 St John Paul II named them patrons of Europe together with St Benedict. Their memory fits the Gospel of the day. Before choosing the Twelve, Jesus “went out to the mountain to pray; and he spent the night in prayer to God. And when day came, he called his disciples and chose twelve of them, whom he also named apostles.” Every true mission begins in that night of prayer. The names that follow, Simon Peter and Andrew, James and John, down to Judas Iscariot, show that the Lord chooses real and different people, and that the Church grows from his choice, not from our cleverness. Cyril and Methodius were not among the Twelve, but they carried the same mission to people the Twelve never saw.

St Paul's hymn in Ephesians shows the goal of all mission. God has made known to us “the mystery of his will,” “a plan for the fullness of time, to gather up all things in him, things in heaven and things on earth.” Christ does not erase the peoples of the earth; he gathers them, with their languages and songs, into one praise. And the seal of this gathering is the Holy Spirit: “In him you also, when you had heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and had believed in him, were marked with the seal of the promised Holy Spirit; this is the pledge of our inheritance.” In five days we shall keep Pentecost, when the apostles spoke and every listener heard “in our own languages” the mighty works of God. Our own Church knows this gift. The Holy Qurbono that our fathers prayed in Syriac is offered today in Malayalam and in many other tongues, while the ancient Syriac words and melodies remain in it like old gold. What Cyril and Methodius did for the Slavs, the Spirit does in every land: he makes the one Gospel at home in every language, so that every people may say “Abba, Father.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Marked with the seal of the promised Holy Spirit.”

On the memory of Sts Cyril and Methodius, in the days before Pentecost: the night of prayer before the choosing, God's plan to gather all peoples in Christ, and the seal of the Spirit.

1. A night of prayer before the choosing

  • Jesus prays, then calls.
  • Twelve names, twelve different men.
  • Mission born from prayer.
“for the Lord does not see as mortals see; they look on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart” 1 Samuel 16:7
“You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last” John 15:16

Apply: Before your next important decision, spend one quiet hour in prayer before the icon of Christ.

2. All things gathered up in Christ

  • Two brothers and a new alphabet.
  • The Gospel at home in every tongue.
  • One Body of many peoples.
“I am coming to gather all nations and tongues; and they shall come and shall see my glory” Isaiah 66:18
“there was a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages, standing before the throne and before the Lamb” Revelation 7:9

Apply: Pray this week for one missionary you know, or support a mission in a part of India where the Gospel is new.

3. Sealed with the promised Spirit

  • The seal of Baptism and Holy Mooron.
  • The pledge of our inheritance.
  • Pentecost five days away.
“A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you” Ezekiel 36:26
“But it is God who establishes us with you in Christ and has anointed us, by putting his seal on us and giving us his Spirit in our hearts as a first installment.” 2 Corinthians 1:21–22

Apply: Each evening until Pentecost, make the sign of the Cross slowly and say, “Come, Holy Spirit, seal me as your own.”

Jesus prayed before he chose; Cyril and Methodius prayed before they wrote; every fruitful mission is born on its knees.

The Spirit who sealed us at Baptism makes the one Gospel speak in every language, until all peoples are gathered in Christ.

Take-home resolution: Pray before your choices, treasure and hand on the prayers of our Church, and ask the Spirit to seal you anew.

Full homily

Dear friends, Christ is risen! In the Gospel today Jesus does something before he chooses his apostles: he prays. He goes up the mountain and spends the whole night in prayer to God. Only when day comes does he call the Twelve by name. Before every great step, Jesus speaks with his Father. If the Son of God prayed through the night before choosing, how much more do we need prayer before our choices of life, of work, of marriage, of vocation.

Today the Panchangam reminds us of two brothers who were chosen for a great mission: Sts Cyril and Methodius. More than a thousand years ago they were sent to the Slav peoples, who had no written language. Cyril made an alphabet for them, and the brothers translated the Gospels and the Liturgy, so that the people could pray to God in their own words. Some people opposed them and said God should be praised only in three great languages. But the Pope welcomed them and blessed their work. They knew that the Holy Spirit does not make every people the same; he makes every people holy.

St Paul tells the Ephesians the great plan of God: “to gather up all things in him, things in heaven and things on earth.” Christ gathers. He brings together Jews and Greeks, Slavs and Syrians, Keralites and people of every state of India, and makes of them one Body. And the seal of this gathering is the Holy Spirit. St Paul says that when we heard the Gospel and believed, we “were marked with the seal of the promised Holy Spirit.” We received that seal at our Baptism and Holy Mooron. In five days, at Pentecost, the Church will celebrate the day when the apostles spoke and everyone heard God's wonders in his own language.

So let us ask ourselves today: do I value the prayers of my Church? Do I teach them to my children? Our fathers prayed the Holy Qurbono in Syriac; today we pray it in Malayalam and in many other languages, and it is the same holy sacrifice. Let us love it, learn it and hand it on. And let us pray for all who carry the Gospel to new peoples, that like Cyril and Methodius they may give others the words of life.

For children

Children, Christ is risen! Do you know how many languages there are in India? Hundreds! Malayalam, Tamil, Hindi, English and so many more. Long ago two brothers named Cyril and Methodius went to a land where people spoke a language that had never been written down. So Cyril made up new letters, a whole new alphabet! Then they wrote the stories of Jesus in that language. Imagine how happy the people were to hear about Jesus in their own words. Jesus wants everybody to know that he loves them. In today's Gospel he prayed all night and then chose twelve friends to help him. This week, learn one new prayer by heart and teach it to a younger brother, sister or cousin.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, before you chose your apostles you spent the night in prayer to the Father, and you sent them to gather all peoples into your Kingdom. We thank you for Sts Cyril and Methodius, who gave the Slav peoples your Gospel and your holy Mysteries in their own tongue. In these days of waiting for the Holy Spirit, seal us again with the promised Spirit, the pledge of our inheritance. Make our homes and our parish places where your word is heard and understood, and let every language on earth sing the praise of your glory. Through the prayers of your holy Mother and of all your saints, gather up all things in yourself. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory for ever. Amen.
