Word for the day

“Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” Luke 14:27

Reflection

Tomorrow our Church keeps Vocations Day, and today's Epistle could hardly be more fitting. St Paul has left his co-worker Titus on the island of Crete with a task: “I left you behind in Crete for this reason, so that you should put in order what remained to be done, and should appoint elders in every town, as I directed you.” The young churches of Crete needed shepherds, and Paul did not leave the choice to chance. He lists what such a man must be: “For a bishop, as God's steward, must be blameless; he must not be arrogant or quick-tempered or addicted to wine or violent or greedy for gain; but he must be hospitable, a lover of goodness, prudent, upright, devout, and self-controlled.” Then the most important quality: “He must have a firm grasp of the word that is trustworthy in accordance with the teaching, so that he may be able both to preach with sound doctrine and to refute those who contradict it.” From the beginning the Church has been built on the apostles and on those they appointed to succeed them. St Ignatius of Antioch, writing on the way to his martyrdom, told the Christians of Smyrna: where the bishop appears, there let the people be, just as where Jesus Christ is, there is the Catholic Church (Letter to the Smyrnaeans 8, in substance). Our own Church has lived this through every generation, from the first bishops to those who shepherd us today.

Paul's list is demanding, and it is meant to be. A bishop is called “God's steward”: the household is not his, it belongs to God, and he will give an account. But the list is also a mirror for every Christian. Hospitality, goodness, prudence, self-control, a firm hold on the faith: these are not only for bishops and priests. And they are learned first at home. Where do priests and religious come from? From families where the evening prayer is kept, where the Qurbono is the centre of Sunday, where a child sees a father who is not quick-tempered and a mother who loves goodness, where the priest is welcomed and prayed for. If our parishes lack vocations, the first question is not about seminaries but about our homes. Tomorrow, on Vocations Day, pray by name for the young people of your parish, and ask whether God may be calling someone in your own family. And pray for the priests and bishops we already have, that they may be the stewards Paul describes.

The Gospel tells us the price of every vocation, lay or ordained. “Now large crowds were traveling with him; and he turned and said to them, ‘Whoever comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters, yes, and even life itself, cannot be my disciple. Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.’” The word “hate” is a Semitic way of speaking about what comes first; St Matthew gives the same saying as loving father or mother “more than me” (Matthew 10:37). Jesus does not ask us to stop loving our families; he asks that no love, not even love of our own life, be placed above him. The Imitation of Christ calls this the royal road of the holy Cross, and says that in the Cross is life and protection from our enemies (Book II, 12, in substance). The Panchangam today remembers St Theodosia. It does not tell us which of the holy women of that name is meant. One whose story is well attested is Theodosia of Tyre: Eusebius of Caesarea records that during the persecution of 307 a young Christian girl from Tyre, not yet eighteen, went up to the confessors who were on trial at Caesarea, greeted them and asked them to remember her when they came before the Lord; for this she was arrested, cruelly tortured and drowned in the sea (The Martyrs of Palestine 7, in substance). Whether or not she is the saint our Church names today, her courage shows what the Gospel means: she loved Christ more than her own life, and she carried the cross to the end.

On Saturday of the first week after Pentecost, the eve of Vocations Day, with St Theodosia: shepherds for every town, the virtues of God's steward, and the cross of every disciple.

“It is good for one to bear the yoke in youth” Lamentations 3:27

Apply: Accept one daily cross this week without complaint, and offer it for those who suffer for their faith.

Full homily

Dear friends, tomorrow is Vocations Day, and St Paul speaks to us about shepherds. He left Titus in Crete so that you should put in order what remained to be done, and should appoint elders in every town (Titus 1:5). The Church has always needed shepherds, and she still does.

Paul says: for a bishop, as God's steward, must be blameless (Titus 1:7). He must be hospitable, a lover of goodness, prudent, upright, devout, and self-controlled (Titus 1:8). He must hold fast to the trustworthy word. These virtues are also for each of us, and they are learned first at home. Good priests come from homes where prayer is kept and the Qurbono is loved.

In the Gospel Jesus tells the crowds the price of following him: whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple (Luke 14:27). He does not ask us to stop loving our families. He asks to be loved first, above everything, even above our own life.

Today we remember St Theodosia. The Panchangam does not tell us which saint of that name is meant, but the martyrs who bore that name loved Christ more than their own lives. Let us pray tomorrow for vocations, let us thank God for our priests, and let us carry our own cross behind Jesus with love. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus said that if we want to be his friends and followers, we must carry our cross. What does that mean? It means doing the right thing even when it is hard: being kind to someone who is not kind to you, helping at home when you would rather play, telling the truth when you are scared. Tomorrow is Vocations Day. Maybe Jesus is calling one of you to be a priest or a sister one day! Let us pray for that.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Good Shepherd, you sent your apostles to appoint shepherds in every town. Give your Church bishops and priests after your own heart: blameless stewards, hospitable and self-controlled, holding firm to your trustworthy word. Bless our families, so that from them may come men and women ready to serve you. Give us the grace to love you above all things and to carry our cross each day behind you. Through the prayers of St Theodosia and all who loved you more than their own lives, keep us faithful to the end. You live and reign for ever. Amen.
