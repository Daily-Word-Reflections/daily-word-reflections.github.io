Word for the day

“For whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother.” Matthew 12:50

Reflection

The Church celebrates today the tradition that Mary, as a little child, was brought by her parents Joachim and Anne to the Temple and given to God. The details come from Tradition, not from the Gospels, but the meaning is biblical: like Samuel, whom Hannah “lent to the LORD”, Mary belonged wholly to God from the beginning. The little girl in the Temple would one day become the Temple, carrying God's Son in her womb.

In the Gospel Jesus' mother and brothers stand outside wanting to speak with him. Pointing to his disciples he says: “Whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother.” This is not a rejection of Mary but her highest praise: no one did the Father's will more completely than she who said, “let it be with me according to your word.” Paul's words fit her perfectly: rejoice, do not worry, pray with thanksgiving, think on whatever is true, honourable, just, pure and lovely.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Church celebrates the tradition that Mary, Mother of God, as a little child was brought by her parents Joachim and Anne to the Temple and given to God. The details come from Tradition, not from the Gospels, but their meaning is clear: from the very beginning Mary's whole life was given to God, like Samuel whom Hannah lent to the Lord.

In the Gospel, when Jesus' mother and brothers stand outside, he points to his disciples and says: here are my mother and my brothers! Whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother (Matthew 12:49–50). Mary is Jesus' mother first not in the body but in a heart that received God's will.

Paul says: rejoice in the Lord always; whatever is true, honourable, just and pure, think about these things (Philippians 4:4, 8). This was Mary's heart. Today let us give our day to God with Mary; pray three Hail Marys and place this day in her hands. Amen.

Prayer

Holy Mary, Mother of God, given to the Lord from childhood, teach us to belong wholly to God, to do the Father's will, and to rejoice always in your Son. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Today the Church celebrates the Entry of Mary, Mother of God, into the Temple. The story of the child Mary brought by Joachim and Anne to the Temple and given to God is not in the Bible; it comes from early Christian tradition, especially the second-century Protoevangelium of James. The feast was kept in the East from the sixth century, linked with the dedication of the New Church of St Mary in Jerusalem (543), and the West received it later. What it celebrates is biblical: Mary belonged wholly to God from the beginning.

The readings do not tell the story of the feast; they show its meaning. Paul's call to rejoice, to pray without anxiety and to think on what is true and pure describes the heart of one given to God. In the Gospel Jesus names as his mother and brothers “whoever does the will of my Father in heaven”, and no one did that will more fully than Mary. The key words are joy, peace and the Father's will.

Philippians 4:4–9 · Rejoice in the Lord always

Setting.

Paul writes to the Philippians from prison (1:7, 13), not knowing whether he will live or die (1:20–24). Yet joy runs through the whole letter. Philippi was a Roman colony in Macedonia, the first city in Europe where Paul founded a church, beginning with Lydia and her household (Acts 16).

Just before today's verses Paul begs two women, Euodia and Syntyche, “to be of the same mind in the Lord” (4:2). His call to joy and peace is addressed to a real community with real quarrels.

Structure.

  • v. 4: rejoice, and again rejoice.
  • v. 5: let your gentleness be known; the Lord is near.
  • vv. 6–7: no anxiety, but prayer with thanksgiving; God's peace will guard you.
  • v. 8: think about whatever is true, honourable, just, pure.
  • v. 9: do what you have learned from me; the God of peace will be with you.

Key words.

  • chairete, rejoice: Also an everyday greeting. The angel greets Mary with the same word, chaire (Luke 1:28). Christian joy is not a mood; it is “in the Lord”, so it can exist even in prison.
  • epieikes, gentleness: Reasonableness, a yielding spirit that does not insist on its rights. Paul wants it “known to everyone”.
  • phrourēsei, will guard: A military word: a garrison keeping a city. Philippi knew Roman soldiers well. God's peace stands guard over heart and mind.
  • logizesthe, think about: The same verb Paul uses for God “reckoning” faith as righteousness. Here: take these things into account, let them fill your mind.

Verse by verse.

“Rejoice in the Lord always; again I will say, Rejoice.” (4:4)

A prisoner commands joy. The repetition shows that he means it.

“Do not worry about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God.” (4:6)

Prayer is the cure for anxiety, and thanksgiving is its key. Every need can become a petition.

“And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.” (4:7)

Not the peace of having no problems, but God's own peace, standing guard in the middle of them.

“Finally, beloved, whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is pleasing, whatever is commendable, if there is any excellence and if there is anything worthy of praise, think about these things.” (4:8)

Paul borrows words that the best Greek moralists also prized. Whatever is truly good, wherever it is found, belongs to Christ.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that every need can become the object of prayer of petition, and that thanksgiving belongs to every prayer, as Paul says (CCC 2629–2633; 2637–2638). On this feast the Church reads Paul's words in the light of Mary: the one who said “my spirit rejoices in God my Saviour” (Luke 1:47) is the model of joy in the Lord and of a mind set on what is pure and true.

Matthew 12:46–50 · Whoever does the will of my Father

Setting.

This scene ends Matthew 12, a chapter of conflict: disputes over the Sabbath, the charge that Jesus casts out demons by Beelzebul, and the demand for a sign. Right after it, Jesus goes out of the house and teaches in parables (13:1). Mark's version adds that his family had come because people said he was out of his mind (Mark 3:21); Matthew does not include that.

Structure.

  • v. 46: his mother and brothers stand outside.
  • v. 47: someone tells him.
  • v. 48: “Who is my mother?”
  • v. 49: he points to his disciples.
  • v. 50: the saying about the Father's will.

Key words.

  • exō, outside: The family stands outside, the disciples sit around him. Matthew is not judging Mary but showing the new family, gathered around Jesus and his word.
  • to thelēma tou patros mou, the will of my Father: The same will of the Lord's Prayer, “Your will be done” (6:10), and of Gethsemane (26:42). The new family is formed by doing it.
  • adelphos, brother: In Hebrew and Aramaic the word for brother also covers cousins and relatives (Genesis 13:8 calls Abraham and his nephew Lot “kindred”, literally brothers).

Verse by verse.

“But to the one who had told him this, Jesus replied, “Who is my mother, and who are my brothers?”” (12:48)

A note on the text. Verse 47 is missing in some important early manuscripts (the first hand of Codex Sinaiticus, and Codex Vaticanus). The NRSV and the POC print it; the meaning of the scene is the same either way.

“And pointing to his disciples, he said, “Here are my mother and my brothers!” (12:49)

The gesture matters as much as the words: Jesus stretches out his hand over his disciples.

“For whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother.”” (12:50)

This is not a rejection of Mary. It names what made her mother in the deepest sense. She said, “let it be with me according to your word” (Luke 1:38).

How the Church reads it.

St Augustine taught that Mary was more blessed in receiving the faith of Christ than in conceiving the flesh of Christ (in substance, On Holy Virginity 3). The Second Vatican Council says that during her Son's preaching Mary received his words praising those who hear the word of God and keep it, as she herself was faithfully doing (Lumen Gentium 58). The Catechism calls her the most perfect embodiment of the obedience of faith (CCC 148).

Questions people ask

Is the story of Mary's presentation in the Temple in the Bible?

No. It comes from the Protoevangelium of James, a second-century writing that the Church did not receive into the Bible. The feast does not ask us to accept its details. It celebrates a truth of faith: Mary, preserved from sin, belonged wholly to God from the start of her life.

Did Jesus push his mother away?

No. Jesus lifts the conversation from family by blood to family by faith, and Mary belongs to both. Luke's version ends with “those who hear the word of God and do it” (Luke 8:21), which is exactly what Luke says of Mary (1:38, 45; 2:19).

Did Jesus have brothers and sisters?

The Church teaches that Mary remained a virgin. The “brothers” of Jesus are relatives: James and Joseph, named among them (Matthew 13:55), are sons of another Mary (Matthew 27:56). The Catechism explains this in CCC 499–501.

For the Syro-Malankara reader

The Syro-Malankara Church, with the other Churches of the East, keeps this feast on 21 November, and in her prayers calls Mary Yoldath Aloho, Mother of God. The feast has a quiet link with last Sunday's reading of Hannah: as Hannah gave Samuel to the Lord at the sanctuary, tradition tells that Joachim and Anne gave Mary. The child given to the Temple would become the true Temple, carrying God's Son. It is a fitting day for parents to give their children to God again, and for all of us to renew the gift of ourselves at the Holy Qurbono.

For study and teaching

  • Paul commands joy from prison. What would “rejoice in the Lord” mean in my present difficulty?
  • Philippians 4:6 joins prayer and thanksgiving. For what can I thank God before I ask him for anything today?
  • Jesus says his true family are those who do the Father's will. What is the Father's will for me this week, in one concrete thing?
  • Mary was given to God as a child. What did my parents give to God when they brought me to baptism, and how can I renew it?
