Word for the day

“And just as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life.” John 3:14–15

Reflection

Today we stand at the middle of the Great Fast, and our Church does something striking: it lifts up the Cross. The Panchangam calls this day Pakuthi Nombu, Mid-Lent, and names it “the setting up of Golgotha.” In the Syriac tradition the faithful who have been walking through the Fast are shown, halfway, where the road leads: to the hill of Calvary, where the Cross will stand, and to the empty tomb beyond it. The Cross set up in the middle of the church is like a milestone and a signpost together. It tells weary travellers: you are not lost; this is where we are going; and this is what will give you strength for the rest of the way. Many of us have begun to tire. Some resolutions have already slipped. The Church does not scold; she lifts up the Cross and says: look.

The readings explain why looking is enough. In the wilderness the people grew “impatient on the way” and spoke against God and Moses, and poisonous serpents bit them. When they confessed, “We have sinned,” God did not simply remove the serpents. He told Moses: “Make a poisonous serpent, and set it on a pole; and everyone who is bitten shall look at it and live.” Jesus himself takes up this image in the Gospel: “And just as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life. For God so loved the world that he gave his only Son, so that everyone who believes in him may not perish but may have eternal life.” St John Chrysostom explained that in the wilderness the bronze image of a serpent healed bodies from the bite of real serpents, while on the Cross Christ, in the likeness of sinful flesh yet without sin, heals souls from the bite of the ancient serpent (in substance). The cure looks like the wound: the Son of God takes our curse upon himself so that we, looking at him in faith, may live.

The other readings fill out the picture. Zechariah foresees a day when God will “pour out a spirit of compassion and supplication” and the people “look on the one whom they have pierced” and mourn for him “as one mourns for an only child.” St John saw this fulfilled at Calvary. Samuel gathers Israel at Mizpah, where they fast, pour out water before the Lord, and confess, “We have sinned against the Lord,” and Samuel cries out to God for them; later he will set up a stone there and say, “Thus far the Lord has helped us.” That is a perfect Mid-Lent prayer. Paul reminds the Corinthians that their ancestors in the desert “drank from the spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ,” warns them not to put Christ to the test “as some of them did, and were destroyed by serpents,” and then encourages them: “God is faithful, and he will not let you be tested beyond your strength, but with the testing he will also provide the way out so that you may be able to endure it.” The apostles in Acts lift needless burdens from the Gentile believers, and the evening Gospel already announces the Passion and the third day. And the Psalm gives the response of the whole day: “Look to him, and be radiant; so your faces shall never be ashamed… O taste and see that the Lord is good.” We look at the Cross, and we taste the Lord's goodness in the Holy Qurbono, where the One lifted up on Golgotha gives us his Body and Blood.

Homily outline

Theme: “So must the Son of Man be lifted up.”

At Mid-Lent the Church lifts up the Cross: the bronze serpent, the pierced one, and the God who has helped us thus far.

1. Look and live

  • Impatient on the way.
  • The serpent on the pole.
  • The Son of Man lifted up.
“Make a poisonous serpent, and set it on a pole; and everyone who is bitten shall look at it and live.” Numbers 21:8
“And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself.” John 12:32

Apply: Spend five minutes each day this week looking at a crucifix in silence.

2. The one whom they have pierced

  • A spirit of compassion and supplication.
  • Mourning as for an only child.
  • God so loved the world.
“And I will pour out a spirit of compassion and supplication on the house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, so that, when they look on the one whom they have pierced, they shall mourn for him, as one mourns for an only child,” Zechariah 12:10
“They will look on the one whom they have pierced.” John 19:37

Apply: Make a good confession before the Passion Week, looking at the Cross as you examine your conscience.

3. Thus far the Lord has helped us

  • Fasting at Mizpah.
  • The rock was Christ.
  • The way out of every testing.
“Then Samuel took a stone and set it up between Mizpah and Jeshanah, and named it Ebenezer; for he said, “Thus far the Lord has helped us.”” 1 Samuel 7:12
“God is faithful, and he will not let you be tested beyond your strength, but with the testing he will also provide the way out so that you may be able to endure it.” 1 Corinthians 10:13

Apply: Write down how God has helped you in the first half of the Fast and take up one dropped resolution again.

Halfway through the Fast, the Church lifts up the Cross so that we may look and live.

The God who has helped us thus far will carry us to Golgotha and to the empty tomb.

Take-home resolution: Look to the Cross every day until Easter, and finish the Fast with fresh courage.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we are halfway through the Great Fast, and the Church lifts up the Cross. The Panchangam calls this day the setting up of Golgotha. It is a signpost for tired travellers: this is where we are going, and this is what gives us strength.

In the desert the people became impatient on the way, and serpents bit them. God told Moses: make a poisonous serpent, and set it on a pole; and everyone who is bitten shall look at it and live (Numbers 21:8). The cure was simply to look, in faith.

Jesus says: just as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life. For God so loved the world that he gave his only Son (John 3:14–16). Zechariah adds: they shall look on the one whom they have pierced, and mourn (Zechariah 12:10).

Paul encourages us: God is faithful, and he will not let you be tested beyond your strength (1 Corinthians 10:13). Let us say with Samuel: thus far the Lord has helped us (1 Samuel 7:12). Look at the Cross, taste the Lord's goodness at the altar, and walk on to Easter. Amen.

For children

Children, today is the middle of the Great Fast! The Church puts up a big Cross for us to see. Long ago, God told Moses to lift up a bronze snake, and everyone who looked at it got better. Jesus was lifted up on the Cross so that everyone who looks at him with love can be healed from sin. Look at the Cross today and say: thank you, Jesus.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, at the middle of this holy Fast your Church lifts up your life-giving Cross. As the bitten Israelites looked at the bronze serpent and lived, let us look at you, lifted up for us, and live. Pour out on us the spirit of compassion and supplication, that we may look on you whom our sins have pierced and turn to you with all our hearts. Thus far you have helped us; carry us through the rest of the Fast to Golgotha and to the joy of your Resurrection. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory for ever. Amen.
