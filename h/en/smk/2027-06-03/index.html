Readings

  • Epistle Galatians 3:7–14 · Those who believe are blessed with Abraham; we receive the promise of the Spirit through faith
  • Gospel Luke 11:24–26 · The house swept and put in order, and the seven spirits

Word for the day

“in order that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.” Galatians 3:14

Reflection

Before the monsoon, every Kerala household does a thorough cleaning: the attic is cleared, the drains are opened, the walls are whitewashed, the old things are thrown out. A swept house is a good thing. But today Jesus tells a strange little story about a swept house that ended badly: “When the unclean spirit has gone out of a person, it wanders through waterless regions looking for a resting place, but not finding any, it says, ‘I will return to my house from which I came.’ When it comes, it finds it swept and put in order. Then it goes and brings seven other spirits more evil than itself, and they enter and live there; and the last state of that person is worse than the first.” The house was clean, but it was empty. Nobody had come to live in it. The point is sharp: it is not enough to get rid of evil; the place it leaves must be filled with good. A person who gives up drinking but finds nothing to fill the evenings, a family that stops quarrelling but never learns to pray together, a young man who leaves a bad company but finds no good one: all these are swept houses, and they are in danger.

St Paul's Epistle tells us who must come to live in the swept house. He writes to the Galatians, who had received the Gospel with joy and were now being told that they must also take on the whole Law of Moses to be true children of Abraham. Paul answers by going back to Abraham himself: “so, you see, those who believe are the descendants of Abraham. And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, declared the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, ‘All the Gentiles shall be blessed in you.’” Abraham was counted righteous because he believed God, long before the Law was given. Paul honours the Law as God's gift; what he insists on is that no one is made right with God by keeping it by his own strength: “Now it is evident that no one is justified before God by the law; for ‘The one who is righteous will live by faith.’” And then he says what Christ has done: “Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us.” The one who hung on the tree took on himself everything that stood against us. St Augustine, commenting on this verse, said that Christ took our punishment without taking our guilt, so that by bearing what was ours he might give us what was his (Commentary on Galatians, in substance).

Why did he do it? Paul answers in the last verse, and it is the verse for this season: “in order that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.” The house is swept by the Cross and filled by the Holy Spirit. This is what happened to us at Baptism: the unclean spirit was driven out by the prayers of exorcism and renunciation, and the Holy Spirit came to dwell in us, sealed in the holy Myron. It is what happens every time we make a good confession: the house is swept. And it is why the Church does not leave us there but calls us to the Holy Qurbono, to prayer, to the word of God, to works of mercy, so that the house is not left empty but is lived in. In these weeks after Pentecost, let us ask the Spirit not only to clean us but to move in and stay. A house where the Spirit lives has no room left for seven other spirits.

Homily outline

Theme: “So that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.”

On Thursday of the second week after Pentecost: children of Abraham through faith, Christ who became a curse for us, and the swept house that must be filled by the Spirit.

1. Children of Abraham by faith

  • Abraham believed, before the Law was given.
  • The Gospel announced beforehand: all nations blessed.
  • We are his children when we believe.
“And he believed the Lord; and the Lord reckoned it to him as righteousness.” Genesis 15:6
“By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to set out for a place that he was to receive as an inheritance; and he set out, not knowing where he was going.” Hebrews 11:8

Apply: Take one step this week that you have put off because you could not see where it would lead, trusting God as Abraham did.

2. Christ became a curse for us

  • The one who hung on the tree.
  • He took our punishment, not our guilt.
  • He gives us what is his.
“But he was wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the punishment that made us whole, and by his bruises we are healed.” Isaiah 53:5
“For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.” 2 Corinthians 5:21

Apply: On Friday make a short visit to a church or a wayside cross and thank Christ, by name, for one burden he has carried for you.

3. The swept house must not stay empty

  • Getting rid of evil is not enough.
  • The Spirit comes to dwell.
  • Fill the empty room with good.
“I will put my spirit within you, and make you follow my statutes and be careful to observe my ordinances.” Ezekiel 36:27
“but be filled with the Spirit,” Ephesians 5:18

Apply: Name one habit you have given up, and choose one good practice to put firmly in its place this week.

Take-home resolution: Do not stop at sweeping: let the Holy Spirit move in and fill your heart with good.

Full homily

Dear friends, before the rains every family cleans the house. Today Jesus tells a story about a house that was swept clean but left empty. The unclean spirit came back, found it swept and put in order, and brought seven other spirits more evil than itself (Luke 11:25–26). A clean house is good. An empty house is dangerous.

St Paul tells us who must live in our house. Those who believe are the descendants of Abraham (Galatians 3:7). Abraham believed God, and God blessed him and promised that all the nations would be blessed in him. Then Paul says what Jesus has done: Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us (Galatians 3:13). On the Cross he took our place.

And why? In order that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith (Galatians 3:14). The Cross sweeps the house clean, and the Holy Spirit comes to live in it. That happened at our Baptism. It happens again in every good confession.

So do not stop at sweeping. If you have given up something wrong, fill that place with something good: prayer, the word of God, the Holy Qurbono, service to others. A heart where the Holy Spirit lives has no room for the seven spirits. Amen.

For children

Children, does your family clean the house before the rainy season? Jesus told a story about a clean house where nobody lived. Because it was empty, the bad things came back! Your heart is like a house. When you say sorry to God, he cleans it. But then you must invite the Holy Spirit to live there, and fill your heart with good things: prayers, kind words, helping others. Then there is no room for the bad things to come back.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you became a curse for us on the tree so that the blessing of Abraham might come to all the nations and we might receive the promise of the Spirit. Sweep our hearts clean of every evil, and do not leave them empty: fill them with your Holy Spirit, with prayer, with love and with good works. Keep us faithful children of Abraham through faith, and guard us from falling back into what you have set us apart from. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
