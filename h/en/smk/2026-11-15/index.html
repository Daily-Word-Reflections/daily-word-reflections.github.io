Word for the day

“Do not be afraid, Zechariah, for your prayer has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you will name him John.” Luke 1:13

Reflection

The season of Suboro, the “good news”, opens not in Nazareth but in the Temple of Jerusalem, with an old priest burning incense. Zechariah and Elizabeth were “righteous before God”, yet they had no child, and they had grown old. God's first announcement in the New Testament is given to people who had prayed for years and seemed to have received no answer.

The Old Testament readings prepare us. Abraham and Sarah were given a son when it was humanly impossible; Abraham even laughed (Genesis 17:17). Hannah, barren and mocked, poured out her soul before the Lord in the sanctuary (1 Samuel 1:15) and became the mother of Samuel. Isaiah hears God say: “Do not fear, for I am with you.” The God of Israel brings life out of barrenness.

Zechariah's question is very human: “How will I know that this is so? For I am an old man.” He asks for a sign, and he receives one: silence until the promise is fulfilled. St Paul shows the opposite attitude in Abraham, who “hoping against hope… believed” and “grew strong in his faith as he gave glory to God” (Romans 4:18, 20). Faith is not certainty that we can see; it is trust that God can do what he has promised.

John's mission is to go before the Lord “with the spirit and power of Elijah… to make ready a people prepared for the Lord” (Luke 1:17). Suboro is that preparation for us: a season in which the Church, like Zechariah in the Holy Place, waits, prays and lets God speak.

Homily outline

Theme: “God hears our prayers, even when we have stopped hoping.”

The season of announcements begins with an old priest and his wife who had prayed for years for a child. Their story is the story of every family that waits, prays and is tempted to give up.

1. Faithful in the long waiting

  • Zechariah and Elizabeth were righteous and blameless, yet childless: holiness does not exempt us from sorrow.
  • Like Hannah in the Temple, they poured out their soul before the Lord year after year.
  • Faithfulness in waiting is itself a gift that God sees and honours.
“I am a woman deeply troubled; I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but I have been pouring out my soul before the LORD.” 1 Samuel 1:15
“Both of them were righteous before God, living blamelessly according to all the commandments and regulations of the Lord. But they had no children.” Luke 1:6–7

Apply: Bring to God again the prayer you have almost stopped praying.

2. God's answer surprises our doubts

  • Abraham laughed at the promise of a son in old age; Zechariah asked for a sign.
  • God's “yes” came in the Temple, at the hour of incense, in the middle of ordinary duty.
  • Zechariah's silence was not only punishment but a school: nine months of listening before he could speak again.
“Then Abraham fell on his face and laughed, and said to himself, “Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old?”” Genesis 17:17
“Do not be afraid, Zechariah, for your prayer has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you will name him John.” Luke 1:13

Apply: Do your daily duties faithfully; God often speaks in the middle of them.

3. Preparing a people for the Lord

  • John will turn hearts of parents to their children and make ready a people prepared for the Lord.
  • Like Ruth's loyalty, true faith commits itself: “your God shall be my God.”
  • This season asks each family to become a place where the Lord is expected.
“Do not fear, for I am with you, do not be afraid, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, I will help you.” Isaiah 41:10
“With the spirit and power of Elijah he will go before him, to turn the hearts of parents to their children … to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.” Luke 1:17

Apply: Restore family prayer this week, even for ten minutes each evening.

The God who heard Zechariah hears us. His timing is not ours, but his promise does not fail.

Let us wait like Elizabeth, listen like Zechariah, and prepare our homes for the coming of the Lord.

Take-home resolution: Each evening this week, pray for one couple waiting for a child or for one family in sorrow.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today the season of Suboro begins, the season of good news. And where does the good news begin? In the Temple, with an old priest named Zechariah, burning incense while the people pray outside.

Zechariah and Elizabeth were good people. St Luke says they were “righteous before God”. And yet they had no child, and now they were old. How many years they must have prayed! And perhaps they had stopped asking. Then the angel says: “Do not be afraid, Zechariah, for your prayer has been heard.” God had not forgotten. God's answer came late, but not too late.

Zechariah could not believe it at once. “How will I know this? I am an old man.” And God gave him a strange sign: silence. For nine months he could not speak. That silence was a school. When John was born, the first thing Zechariah did with his freed tongue was to praise God.

Perhaps some of us here carry a prayer that God seems not to have answered: for a child, for a job, for a son who has gone away from the Church, for healing. Do not stop. Like Abraham, who “hoped against hope”, keep trusting. And use this season to prepare your heart, as John prepared a people for the Lord. Amen.

For children

Children, do you like waiting? No! Waiting is hard. Zechariah and Elizabeth waited many, many years for a baby.

Then an angel came and said: “God has heard your prayer!” And God gave them a baby called John. John grew up to tell everyone: “Jesus is coming!”

When you pray, God always listens. Sometimes we must wait. Let's say together: “God hears my prayer!”

Prayer

Lord God, you heard the prayer of Zechariah and Elizabeth and gave them a son to prepare the way of your Christ. Hear the prayers we have carried for so long, strengthen our faith when we cannot see, and make us a people prepared for the Lord. Amen.
