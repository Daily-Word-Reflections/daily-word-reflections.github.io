Word for the day

“Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” Luke 14:27

Reflection

Large crowds are following Jesus, and he turns and speaks to them plainly: discipleship costs everything. To “hate” father and mother is a Semitic way of saying “to prefer less”; no love, however good, may come before him. Then two short parables: the man building a tower and the king going to war. Both sit down first and count the cost. Jesus wants disciples who know what they are choosing.

Paul tells the Philippians to “work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who is at work in you”. Grace and effort are not rivals: God gives both the will and the work. Then we shine “like stars in the world”. St Charles Borromeo, Archbishop of Milan after the Council of Trent, counted the cost: he reformed his diocese, founded seminaries, and stayed with his people through the plague of 1576, when many fled.

Homily outline

Theme: “Count the cost.”

Jesus wants disciples who choose him with open eyes; God's grace makes the choice possible.

1. Carry the cross

  • No love may come before Christ.
  • The cross is the shape of love.
  • Following is daily, not occasional.
“Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains that I shall show you.” Genesis 22:2
“Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” Luke 14:27

Apply: Accept one cross today without complaint.

2. Count the cost

  • The tower and the king: think before you start.
  • Half-built faith is a sad sight.
  • Only God's help completes the building.
“Unless the LORD builds the house, those who build it labor in vain. Unless the LORD guards the city, the guard keeps watch in vain.” Psalm 127:1
“For which of you, intending to build a tower, does not first sit down and estimate the cost, to see whether he has enough to complete it?” Luke 14:28

Apply: Renew one promise you made to God.

3. Shine like stars

  • God works in us the will and the deed.
  • A disciple shines in a crooked generation.
  • St Charles shone in the plague.
“Those who are wise shall shine like the brightness of the sky, and those who lead many to righteousness, like the stars forever and ever.” Daniel 12:3
“In the same way, let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.” Matthew 5:16

Apply: Do one good work today for God's glory, not your own.

Discipleship costs everything, and Christ is worth it.

God who calls also gives the strength.

Take-home resolution: Read Luke 14:25–33 again tonight and examine your priorities.

Full homily

Dear friends, large crowds were following Jesus, and he turned and spoke plainly: whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple (Luke 14:27). He does not want admirers; he wants disciples who know what it costs.

Two short pictures make the point. A man about to build a tower first sits down and counts the cost. A king about to go to war first sits down and considers whether he can win (Luke 14:28–31). Faith is not a sudden feeling; it is a decision taken with open eyes.

Paul tells the Philippians to work out their salvation with fear and trembling, because it is God who is at work in them, and to shine like stars in a crooked world (Philippians 2:12–15). We count the cost, but the strength to pay it comes from God.

Today we remember St Charles Borromeo, a rich young cardinal who counted the cost and gave everything for the reform of the Church and the care of the sick in time of plague. Tonight, let each of us ask: what comes before Christ in my life? Amen.

For children

Children, following Jesus is like building a tower: we plan, we pray and we never give up. Shine like little stars by being kind and honest!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you call me to carry my cross and follow you. Give me courage to count the cost and to choose you above all, and let me shine in the world like St Charles, faithful to the end. Amen.
