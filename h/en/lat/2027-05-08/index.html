Word for the day

“For the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me and have believed that I came from God.” John 16:27

Reflection

Today's short reading opens Paul's third great journey. After a visit to Antioch, his home church, “he departed and went from place to place through the region of Galatia and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.” Luke gives only one line to hundreds of kilometres on foot, but the purpose is clear: a missionary does not only found churches; he goes back to strengthen them. Then the scene moves to Ephesus, where Priscilla and Aquila have settled, and a new figure appears: Apollos, a Jew from Alexandria in Egypt, the great city of books and scholars. Luke paints him warmly: “an eloquent man, well-versed in the scriptures,” instructed in the Way of the Lord, speaking “with burning enthusiasm” and teaching “accurately the things concerning Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John.” He had a gift, a fire, and a gap.

What happens next is a quiet model of Christian correction. “When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained the Way of God to him more accurately.” They do not interrupt him in the synagogue or denounce him to the apostles. They take him aside, probably into their home and workshop, and teach him. A tentmaking couple instruct a brilliant preacher, and he is humble enough to learn. Notice too that Luke again names Priscilla first: a woman and her husband together hand on the faith. Then the believers write a letter of recommendation, Apollos crosses to Achaia, and there “he greatly helped those who through grace had become believers.” Paul will later sum up the partnership: “I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth” (1 Corinthians 3:6). No one owns the Gospel; each hands on what was received.

In the Gospel Jesus, at the Last Supper, speaks of a new way of praying: “if you ask anything of the Father in my name, he will give it to you … Ask and you will receive, so that your joy may be complete.” To ask in his name is not to add a formula at the end of a prayer; it is to pray as one who belongs to him, wanting what he wants. And then the most tender sentence: “the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me and have believed that I came from God.” We do not need Jesus to persuade an unwilling Father; the Father already loves us. Jesus sums up his whole life in one line: “I came from the Father and have come into the world; again, I am leaving the world and am going to the Father.” That is the movement the Church celebrates tomorrow in India, the Ascension. On this Saturday, a day dedicated to Our Lady, we remember that the apostles waited for the Spirit with Mary, the mother of Jesus (Acts 1:14). She, who asked so simply at Cana, teaches us to ask in his name.

Homily outline

Theme: “The Father himself loves you.”

A missionary who goes back to strengthen, a couple who correct with love, and the Son who teaches us to ask in his name as he returns to the Father.

1. Strengthening all the disciples

  • The third journey begins.
  • Going back to the churches.
  • Strength for the weak.
“Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble knees.” Isaiah 35:3
“After spending some time there he departed and went from place to place through the region of Galatia and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.” Acts 18:23

Apply: This week visit or call one person whose faith has grown weak, only to encourage them.

2. They took him aside

  • Apollos: a gift, a fire and a gap.
  • Correction at the table, not from the pew.
  • I planted, Apollos watered.
“Give instruction to the wise, and they will become wiser still; teach the righteous and they will gain in learning.” Proverbs 9:9
“He began to speak boldly in the synagogue; but when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained the Way of God to him more accurately.” Acts 18:26

Apply: If you must correct someone this week, do it privately and kindly; and when you are corrected, say thank you.

3. The Father himself loves you

  • Asking in Jesus' name.
  • Not persuading an unwilling God.
  • From the Father, to the Father.
“Can a woman forget her nursing child, or show no compassion for the child of her womb? Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you.” Isaiah 49:15
“For the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me and have believed that I came from God.” John 16:27

Apply: Begin your prayers this week with the words: Father, you love me; in the name of Jesus I ask...

Paul strengthened the disciples, Priscilla and Aquila taught Apollos with love, and Apollos humbly learned: the Gospel is handed on in a chain of humble service.

Jesus, returning to the Father, teaches us to pray in his name to a Father who already loves us; with Mary we ask for the Holy Spirit.

Take-home resolution: Encourage one tired believer this week, and pray every day: Father, in Jesus' name, send us your Spirit.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we meet a brilliant young preacher, Apollos from Alexandria. He was eloquent, he knew the Scriptures well, and he spoke about Jesus with burning enthusiasm. But there was something missing: he knew only the baptism of John.

Who noticed? Not a bishop, not an apostle, but a tentmaking couple, Priscilla and Aquila. And what did they do? They did not stand up in the synagogue and shout: you are wrong! They took him aside and explained the Way of God to him more accurately (Acts 18:26). Probably at their own table, in their own home.

This is how Christians correct one another: privately, kindly, with the wish to help, not to win. And see the humility of Apollos: a learned man from the great city of Alexandria, he accepted teaching from two workers. Because he was humble, he became an even greater blessing to the Church.

In the Gospel Jesus teaches us to pray in his name: if you ask anything of the Father in my name, he will give it to you (John 16:23). And then he says: the Father himself loves you (John 16:27). Many of us pray as if God were an angry officer who must be persuaded. Jesus says no. The Father already loves you.

Jesus also says: I came from the Father and have come into the world; again, I am leaving the world and am going to the Father. Tomorrow, in India, we celebrate that return to the Father, the Ascension of the Lord.

Today is Saturday, a day dedicated to Mary. After the Ascension the apostles waited for the Holy Spirit, praying with Mary the mother of Jesus. Let us do the same: ask the Father, in Jesus' name, with Mary beside us, for the Holy Spirit. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, a clever preacher named Apollos did not know everything about Jesus. So Aquila and Priscilla invited him home and taught him kindly. We can help our friends kindly too!

Jesus says: the Father loves you! When you pray, you are talking to someone who loves you very much. Say: Father, I love you too!

Prayer

Father, you love us because we have loved your Son and believed that he came from you. In his name we come to you, trusting that you hear us, and we ask above all for the gift of your Holy Spirit. Make us like Paul, strengthening the disciples; like Priscilla and Aquila, teaching the Way gently and truly; like Apollos, burning with zeal and humble enough to learn. With Mary, who prayed with the apostles while they waited, we wait for the Spirit and for the joy you promised to make complete. As your Son goes to you, let our hearts follow him. Through Christ our Lord. Alleluia. Amen.
