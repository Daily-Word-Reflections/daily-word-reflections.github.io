Word for the day

“Nevertheless I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Advocate will not come to you; but if I go, I will send him to you.” John 16:7

Reflection

Paul and Silas have barely arrived in Philippi, the first city of Macedonia where the Gospel is preached, when trouble begins. They have driven a spirit of fortune-telling out of a slave girl, and her owners, who made money from her, drag them before the magistrates. The crowd joins in; the magistrates have them stripped and beaten with rods, and they are thrown into the innermost cell with their feet fastened in the stocks. Then comes one of the most beautiful sentences in the Acts of the Apostles: “About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them.” Bleeding, chained, in the dark, they sing. Suddenly an earthquake shakes the foundations, the doors fly open and everyone's chains are unfastened. The jailer wakes, sees the open doors, and draws his sword to kill himself, for a Roman jailer who lost his prisoners could pay with his life. “But Paul shouted in a loud voice, ‘Do not harm yourself, for we are all here.’” The prisoners have not fled. The jailer falls trembling before them and asks the question of every human heart: “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?” The answer is simple: “Believe on the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household.” That same night the jailer washes their wounds, and he and his family are baptized. He brings them into his house, sets food before them, and “he and his entire household rejoiced that he had become a believer in God.” The night that began with beatings ends with Baptism and a meal of joy.

The Psalm is the song of Paul and Silas at midnight: “I give you thanks, O Lord, with my whole heart.” “On the day I called, you answered me, you increased my strength of soul.” And even in the middle of trouble: “you stretch out your hand, and your right hand delivers me.” In the Gospel Jesus prepares the disciples for his going away. They are full of sorrow; they cannot imagine life without him. But he tells them something surprising: “it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Advocate will not come to you; but if I go, I will send him to you.” The Ascension is not a loss but the condition of a greater gift. While Jesus walked in Galilee he could be in only one place at a time; through the Holy Spirit he will be present in every disciple, in every prison cell and every home. And the Spirit will “prove the world wrong about sin and righteousness and judgment”: he will show that the real sin is refusing to believe in Jesus, that the one condemned on the cross was the righteous one now gone to the Father, and that the ruler of this world, not Jesus, has been judged. The same Spirit sang in Paul and Silas at midnight, shook the prison, and turned a jailer into a brother. In these days before the Ascension, the Church learns to trust that the Lord who goes away is not leaving us alone.

Theme: “If I go, I will send him to you.”

Full homily

Dear friends, picture the scene. Paul and Silas have been stripped and beaten with rods in Philippi. They are in the innermost cell, their feet locked in the stocks. It is dark, it is cold, every bone aches. And about midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them (Acts 16:25).

The other prisoners were listening. People are always listening to how Christians suffer. When we complain, curse and despair, they hear. When we pray and praise God in the middle of pain, they hear that too. A Christian singing at midnight is one of the strongest sermons in the world.

Then the earthquake, the open doors, the jailer with his sword. And Paul's cry: do not harm yourself, for we are all here (Acts 16:28). The prisoners could have run away; they stayed, and their staying saved a life. The jailer asks: what must I do to be saved? Believe on the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household (Acts 16:31). By morning there is a baptized family and a joyful meal.

In the Gospel Jesus says something hard: it is to your advantage that I go away (John 16:7). The disciples could not understand. How could losing him be good? But if he does not go, the Advocate will not come. In a few days we will celebrate his Ascension, and then Pentecost. He goes so that his Spirit can be with each of us, everywhere, even in a prison at midnight. Let us trust him in our losses, praise him in our darkness, and wait for the Spirit. Amen.

For children

Children, Paul and Silas were in jail, with their feet locked up. Did they cry? No, they sang songs to God in the middle of the night! Then God sent an earthquake and the doors opened. The jailer was so amazed that he and his family came to know Jesus. When you are sad or scared, try singing a little song to Jesus. He hears you!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you went away to the Father so that the Advocate might come to us. As Paul and Silas sang to you at midnight, teach us to praise you in our darkest hours. Stretch out your right hand and deliver all who are imprisoned, in body or in spirit. Send us your Holy Spirit, to convince the world of the truth and to make us messengers of salvation to everyone who asks, what must I do to be saved? Amen.
