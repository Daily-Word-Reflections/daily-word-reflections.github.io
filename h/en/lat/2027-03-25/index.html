Word for the day

“Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.” John 13:1

Reflection

Tonight the Church enters the three most holy days of the year. Lent ends quietly before this evening Mass, and the Easter Triduum begins: the Lord's Supper, his Passion and death, his rest in the tomb and his Resurrection, celebrated as one great feast over three days. There is no final blessing tonight, and no greeting at the start of tomorrow's liturgy; the Church simply keeps vigil until the Easter Vigil ends with the Alleluia. Earlier today, or on another day this week, each bishop gathers his priests at the cathedral for the Chrism Mass. There the oils used all year for Baptism, Confirmation, the Anointing of the Sick and Holy Orders are blessed, and the priests renew the promises of their ordination. The Gospel of that Mass is Jesus in the synagogue of Nazareth: “The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me.” From the one Anointed, Christ, the anointing flows into the whole Church.

The first reading of the evening takes us back to Egypt, to the night of liberation. Each household is to take a lamb without blemish, slaughter it at twilight, put its blood on the doorposts and eat it in haste, “your loins girded, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand.” “It is the passover of the LORD… when I see the blood, I will pass over you.” This night was to be “a day of remembrance… a perpetual ordinance.” The Jewish people have kept it faithfully to this day, and Jesus kept it with his disciples. Into this ancient memorial of freedom he poured a new meaning. He is the Lamb without blemish; his blood marks those who are saved; his Passover, from death to life and from this world to the Father, sets free not one nation from Pharaoh but every human being from sin and death. As St Paul says, “our paschal lamb, Christ, has been sacrificed.”

Paul's account of the Last Supper is the oldest we have, written about twenty-five years after that night: “the Lord Jesus on the night when he was betrayed took a loaf of bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and said, ‘This is my body that is for you. Do this in remembrance of me.’ In the same way he took the cup also, after supper, saying, ‘This cup is the new covenant in my blood.’” These words are not a parable. The Church believes, as she has always believed, that by the power of Christ's word and the Holy Spirit the bread and wine become his true Body and Blood, while their appearance remains (Catechism 1374–1376). The Mass is not a new sacrifice added to the cross; it makes present the one sacrifice of Calvary, so that “as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes.” And with the command “Do this,” Jesus made the apostles priests of the New Covenant, so that this sacrifice would be offered “from the rising of the sun to its setting” (Malachi 1:11). Tonight we thank God for the gift of the Eucharist and for the gift of the priesthood.

St John does not tell the institution of the Eucharist. In its place he tells what it means. “Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.” Jesus rises from the table, takes off his outer robe, ties a towel around himself, pours water into a basin and washes the feet of his disciples, the work of a slave. Peter protests: “You will never wash my feet.” Jesus answers: “Unless I wash you, you have no share with me.” The washing is a sign of the cross that is coming, where the Lord will be stripped and will pour out his life to cleanse us. And it is a command: “So if I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have set you an example.” The Eucharist and the towel belong together. Whoever receives the Body given for us must become bread broken for others.

Tonight's liturgy ends not with a dismissal but with a procession. The Blessed Sacrament is carried to a place of repose, and the people stay to watch with the Lord, remembering his words in Gethsemane: “could you not keep awake with me one hour?” (Matthew 26:40). The altar is stripped, and the church grows bare and silent. In 2027 Holy Thursday falls on 25 March, the day on which the Church usually celebrates the Annunciation, when the Word became flesh in the womb of Mary. The feast is moved this year to Monday 5 April. Yet the two mysteries meet: the body that Mary gave to the Son of God at Nazareth is the Body he gives us tonight in the Upper Room. Let us watch with him, and love as he has loved us.

Homily outline

Theme: “Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.”

Holy Thursday: the Passover Lamb, the gift of the Eucharist, the gift of the priesthood and the towel of love.

1. The Lamb and the blood

  • The night of liberation in Egypt.
  • A memorial for ever.
  • Christ, our Passover.
“The blood shall be a sign for you on the houses where you live: when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague shall destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt.” Exodus 12:13
“For our paschal lamb, Christ, has been sacrificed.” 1 Corinthians 5:7

Apply: Before Communion tonight, say slowly: Lamb of God, you take away the sins of the world; have mercy on me.

2. This is my body

  • On the night when he was betrayed.
  • Bread and wine become his Body and Blood.
  • The sacrifice of the cross made present.
“Moses took the blood and dashed it on the people, and said, “See the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you in accordance with all these words.”” Exodus 24:8
“the Lord Jesus on the night when he was betrayed took a loaf of bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and said, “This is my body that is for you. Do this in remembrance of me.”” 1 Corinthians 11:23–24

Apply: Stay at the place of repose tonight and adore the Lord truly present.

3. Do this

  • The apostles made priests.
  • A pure offering in every place.
  • Pray for priests and vocations.
“For from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations, and in every place incense is offered to my name, and a pure offering;” Malachi 1:11
“but he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever. Consequently he is able for all time to save those who approach God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them.” Hebrews 7:24–25

Apply: Write a short note of thanks to a priest who has served you, and pray daily this Easter season for vocations.

4. Wash one another's feet

  • Love to the end.
  • The towel and the basin.
  • Bread broken for others.
“How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of the messenger who announces peace, who brings good news, who announces salvation, who says to Zion, “Your God reigns.”” Isaiah 52:7
“So if I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have set you an example, that you also should do as I have done to you.” John 13:14–15

Apply: Do one humble service at home tonight, and one for a poor or sick person during the Easter Octave.

Jesus is the true Passover Lamb; he gives us his Body and Blood and the priests who offer his sacrifice.

He loves us to the end, washes our feet and sends us to love one another in the same way.

Take-home resolution: Watch one hour with the Lord tonight, and take up the towel of service tomorrow.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, tonight the Church enters the holiest days of the year. Lent is over; the Triduum has begun. And it begins at a table. The first reading takes us to Egypt: take a lamb without blemish, put its blood on the doorposts, eat it in haste with your sandals on. It is the passover of the LORD… when I see the blood, I will pass over you (Exodus 12:11, 13).

Jesus kept that Passover with his disciples. But on that night he became the Lamb. St Paul writes: on the night when he was betrayed, the Lord Jesus took a loaf of bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it and said, this is my body that is for you. Do this in remembrance of me. And the cup: this cup is the new covenant in my blood (1 Corinthians 11:23–25).

These are not just beautiful words. When the priest says them at the altar, Christ himself acts, and the bread and wine become his true Body and Blood. The sacrifice of the cross is made present among us. That is why we kneel, why we prepare for Communion with a clean heart, and why we stay with him tonight in adoration.

Do this, he said. With those words he gave us priests. Tonight let us thank God for every priest who has baptised us, absolved us and fed us. And let us pray that young men in our parishes may hear his call.

Then St John tells us what the Eucharist means. Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end (John 13:1). Jesus rises from the table, ties a towel around himself and washes their feet. Peter protests, but Jesus says: unless I wash you, you have no share with me. And then: if I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet (John 13:14).

In a moment the priest will kneel and wash feet, as Jesus did. Then we will carry the Lord to the place of repose, and the altar will be stripped bare. Stay with him tonight. Could you not keep awake with me one hour? And tomorrow, at home and at work, take up the towel. The one who receives the Body given for us must become bread broken for others. Amen.

For children

Children, tonight Jesus gave us a wonderful gift. At his Last Supper he took bread and said: this is my body. Every time we go to Mass, Jesus gives himself to us in Holy Communion!

Then Jesus knelt down and washed his friends' feet, like a servant. He wants us to help one another too. Tonight, help at home without being asked, and say thank you to Jesus for the Holy Mass.

Prayer

O God, who have called us to participate in this most sacred Supper, in which your Only Begotten Son, when about to hand himself over to death, entrusted to the Church a sacrifice new for all eternity, the banquet of his love, grant, we pray, that we may draw from so great a mystery, the fullness of charity and of life. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, on the night you were betrayed you gave us your Body and Blood, you gave us priests, and you washed our feet. Keep us with you in Gethsemane tonight, make us one at your table, and send us out to wash one another's feet. Amen.
