Word for the day

“Let the little children come to me; do not stop them; for it is to such as these that the kingdom of God belongs.” Mark 10:14

Reflection

Ben Sira today meditates on the first pages of Genesis, and he holds together two truths about us that are easy to separate. The first is humility: “The Lord created human beings out of earth, and makes them return to it again. He gave them a fixed number of days.” We are dust, and our days are counted. The second is dignity: he “granted them authority over everything on the earth. He endowed them with strength like his own, and made them in his own image.” God gave us “discretion and tongue and eyes, ears and a mind for thinking,” filled us “with knowledge and understanding,” and “showed them good and evil.” Why? So that we might see his works and praise him: “they will praise his holy name, to proclaim the grandeur of his works.” Then Ben Sira moves from creation to covenant: “He established with them an eternal covenant, and revealed to them his decrees. Their eyes saw his glorious majesty,” a memory of Sinai, and he gave each one “commandment … concerning the neighbor.” To be made in God's image is to be made for praise and for love of neighbour. The Psalm adds the tenderness that makes this bearable: “As a father has compassion for his children, so the Lord has compassion for those who fear him. For he knows how we were made; he remembers that we are dust.”

The Gospel shows us that compassion in action. “People were bringing little children to him in order that he might touch them; and the disciples spoke sternly to them.” Perhaps they thought the Master had more important work than blessing babies. Mark uses a strong word for Jesus' reaction: “he was indignant.” Only here in the Gospels is Jesus said to be indignant with his own disciples. “Let the little children come to me; do not stop them; for it is to such as these that the kingdom of God belongs. Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God as a little child will never enter it.” A child does not earn a gift; a child receives it with open hands. That is how the Kingdom is entered: not by achievement but by trust. “And he took them up in his arms, laid his hands on them, and blessed them.” The Church has always seen in this scene a reason to bring even infants to Baptism (CCC 1250–1252). Today we may honour St Rita of Cascia (1381–1457), wife, mother, widow and Augustinian nun, whom many in Kerala and across India call upon in desperate situations. She lived through a hard marriage, the murder of her husband and the death of her two sons, and she answered violence with forgiveness and prayer. Like a child, she kept receiving everything from God's hands, even when those hands seemed to hold only thorns.

Homily outline

Theme: “Receive the kingdom of God as a little child.”

Dust and divine image, the compassion of the Father, and the Kingdom given to children.

1. Dust and image

  • Made from the earth.
  • Made in God's image.
  • Made for praise and for the neighbour.
“He endowed them with strength like his own, and made them in his own image.” Sirach 17:3
“and have clothed yourselves with the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge according to the image of its creator.” Colossians 3:10

Apply: Each evening this week, thank God for one gift of your senses or mind that you used well that day.

2. He remembers that we are dust

  • Our weakness does not surprise God.
  • A Father's compassion.
  • Mercy from everlasting to everlasting.
“As a father has compassion for his children, so the Lord has compassion for those who fear him. For he knows how we were made; he remembers that we are dust.” Psalm 103:13–14
“See what love the Father has given us, that we should be called children of God; and that is what we are.” 1 John 3:1

Apply: When you fail this week, say: Father, you remember that I am dust; and begin again.

3. Let the children come

  • The disciples' sternness.
  • Jesus is indignant.
  • Receive the Kingdom with open hands.
“Can a woman forget her nursing child, or show no compassion for the child of her womb? Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you.” Isaiah 49:15
“Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God as a little child will never enter it.” Mark 10:15

Apply: Bless a child with the sign of the cross each night this week, or pray for the children of your neighbourhood.

We are dust and we are made in God's image; the Father's compassion holds both truths together.

The Kingdom is not earned but received, as a child receives, as St Rita received even the thorns of her life from God's hands.

Take-home resolution: Come to God this week as a child: one short prayer each hour, “Abba, Father, I trust you.”

Full homily

Dear friends, Ben Sira reminds us of two truths today. First, we are dust: the Lord created human beings out of earth, and makes them return to it again (Sirach 17:1). Second, we are precious: he made them in his own image. He gave us eyes, ears, a tongue and a mind, so that we might praise him and love our neighbour.

If we forget that we are dust, we become proud. If we forget that we are made in his image, we despair. The Psalm holds both together: as a father has compassion for his children, so the Lord has compassion for those who fear him. He knows how we were made; he remembers that we are dust (Psalm 103:13–14). Our weakness does not surprise God.

In the Gospel mothers bring their little ones to Jesus, and the disciples try to send them away. Jesus is indignant. Let the little children come to me; do not stop them; for it is to such as these that the kingdom of God belongs (Mark 10:14). Children do not earn gifts; they receive them. This is how we must come to God: not with a list of our achievements, but with open hands.

Today many of us remember St Rita of Cascia, a wife and mother who suffered a hard marriage and the loss of her husband and sons, and who answered violence with forgiveness. She is called the saint of impossible causes because she trusted God like a child even when everything seemed lost. Tomorrow we celebrate the Most Holy Trinity, the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, who has made us his children. Let us come to him as children. Amen.

For children

Children, once mothers brought their babies and little children to Jesus, but the disciples said: go away! Jesus was not happy with the disciples. He said: let the children come to me! Then he hugged them and blessed them. Jesus loves you very much and always has time for you. You can talk to him anytime!

Prayer

Lord God, you formed us from the dust of the earth and made us in your image; you remember that we are dust and you have compassion on us as a father on his children. Give us the heart of a little child, to receive your Kingdom with open hands. Through the prayers of St Rita, who answered violence with forgiveness and trusted you in every sorrow, bring peace to our homes, and grant us what seems impossible, if it is your will. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
