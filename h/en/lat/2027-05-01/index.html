Word for the day

“If the world hates you, be aware that it hated me before it hated you.” John 15:18

Reflection

Paul sets out on his second missionary journey, and the first thing Luke tells us is about a young man. In Lystra, the town where Paul had once been stoned and left for dead, there is a disciple named Timothy, “the son of a Jewish woman who was a believer; but his father was a Greek.” The believers speak well of him, and Paul wants him as a companion. Then the little band travels from town to town, handing on the decisions of the apostles and elders in Jerusalem, “so the churches were strengthened in the faith and increased in numbers daily.” What follows is strange. The missionaries try to preach in the province of Asia, and the Holy Spirit forbids them. They try to go north into Bithynia, and “the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them.” Two doors close, one after the other. They end up at Troas, on the coast, with nowhere obvious to go. And there, in the night, Paul sees a man of Macedonia standing and pleading: “Come over to Macedonia and help us.” At once they cross the sea. Notice the small change in the text: from this verse Luke begins to write “we.” The writer has joined the journey. The Gospel is about to enter Europe, and it enters through closed doors, a dream in the night, and a plea for help.

The Gospel moves from the joy of the mission to its cost. At the Last Supper Jesus warns his friends: “If the world hates you, be aware that it hated me before it hated you.” In John's Gospel “the world” here does not mean the earth God made and loves (John 3:16), but human life organised without God and against him. The disciple who belongs to Christ will not always be welcome in it. “Remember the word that I said to you, ‘Servants are not greater than their master.’ If they persecuted me, they will persecute you; if they kept my word, they will keep yours also.” Paul would learn this soon enough in Macedonia: beatings in Philippi, riots in Thessalonica. And yet the plea “help us” was real. The same world that resists the Gospel is crying out for it. Today, on the first of May, the Church also honours St Joseph the Worker. Pope Pius XII gave this memorial to the Church in 1955, so that the workers' day would also be a day of the carpenter of Nazareth. The people of Nazareth once asked of Jesus, “Is not this the carpenter's son?” Joseph's hidden work, with the Blessed Virgin Mary beside him, made a home where the Son of God grew up. On this day we pray for all who work, for all who cannot find work, and for all whose work is unjust or unsafe; and we begin the month of May with the Mother of Jesus.

Homily outline

Theme: “Come over to Macedonia and help us.”

Doors closed by the Spirit, a cry for help, the cost of discipleship, and the carpenter of Nazareth.

1. Two closed doors

  • Asia forbidden.
  • Bithynia refused.
  • Troas: waiting without a map.
“The human mind plans the way, but the Lord directs the steps.” Proverbs 16:9
“When they had come opposite Mysia, they attempted to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them;” Acts 16:7

Apply: Bring one disappointment of this year to the Lord in prayer and ask, not why, but where: where are you leading me now?

2. Come over and help us

  • A dream in the night.
  • Luke begins to say “we.”
  • The Gospel enters Europe.
“Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” And I said, “Here am I; send me!”” Isaiah 6:8
“During the night Paul had a vision: there stood a man of Macedonia pleading with him and saying, “Come over to Macedonia and help us.”” Acts 16:9

Apply: Listen this week for one person's plea for help that you have been ignoring, and answer it.

3. Hated before you

  • What John means by “the world.”
  • The servant and the master.
  • Hostility and hope together.
“More in number than the hairs of my head are those who hate me without cause” Psalm 69:4
“Remember the word that I said to you, ‘Servants are not greater than their master.’ If they persecuted me, they will persecute you; if they kept my word, they will keep yours also.” John 15:20

Apply: Pray by name for one persecuted Christian community today, and do not be ashamed to make the sign of the cross in public.

4. The carpenter's son

  • Joseph's hidden work.
  • Work done for the Lord.
  • May Day and the month of Mary.
“The Lord God took the man and put him in the garden of Eden to till it and keep it.” Genesis 2:15
“Whatever your task, put yourselves into it, as done for the Lord and not for your masters,” Colossians 3:23

Apply: Begin each working day this week with a short offering: Lord, I do this work for you; St Joseph, pray for me.

The Spirit who closed two doors opened a third, and the Gospel crossed the sea in answer to a cry for help.

The disciple will share the Master's rejection, but also his fruitfulness; and with St Joseph and the Blessed Virgin Mary, our daily work becomes part of that mission.

Take-home resolution: Offer your work to God each morning this May and pray one decade of the Rosary each evening.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul and his companions had good plans. They wanted to preach in Asia; the Holy Spirit said no. They tried Bithynia; the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them (Acts 16:7). Two closed doors. And then, at Troas, by the sea, a dream: a man of Macedonia saying, come over to Macedonia and help us (Acts 16:9).

We do not like closed doors. An exam failed, a job refused, a marriage proposal that did not work out, a transfer we did not want. But sometimes God closes a door to keep us for another. Paul did not sit down and sulk at Troas. He prayed, he listened, and when the call came he crossed the sea at once. Because of that crossing, the Gospel came to Europe.

Jesus is honest with us in the Gospel. Not every door will be opened by the world. If the world hates you, be aware that it hated me before it hated you (John 15:18). The servant is not greater than the master. Paul would be beaten in Philippi only days after he landed. Following the Spirit is not always comfortable. But it is always fruitful.

Today is also the first of May, and the Church shows us St Joseph the Worker. People in Nazareth looked down a little on Jesus: is not this the carpenter's son? But Joseph's hidden, honest work kept the Holy Family alive. Let us offer our work to God today, pray for every worker and for those who have no work, and begin this month of May close to our Mother Mary. Amen.

For children

Children, Paul wanted to go somewhere, but God said no. Then God showed him in a dream a man saying: come and help us! So Paul went, and many people came to know Jesus. Sometimes when we do not get what we want, God has a better plan. And today we remember St Joseph, the carpenter who worked hard for Jesus and Mary. Let us help at home today, like Jesus helped Joseph!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, risen from the dead, you sent your apostles across the sea in answer to the cry “Come over and help us.” When our plans fail and doors close, give us the humility to follow your Spirit. When the world resists your word, keep us faithful, for you were hated before us. Through the prayers of St Joseph, the carpenter of Nazareth, bless all who work and all who look for work, and give us a share in the Easter joy of your Mother, whose month begins today. Amen.
