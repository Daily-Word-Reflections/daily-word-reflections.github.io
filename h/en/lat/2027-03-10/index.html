Word for the day

“Can a woman forget her nursing child, or show no compassion for the child of her womb? Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you.” Isaiah 49:15

Reflection

The exiles in Babylon had a heavy thought: God has left us. “Zion said, ‘The LORD has forsaken me, my Lord has forgotten me.’” Isaiah answers with a promise of homecoming: in “a time of favor” God will say to the prisoners, “Come out,” and to those in darkness, “Show yourselves.” He will lead them like a shepherd by springs of water; he will turn mountains into roads; heaven and earth will sing. Then comes one of the tenderest lines in all Scripture. God compares himself to a mother: “Can a woman forget her nursing child, or show no compassion for the child of her womb? Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you.” The Hebrew word for compassion is close to the word for womb; God's mercy is a mother's love from deep within. And the next verse, which the Lectionary leaves just outside today's reading, says: “See, I have inscribed you on the palms of my hands.” The Psalm sums it up: “The LORD is gracious and merciful, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love.”

In the Gospel Jesus answers those who were angry that he healed on the sabbath: “My Father is still working, and I also am working.” God rested on the seventh day of creation, but he never stops giving life and judging the world; and Jesus does the same work as the Father. His hearers understand at once that he is “making himself equal to God.” Jesus does not take back the claim; he explains it. The Son does nothing on his own, but only what he sees the Father doing; the Father loves the Son and shows him everything. “Just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whomever he wishes.” Here Lent opens a window onto Easter. “Anyone who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life… and has passed from death to life.” And one day “all who are in their graves will hear his voice and will come out.” The God who cannot forget his children has sent his Son, who will not leave them in the grave. That is why we can face our own fears of being forgotten, of failure and of death, with hope.

Homily outline

Theme: “Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you.”

God's mother-love, the Son who works with the Father, and the voice that calls us from death to life.

1. I will not forget you

  • Zion's complaint.
  • A mother's compassion.
  • Names on God's palms.
“Can a woman forget her nursing child, or show no compassion for the child of her womb? Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you.” Isaiah 49:15
“But even the hairs of your head are all counted. Do not be afraid; you are of more value than many sparrows.” Luke 12:7

Apply: When fear says you are forgotten, pray Isaiah 49:15 slowly three times.

2. My Father is still working

  • The sabbath and the works of God.
  • The Son equal to the Father.
  • Love that shows everything.
“The LORD is good to all, and his compassion is over all that he has made.” Psalm 145:9
“But Jesus answered them, “My Father is still working, and I also am working.”” John 5:17

Apply: Each evening this week, name one place where you saw God at work that day.

3. From death to life

  • Hearing and believing.
  • Eternal life now.
  • The voice at the grave.
“Many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt.” Daniel 12:2
“Very truly, I tell you, anyone who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and does not come under judgment, but has passed from death to life.” John 5:24

Apply: Pray for one person who has died, and for one who is far from God, that both may hear the voice of the Son.

God's love is stronger than a mother's and never forgets.

The Son gives life now and will raise the dead; we hear his word and live.

Take-home resolution: Be the memory of God for one forgotten person this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, the exiles in Babylon said: the LORD has forsaken me, my Lord has forgotten me. Maybe you have said that too, in a hospital, at a graveside, or in a long struggle with sin.

God answers through Isaiah: can a woman forget her nursing child, or show no compassion for the child of her womb? Even these may forget, yet I will not forget you (Isaiah 49:15). And he adds: see, I have inscribed you on the palms of my hands. On the cross those palms were pierced for us. Our names are written there.

In the Gospel Jesus says: my Father is still working, and I also am working (John 5:17). God never stops loving and giving life, and Jesus does the same works as the Father. Just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whomever he wishes.

And he promises: anyone who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life… and has passed from death to life (John 5:24). Today, let us hear his word and believe. And let us become the memory of God for someone who feels forgotten. Amen.

For children

Children, a mother never forgets her baby. God says: even if a mother forgot, I will never forget you! God has written your name on his hands. Look at your hand today and remember: God is holding you.

Prayer

Father of mercy, you love us more than a mother loves her child, and you have written our names on the palms of your hands. When we feel forgotten, let us hear the voice of your Son, who gives life to the dead. Bring us with the elect from death to life this Easter. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
