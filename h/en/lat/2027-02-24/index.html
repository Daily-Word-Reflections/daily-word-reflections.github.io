Word for the day

“just as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.”” Matthew 20:28

Reflection

Jeremiah has spoken God's word faithfully, and now the people plot against him. They say: let us bring charges against him and not heed any of his words. The prophet turns to God in pain: “Is evil a recompense for good? Yet they have dug a pit for my life. Remember how I stood before you to speak good for them, to turn away your wrath from them.” He had prayed for the very people who now want him dead. Jeremiah is a figure of Christ, the innocent one who intercedes for those who reject him. The Psalm puts the words of trust on our lips that Jesus himself prayed on the cross: “Into your hand I commit my spirit; you have redeemed me, O LORD, faithful God.” And again: “My times are in your hand.”

In the Gospel Jesus, going up to Jerusalem, tells the Twelve for the third time what awaits him: he will be handed over, condemned, mocked, flogged and crucified, “and on the third day he will be raised.” At that very moment the mother of James and John kneels before him and asks for the best places in his kingdom. Jesus answers gently: “You do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup that I am about to drink?” They say, “We are able,” not knowing that the cup is suffering. And the other ten are angry, perhaps because they wanted the same places. So Jesus teaches them all: among the nations rulers lord it over others, “It will not be so among you; but whoever wishes to be great among you must be your servant.” Then he gives the reason, the heart of the whole Gospel: “the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.” In Lent we follow him up to Jerusalem. The question is not which seat we will have, but whether we will drink his cup with him.

Homily outline

Theme: “Not to be served but to serve.”

The persecuted prophet, the cup of the Lord and the greatness of service.

  • The plot against Jeremiah.

Full homily

Dear friends, Jeremiah had prayed for his people, and they plotted to kill him. He cried to God: is evil a recompense for good? Yet they have dug a pit for my life. Remember how I stood before you to speak good for them (Jeremiah 18:20). He is a picture of Jesus, who prayed for those who crucified him.

In the Gospel Jesus tells the Twelve for the third time that he will be condemned and crucified and raised on the third day. And at that very moment the mother of James and John asks for the best seats for her sons. We do the same: we bring Jesus our ambitions while he is speaking of the cross.

Jesus answers: you do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup that I am about to drink? (Matthew 20:22). The cup is his suffering. Later, James would be the first of the apostles to die as a martyr. He did drink the cup.

Then Jesus teaches all of us: it will not be so among you; whoever wishes to be great among you must be your servant… just as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many (Matthew 20:26–28). This Lent, let us stop asking for seats and start taking up towels. Amen.

For children

Children, two friends of Jesus wanted to be the most important. Jesus said: the most important person is the one who helps everyone. Jesus came to help us and to give his life for us. Today, help someone at home without being asked!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you went up to Jerusalem to give your life as a ransom for many. Free us from the desire for the first places, teach us to drink your cup with trust, and make us servants of all. Into your hands we commit our spirit. Amen.
