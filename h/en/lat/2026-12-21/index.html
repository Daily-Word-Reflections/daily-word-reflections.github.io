Word for the day

“And blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” Luke 1:45

Reflection

Today's O Antiphon: “O Dayspring, splendour of eternal light and sun of justice: come and enlighten those who dwell in darkness and the shadow of death.” On the shortest days of the year in the northern hemisphere, the Church sings of the Sun that rises from on high. The Song of Songs gives the voice of the bride: “The voice of my beloved! Look, he comes, leaping upon the mountains.”

Mary, having said yes, does not stay at home. She “set out and went with haste” to the hill country to visit Elizabeth. At her greeting, John leaps in his mother's womb, and Elizabeth, filled with the Holy Spirit, cries out: “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb… And why has this happened to me, that the mother of my Lord comes to me?” The first person to call Jesus “Lord” in Luke's Gospel is an unborn child's mother, and the first to rejoice at his presence is an unborn child. Elizabeth's last word praises Mary's faith: “Blessed is she who believed.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Blessed is she who believed.”

The Beloved comes; Mary hastens; the unborn John leaps for joy.

1. The Beloved comes

  • The bride hears his voice.
  • He comes over mountains and hills.
  • God hurries toward us.
“The voice of my beloved! Look, he comes, leaping upon the mountains, bounding over the hills.” Song of Solomon 2:8
“In those days Mary set out and went with haste to a Judean town in the hill country.” Luke 1:39

Apply: Visit someone who is lonely before Christmas.

2. John leaps for joy

  • The unborn child recognises his Lord.
  • Elizabeth is filled with the Spirit.
  • Every life is precious from the womb.
“Listen to me, O coastlands, pay attention, you peoples from far away! The LORD called me before I was born, while I was in my mother's womb he named me.” Isaiah 49:1
“When Elizabeth heard Mary's greeting, the child leaped in her womb. And Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit and exclaimed with a loud cry, “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb.”” Luke 1:41–42

Apply: Pray for unborn children and their mothers.

3. Blessed is she who believed

  • Abraham believed and was reckoned righteous.
  • Mary believed the angel's word.
  • Faith is the root of Mary's greatness.
“And he believed the LORD; and the LORD reckoned it to him as righteousness.” Genesis 15:6
“And why has this happened to me, that the mother of my Lord comes to me? For as soon as I heard the sound of your greeting, the child in my womb leaped for joy. And blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” Luke 1:43–45

Apply: Renew your trust in one promise of God.

Faith hurries to serve.

Christ comes, and the little ones leap for joy.

Take-home resolution: Make one visit of charity before Christmas.

Full homily

Dear friends, today's O Antiphon prays: O Dayspring, splendour of eternal light and sun of justice: come and enlighten those who dwell in darkness and the shadow of death.

The Song of Songs sings of love: the voice of my beloved! Look, he comes, leaping upon the mountains, bounding over the hills. Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away; for now the winter is past (Song of Songs 2:8, 10–11). The Church reads it in Advent as God coming to his people with the eagerness of love.

In the Gospel Mary sets out and goes with haste to the hill country, to the house of Zechariah, and greets Elizabeth. When Elizabeth hears Mary's greeting, the child leaps in her womb, and Elizabeth is filled with the Holy Spirit. She exclaims: blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb! And blessed is she who believed (Luke 1:39–45).

Mary carries Jesus hidden within her, and wherever she goes, joy follows. John the Baptist leaps for joy even before birth. Elizabeth is filled with the Spirit. The Visitation is the first Christmas in a way: Christ arrives at a home, and the home is transformed.

We too carry Christ, especially after Holy Communion. When we visit others with love, Christ comes with us. A visit to a sick neighbour, a lonely grandparent, a family in grief, can be a Visitation.

Elizabeth praises Mary above all for her faith: blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfilment of what was spoken to her by the Lord (Luke 1:45). This Christmas, let us believe God's promises, and carry Christ with haste to someone who needs his joy. Amen.

For children

Children, Mary visited her cousin and brought Jesus with her. When you visit someone with love, you bring Jesus too!

Prayer

O Dayspring, splendour of eternal light and sun of justice, come and enlighten those who sit in darkness and the shadow of death. Like Mary, let me hasten to serve, and let me be blessed in believing your word. Amen.
