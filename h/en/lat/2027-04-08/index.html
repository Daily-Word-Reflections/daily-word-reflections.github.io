Word for the day

“He whom God has sent speaks the words of God, for he gives the Spirit without measure.” John 3:34

Reflection

The apostles, brought back from the Temple, stand before the council. The high priest reminds them: “We gave you strict orders not to teach in this name.” He will not even say the name of Jesus. Peter and the apostles answer with a sentence that has given courage to believers in every century: “We must obey God rather than any human authority.” This is not a contempt for authority, which the apostles will later teach Christians to respect; it is the freedom of conscience before God when a human command forbids what God commands. Then Peter proclaims the Easter message once more: “The God of our ancestors raised up Jesus… God exalted him at his right hand as Leader and Savior that he might give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins.” Notice the purpose of his preaching: not accusation but forgiveness, offered first to Israel, the people of the promise. The Church teaches that the responsibility for the Passion cannot be laid on the Jews of that time as a whole, still less on the Jews of today, and that all of us sinners share in it (Catechism 597–598). And Peter adds a second witness beside the apostles: “And we are witnesses to these things, and so is the Holy Spirit whom God has given to those who obey him.” The Spirit does not replace our witness; he speaks with it.

The Gospel closes the third chapter of St John with a meditation on the one who comes from above. John the Baptist has just said that he must decrease; now the evangelist explains why. “The one who comes from above is above all.” He does not speak second-hand; “he testifies to what he has seen and heard.” Whoever accepts his testimony “has certified this, that God is true,” like a man who puts his seal on a document. Then a phrase of great richness: “He whom God has sent speaks the words of God, for he gives the Spirit without measure.” The prophets received the Spirit by measure, for a time and a task; the Son receives and gives him without limit. “The Father loves the Son and has placed all things in his hands.” Everything the Father has is in the hands that were pierced and are now risen. The chapter ends with a choice: “Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life.” The apostles in the council are living that choice. They have believed, they have received the Spirit, and so, before a powerful court, they can speak the words of God.

Full homily

Dear friends, the apostles stand before the most powerful court in their nation. They are told: stop teaching in this name. And Peter says: we must obey God rather than any human authority (Acts 5:29). These are not proud words. These are the words of a man who once denied Jesus out of fear, and who has now been changed by the Resurrection and the Spirit.

And what does Peter preach to his judges? Not anger. He says God raised Jesus to give repentance and forgiveness of sins. Even to those who opposed him, the risen Lord offers mercy. The Easter message is never a weapon against anyone; it is a door opened to everyone.

Peter says one more thing: we are witnesses, and so is the Holy Spirit. We are never alone when we speak of Jesus. In the Gospel John says that Jesus gives the Spirit without measure (John 3:34). He does not give the Spirit with a teaspoon. He pours him out.

So let us ask today for that Spirit. In our offices, our schools, our families, there are moments when we must choose between what people want and what God wants. Let us ask for the courage of Peter, and the gentleness of Peter too, so that our witness may be both firm and kind. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, the leaders told Peter: stop talking about Jesus! Peter was not afraid any more, because the Holy Spirit made him brave. He said: we must obey God first. When someone asks you to do something wrong, you can say: I want to obey God. The Holy Spirit will help you!

Prayer

God of our ancestors, you raised up Jesus and exalted him at your right hand as Leader and Saviour. Give us the courage of the apostles to obey you rather than any human voice that would silence your truth, and pour out on us without measure the Spirit of your Son, that we may be faithful witnesses of his Resurrection. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

The apostles brought back from the temple now stand before the council, and Peter gives the answer that has steadied Christians ever since: “We must obey God rather than any human authority.” He calls himself a witness, together with the Holy Spirit. The Gospel speaks of another witness, the one who comes from above and testifies to what he has seen and heard. Psalm 34 again promises that the Lord is near to the brokenhearted.

Acts 5:27–33 · Obey God rather than any human authority

Setting.

This follows straight on from yesterday’s reading in Acts 5:17–26. The council, the Sanhedrin, was the highest Jewish court in Jerusalem, presided over by the high priest; at this time that was probably Caiaphas, who held office until about AD 36. Earlier the apostles had been ordered not to speak in the name of Jesus (4:18). They have been found teaching in the temple.

Structure.

  • vv. 27–28: the high priest’s charge: disobedience, and an accusation of blame.
  • vv. 29–32: Peter’s answer: obedience to God, the Resurrection, and witness.
  • v. 33: the council’s fury.

Key words.

  • peitharchein, to obey: Literally “to obey one in authority”. It frames Peter’s answer: we must obey God (v. 29), and God gives the Spirit to those who obey him (v. 32). Obedience to God is the source of their freedom before men.
  • archēgos, Leader: A rich word: leader, founder, pioneer, the one who goes first and opens the way. Peter called Jesus “the Author of life” with the same word (3:15), and Hebrews calls him “the pioneer” of our salvation and of our faith (2:10; 12:2).
  • martyres, witnesses: A witness tells what he has seen. In the Church the word came to mean those who bear witness to the point of death, the martyrs. Here the human witness and the Spirit’s witness go together.

Verse by verse.

“you are determined to bring this man’s blood on us.” (5:28)

The leaders fear being held responsible for the death of Jesus. Peter does not answer with revenge. He offers repentance and forgiveness of sins to Israel (v. 31). The Church teaches that the Passion cannot be charged against all the Jews then living, nor against the Jews of today (Nostra Aetate 4; CCC 597).

“We must obey God rather than any human authority.” (5:29)

Educated readers of Greek would have thought of Socrates, who told his judges in Athens that he would obey the god rather than them (Plato, Apology 29d). The Catechism cites this verse: a citizen is bound in conscience not to follow the directives of civil authorities when they are contrary to the moral order and to the Gospel (CCC 2242).

“whom you had killed by hanging him on a tree.” (5:30)

“Tree” recalls Deuteronomy 21:22–23, where one hanged on a tree is under a curse. Paul says that Christ became a curse for us to redeem us (Galatians 3:13). What looked like God’s rejection was the way of our redemption.

“And we are witnesses to these things, and so is the Holy Spirit” (5:32)

At the Last Supper Jesus promised this double witness: the Spirit “will testify on my behalf. You also are to testify” (John 15:26–27).

How the Church reads it.

The Church has always read verse 29 as the charter of conscience. It does not make Christians disobedient citizens: Peter and Paul teach respect for rulers (Romans 13:1–7; 1 Peter 2:13–17). But when a human command contradicts God’s law, obedience to God comes first. The martyrs of every age, from Stephen onward, have lived this verse.

John 3:31–36 · The one who comes from above

Setting.

Just before this passage John the Baptist gives his last witness to Jesus: “He must increase, but I must decrease” (3:30). It is not clear whether vv. 31–36 continue the Baptist’s words or are the evangelist’s comment. Many commentators read them as the evangelist’s reflection, gathering up the themes of the whole chapter: from above, testimony, Spirit, life.

Structure.

  • vv. 31–32: the one from above and the one from the earth.
  • vv. 33–34: accepting his testimony; the words of God and the Spirit.
  • vv. 35–36: the Father’s love for the Son and the choice it sets before us.

Key words.

  • anōthen, from above: The same word as in 3:3, where it can mean both “again” and “from above”. Jesus is from above by origin; believers are born from above by grace.
  • sphragizein, to certify, to seal: To set one’s seal on a document is to confirm it as true. Whoever accepts Christ’s testimony puts his own seal on the truth of God.
  • orgē, wrath: Not a passing temper in God, but his holiness turned against sin. The Greek says it “remains” (menei) on the one who refuses the Son: it is what is left when life is refused.

Verse by verse.

“The one who comes from above is above all;” (3:31)

John the Baptist was the greatest of the prophets, yet he speaks as one “of the earth”. Jesus alone speaks from heaven, because he comes from there.

“for he gives the Spirit without measure.” (3:34)

The Greek does not say who gives. It may mean that God gives the Spirit to the Son without measure, or that the Son gives the Spirit without measure to believers. Both are true: the Son receives all from the Father and pours it out (John 1:16).

“The Father loves the Son and has placed all things in his hands.” (3:35)

John repeats this at the Last Supper: “Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands” (13:3) rose and washed the disciples’ feet. Everything placed in his hands is used to serve.

How the Church reads it.

The Church reads these verses as a summary of faith in the Son: he comes from the Father, speaks the Father’s words, and gives the Spirit. Verse 36 places eternal life in the present tense: the believer “has” it already, as a gift begun in Baptism. The Church also teaches that this gift can be lost by grave sin and found again by repentance; final salvation remains a hope we pray for (CCC 1861; CCC 1821).

For study and teaching

  • When has obeying God cost me something before other people? What helped me stand?
  • The Father has put all things into the hands of Jesus. What do I need to place there today?
