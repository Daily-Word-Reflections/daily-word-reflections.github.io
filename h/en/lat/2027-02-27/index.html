Word for the day

“So he set off and went to his father. But while he was still far off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion; he ran and put his arms around him and kissed him.” Luke 15:20

Reflection

The prophet Micah ends his book with a prayer and a song of wonder. He asks God to shepherd his people “as in the days of old,” and then breaks out: “Who is a God like you, pardoning iniquity and passing over the transgression of the remnant of your possession? He does not retain his anger forever, because he delights in showing clemency.” God does not merely tolerate forgiving; he delights in it. “You will cast all our sins into the depths of the sea.” Sins thrown into the deepest sea are not fished out again. The Psalm adds its own picture: “as far as the east is from the west, so far he removes our transgressions from us.” And it gives us today's refrain: the LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love.

Then Jesus tells the parable that has brought more sinners home than perhaps any other page of Scripture. The Pharisees and scribes grumble: “This fellow welcomes sinners and eats with them.” So he tells of a father with two sons. The younger demands his inheritance, as if his father were already dead, and wastes it in a distant country. When famine comes he feeds pigs and longs for their pods. Then “he came to himself” and says: “I will get up and go to my father.” He rehearses a speech, but the father does not let him finish. “While he was still far off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion; he ran and put his arms around him and kissed him.” Robe, ring, sandals, the fatted calf: the son is restored, not as a hired hand but as a son. Then the elder son, who never left home, refuses to come in. The father goes out to him too: “Son, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours.” The story ends with the question hanging in the air: will he come in? Both sons are lost; both are loved. Today we also remember St Gregory of Narek, the tenth-century Armenian monk and Doctor of the Church, whose Book of Lamentations is a long prayer “speaking with God from the depths of the heart,” the prayer of a sinner who trusts the Father's mercy more than his own sin.

Homily outline

Theme: “While he was still far off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion.”

The God who delights in mercy, the son who came home, and the son who must still come in.

1. Who is a God like you?

  • Pardoning iniquity.
  • Delighting in clemency.
  • Sins cast into the sea.
“He will again have compassion upon us; he will tread our iniquities under foot. You will cast all our sins into the depths of the sea.” Micah 7:19
“Be merciful, just as your Father is merciful.” Luke 6:36

Apply: Throw one old sin you keep fishing out back into the sea: believe it is forgiven, and stop accusing yourself.

2. The younger son

  • The distant country.
  • He came to himself.
  • The father who runs.
“as far as the east is from the west, so far he removes our transgressions from us.” Psalm 103:12
“So he set off and went to his father. But while he was still far off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion; he ran and put his arms around him and kissed him.” Luke 15:20

Apply: Go to confession before the Third Sunday of Lent if you can.

3. The elder son

  • Near but far.
  • Resentment at mercy.
  • The invitation to come in.
“The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love.” Psalm 103:8
“And the Pharisees and the scribes were grumbling and saying, “This fellow welcomes sinners and eats with them.”” Luke 15:2

Apply: Welcome back one person who has returned to the Church or to your family, and rejoice with them.

God delights to forgive, and runs to meet us.

Both sons are invited to the feast; the door is open.

Take-home resolution: Make a good confession this Lent, and forgive as you have been forgiven.

Full homily

Dear friends, Micah asks in wonder: who is a God like you, pardoning iniquity? He does not retain his anger forever, because he delights in showing clemency (Micah 7:18). God does not forgive reluctantly. He delights in it. He casts our sins into the depths of the sea.

Jesus shows us this God in a story. A younger son takes his inheritance and wastes it. When he has nothing left, he comes to himself and says: I will get up and go to my father. He prepares a speech.

But while he was still far off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion; he ran and put his arms around him and kissed him (Luke 15:20). The father does not wait. He runs. He does not let the son finish his speech. He gives him a robe, a ring and sandals: you are still my son.

And the elder son, who stayed at home, refuses to come in. The father goes out to him too: son, you are always with me. Today, whether we are the younger son or the elder, the Father is coming out to meet us. St Gregory of Narek prayed: my sins are many, but your mercy is greater (in substance). Let us go home. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told about a boy who ran away and did wrong things. When he came home, his father ran to him and hugged him and had a party! God is like that father. When we say sorry, he runs to hug us.

Prayer

Father of mercies, you run to meet us while we are still far off. Bring us home from every distant country, cast our sins into the depths of the sea, and free us from the resentment of the elder son. Through the prayers of St Gregory of Narek, teach us to speak with you from the depths of our hearts. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
