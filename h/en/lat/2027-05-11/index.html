Word for the day

“And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” John 17:3

Reflection

Paul is on his way to Jerusalem, hurrying to arrive before Pentecost (Acts 20:16). His ship stops at Miletus, a port about fifty kilometres south of Ephesus, and he sends for the elders of the Ephesian church. When they arrive he speaks to them as a father who knows he will not see his children again. This is the only speech in Acts that Paul gives to Christian leaders, and it is his farewell. He looks back first: “You yourselves know how I lived among you the entire time from the first day that I set foot in Asia, serving the Lord with all humility and with tears.” He reminds them of the trials he endured, including plots by those who opposed him, and of how he held nothing back: “I did not shrink from doing anything helpful, proclaiming the message to you and teaching you publicly and from house to house.” His message was simple and complete: “repentance toward God and faith toward our Lord Jesus,” to Jews and Greeks alike.

Then he looks ahead: “as a captive to the Spirit, I am on my way to Jerusalem, not knowing what will happen to me there, except that the Holy Spirit testifies to me in every city that imprisonment and persecutions are waiting for me.” Paul does not know the details, only the direction. And he is at peace: “I do not count my life of any value to myself, if only I may finish my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the good news of God's grace.” The word “course” is the runner's track; Paul sees his life as a race to be run to the end. He can say, with a clear conscience, “I did not shrink from declaring to you the whole purpose of God.” Not a selection of pleasant truths, but the whole of it. The Psalm gives thanks to the God “who daily bears us up,” and to whom “belongs escape from death”: that is the confidence with which Paul walks towards chains.

In the Gospel we hear the beginning of the great prayer of Jesus. “He looked up to heaven and said, ‘Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you.’” The hour is the cross, and Jesus calls it glory: the moment when the love of the Father and the Son is most fully shown. Then he defines eternal life, not as endless time but as a relationship: “this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” To know, in the Bible, is to love and be united. Jesus can say what Paul will echo at Miletus: “I glorified you on earth by finishing the work that you gave me to do.” And he prays for his disciples: “I am asking on their behalf … because they are yours … And now I am no longer in the world, but they are in the world, and I am coming to you.” These are words of the Ascension. Jesus goes to the Father, and he leaves his friends in the world, held in his prayer. Paul at Miletus, handing his flock to God, is praying the same prayer in his own way.

Homily outline

Theme: “This is eternal life, that they may know you.”

Paul's farewell at Miletus, a life run to the finish, and the prayer of Jesus for his own as he goes to the Father.

1. Serving the Lord with humility and tears

  • A father's farewell.
  • Publicly and from house to house.
  • Holding nothing back.
“He has told you, O mortal, what is good; and what does the Lord require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?” Micah 6:8
“I did not shrink from doing anything helpful, proclaiming the message to you and teaching you publicly and from house to house,” Acts 20:20

Apply: Visit or call one family of your parish or neighbourhood this week, simply to share faith and friendship.

2. If only I may finish my course

  • Bound by the Spirit.
  • Not knowing, but going.
  • A race run to the end.
“So teach us to count our days that we may gain a wise heart.” Psalm 90:12
“But I do not count my life of any value to myself, if only I may finish my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the good news of God’s grace.” Acts 20:24

Apply: Name the ministry the Lord has given you in your present state of life, and finish one neglected part of it this week.

3. This is eternal life

  • The hour of glory.
  • Knowing as loving.
  • I am coming to you.
“but let those who boast boast in this, that they understand and know me, that I am the Lord; I act with steadfast love, justice, and righteousness in the earth, for in these things I delight, says the Lord.” Jeremiah 9:24
“And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” John 17:3

Apply: Read John 17 slowly once this week, and let Jesus pray for you.

Paul, leaving Ephesus, gave his people all of the Gospel with humility and tears, and walked towards chains wanting only to finish his course.

Jesus, going to the Father, prays for us who remain in the world; eternal life begins now, in knowing and loving the Father and the Son through the Holy Spirit.

Take-home resolution: Finish one neglected duty this week as an offering to the Lord, and pray John 17 slowly before Pentecost.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we stand on the beach at Miletus and listen to a farewell. Paul has called the elders of Ephesus, and he speaks to them for the last time. What does he remember? Not his success, not his miracles, but this: I served the Lord with all humility and with tears (Acts 20:19).

He tells them: I did not shrink from doing anything helpful; I taught you publicly and from house to house. I did not shrink from declaring to you the whole purpose of God (Acts 20:20, 27). Paul did not give his people only the easy parts of the Gospel. He gave them all of it, because he loved them.

Then he looks ahead. He is going to Jerusalem, and he does not know exactly what will happen, only that chains are waiting. Yet he says: I do not count my life of any value to myself, if only I may finish my course (Acts 20:24). Paul sees his life as a race. What matters is not how long it is, but that it is finished well.

Each of us has a course to run. A mother, a farmer, a student, a priest, a sick person in bed: each has a ministry received from the Lord Jesus. The question is not whether it is easy, but whether we will finish it with faith.

In the Gospel Jesus lifts his eyes to heaven and prays: Father, the hour has come. And he tells us what eternal life is: this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent (John 17:3). Eternal life is not only after death. It begins now, when we know God, not just about him, but as a friend knows a friend.

Jesus says: I am no longer in the world, but they are in the world, and I am coming to you. These are Ascension words. He has gone to the Father, but he has not stopped praying for us. In these days before Pentecost, let us let him pray for us, and let us run our course to the end, with the Holy Spirit as our strength. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, St Paul had to say goodbye to his friends in Ephesus. He told them: I loved you, I taught you everything about Jesus, and I even cried for you. Now I must go where God sends me.

Jesus prayed to his Father for his friends, and he prays for you too. He wants you to know God as your best friend. Say: Jesus, I want to know you more!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, when your hour came, you lifted up your eyes to heaven and prayed for your friends. Pray for us now, as you go to the Father. Teach us that eternal life is to know the only true God and you whom he has sent; let us know you, love you and be one with you. Give us the heart of Paul, who served you with humility and tears, held nothing back from your people, and ran his course to the end. Bless all pastors of your Church, that they may shepherd your flock faithfully and declare the whole purpose of God. Keep us in your name while we remain in the world, and send us your Holy Spirit, for whom we wait with Mary. You live and reign for ever and ever. Alleluia. Amen.
