Word for the day

“From that time Jesus began to proclaim, “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near.”” Matthew 4:17

Reflection

After the Epiphany the Gospel shows the light moving out into the world. Jesus leaves Nazareth and settles in Capernaum, in “Galilee of the Gentiles”, a border land mixed with pagans. Matthew sees Isaiah fulfilled: “the people who sat in darkness have seen a great light.” The first word of his preaching is “Repent”, turn around, because the kingdom has come near. Then he goes through Galilee teaching, proclaiming and curing “every disease and every sickness”, and crowds come even from the Decapolis and beyond the Jordan.

St John gives a rule for discernment: “do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits.” The test is Christ: every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God. Many voices offer light today; not all lead to the Light. And John adds a word of courage to the small community: “the one who is in you is greater than the one who is in the world.”

Full homily

Dear friends, after the Epiphany the Church follows the light as it spreads. Jesus leaves Nazareth and makes his home in Capernaum, by the Sea of Galilee. Matthew calls this region Galilee of the Gentiles, a border land where Jews and pagans lived side by side.

There Isaiah's words come true: the people who sat in darkness have seen a great light (Matthew 4:16). Jesus does not start in Jerusalem, among the important people. He starts on the margins, where people felt far from God.

His first word is: repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near (Matthew 4:17). To repent means to turn around. God has come close; we must turn to face him. Then Jesus heals every disease and sickness, and crowds come from everywhere.

St John warns us to test the spirits (1 John 4:1). Not every voice that speaks of God leads to God. The test is simple: does it confess Jesus Christ come in the flesh? Does it lead to the Church, the sacraments, love of neighbour?

Let us welcome the light today. Let us turn from one thing that keeps us in darkness. And let us take courage: the one who is in you is greater than the one who is in the world (1 John 4:4). Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is like a big light that came to people who were in the dark. When we say sorry and do good, we let his light shine in us!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, light of the nations, you began your preaching in Galilee of the Gentiles. Shine into the dark places of my life, turn my heart back to you, and teach me to recognise your Spirit. Amen.
