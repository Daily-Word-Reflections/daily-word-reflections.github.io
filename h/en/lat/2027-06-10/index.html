Word for the day

“For it is the God who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,” who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” 2 Corinthians 4:6

Reflection

Yesterday Paul spoke of Moses coming down from Sinai with a face shining so brightly that the people could not look at him. Exodus tells us that Moses then put a veil over his face, taking it off only when he went in to speak with the Lord. Paul turns this into a picture of how anyone, then or now, can read the Scriptures without seeing their goal: “Indeed, to this very day whenever Moses is read, a veil lies over their minds; but when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed.” We must hear this as Paul meant it. He is speaking with grief about his own people, whom he loved so much that he wished he could be cut off for their sake (Romans 9:3); the Church today, following the Second Vatican Council, rejects every blame laid on the Jewish people as a whole and honours them as the people of the covenant God has never revoked. And the veil is not only theirs. Every Christian who reads the Bible coldly, as a book of rules or old stories, has a veil over the heart. The veil falls when one turns to the Lord. Then comes the most beautiful sentence of the chapter: “Now the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom. And all of us, with unveiled faces, seeing the glory of the Lord as though reflected in a mirror, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another.” Moses' face shone for a time; the Christian is being changed day by day into the likeness of Christ. The reading ends at the first page of the Bible: “For it is the God who said, ‘Let light shine out of darkness,’ who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” The Psalm promises the same: “that his glory may dwell in our land,” where “righteousness and peace will kiss each other.”

In the Gospel Jesus begins to show what he meant by fulfilling the Law. “Unless your righteousness exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.” The scribes and Pharisees were serious, devout men; Jesus asks his disciples to go deeper still, to the root. “You have heard that it was said to those of ancient times, ‘You shall not murder’ … But I say to you that if you are angry with a brother or sister, you will be liable to judgment.” Murder begins in the heart, with anger nursed, with insults like “You fool.” Then Jesus gives an astonishing instruction: “So when you are offering your gift at the altar, if you remember that your brother or sister has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift.” God would rather wait for our offering than receive it from a heart at war with a brother. The face of Christ that Paul speaks of is turned towards each person we meet; we cannot see his glory and refuse to look at our neighbour. In these monsoon days the sky can be dark at noon and suddenly bright when the clouds open. So it is with a reconciled heart: the light of Christ breaks through as soon as the quarrel is ended.

Homily outline

Theme: “From one degree of glory to another.”

A veil removed, a face transformed by the glory of Christ, and a peace made before the altar.

1. The veil removed

  • Moses' shining face.
  • Reading without seeing.
  • Turning to the Lord.
“Moses did not know that the skin of his face shone because he had been talking with God.” Exodus 34:29
“but when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed.” 2 Corinthians 3:16

Apply: Each day this week read a short passage of the Gospel, asking first: Lord, remove the veil.

2. From glory to glory

  • The Lord is the Spirit.
  • Changed into his image.
  • Light in the face of Christ.
“Then God said, “Let there be light”; and there was light.” Genesis 1:3
“I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.” John 8:12

Apply: Spend five minutes today before the Blessed Sacrament or a crucifix, simply looking at the Lord and letting him look at you.

3. First be reconciled

  • Beyond the scribes and Pharisees.
  • Anger is the root of murder.
  • Leave your gift and go.
“Steadfast love and faithfulness will meet; righteousness and peace will kiss each other.” Psalm 85:10
“leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift.” Matthew 5:24

Apply: Before Sunday, take one step towards peace with someone, and make the sign of peace at Mass with that person in your heart.

When we turn to the Lord the veil falls, and we are changed from glory to glory into the image of Christ, in whose face God's light shines.

Jesus asks for a righteousness that reaches the heart: no anger nursed, no insult spoken, and reconciliation before we offer our gift.

Take-home resolution: This week, end one quarrel and let the light of Christ shine through the peace you make.

Full homily

Dear friends, Moses came down from Mount Sinai with his face shining, and he covered it with a veil. Paul uses this story to speak about our hearts. Sometimes we read the Bible, we hear it at Mass, but it does not touch us. There is a veil over our hearts. Paul says: when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed (2 Corinthians 3:16).

And then something wonderful happens. All of us, with unveiled faces, seeing the glory of the Lord as though reflected in a mirror, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another (2 Corinthians 3:18). Every time we pray, every time we receive Holy Communion, every time we forgive, we become a little more like Jesus. Holiness is not a sudden jump. It is a slow change, from glory to glory.

But the Gospel tells us where this change must begin. Jesus says: you have heard that it was said, You shall not murder. But I say to you that if you are angry with a brother or sister, you will be liable to judgment (Matthew 5:21–22). Most of us have never killed anyone. But how many of us carry anger for years? How many insults have we spoken? Jesus goes to the root.

Then he says something that should make us stop: if you are offering your gift at the altar and remember that your brother has something against you, leave your gift there; first be reconciled, and then come and offer your gift (Matthew 5:24). God would rather wait for our gift than receive it from a heart at war. Before the next Mass, let us take one step towards peace. Then our faces too will shine with the light of Christ. Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever had a fight with your brother, sister or friend, and then you did not want to look at them? Jesus says that God wants us to make peace quickly. If you come to church to pray and you remember that you hurt someone, go and say sorry first! St Paul says that when we turn to Jesus, our faces shine with his light, like a mirror in the sun. A face that has said sorry shines the brightest!

Prayer

God of light, you said at the beginning, Let there be light, and now you have shone in our hearts in the face of Jesus Christ. Take away every veil from our minds when we read your word, and change us from glory to glory into the image of your Son. Lord Jesus, you see the anger we hide and the quarrels we do not end; give us courage to be reconciled before we bring our gifts to your altar. Let righteousness and peace kiss in our homes and in our land. Amen.
