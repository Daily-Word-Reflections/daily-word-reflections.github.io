Word for the day

“The Israelites went into the sea on dry ground, the waters forming a wall for them on their right and on their left.” Exodus 14:22

Reflection

The night of the sea is the centre of the whole Old Testament, and today's reading tells it slowly. “Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. The Lord drove the sea back by a strong east wind all night, and turned the sea into dry land; and the waters were divided.” Notice that the work of the night is God's, and that Moses' part is the stretched-out hand. The Israelites walk into the sea “on dry ground,” the waters forming a wall on the right and on the left. The Egyptians follow. At “the morning watch,” the last part of the night, the Lord “in the pillar of fire and cloud looked down upon the Egyptian army, and threw the Egyptian army into panic.” The pillar that was a light for Israel is a terror for the pursuers. The wheels clog, and the Egyptians themselves cry: “Let us flee from the Israelites, for the Lord is fighting for them against Egypt.” Moses stretches out his hand again, and at dawn the sea returns. The Bible makes no triumph out of the scene: it says simply, “not one of them remained,” and then describes the Israelites seeing the dead on the shore. After this the Bible tells us how Israel's faith began: “Israel saw the great work that the Lord did against the Egyptians. So the people feared the Lord and believed in the Lord and in his servant Moses.” Faith in Israel does not begin with an idea; it begins with a rescue. Then they sing. The psalm shows the singing in Hebrew poetry: “At the blast of your nostrils the waters piled up, the floods stood up in a heap.” The poet speaks of God with the picture of breath and wind, because the image of a mighty breath matches the east wind of the night. And the song looks past the sea to the destination: God brought his people in and “planted them on the mountain of your own possession, the place, O Lord, that you made your abode.” The sea is not the goal; the sanctuary is. From earliest times Christians have read this night as a picture of baptism: St Paul says that our ancestors “were all under the cloud and all passed through the sea” (1 Corinthians 10:1), and the great Easter hymn, the Exsultet, says to God, “This is the night when once you led our forebears, Israel's children, from slavery in Egypt, and made them pass dry-shod through the Red Sea.”

In the Gospel, Jesus is teaching the crowds when “his mother and his brothers” arrive and stand outside, “wanting to speak to him.” Someone passes on the message. Jesus asks, “Who is my mother, and who are my brothers?” and stretches out his hand toward his disciples: “Here are my mother and my brothers! For whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother.” This is not a rejection of his family. In the Jewish world, a teacher's family could be expected to look after him, and Jesus is lifting the question to a higher level: the new family of God, whose bond is not blood but obedience to the Father. The Church has always understood this verse in the light of what Luke tells us about Mary. At the Annunciation she said, “Here am I, the servant of the Lord; let it be with me according to your word,” and Elizabeth called her blessed because she believed (Luke 1:38, 45). When a woman in the crowd praised the womb that bore Jesus, he answered, “Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and obey it!” (Luke 11:28), and no one has heard and obeyed more perfectly than his Mother. The Second Vatican Council teaches that in the course of her Son's preaching Mary “received the words whereby in extolling a kingdom beyond the calculations and bonds of flesh and blood, he declared blessed those who heard and kept the word of God, as she was faithfully doing” (Lumen Gentium 58). So Jesus' word does not diminish Mary; it points to the reason for her greatness. The “brothers” mentioned here are, according to the Church's constant understanding and the Semitic way of speaking, his close relatives; the Catechism recalls that James and Joseph, called his brothers, are the sons of another Mary, a disciple of Christ (Catechism 500), and the Church holds Mary ever-virgin. The Gospel opens a door to all of us: whoever does the Father's will belongs to Jesus' own household. Today the Church also remembers St Apollinaris, traditionally the first bishop of Ravenna in Italy and a disciple of St Peter, who is said to have been sent from Rome to Ravenna and to have suffered many tortures for the faith before dying as a martyr in the second century. Historians cannot prove all the details, but his cult in Ravenna is very ancient, and in the great basilica of Sant'Apollinare in Classe a sixth-century mosaic shows him in the apse, in his bishop's vestments, with his arms raised in prayer in the middle of a green meadow, with twelve sheep looking up at him. He is the good shepherd's image of the family that the Lord gathers from the waters: sheep who hear their shepherd's voice.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother.”

The night of the sea, the faith that was born from a rescue, and the family that the Lord gathers around his Father's will.

1. Through the sea on dry ground

  • The east wind all night.
  • The wall of water on either side.
  • The pillar that terrifies the pursuers.
“The Israelites went into the sea on dry ground, the waters forming a wall for them on their right and on their left.” Exodus 14:22
“I do not want you to be unaware, brothers and sisters, that our ancestors were all under the cloud and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea,” 1 Corinthians 10:1–2

Apply: Make the sign of the cross with holy water slowly each time you enter a church this week, and remember your baptism.

2. They believed in the Lord

  • Faith begins with a rescue.
  • They feared, believed and sang.
  • The sea is not the goal.
“Israel saw the great work that the Lord did against the Egyptians. So the people feared the Lord and believed in the Lord and in his servant Moses.” Exodus 14:31
“Therefore we have been buried with him by baptism into death, so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.” Romans 6:4

Apply: Write down one moment when God rescued you, and thank him for it at Mass on Sunday.

3. My brother and sister and mother

  • His mother and brothers outside.
  • The family of the Father's will.
  • Mary, the first to hear and obey.
“If my father and mother forsake me, the Lord will take me up.” Psalm 27:10
“For whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother.” Matthew 12:50

Apply: Do one thing this week for someone who has no family nearby: a call, a visit, a meal.

The Lord brings Israel through the sea, and the people believe in him and sing; in baptism he has brought us through the same waters.

Jesus calls those who do the Father's will his own family, and Mary, who said yes first, stands at the head of that family.

Take-home resolution: Each day this week do one thing that you know the Father asks of you, and thank him for the family of faith.

Full homily

Dear friends, today's first reading is the night of the sea. The Lord drove the sea back by a strong east wind all night, and the waters were divided (Exodus 14:21). Look at what happens. The Israelites go into the sea on dry ground. The Egyptians follow and are thrown into panic: let us flee, for the Lord is fighting for them! At dawn the sea returns. It is a night of terror and a night of rescue.

And what is the result? Israel saw the great work that the Lord did. So the people feared the Lord and believed in the Lord and in his servant Moses (Exodus 14:31). Faith begins with a rescue. And then they sing. That is why the Church reads this story every year at the Easter Vigil: because in baptism each of us has been through that sea. We were brought out of slavery to sin, through the water, into the family of God.

In the Gospel Jesus says something that surprises us. His mother and his brothers are standing outside, and he says: who is my mother, and who are my brothers? Does he not love his family? Of course he does. But he opens the door wider: whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother. And who did the Father's will more perfectly than Mary? Let it be with me according to your word (Luke 1:38). Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and obey it. Mary is not left outside; she is first among those inside.

So today let us ask: do I belong to this family? The test is not that I am baptised, nor that I come to church, but that I do the Father's will. Today the Church remembers St Apollinaris, an early bishop of Ravenna, a shepherd who gave his life for his flock. Let us ask him to pray for all our shepherds, and for each of us to be a brother and sister to someone who needs a family. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what happened on the night of the sea? God sent a strong wind, and the water of the sea moved back, and God's people walked through on dry ground! Then the water came back and the king's army could not follow. God's people sang and danced for joy. In the Gospel Jesus says that everyone who does what God wants is in his family. Mary always did what God wanted, so she is the first in that family. And you can be in it too! Do one thing today that you know God wants: share, help, or say sorry.

Prayer

God of our fathers, you brought Israel through the sea on dry ground and made them your own people. Bring us through the waters of baptism into your family, and let us be those who hear your word and do it. Lord Jesus, you call us your brothers and sisters and your mother: make us faithful to the Father's will, as the Blessed Virgin Mary was faithful. Through the prayers of St Apollinaris, shepherd and martyr, give your Church shepherds who watch over their flocks with love, and keep us in your sheepfold. Amen.
