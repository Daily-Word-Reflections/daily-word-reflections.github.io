Thursday of the Fourth Week of Lent

Readings

  • First Reading Exodus 32:7–14 · Turn from your fierce wrath; change your mind and do not bring disaster on your people
  • Responsorial Psalm Psalm 106:19–20, 21–22, 23 · Remember us, O Lord, as you favor your people
  • Gospel John 5:31–47 · Your accuser is Moses, on whom you have set your hope

Word for the day

“You search the scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that testify on my behalf. Yet you refuse to come to me to have life.” John 5:39–40

Reflection

At the foot of Sinai, only weeks after the covenant, the people grow tired of waiting for Moses and make a calf of gold. God says to Moses: “Go down at once! Your people… have acted perversely.” They have “cast for themselves an image of a calf” and said, “These are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt!” The Psalm puts it sharply: “They exchanged the glory of God for the image of an ox that eats grass.” Then God tests Moses: “Now let me alone, so that my wrath may burn hot against them… and of you I will make a great nation.” Moses refuses the offer. He stands, as the Psalm says, “in the breach,” between God and the people, and pleads: they are your people, whom you brought out of Egypt; what will the nations say? “Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, your servants.” And “the LORD changed his mind about the disaster that he planned to bring on his people.” God does not change like a moody human being; Scripture speaks in human terms to show that he has willed to let the prayer of his friends shape history. Moses is the great intercessor, and a figure of Christ, who “always lives to make intercession” for us.

In the Gospel Jesus calls witnesses, as in a court. John the Baptist testified: he was “a burning and shining lamp.” The works the Father gave Jesus to do testify. The Father himself testifies. And the Scriptures testify: “You search the scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that testify on my behalf. Yet you refuse to come to me to have life.” It is possible to know the Bible well and still miss the Person to whom it points. Then a surprise: “Your accuser is Moses, on whom you have set your hope… he wrote about me.” The same Moses who once pleaded for a sinful people now bears witness to Jesus. Jesus is speaking to the religious leaders who opposed him, not condemning the Jewish people; the Church received from Israel the Scriptures, the covenants and the promises, and reads Moses with deep reverence. The question is for us too: do we read the Scriptures in order to come to Christ? Lent invites us to put away our own golden calves, the things we worship instead of God, and to come to the One of whom the whole of Scripture speaks.

Full homily

Dear friends, at the foot of Mount Sinai the people grew impatient and made a golden calf. They exchanged the glory of God for the image of an ox that eats grass, as the Psalm says. We laugh at them, but we do the same: we put money, success, our phones or our pride in the place of God.

God says to Moses: let me alone… and of you I will make a great nation. But Moses will not leave his people. He pleads: O LORD, why does your wrath burn hot against your people, whom you brought out of the land of Egypt? (Exodus 32:11). Remember Abraham, Isaac and Israel. And God spares them. That is the power of intercession.

Moses points to Jesus, who stands between God and us, and always lives to make intercession for us. In the Gospel Jesus says: you search the scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that testify on my behalf. Yet you refuse to come to me to have life (John 5:39–40). The Bible is not just a book to study. It is a road to a Person.

This Lent, let us break our golden calves. Let us pray for someone who has wandered, as Moses prayed, as many mothers pray. And let us open the Scriptures every day to meet Jesus there. Amen.

For children

Children, God's people made a golden statue of a calf and prayed to it instead of to God. Moses prayed for them and God forgave them. Let us pray today for someone we love, like Moses did.

Prayer

Lord God, you listened to the prayer of Moses and spared your people. Free us from every golden calf, and give us hearts that intercede for others. Open our minds to understand the Scriptures, that we may come to your Son and have life. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
