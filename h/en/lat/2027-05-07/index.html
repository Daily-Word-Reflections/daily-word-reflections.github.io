Word for the day

“So you have pain now; but I will see you again, and your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you.” John 16:22

Reflection

Paul has been in Corinth for some time. He has been opposed, he has moved next door to the synagogue, and he has seen the synagogue official believe. Yet he is afraid. Writing to the Corinthians later, he admits it: “And I came to you in weakness and in fear and in much trembling” (1 Corinthians 2:3). He knew what usually came next in a city: riots, beatings, flight by night. So the Lord speaks to him in a vision: “Do not be afraid, but speak and do not be silent; for I am with you, and no one will lay a hand on you to harm you, for there are many in this city who are my people.” The Lord does not promise that there will be no trouble. He promises his presence, and he gives Paul a new way of looking at the noisy port city: many in this city are already mine, though they do not know it yet. So Paul stays “a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them,” the longest stay of his journeys so far.

Then the trouble comes. When Gallio becomes proconsul of Achaia, some leaders of the Jewish community bring Paul before the tribunal, accusing him of persuading people to worship God against the law. Gallio, the brother of the philosopher Seneca, will not even hear Paul's defence: this is “a matter of questions about words and names and your own law, see to it yourselves.” An inscription found at Delphi dates Gallio's year in Corinth to about 51 or 52, and so this scene is one of the fixed points by which we date Paul's life. A Roman judge's indifference becomes, for a time, the protection of the Gospel. Paul stays on, then sails for Syria with Priscilla and Aquila, cutting his hair at Cenchreae because of a vow, a sign that he still lived as a devout Jew. Luke's phrase “the Jews” here means the group in that synagogue who opposed Paul, not the Jewish people; the official Crispus had believed, and Sosthenes, perhaps the same “brother Sosthenes” who later writes with Paul (1 Corinthians 1:1), suffers in the uproar.

The Gospel gives the image of a woman in labour: “she has pain, because her hour has come. But when her child is born, she no longer remembers the anguish because of the joy of having brought a human being into the world.” Jesus uses the word “hour,” the word he uses for his own Passion. His cross is a birth. On this First Friday the Church looks at the pierced Heart of Jesus. From his opened side came blood and water (John 19:34), and the Fathers saw there the birth of the Church, as Eve was formed from the side of the sleeping Adam. The pain of Good Friday was a labour, and its child is a new people. That is why the joy Jesus promises is a joy that “no one will take from you”: it is not a mood that comes and goes, but the life of the Risen One born in us. And the Psalm, an ancient song for the Lord's enthronement, already rings with Ascension joy: “Clap your hands, all you peoples; shout to God with loud songs of joy.”

Homily outline

Theme: “No one will take your joy from you.”

The Lord's word to a fearful apostle, a judge who opened a door without knowing it, and the labour of the cross that gives birth to joy.

1. Do not be afraid, but speak

  • Paul in weakness and fear.
  • I am with you.
  • Many in this city are my people.
“Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you, says the Lord.” Jeremiah 1:8
“One night the Lord said to Paul in a vision, “Do not be afraid, but speak and do not be silent; for I am with you, and no one will lay a hand on you to harm you, for there are many in this city who are my people.”” Acts 18:9–10

Apply: Speak one word of faith this week that you have been holding back out of fear.

2. The Lord stood by me

  • Dragged before Gallio.
  • A judge who would not judge.
  • Protection through strange doors.
“do not fear, for I am with you, do not be afraid, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, I will help you, I will uphold you with my victorious right hand.” Isaiah 41:10
“But the Lord stood by me and gave me strength, so that through me the message might be fully proclaimed and all the Gentiles might hear it.” 2 Timothy 4:17

Apply: Thank God tonight for one time he protected you through people who did not even know they were helping.

3. Her hour has come

  • The image of labour.
  • The pierced Heart and the birth of the Church.
  • A joy that cannot be taken.
“Shall I open the womb and not deliver? says the Lord; shall I, the one who delivers, shut the womb? says your God.” Isaiah 66:9
“When a woman is in labor, she has pain, because her hour has come. But when her child is born, she no longer remembers the anguish because of the joy of having brought a human being into the world.” John 16:21

Apply: Consecrate your family to the Sacred Heart this First Friday and pray the novena to the Holy Spirit each day until Pentecost.

The Lord does not always remove danger, but he removes fear by his presence: I am with you.

The cross was a labour of love; from the pierced Heart of Jesus the Church was born, and with her a joy that no one can take away.

Take-home resolution: Begin the novena to the Holy Spirit today, and pray it with Mary every day until Pentecost.

Full homily

Dear friends, we think of St Paul as a man without fear. Today's reading tells us otherwise. One night in Corinth the Lord speaks to him in a vision: do not be afraid, but speak and do not be silent; for I am with you (Acts 18:9–10). The Lord would not say do not be afraid to someone who was not afraid.

Paul himself told the Corinthians later: I came to you in weakness and in fear and in much trembling. He had been beaten in other cities, chased out at night. Now in Corinth he wondered: will it happen again? And the Lord says: I am with you, and there are many in this city who are my people.

Notice what the Lord promises and what he does not. He does not promise that nobody will make trouble. Trouble does come: Paul is dragged before the judge Gallio. But the Lord keeps his word: no one harms him. The judge refuses the case, and Paul goes on preaching. God's protection sometimes comes through strange doors, even through the indifference of a Roman official.

In the Gospel Jesus speaks of a mother in labour. She has pain because her hour has come, but when the child is born she forgets the pain because of her joy. Mothers here know this better than any preacher. Jesus is speaking of his own hour, his cross. His Passion was a birth.

Today is First Friday. We look at the Heart of Jesus, opened by the soldier's spear. From it came blood and water, and from it the Church was born. That is why our joy is a joy that no one can take away: it was born from his love, and his love does not die.

So if you are afraid today, hear the Lord's word to Paul: do not be afraid; I am with you. Come to his Heart in confession and in Communion. And from today, with Mary, let us begin to pray for the Holy Spirit, who will give us courage to speak. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, even St Paul was sometimes afraid! But Jesus told him: do not be afraid, I am with you. Jesus says the same to you when you are scared.

Today is First Friday, a day to remember Jesus' Heart, full of love. Put your hand on your heart and say: Jesus, I love you too!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you said to Paul in the night: do not be afraid, but speak, for I am with you. Speak that word to us. When we are silent from fear, give us courage; when we are opposed, give us patience; when we are protected, give us thanks. On this First Friday we come to your pierced Heart. From your opened side the Church was born; from your labour of love comes our joy. Take our hearts, heal them, and make them like yours. Send us your Holy Spirit, for whom we begin to wait with Mary your Mother, and give us the joy that no one can take from us. You live and reign for ever and ever. Alleluia. Amen.
