Word for the day

“When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd.” Matthew 9:36

Reflection

Isaiah speaks tenderly to a people in distress: God “waits to be gracious to you”; “he will surely be gracious to you at the sound of your cry”; your Teacher will no longer hide, and “your ears shall hear a word behind you, saying, ‘This is the way; walk in it.’” He promises rain, harvest, and the day when the Lord “binds up the injuries of his people”.

Jesus goes through all the towns teaching, proclaiming and healing. Seeing the crowds “harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd”, he is moved with compassion. His response is twofold: pray (“ask the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers”) and send (he calls the Twelve and gives them authority). “You received without payment; give without payment.” Advent compassion becomes mission.

Full homily

Dear friends, Isaiah speaks tenderly to a suffering people: he will surely be gracious to you at the sound of your cry; when he hears it, he will answer you. Though the Lord may give you the bread of adversity and the water of affliction, yet your Teacher will not hide himself any more (Isaiah 30:19–20).

And then a beautiful promise: your ears shall hear a word behind you, saying, this is the way; walk in it (Isaiah 30:21). God guides us, not always by showing the whole road, but by a quiet word at each turning.

In the Gospel Jesus goes about all the cities and villages, teaching, proclaiming the good news and curing every sickness. When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd. He tells his disciples: the harvest is plentiful, but the labourers are few (Matthew 9:35–38).

Then he does something surprising: he calls the Twelve and sends them out to do what he has been doing. Proclaim the good news, cure the sick, cleanse lepers. You received without payment; give without payment (Matthew 10:8).

Advent is not only waiting for Christ; it is also being sent by him. The crowds of our time are harassed and helpless too: young people without direction, families under stress, people lost in addictions. Jesus looks at them with compassion, and he sends us.

Let us pray today for labourers in the harvest: priests, religious, catechists and committed lay people. Let us listen for that quiet word behind us: this is the way. And let us give freely what we have freely received. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus feels sorry for people who are lost and sad. Pray today that God will send many good priests and sisters!

Prayer

Lord of the harvest, you wait to be gracious to us. Show me the way and let me walk in it. Send labourers into your harvest, and make me generous, giving freely what I have freely received. Amen.
