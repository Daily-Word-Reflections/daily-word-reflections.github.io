Word for the day

“for he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith. And a great many people were brought to the Lord.” Acts 11:24

Reflection

The Acts of the Apostles first introduces Barnabas as a generous man: a Levite from Cyprus named Joseph, “to whom the apostles gave the name Barnabas (which means ‘son of encouragement’),” who sold a field and laid the money at the apostles' feet (Acts 4:36–37). The name fits him perfectly. When Saul, the former persecutor, came to Jerusalem and the disciples were all afraid of him, it was Barnabas who “took him, brought him to the apostles,” and vouched for him (Acts 9:27). Today's reading shows him at his best. After the persecution that followed Stephen's death, believers from Cyprus and Cyrene went to Antioch, the great city of Syria, and began to preach the Lord Jesus to Greeks as well as Jews. “The hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number became believers and turned to the Lord.” The church in Jerusalem sent Barnabas to look into this new thing. He did not come with suspicion or a list of rules: “When he came and saw the grace of God, he rejoiced, and he exhorted them all to remain faithful to the Lord with steadfast devotion; for he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith.” Then, seeing that the work was too big for him, he did something humble and wise: “Barnabas went to Tarsus to look for Saul, and when he had found him, he brought him to Antioch.” For a whole year the two taught together, “and it was in Antioch that the disciples were first called ‘Christians.’” The second part of the reading shows the Church of Antioch at prayer: “While they were worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, ‘Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.’ Then after fasting and praying they laid their hands on them and sent them off.” The great missionary journeys of the Church begin with worship, fasting and the laying on of hands. The Psalm is the song of that mission: “All the ends of the earth have seen the victory of our God.”

The Gospel is part of the instructions Jesus gave the Twelve when he sent them out for the first time. “As you go, proclaim the good news, ‘The kingdom of heaven has come near.’ Cure the sick, raise the dead, cleanse the lepers, cast out demons.” And then a principle for every missionary: “You received without payment; give without payment.” The apostle travels light, with no gold or silver or copper in his belt, trusting that “laborers deserve their food.” He brings peace into every house he enters: “If the house is worthy, let your peace come upon it.” Barnabas lived this. He gave away his field before he gave away his life. He was big enough to step aside so that Saul, the more gifted preacher, could shine; Acts soon begins to say “Paul and Barnabas” instead of “Barnabas and Saul.” And when Paul refused to take John Mark on a second journey because the young man had left them once before, Barnabas, his cousin, took Mark with him to Cyprus and gave him a second chance (Acts 15:37–39). Years later Paul himself wrote that Mark was useful to him in his ministry (2 Timothy 4:11). The son of encouragement had been right. Tradition says that Barnabas preached in Cyprus and died a martyr at Salamis; the Church of Cyprus honours him as its founder. Every parish needs a Barnabas: someone who sees the grace of God in others and rejoices, who brings the newcomer in, who gives the one who failed a second chance.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we celebrate St Barnabas. His real name was Joseph, but the apostles gave him a nickname: Barnabas, son of encouragement (Acts 4:36). Imagine being known in the whole Church for one thing: you make people stronger. What a beautiful reputation!

Look at what he did at Antioch. Something new was happening there: Greeks, not only Jews, were coming to believe in Jesus. Some people in Jerusalem may have been worried. Barnabas came, and when he saw the grace of God, he rejoiced (Acts 11:23). He did not look first for problems. He looked first for grace. Then he encouraged them to remain faithful. That is the first lesson of Barnabas: look for the good God is doing in others, and rejoice in it.

The second lesson is humility. The work at Antioch grew too big for Barnabas, so he went to Tarsus to look for Saul and brought him to Antioch (Acts 11:25–26). He knew Saul was a brilliant teacher, maybe more gifted than himself. He did not mind. Soon everyone was speaking of Paul and Barnabas, not Barnabas and Paul. Later, when Paul did not want to take young Mark on a journey because Mark had once left them, Barnabas gave Mark a second chance. That Mark became a great helper of the Church.

In the Gospel Jesus says to his apostles: you received without payment; give without payment (Matthew 10:8). Barnabas gave his field, his time, his trust, and finally, tradition says, his life in Cyprus. Today let us ask him for a heart like his. Let us look for the grace of God in others, make room for their gifts, give second chances, and be sons and daughters of encouragement. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know what an encourager is? It is someone who says: you can do it! Don't give up! St Barnabas was the best encourager in the Bible. When nobody trusted Saul, Barnabas trusted him, and Saul became St Paul. When young Mark made a mistake, Barnabas gave him another chance, and Mark wrote a Gospel! Today, say an encouraging word to a friend. You might be helping a future saint!

Prayer

Lord God, you chose St Barnabas, a good man full of the Holy Spirit and of faith, to strengthen your Church and to carry the Gospel to the nations. Give us his eyes, to see your grace in others and rejoice; his humility, to make room for gifts greater than our own; and his generous heart, to give without payment what we have received without payment. Bless all missionaries, especially those sent from India to other lands, and make us all sons and daughters of encouragement. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
