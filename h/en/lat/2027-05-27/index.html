Word for the day

“Then Jesus said to him, “What do you want me to do for you?” The blind man said to him, “My teacher, let me see again.”” Mark 10:51

Reflection

Near the end of his book Ben Sira lifts his eyes from human affairs and begins a great hymn to the Creator: “I will now call to mind the works of the Lord, and will declare what I have seen. By the word of the Lord his works are made; and all his creatures do his will.” He looks at the sun, which “looks down on everything with its light,” and says, “the work of the Lord is full of his glory.” He confesses that even the angels, God's “holy ones,” cannot tell all his wonders. Then he turns inward: God “searches out the abyss and the human heart; he understands their innermost secrets.” Nothing is hidden from him; “he is from all eternity one and the same,” and “he needs no one to be his counselor.” What moves Ben Sira most is the order and harmony of things: “All things come in pairs, one opposite the other, and he has made nothing incomplete. Each supplements the virtues of the other. Who could ever tire of seeing his glory?” Day and night, sea and land, male and female, rain and sunshine: each completes the other. The Psalm sings the same wonder: “By the word of the LORD the heavens were made, and all their host by the breath of his mouth,” “for he spoke, and it came to be; he commanded, and it stood firm.” Christians hear in these lines the whole Trinity at work: the Father who speaks, the Word through whom all things were made, and the Breath, the Spirit, who gives life.

In the Gospel the same powerful Word walks on a dusty road out of Jericho. Jesus is on his last journey to Jerusalem, and a large crowd is with him. By the roadside sits “Bartimaeus son of Timaeus, a blind beggar.” He cannot see the works of God that Ben Sira praised, not even the sun. But he can hear, and when he hears that Jesus of Nazareth is passing, he shouts: “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!” Many tell him to be quiet. Beggars are supposed to sit still and not disturb important people. “But he cried out even more loudly.” And then: “Jesus stood still.” The one through whom the heavens were made stops for a blind man. “Call him here.” The crowd that was silencing him now says, “Take heart; get up, he is calling you.” Bartimaeus throws off his cloak, perhaps the only thing he owned, the cloak spread on the ground to catch coins, and springs up. Jesus asks him the very question he asked James and John a few verses earlier: “What do you want me to do for you?” They had asked for seats of glory. Bartimaeus asks for the one thing he needs: “My teacher, let me see again.” “Go; your faith has made you well.” And the healed man does not go away; he “followed him on the way,” up the road to Jerusalem and the cross. His cry has become one of the great prayers of Christians, the Jesus Prayer: Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me. Today the Church also remembers St Augustine of Canterbury, the monk sent by Pope St Gregory the Great to bring the Gospel to the English, who landed in Kent in 597. He too had to throw off his fears: on the way, his monks were so afraid of the journey that they wanted to turn back, but Gregory sent them on with encouragement, and Augustine became the first Archbishop of Canterbury.

Homily outline

Theme: “What do you want me to do for you?”

A Creator whose works are full of glory, and a Saviour who stands still for a blind man.

1. Who could ever tire of his glory?

  • All made by his word.
  • The sun full of his glory.
  • He searches the human heart.
“I will now call to mind the works of the Lord, and will declare what I have seen. By the word of the Lord his works are made; and all his creatures do his will.” Sirach 42:15
“All things came into being through him, and without him not one thing came into being.” John 1:3

Apply: Each evening this week name one beautiful thing you saw that day and thank the Creator for it.

2. He cried out even more loudly

  • A beggar by the road.
  • Voices that say: be quiet.
  • Jesus stood still.
“This poor soul cried, and was heard by the Lord, and was saved from every trouble.” Psalm 34:6
“Many sternly ordered him to be quiet, but he cried out even more loudly, “Son of David, have mercy on me!”” Mark 10:48

Apply: Bring to Jesus this week the prayer you have stopped praying because it seemed useless, and pray it again with confidence.

3. Let me see again, and follow

  • The cloak thrown off.
  • The one thing needed.
  • Following on the way.
“Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped;” Isaiah 35:5
“Jesus said to him, “Go; your faith has made you well.” Immediately he regained his sight and followed him on the way.” Mark 10:52

Apply: Name one “cloak” you cling to, a habit, a fear or a possession, and let it go this week in order to follow Jesus more closely.

Ben Sira cannot tire of the glory of the Creator who made all things by his word.

That same Word stood still for blind Bartimaeus, opened his eyes, and let him follow on the way.

Take-home resolution: Pray the prayer of Bartimaeus every day this week: Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me.

Full homily

Dear friends, Ben Sira looks at the world and cannot stop praising. The sun looks down on everything with its light, and the work of the Lord is full of his glory (Sirach 42:16). He sees that God made all things by his word, that nothing is incomplete, that each thing completes the other. And he asks a beautiful question: who could ever tire of seeing his glory? (Sirach 42:25).

But in the Gospel there is a man who cannot see any of it. Bartimaeus is blind. He sits by the road outside Jericho, begging. He has never seen the sun or the trees or the faces of the people who pass by. But he hears that Jesus is passing, and he shouts: Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me! (Mark 10:47).

People tell him to be quiet. Do not disturb the Master. But he cries out even more loudly. And then the most wonderful line: Jesus stood still. The one through whom the heavens were made stops for a blind beggar. He calls him. And Bartimaeus throws off his cloak, jumps up and comes to Jesus. That cloak was probably all he owned. He leaves it behind because he has found something better.

Jesus asks him: what do you want me to do for you? He says: my teacher, let me see again. Go; your faith has made you well. And Bartimaeus does not go home. He follows Jesus on the way (Mark 10:52). This is our story too. We are often blind to God's glory and to our neighbour's need. Let us cry out today: Jesus, have mercy on me. Let us throw off whatever holds us back, ask him to open our eyes, and then follow him. Amen.

For children

Children, close your eyes for a moment. Now you cannot see anything! That is how Bartimaeus lived every day. But when he heard that Jesus was coming, he shouted as loudly as he could: Jesus, help me! People told him to be quiet, but he shouted even louder. Jesus stopped, called him, and healed his eyes. Now open your eyes! Let us thank God for our eyes and for all the beautiful things we can see.

Prayer

Lord God, by your word the heavens were made, and all their host by the breath of your mouth. Your works are full of your glory, and you search the depths of every heart. Open our eyes to see your wonders. Lord Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on us; when the crowd tells us to be silent, give us courage to cry out more loudly; and when you call us, help us to throw off our cloak and follow you on the way. Through the prayers of St Augustine of Canterbury, make us bold messengers of the Gospel. Amen.
