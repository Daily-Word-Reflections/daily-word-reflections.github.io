Word for the day

“You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last,” John 15:16

Reflection

Of all the apostles, Matthias is the one we know least, and yet his feast falls at exactly the right moment. The Lord has ascended; the disciples have gone back to Jerusalem and are praying in the upper room “together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus.” In those days of waiting, Peter stands up among about a hundred and twenty believers. There is a wound in the Twelve. Judas, who “was numbered among us and was allotted his share in this ministry,” has gone “to his own place.” Peter reads the tragedy in the light of the Psalms: “Let his homestead become desolate,” and “Let another take his position of overseer.” The number twelve is not a detail. Jesus chose twelve to stand for the twelve tribes of Israel, the whole People of God gathered anew, and he promised that they would sit on thrones judging those tribes (Luke 22:30). Before the Spirit comes and the Church goes out to the nations, the circle must be complete.

What kind of man must fill the empty place? Peter names the condition: “one of the men who have accompanied us during all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us, beginning from the baptism of John until the day when he was taken up from us.” Above all, “one of these must become a witness with us to his resurrection.” An apostle is not chosen for his talents or his eloquence, but because he was with Jesus and has seen him risen. Two names are put forward: “Joseph called Barsabbas, who was also known as Justus, and Matthias.” Then the whole community prays: “Lord, you know everyone's heart. Show us which one of these two you have chosen.” They cast lots, an ancient way in Israel of leaving the decision to God (“The lot is cast into the lap, but the decision is the Lord's alone,” Proverbs 16:33), and “the lot fell on Matthias; and he was added to the eleven apostles.” It is the last time lots are used in the New Testament. After Pentecost the Church will discern by the Holy Spirit, by prayer and by the laying on of hands.

The Psalm sings what happened to Matthias: “He raises the poor from the dust, and lifts the needy from the ash heap, to make them sit with princes, with the princes of his people.” An unknown disciple, who had followed Jesus quietly from the Jordan to the Mount of Olives without ever being named, is suddenly placed among the foundations of the Church. The book of Revelation will say that the wall of the holy city “has twelve foundations, and on them are the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb.” Matthias's name is written there. Joseph Barsabbas was not chosen, and we hear no complaint from him; he too is part of the story, a reminder that God's choice is never a rejection of the one who serves elsewhere.

The Gospel tells us where every apostolic call begins: “You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last.” Matthias did not campaign for the place; the Lord, who knows every heart, chose him. And the choice is an act of friendship: “I do not call you servants any longer … but I have called you friends.” The heart of the apostle's work is the commandment of love: “love one another as I have loved you. No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends.” Of Matthias's later life we know almost nothing for certain. Eusebius records a tradition that he was one of the Seventy whom Jesus sent out (Luke 10:1). Ancient accounts send him to preach in Judea, in Cappadocia, or on the shores of the Black Sea, and say he died a martyr; the Abbey of St Matthias at Trier in Germany has honoured relics believed to be his since the Middle Ages. What is certain is enough: he was with Jesus, he saw him risen, he was chosen by the Lord, and he received the Holy Spirit with the others at Pentecost. His name means “gift of the Lord.”

2. Lord, you know everyone's heart

  • Prayer before choice.
  • The lot in Israel.
  • After Pentecost, the Spirit.
“For the Lord does not see as mortals see; they look on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart.” 1 Samuel 16:7
“Then they prayed and said, “Lord, you know everyone's heart. Show us which one of these two you have chosen” Acts 1:24

Apply: Before every important decision this week, stop and pray: Lord, you know the heart; show me what you have chosen.

  • The call is the Lord's.
  • Friends, not servants.
  • Fruit that will last.

“You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last,” John 15:16

Apply: Each morning this week, thank Jesus for choosing you, and ask what fruit he wants from you that day.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we celebrate an apostle who is mentioned by name only twice in the whole Bible, both times in one chapter of the Acts of the Apostles. We know almost nothing of his words or his travels. And yet Matthias is one of the twelve foundations of the Church.

The apostles had returned from the Mount of Olives and were praying in the upper room with Mary and the other women. There was an empty place. Judas, who had been one of them, had gone to his own place. Jesus had chosen twelve, for the twelve tribes of Israel. Before the Holy Spirit came, the circle had to be complete.

Peter says what kind of person is needed: one who had been with Jesus from the baptism of John to the Ascension, and who could become a witness with us to his resurrection (Acts 1:22). Not the cleverest, not the richest, not the most popular. The one who had stayed with Jesus, and had seen him alive.

Then the community did something we often forget to do: they prayed before they decided. Lord, you know everyone's heart. Show us which one of these two you have chosen (Acts 1:24). They did not want their own choice; they wanted the Lord's. And the lot fell on Matthias. How many of our family quarrels and wrong decisions would be avoided if we prayed like this first?

In the Gospel Jesus tells us that this is how every vocation works. You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last (John 15:16). Our faith is first of all the Lord's choice of us. And he chooses us as friends: I do not call you servants any longer, but I have called you friends. The mark of his friends is love: love one another as I have loved you.

Perhaps you feel like Matthias before that day: following Jesus quietly, never noticed, never named. Do not be discouraged. The Lord sees you. He raises the poor from the dust to make them sit with princes. Stay with him, be a witness of his resurrection where you are, and when he calls, be ready. St Matthias, pray for us, and with Mary and the apostles, let us pray: Come, Holy Spirit. Amen.

For children

Children, Matthias followed Jesus for a long, long time, but nobody wrote his name in the Gospels. He was a quiet friend of Jesus. When the apostles needed one more, they prayed, and God chose Matthias!

Jesus says to you: I chose you, and you are my friend. Even when nobody notices you, Jesus sees you. Say: Thank you, Jesus, for choosing me!

Prayer

O God, who assigned Saint Matthias a place in the college of Apostles, grant us, through his intercession, that, rejoicing at your love for us, we may merit to be numbered among the elect. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, you did not wait for us to choose you; you chose us and called us your friends. As you chose Matthias to be a witness of your resurrection, make us witnesses in our homes and workplaces. Teach us to pray before we decide, to serve without seeking a name, and to love one another as you have loved us. With Mary and the apostles we wait for your Spirit: come, Holy Spirit, and fill the empty places in our hearts and in your Church. Amen.
