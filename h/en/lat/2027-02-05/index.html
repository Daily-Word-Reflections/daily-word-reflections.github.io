Word for the day

“Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.” Hebrews 13:8

Reflection

Hebrews ends with practical counsel for a community under pressure: “Let mutual love continue. Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it. Remember those who are in prison, as though you were in prison with them.” It asks that marriage be honoured, that lives be kept “free from the love of money”, and gives the reason for courage: “he has said, ‘I will never leave you or forsake you.’ So we can say with confidence, ‘The Lord is my helper; I will not be afraid.’” And then one of the great verses of Scripture: “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.” The Lord who strengthened the martyrs is the same Lord who walks with us.

Mark tells the death of John the Baptist with a sharp eye. Herod “feared John, knowing that he was a righteous and holy man… he liked to listen to him.” Yet at a drunken birthday banquet, trapped by a rash oath and by what his guests would think, he orders the prophet's head brought on a platter. Herod is not a monster; he is a weak man who knows the truth and will not obey it. John, in prison, is the free one. Today we honour St Agatha of Catania in Sicily, a young woman martyred about the year 251 because she refused to give herself to a powerful official and would not deny Christ. Like John, she chose truth over survival. Her name is spoken in the First Eucharistic Prayer, and Christians have prayed to her for centuries in sickness and danger.

Homily outline

Theme: “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.”

Mutual love, the Lord who never leaves, and the courage of John and St Agatha.

1. Let mutual love continue

  • Hospitality to strangers.
  • Remember prisoners.
  • Free from the love of money.
“you shall love the alien as yourself, for you were aliens in the land of Egypt” Leviticus 19:34
“Let mutual love continue. Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it.” Hebrews 13:1–2

Apply: Welcome one stranger or newcomer this week.

2. The same yesterday, today and forever

  • I will never leave you.
  • The Lord is my helper.
  • Remember your leaders.
“The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The LORD is the stronghold of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?” Psalm 27:1
“Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.” Hebrews 13:8

Apply: Repeat Hebrews 13:8 whenever you feel afraid.

3. Herod's weakness, John's freedom

  • He liked to listen.
  • Trapped by an oath and guests.
  • The martyr is free.
“Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you, says the LORD.”” Jeremiah 1:8
“For John had been telling Herod, “It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife.”” Mark 6:18

Apply: Refuse one compromise made only to please others.

Love, hospitality and courage flow from a Lord who never changes.

Like John and St Agatha, let us fear God more than people.

Take-home resolution: Ask St Agatha each day this week for courage and purity of heart.

Full homily

Dear friends, Hebrews says: let mutual love continue. Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it (Hebrews 13:1–2). Remember prisoners, honour marriage, keep free from the love of money.

Why can we live like this? Because he has said: I will never leave you or forsake you (Hebrews 13:5). Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever (Hebrews 13:8).

In the Gospel Herod liked to listen to John, yet out of regard for his oaths and for the guests he ordered his death (Mark 6:26). He knew the truth but feared people more than God. John, in chains, was the free man.

Today we honour St Agatha, the young martyr of Sicily, who chose Christ over safety and purity over the will of a powerful man. The Christ who strengthened her is the same today. Let us ask for her courage. Amen.

For children

Children, St Agatha was a brave girl who loved Jesus more than anything. Even when she was hurt, she stayed faithful. Jesus helps us to be brave too!

Prayer

May the Virgin Martyr Saint Agatha implore your compassion for us, O Lord, we pray, for she found favour with you by the courage of her martyrdom and the chastity of her life. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen.
