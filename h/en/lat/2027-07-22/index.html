Word for the day

“Mary Magdalene went and announced to the disciples, “I have seen the Lord”; and she told them that he had said these things to her.” John 20:18

Reflection

The Church gives us today two of the most loving texts in the Bible. In the first, from the Song of Songs, a woman is awake in the night, searching for the one she loves. “Upon my bed at night I sought him whom my soul loves; I sought him, but found him not; I called him, but he gave no answer.” She does not stay in bed with her longing; she says, “I will rise now and go about the city, in the streets and in the squares; I will seek him whom my soul loves.” The watchmen on their rounds find her, and she asks them at once, “Have you seen him whom my soul loves?” Then, “Scarcely had I passed them, when I found him whom my soul loves. I held him, and would not let him go.” Since early times the Church has read this poem as the story of God and the human soul, and the Christian tradition has heard in it the voice of Mary Magdalene at the tomb. The third-century writer Hippolytus of Rome, in one of the oldest Christian commentaries on the Song of Songs, already compares the women who went to the tomb with the bride who seeks her beloved. The setting matches: the Song's bride seeks him at night in a garden city; John says that Mary came “while it was still dark,” and that the tomb was in a garden (John 19:41). The psalm is the prayer of the same seeker: “O God, you are my God, I seek you, my soul thirsts for you; my flesh faints for you, as in a dry and weary land where there is no water.” And it knows the answer: “Because your steadfast love is better than life, my lips will praise you.” If the alternative reading is used, St Paul gives us the other side of the same love: “The love of Christ urges us on,” and “if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation: everything old has passed away; see, everything has become new!” (2 Corinthians 5:14, 17). Mary of Magdala is the first person who sees what that new creation looks like.

Magdala was a fishing town on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, and excavations there have uncovered a synagogue of the time of Jesus. Mary of Magdala is named in all four Gospels. Luke tells us that among the women who travelled with Jesus and supported him and the Twelve “out of their resources” was “Mary, called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out” (Luke 8:2). The Gospels say nothing more about the nature of her illness. She remained with Jesus to the end: she stands near the cross with his mother (John 19:25), she sees where he is laid (Mark 15:47), and on Sunday morning she comes back. Here we need to be careful. A tradition in the Western Church, which became widespread after a homily of Pope St Gregory the Great in the sixth century, joined her with the unnamed sinful woman who anointed Jesus' feet in Luke 7:36–50 and also with Mary of Bethany, the sister of Martha. The Gospels never say so. Today the Church keeps these figures distinct, and reads on this feast only what Scripture tells us about Mary of Magdala: a woman healed by Jesus, a faithful disciple, a witness of the cross and the empty tomb, and the first to see the risen Lord. John's account is among the most moving in the Gospels. “Early on the first day of the week, while it was still dark,” she sees that the stone has been removed, and runs to Simon Peter and the beloved disciple: “They have taken the Lord out of the tomb, and we do not know where they have laid him.” The reading omits the visit of Peter and John to the tomb. The two men see the burial cloths and go home. “But Mary stood weeping outside the tomb.” She bends down and sees two angels in white, who ask, “Woman, why are you weeping?” and she says, “They have taken away my Lord”: it is my Lord, not only the Lord. Then she turns round and sees Jesus standing there but does not recognise him, taking him for the gardener, and says to him, “Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him away.” Her love will carry the body of Jesus herself if she must. And then the whole scene turns on one word: “Jesus said to her, ‘Mary!’” The Good Shepherd “calls his own sheep by name” (John 10:3). “She turned and said to him in Hebrew, ‘Rabbouni!’ (which means Teacher).” She turned a second time: the first was her body, the second her heart. It is the moment of faith.

Then Jesus says something strange: “Do not hold on to me, because I have not yet ascended to the Father.” The Greek is a command to stop clinging, as if she were taking hold of him to keep him as before. Jesus is not rejecting her; he is telling her that the old way of being with him is over and a new one begins. He will not be held in the garden; he will be with his disciples always through the Spirit, in the Church. He sends her: “But go to my brothers and say to them, ‘I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.’” For the first time he calls the disciples his brothers, and he gives them, through her, the whole meaning of Easter: his Father is now our Father. Mary Magdalene “went and announced to the disciples, ‘I have seen the Lord.’” In these few words the Church's whole message is already present, and she is the first to say it. That is why the Church, since the early centuries, has called her the “apostle of the apostles”: apostle means “one who is sent,” and the risen Lord sent her to the apostles. St Thomas Aquinas, commenting on this passage, uses exactly this title. The Catechism puts it plainly: Mary Magdalene and the holy women “were the first to encounter the Risen One. Thus the women were the first messengers of Christ's Resurrection for the apostles themselves” (Catechism 641). The apostles, at first, did not believe the women's words (Luke 24:11), as people sometimes do not believe the testimony of those who are overlooked; but the Church, which rests on the testimony of Peter and the Twelve, rests also on this first testimony. In 2016 the Church raised her day to the rank of a feast, so that all the faithful, and women in particular, may remember the place of this witness. This is also a day for those who weep. In the monsoon weeks, when for days together the sky itself seems to weep, we remember that Mary's tears were seen by someone: the Lord was already there, and he was calling her by name.

Homily outline

Theme: “I have seen the Lord.”

A woman who sought him in the dark, was called by name, and was sent to the apostles with the first Easter news.

1. Seeking in the dark

  • The Song of Songs: rising to search.
  • While it was still dark.
  • A garden and an empty tomb.
“Upon my bed at night I sought him whom my soul loves; I sought him, but found him not; I called him, but he gave no answer.” Song of Songs 3:1
“Early on the first day of the week, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene came to the tomb and saw that the stone had been removed from the tomb.” John 20:1

Apply: Choose one prayer to say at the start of the day this week, and say it before the light comes.

2. Why are you weeping?

  • Everyone else went home.
  • My Lord.
  • A love ready to carry the body.
“O God, you are my God, I seek you, my soul thirsts for you; my flesh faints for you, as in a dry and weary land where there is no water.” Psalm 63:1
“They said to her, “Woman, why are you weeping?” She said to them, “They have taken away my Lord, and I do not know where they have laid him.”” John 20:13

Apply: Stay beside someone who is grieving this week, in silence if necessary, and let them know they are not alone.

3. Mary!

  • The Shepherd calls by name.
  • She turned, and knew him.
  • The voice that calls us out of grief.
“But now thus says the Lord, he who created you, O Jacob, he who formed you, O Israel: Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” Isaiah 43:1
“Jesus said to her, “Mary!” She turned and said to him in Hebrew, “Rabbouni!” (which means Teacher).” John 20:16

Apply: Spend five minutes in silence before the tabernacle or with the Gospel, and let the Lord say your name.

4. I have seen the Lord

  • Do not cling; go.
  • My Father and your Father.
  • The apostle of the apostles.
“I shall not die, but I shall live, and recount the deeds of the Lord.” Psalm 118:17
“Mary Magdalene went and announced to the disciples, “I have seen the Lord”; and she told them that he had said these things to her.” John 20:18

Apply: Tell one person this week, in your own words, what the risen Lord has done for you.

Mary Magdalene stays at the tomb, is called by name, and is sent by the risen Lord to his brothers: the first witness of the Resurrection.

The Church keeps her day as a feast, and calls her the apostle of the apostles; with her we say: I have seen the Lord.

Take-home resolution: Each day this week listen for the Lord calling you by name, and tell someone one thing he has done for you.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Church keeps the feast of a woman who stood at the cross and went to the tomb: Mary of Magdala. We know a few things about her from the Gospels. Jesus drove seven demons out of her. She followed him with other women and helped to support him. She stood near the cross. And on Sunday morning, while it was still dark, she went to the tomb. We have to be careful not to add what the Gospels do not say. The Gospels do not name her as the sinful woman of Luke's seventh chapter, and the Church keeps them apart. What we know is already enough: a healed woman, a faithful disciple, and the first witness of the Resurrection.

John tells the story slowly. Mary sees the stone rolled away and runs to Peter and the other disciple. They run to the tomb, see the cloths and go home. But Mary stood weeping outside the tomb (John 20:11). Everyone else had gone home; she stays. Two angels ask: woman, why are you weeping? She says: they have taken away my Lord, and I do not know where they have laid him. Notice: my Lord. Then she turns and sees a man standing there; she thinks he is the gardener. Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him away. She is ready to carry a dead body on her own shoulders. That is love.

And then one word changes everything: Mary! (John 20:16). He calls her by name. The Good Shepherd calls his own sheep by name. And she turns, and says: Rabbouni! My Teacher! It was not a stranger's voice. It is the voice that called her out of darkness, and now it calls her out of grief. Do you hear your name when the Lord calls you? In prayer, in the Scriptures, in the Eucharist, in the cry of a poor person: he calls you by name.

Then Jesus says: do not hold on to me. She wants to keep him as before, and he says: that way is over, now I am going to the Father. But go to my brothers and say to them: I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God. And Mary Magdalene went and announced to the disciples: I have seen the Lord (John 20:18). This is why the Church calls her the apostle of the apostles. The first to see the Lord alive was the first to tell. Peter and the Twelve would announce it to the world, but a woman announced it to them.

Brothers and sisters, how do we honour this feast? First, by staying: with those who weep, with those everybody has given up on. Second, by letting the Lord call us by name. Third, by not clinging to the past, and by going. Fourth, by saying out loud what Mary said: I have seen the Lord. In this rainy season, when the sky itself seems to weep, let us remember that no tear is unseen, because the Lord was already there, behind Mary, calling her by name. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know who was the very first person to see Jesus alive on Easter morning? A friend of Jesus named Mary Magdalene! She went to the tomb very early and cried because Jesus was not there. Then a man said: Mary! She knew his voice at once. It was Jesus, alive! He said: go and tell my friends. And she ran and said: I have seen the Lord!

Jesus knows your name too, and he loves you. Today, tell somebody the happiest news in the world: Jesus is alive! And you can say, like Mary: I have seen the Lord!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, you called Mary of Magdala by her name and sent her to announce your Resurrection to the apostles. Make us seekers like her, who rise before dawn, do not stop weeping before the empty tomb, and know your voice when you call us by name. Teach us not to cling to what is past, but to go to our brothers and sisters and say: I have seen the Lord. Through the prayers of St Mary Magdalene, the apostle of the apostles, give your Church the faith that proclaims you without fear, and bring us all to the joy of your presence, where you live and reign for ever and ever. Amen.
