Word for the day

“Do not delay to turn back to the Lord, and do not postpone it from day to day.” Sirach 5:7

Reflection

Ben Sira today writes a list of things we must never say. “Do not rely on your wealth, or say, ‘I have enough.’” “Do not say, ‘Who can have power over me?’” “Do not say, ‘I sinned, yet what has happened to me?’ for the Lord is slow to anger.” Each sentence names a false security. Money that makes us think we need nobody, strength that makes us think we answer to nobody, and, most subtle of all, a kind of presumption on God's patience: nothing happened when I sinned, so it cannot matter. Ben Sira goes further: “Do not be so confident of forgiveness that you add sin to sin. Do not say, ‘His mercy is great, he will forgive the multitude of my sins.’” God's mercy is real and vast, but it is the mercy of a holy God, never permission to keep sinning. And so the urgent counsel: “Do not delay to turn back to the Lord, and do not postpone it from day to day.” The sinner always means to repent tomorrow. Ben Sira asks: why not today? The Psalm paints the two ways: the one who delights in the law of the Lord is “like trees planted by streams of water,” while the wicked are “like chaff that the wind drives away.”

The Gospel continues the teaching Jesus gave at Capernaum. It opens with a promise so small it is easy to miss: “whoever gives you a cup of water to drink because you bear the name of Christ will by no means lose the reward.” No kindness done for Christ is ever wasted. Then the tone becomes severe. To cause one of “these little ones who believe” to stumble is so grave that a millstone around the neck would be better. Jesus speaks of cutting off a hand or a foot, tearing out an eye, rather than being thrown into “hell, where their worm never dies, and the fire is never quenched.” The Church has never taken this as a command to injure the body; it is the vivid language of a teacher who wants us to see that nothing, not even what is dearest to us, is worth losing eternal life for. Whatever leads us into sin, a habit, a phone, a friendship, a place, must be cut away decisively. And he ends with salt: “Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another.” Today the Church remembers St Bernardine of Siena (1380–1444), the Franciscan who preached across Italy with this same urgency, calling crowds to conversion and teaching them to love the Holy Name of Jesus. He did not let people postpone; he told them that today is the day.

Homily outline

Theme: “Do not delay to turn back to the Lord.”

False securities, the danger of tomorrow, and the salt of a heart converted today.

1. Do not say “I have enough”

  • Wealth that needs nobody.
  • Strength that answers to nobody.
  • Mercy taken for granted.
“Do not be so confident of forgiveness that you add sin to sin.” Sirach 5:5
“Or do you despise the riches of his kindness and forbearance and patience? Do you not realize that God's kindness is meant to lead you to repentance?” Romans 2:4

Apply: Write down one false security you lean on, and give it to God in prayer.

2. Not tomorrow, but today

  • The sinner's favourite word.
  • Cut off what makes you stumble.
  • Nothing is worth losing life for.
“Do not delay to turn back to the Lord, and do not postpone it from day to day.” Sirach 5:7
“See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” 2 Corinthians 6:2

Apply: Make your confession this week, or take one decisive step away from an occasion of sin.

3. A cup of water and the salt of peace

  • No kindness for Christ is lost.
  • Do not scandalise the little ones.
  • Salt and peace with one another.
“Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it.” Psalm 34:14
“Salt is good; but if salt has lost its saltiness, how can you season it? Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another.” Mark 9:50

Apply: Make peace this week with one person from whom you are estranged.

God's patience is not permission; it is an invitation to turn back today.

A heart that turns to Christ cuts away what leads to sin, gives cups of water in his name, and lives at peace.

Take-home resolution: Say the name of Jesus with love each hour today, as St Bernardine taught, and turn to him without delay.

Full homily

Dear friends, there is one word the devil loves: tomorrow. I will pray tomorrow. I will go to confession next month. I will forgive my brother after he apologises. Ben Sira knew this temptation well, and he says today: do not delay to turn back to the Lord, and do not postpone it from day to day (Sirach 5:7).

He also warns us against sentences we say inside. I have enough. Who can control me? I sinned and nothing happened. God is merciful, he will forgive anything. Yes, God is merciful, wonderfully merciful. But his mercy is meant to lead us to repentance, not to give us permission to add sin to sin.

In the Gospel Jesus uses very strong words. If your hand, your foot, your eye causes you to sin, cut it off. He does not want us to harm our bodies. He wants us to be serious. If a mobile phone, a bottle, a bad friendship or a habit keeps pulling me into sin, I must cut it off decisively. Nothing is worth more than eternal life.

But Jesus also says something very gentle: whoever gives you a cup of water because you bear the name of Christ will by no means lose the reward (Mark 9:41). Today we remember St Bernardine of Siena, who preached conversion across Italy and taught people to love the Holy Name of Jesus. Let us take his advice: say the name of Jesus often, turn to him today, not tomorrow, and be at peace with one another. Amen.

For children

Children, sometimes we say: I will say sorry tomorrow, I will clean my room later, I will pray next time. The Bible says: do not wait! Turn to God today. And Jesus says that even giving a cup of water to someone with love makes God happy. Can you say sorry and do a small kindness today?

Prayer

Holy and merciful God, you are slow to anger and rich in mercy; do not let us presume on your patience. Give us the grace to turn back to you today, without delay, and to cut away whatever leads us into sin. Through the prayers of St Bernardine, who preached the Holy Name of Jesus with burning love, write that name on our hearts, so that we may have salt in ourselves and be at peace with one another. Amen.
