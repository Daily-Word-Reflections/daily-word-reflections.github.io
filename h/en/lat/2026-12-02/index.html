Word for the day

“I have compassion for the crowd, because they have been with me now for three days and have nothing to eat; and I do not want to send them away hungry, for they might faint on the way.” Matthew 15:32

Reflection

Isaiah promises a mountain banquet for all peoples, rich food and fine wine, and more: God “will swallow up death forever” and “wipe away the tears from all faces”. In the Gospel that promise begins to come true on a mountain by the Sea of Galilee. Crowds bring the lame, the blind, the mute; Jesus heals them. Then, moved with compassion, he takes seven loaves and a few fish, gives thanks, breaks them and gives them to the disciples to distribute. All eat and are filled, with seven baskets left over.

The words “took, gave thanks, broke, gave” are the words of the Last Supper. Advent prepares us to receive the One who is Bread from heaven, born in Bethlehem, whose name means “house of bread”. And the disciples are his hands: Christ multiplies what we bring, but asks us to distribute it to the hungry.

Full homily

Dear friends, Isaiah today describes a great banquet on the mountain of the Lord: a feast of rich food and well-aged wines for all peoples (Isaiah 25:6). It is a feast for all nations, not one people only. And at that feast God will destroy the shroud that covers all peoples; he will swallow up death forever and wipe away the tears from all faces (Isaiah 25:7–8).

This is the hope of Advent: not only a baby in a manger, but the final banquet of the Kingdom, where death is no more and every tear is wiped away. Christmas begins what that banquet will complete.

In the Gospel Jesus goes up a mountain by the Sea of Galilee. Great crowds come, bringing the lame, the blind, the mute and many others, and he heals them. Then he has compassion on the hungry crowd, and with seven loaves and a few fish he feeds four thousand. All ate and were filled, and seven baskets were left over (Matthew 15:29–37).

Notice the order: first Jesus heals, then he feeds. And notice his words: I have compassion for the crowd. Jesus sees physical hunger as well as spiritual hunger. Isaiah's mountain banquet is already beginning on the hillside in Galilee.

The disciples asked: where are we to get enough bread in the desert? They looked at what they lacked. Jesus asked: how many loaves have you? He looked at what they had. Advent invites us to give our little loaves to the Lord, our time, our money, our talents, and let him multiply them.

This Advent, let us feed someone who is hungry: a migrant worker, a poor family, a lonely person who needs company more than food. And at each Mass let us remember that we are already tasting the banquet of the Kingdom. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus fed thousands with just a few loaves. When you share even a little, Jesus can do great things with it!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, compassionate Shepherd, you healed the sick and fed the hungry on the mountain. Feed me with your Bread of life, and make my hands generous to those who have nothing to eat. Amen.
