Word for the day

“He asked for a writing tablet and wrote, “His name is John.” And all of them were amazed. Immediately his mouth was opened and his tongue freed, and he began to speak, praising God.” Luke 1:63–64

Reflection

The last O Antiphon: “O Emmanuel, our King and Lawgiver, the hope of the nations and their Saviour: come and save us, O Lord our God.” Malachi, the last of the prophets, announces a messenger who will prepare the way, and the Lord who will suddenly come to his temple, like a refiner's fire. He will send Elijah “to turn the hearts of parents to their children”.

In the Gospel the messenger is born. Neighbours rejoice with Elizabeth because the Lord has shown great mercy to her. At the circumcision they want to call the child Zechariah, after his father, but Elizabeth says, “No; he is to be called John.” Zechariah, mute for nine months, writes, “His name is John”, and his tongue is freed to praise God. John means “The Lord has shown favour”. The name is not the family's choice but God's gift: this child belongs to God's plan. “What then will this child become?” For “the hand of the Lord was with him.”

Homily outline

Theme: “His name is John.”

The messenger is born; the Lord comes as refiner's fire; silence breaks into praise.

1. The messenger

  • Malachi promised a messenger.
  • Jesus says John is that messenger.
  • He prepares the way of the Lord.
“See, I am sending my messenger to prepare the way before me, and the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple. The messenger of the covenant in whom you delight—indeed, he is coming, says the LORD of hosts.” Malachi 3:1
“This is the one about whom it is written, ‘See, I am sending my messenger ahead of you, who will prepare your way before you.’” Matthew 11:10

Apply: Prepare one practical thing for Christmas with prayer.

2. Refined by fire

  • Who can endure his coming?
  • He purifies like a refiner.
  • Christ baptises with Spirit and fire.
“But who can endure the day of his coming, and who can stand when he appears? For he is like a refiner's fire and like fullers' soap; he will sit as a refiner and purifier of silver, and he will purify the descendants of Levi and refine them like gold and silver, until they present offerings to the LORD in righteousness.” Malachi 3:2–3
“I baptize you with water for repentance, but one who is more powerful than I is coming after me; I am not worthy to carry his sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.” Matthew 3:11

Apply: Make your Christmas Confession today or tomorrow.

  • The family wanted “Zechariah”.
  • God had chosen “John”.

Full homily

Dear friends, the last O Antiphon is: O Emmanuel, our King and Lawgiver, the hope of the nations: come and save us, O Lord our God. Malachi, the last of the prophets, announces: I am sending my messenger to prepare the way before me; and the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to His temple (Malachi 3:1).

Malachi warns that the Lord will be like a refiner's fire, purifying His people like gold and silver (Malachi 3:2–3). His coming is joyful, but it also purifies.

In the Gospel John is born. The neighbours want to call him Zechariah after his father, but Elizabeth says: no, he is to be called John. Zechariah, still mute, writes: his name is John. Immediately his mouth is opened, and he begins to praise God (Luke 1:59–64). John means God is gracious.

When Zechariah obeyed, his tongue was freed. Our hearts too are freed when we obey God. With two days left before Christmas, let us let the refiner's fire purify us, perhaps in confession, so that we may praise God with free hearts. Amen.

For children

Children, Zechariah could not speak until he obeyed God; then he praised God. When we obey God, our hearts are happy!

Prayer

O Emmanuel, our King and Lawgiver, hope and Saviour of the nations, come and save us, O Lord our God. As you freed Zechariah's tongue, open my lips to praise you, and let your hand be with me. Amen.
