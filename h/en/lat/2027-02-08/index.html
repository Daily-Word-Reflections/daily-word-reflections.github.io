Word for the day

“And wherever he went, into villages or cities or farms, they laid the sick in the marketplaces, and begged him that they might touch even the fringe of his cloak; and all who touched it were healed.” Mark 6:56

Reflection

The weekday readings now open the Bible at its first page: “In the beginning when God created the heavens and the earth, the earth was a formless void and darkness covered the face of the deep.” Into that darkness God speaks: “Let there be light”; and there was light. Day by day the formless becomes ordered: light and darkness, sky and sea, dry land and plants, sun, moon and stars. Again and again we hear the refrain: “And God saw that it was good.” Creation is not an accident or a battle of gods, as the neighbouring peoples imagined; it is the free gift of one good God who speaks and it is done. The sun and moon that others worshipped are simply lamps hung by God to mark the seasons.

The Gospel shows the same creative goodness at work in Jesus. When he lands at Gennesaret, the people recognize him at once and rush about the whole region, carrying the sick on mats to wherever they hear he is. In villages, cities and farms they lay the sick in the marketplaces and beg “that they might touch even the fringe of his cloak; and all who touched it were healed.” The Word through whom all things were made now walks among his creatures, and his touch restores what sin and sickness have marred. Today the Church may honour St Josephine Bakhita, the Sudanese slave girl who found in Christ “a Master” who loved her, and became a Canossian sister. In her the Creator's goodness was restored to a life that cruelty had tried to destroy.

Homily outline

Theme: “All who touched it were healed.”

The good Creator who speaks light into darkness, and the Word made flesh whose touch heals.

1. Let there be light

  • Darkness over the deep.
  • God speaks.
  • Light comes to be.
“Then God said, “Let there be light”; and there was light.” Genesis 1:3
“For it is the God who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,” who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” 2 Corinthians 4:6

Apply: Begin each day this week with a short prayer of light: Lord, shine in my heart.

2. It was good

  • The refrain of Genesis.
  • Gift, not god.
  • Care for the gift.
“O LORD, how manifold are your works! In wisdom you have made them all; the earth is full of your creatures.” Psalm 104:24
“Ever since the creation of the world his eternal power and divine nature, invisible though they are, have been understood and seen through the things he has made.” Romans 1:20

Apply: Waste less this week: food, water or electricity, as a thank-you to the Creator.

3. The fringe of his cloak

  • Gennesaret recognizes him.
  • The sick on mats.
  • His touch heals.
“But he was wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the punishment that made us whole, and by his bruises we are healed.” Isaiah 53:5
“And wherever he went, into villages or cities or farms, they laid the sick in the marketplaces, and begged him that they might touch even the fringe of his cloak; and all who touched it were healed.” Mark 6:56

Apply: Visit, call or pray for one sick person by name.

The world is the good gift of a good God.

In Jesus the Creator walks among us and heals.

Take-home resolution: Thank God for one created thing every day this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we begin to read the first page of the Bible: in the beginning when God created the heavens and the earth (Genesis 1:1). Darkness covered the deep, and God said: let there be light; and there was light (Genesis 1:3).

Day after day God gives order and life, and again and again we hear: God saw that it was good. Creation is not a god to be feared or worshipped; it is a gift from the one good God. Even the sun and moon are lamps he has hung in the sky.

In the Gospel the people of Gennesaret run through the whole region bringing their sick to Jesus. They beg to touch even the fringe of his cloak, and all who touch it are healed (Mark 6:56). The Word who made all things now walks among us and heals.

Today we may remember St Josephine Bakhita, sold as a child slave, who found in Jesus a Master who loved her. Let us thank God for his creation, bring our sick to Jesus, and pray for all who are not free. Amen.

For children

Children, God said: Let there be light! And there was light. Everything God made is good, and you are good too! Let us say thank you to God.

Prayer

Lord God, Creator of light and of all that is good, you spoke and the world came to be, and in your Son you touch and heal what is broken. Open our eyes to the goodness of your works, bring our sick to the fringe of his cloak, and through the prayers of St Josephine Bakhita set free all who are held in slavery. Amen.
