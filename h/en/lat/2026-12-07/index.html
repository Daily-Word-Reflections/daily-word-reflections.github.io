Word for the day

“When he saw their faith, he said, “Friend, your sins are forgiven you.”” Luke 5:20

Reflection

Isaiah 35 is a song of the desert in bloom: “The wilderness and the dry land shall be glad… Strengthen the weak hands… Say to those who are of a fearful heart, ‘Be strong, do not fear! Here is your God… He will come and save you.’” Then the eyes of the blind are opened, the lame leap like a deer, and the redeemed walk the Holy Way home with singing.

In the Gospel the prophecy walks into a crowded house. Friends cannot get their paralysed companion near Jesus, so they open the roof and lower him down. “When he saw their faith”, Jesus first forgives his sins, then, to show that “the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”, tells him to stand up and walk. The deepest healing is forgiveness; the first miracle Advent asks for is a good Confession. St Ambrose, bishop of Milan, preached this mercy and baptised St Augustine at Easter 387.

Full homily

Dear friends, Isaiah paints a picture of joy: the wilderness and the dry land shall be glad, the desert shall rejoice and blossom. Strengthen the weak hands, make firm the feeble knees. Say to those who are of a fearful heart: be strong, do not fear! Here is your God (Isaiah 35:1–4).

He promises that the eyes of the blind will be opened, the lame will leap like a deer and the tongue of the speechless sing for joy (Isaiah 35:5–6). These are the signs of the Messiah.

In the Gospel some men bring a paralysed man on a bed to Jesus. The crowd is too great, so they go up on the roof and let him down through the tiles right in front of Jesus. When he saw their faith, he said: friend, your sins are forgiven you (Luke 5:18–20).

Notice: their faith. The paralysed man could not come by himself. His friends carried him, and they did not give up when the door was blocked. Jesus saw their faith and healed their friend. We can carry others to Jesus by our prayer and our love.

Notice too that Jesus first forgives sins, then heals the body. The deepest paralysis is sin. Then he says: stand up, take your bed and go home. And the man went home glorifying God (Luke 5:24–25).

Today we remember St Ambrose, bishop of Milan, who carried many to Christ, among them the young Augustine. Let us think of someone who cannot come to Jesus alone: a relative far from the faith, a sick friend, a struggling neighbour. Let us carry them in prayer this Advent, and not give up when the door seems blocked. Amen.

For children

Children, four friends carried a sick man to Jesus. You can carry your friends to Jesus by praying for them!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you saw the faith of the friends and forgave the paralysed man. Strengthen my weak hands, make the dry places of my life bloom, and give me the joy of your forgiveness. Amen.
