Word for the day

“But not a hair of your head will perish. By your endurance you will gain your souls.” Luke 21:18–19

Reflection

Jesus does not promise his disciples an easy road. They will be arrested, handed over to synagogues and prisons, brought before kings and governors, even betrayed by family. But he turns it around: “This will give you an opportunity to testify.” He promises “words and a wisdom that none of your opponents will be able to withstand”, and a paradox: some will be put to death, yet “not a hair of your head will perish”. Ultimate safety is not physical survival but belonging to God.

Revelation shows the end of that endurance: the victors standing beside a sea of glass mixed with fire, holding harps, singing “the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb”. As Israel sang after crossing the Red Sea, the martyrs sing after passing through persecution. St Catherine of Alexandria, remembered today, is honoured in tradition as a young woman who defended the faith with wisdom before philosophers, a living sign of Luke 21:15.

Full homily

Dear friends, Jesus does not promise his disciples an easy road. They will be arrested and persecuted, handed over to synagogues and prisons, brought before kings and governors, even betrayed by parents, brothers and friends. But he turns it around: this will give you an opportunity to testify (Luke 21:12–13).

He promises words and a wisdom that none of their opponents will be able to withstand, and then a paradox: some of you will be put to death, yet not a hair of your head will perish. By your endurance you will gain your souls (Luke 21:16–19). Our final security is not survival, but belonging to God.

Revelation shows where this endurance ends: the conquerors stand beside a sea of glass mixed with fire, holding harps, and sing the song of Moses and the song of the Lamb (Revelation 15:2–3). As Israel sang after crossing the sea, the faithful sing after the trial.

Today the Church remembers St Catherine of Alexandria, honoured by tradition as a young woman who defended the faith with wisdom. When your faith is mocked, at school, at work or online, answer calmly and kindly. And keep one good resolution you are tempted to drop. Endurance wins the soul. Amen.

For children

Children, being a friend of Jesus is not always easy, but Jesus promises to give us the right words. If someone laughs at your faith, stay kind and brave!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, when my faith is tested, give me words and wisdom, patience and courage. Keep me in your hand, so that by endurance I may gain my soul and sing the song of the Lamb. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Revelation shows the conquerors standing by a sea of glass, singing the song of Moses and the Lamb: a new Exodus has taken place. Luke shows the road that leads there: arrest, trial, betrayal even by family, and an unexpected promise, “not a hair of your head will perish”. The song belongs to those who endured.

Revelation 15:1–4 · The song of Moses and of the Lamb

Setting.

Chapter 15 opens the last cycle of judgments, the seven bowls (chapters 15–16). Before they are poured out, John is shown the victors already safe. As Israel sang on the shore after crossing the Red Sea (Exodus 15), so the martyrs who came through the beast's persecution sing beside a heavenly sea.

Structure.

  • v. 1: a great sign: seven angels with the last seven plagues.
  • v. 2: the sea of glass mixed with fire, and the conquerors with harps.
  • vv. 3–4: their song.

Key words.

  • thalassa hyalinē, sea of glass: The sea before God's throne (4:6). Mixed with fire, it recalls the Red Sea crossed through danger and judgment.
  • ōdē Mōuseōs … kai tou arniou, song of Moses and of the Lamb: Not two songs but one: the deliverance from Egypt and the deliverance won by Christ are one story.
  • basileus tōn ethnōn, King of the nations: A note on the text: some manuscripts read “King of the ages”, which the Latin Vulgate follows. The NRSV and the Malayalam POC read “King of the nations”, echoing Jeremiah 10:7.

Verse by verse.

“those who had conquered the beast and its image and the number of its name, standing beside the sea of glass with harps of God in their hands.” (15:2)

They “conquered” by not giving in, many of them by dying. In Revelation victory looks like the Cross.

“Great and amazing are your deeds, Lord God the Almighty! Just and true are your ways, King of the nations!” (15:3)

Almost every phrase comes from the Old Testament (Psalm 111:2; Deuteronomy 32:4). The song does not praise the singers; it praises God alone.

“All nations will come and worship before you, for your judgments have been revealed.” (15:4)

An echo of Psalm 86:9. The hope is not that the nations will be destroyed, but that they will come and worship.

How the Church reads it.

The Easter Vigil keeps this connection: the Church sings the song of Moses after the reading of the Red Sea crossing, because baptism is our passage through the water. The Catechism sees the crossing of the Red Sea as a figure of baptism (CCC 1221).

Luke 21:12–19 · By your endurance you will gain your souls

Setting.

Jesus continues his discourse in the Temple. Before the cosmic signs, the disciples will face persecution. Luke's second book, the Acts of the Apostles, shows these words coming true: Peter and John before the council (Acts 4), Stephen whose wisdom no one could withstand (Acts 6:10), Paul before governors and King Agrippa (Acts 24–26).

Structure.

  • vv. 12–13: arrests, synagogues, prisons, kings and governors: an opportunity to testify.
  • vv. 14–15: do not prepare a defence; I will give you words and wisdom.
  • vv. 16–17: betrayal by family and friends; hatred.
  • vv. 18–19: not a hair will perish; endurance.

Key words.

  • martyrion, testimony: The word from which “martyr” comes. Persecution becomes the place where the Gospel is proclaimed.
  • hypomonē, endurance: Literally “remaining under” a burden. Not passive waiting, but steady faithfulness under pressure.
  • psychē, soul, life: “Gain your souls” means to gain your true life, the life that cannot be taken away.

Verse by verse.

“This will give you an opportunity to testify.” (21:13)

The courtroom becomes a pulpit. Disaster is turned into mission.

“for I will give you words and a wisdom that none of your opponents will be able to withstand or contradict.” (21:15)

In Matthew and Mark it is the Spirit who speaks; in Luke Jesus says “I will give”. The risen Christ is present with his witnesses.

“But not a hair of your head will perish.” (21:18)

Just two verses earlier he said “they will put some of you to death”. The promise is not about physical safety but about final safety in God's hands (compare 12:7).

“By your endurance you will gain your souls.” (21:19)

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism calls martyrdom the supreme witness given to the truth of the faith (CCC 2473). Tertullian told the persecutors that the blood of Christians is seed (in substance). The Church does not seek persecution, but she knows that her Lord's words prove true in every age.

For study and teaching

  • Revelation calls the martyrs “conquerors”. What kind of victory is Christian victory?
  • When have I been given words I did not prepare, at a moment when I had to speak for my faith?
