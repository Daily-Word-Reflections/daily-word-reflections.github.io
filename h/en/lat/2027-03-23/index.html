Word for the day

“I will give you as a light to the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth.” Isaiah 49:6

Reflection

Today the second Song of the Servant lets the Servant himself speak. “The LORD called me before I was born, while I was in my mother's womb he named me.” God has shaped him like a sharp sword and a polished arrow, hidden in his quiver until the right moment. Yet the Servant also knows the taste of failure: “I have labored in vain, I have spent my strength for nothing and vanity; yet surely my cause is with the LORD.” God's answer is astonishing. Instead of reducing the mission, he enlarges it: “It is too light a thing that you should be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob… I will give you as a light to the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth.” On Tuesday of Holy Week we hear this as the prayer of Jesus. To human eyes his work in Jerusalem is collapsing; one of the Twelve is about to sell him, another will deny him, the rest will run. Yet through that apparent failure the light will reach the whole world, even to the shores of India. The Psalm is the prayer of one who has trusted God from the womb: “Upon you I have leaned from my birth.”

The Gospel takes us into the Upper Room. Jesus has just washed the feet of the disciples, and now he is “troubled in spirit” and says: “Very truly, I tell you, one of you will betray me.” The disciples look at one another. The beloved disciple, reclining next to Jesus, asks: “Lord, who is it?” Jesus dips a piece of bread and gives it to Judas. In the East, to give a morsel from the dish to a guest was a sign of honour and friendship. Even at this moment Jesus offers Judas love. But Judas takes the bread and goes out. St John adds four words that fall like a stone: “And it was night.” Not only the night outside, but the night of a heart that refuses the light. And yet Jesus says: “Now the Son of Man has been glorified.” The hour of betrayal is also the hour of glory, because love is about to be shown to the end.

Then Peter speaks with all his warm, impulsive heart: “Lord, why can I not follow you now? I will lay down my life for you.” Jesus answers gently and truly: “Will you lay down your life for me? Very truly, I tell you, before the cock crows, you will have denied me three times.” Two disciples fail the Lord on the same night. The difference between them is not the size of their sin, but what they do afterwards. Judas goes out into the night and despairs. Peter will meet the gaze of Jesus, weep bitterly, and return (Luke 22:61–62). Jesus had said, “Where I am going, you cannot follow me now; but you will follow afterward”; and Peter did follow, to Rome and to his own cross. Holy Week is the time to be honest about our betrayals and denials, and to choose Peter's tears instead of Judas's night. No one is too far gone for the mercy of the One who gave the morsel even to his betrayer.

Full homily

Dear friends, in today's first reading the Servant speaks: the LORD called me before I was born, while I was in my mother's womb he named me (Isaiah 49:1). And then he admits: I have labored in vain, I have spent my strength for nothing (Isaiah 49:4). But God answers: it is too small a thing to restore only Israel; I will give you as a light to the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth (Isaiah 49:6).

In the Upper Room this light seems to be going out. Jesus is troubled in spirit and says: one of you will betray me. He gives a piece of bread, a sign of friendship, to Judas. Judas takes it and goes out. And it was night (John 13:30).

Then Peter says: I will lay down my life for you. Jesus answers: before the cock crows, you will have denied me three times (John 13:38). Two apostles fail him that night. Judas despairs; Peter weeps and returns. The difference is not that Peter's sin was small, but that Peter trusted the mercy of Jesus.

This Holy Week, do not go out into the night. Whatever you have done, come back to the light. Go to confession. Let Jesus look at you as he looked at Peter. From that weak, forgiven fisherman the light of Christ reached to the end of the earth, and it reaches you today. Amen.

For children

Children, Peter said he would never leave Jesus, but he got scared and said he did not know him. Then he was very sorry and cried, and Jesus forgave him and made him the leader of the apostles! When we do wrong, let us say sorry quickly and come back to Jesus.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you were called from the womb to be a light to the nations, and in the night of betrayal you went on loving to the end. When we fail you, do not let us go out into the night like Judas, but look at us as you looked at Peter, so that we may weep, return and follow you. Make your salvation reach to the ends of the earth. Amen.
