Word for the day

“Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live, and everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?”” John 11:25–26

Reflection

Moses finishes the work that he began at the foot of Sinai, and the reading says again and again: he did it “as the Lord had commanded Moses.” The tabernacle is set up on the first day of the first month of the second year, a year, almost to the day, after the Exodus. The ark is brought in, the tablets of the covenant are laid inside it, the mercy seat is set above it, and a curtain screens the ark from sight. Then the Lord answers: “the cloud covered the tent of meeting, and the glory of the Lord filled the tabernacle. Moses was not able to enter.” God has come to live in the middle of his people, and he is too great to be entered or used. When the cloud lifts, Israel moves; when it stays, Israel stays; and by night “fire was in the cloud.” The Psalm is the song of one who longs for such a house: “My soul longs, indeed it faints for the courts of the Lord... Even the sparrow finds a home, and the swallow a nest for herself... at your altars, O Lord of hosts.” The Church gives us this reading on the day of three friends of Jesus because Bethany was, for him, such a dwelling place: a house where he was welcome.

The proper Gospel of the memorial is from St John. Bethany is “near Jerusalem, some two miles away,” and Lazarus has been dead four days. “Many of the Jews had come to Martha and Mary to console them.” When she hears that Jesus is coming, Martha goes out to meet him, while Mary stays at home. Martha is practical and honest, and the Gospels show her often at the centre of things. She says what many of us have said at a graveside: “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.” But she goes on: “even now I know that God will give you whatever you ask of him.” Jesus tells her, “Your brother will rise again.” She answers with the faith of Israel: “I know that he will rise again in the resurrection on the last day.” And Jesus lifts her from a future hope to a present Person: “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live, and everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?” Her answer is one of the great confessions of the Gospels, like the one Peter gives at Caesarea Philippi: “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.” St Luke gives the other well-known scene, the alternative Gospel today: Martha “welcomed him into her home,” and was “distracted by her many tasks,” while Mary “sat at the Lord's feet and listened to what he was saying.” Jesus does not scold Martha for serving, but for being “worried and distracted by many things”: “there is need of only one thing. Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken away from her.” In St John's account of the supper at Bethany a week before the Passion, all three are together: “Martha served, and Lazarus was one of those at the table with him. Mary took a pound of costly perfume... The house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume.” Martha serves, Lazarus sits at table as a witness, and Mary anoints. The Church honours them together as a household where Jesus was a friend, since 2021 on one day. Eastern tradition says Lazarus became bishop of Kition in Cyprus, and Western legends place the family in Provence; these are traditions, not Scripture. The Gospels give us the three of them in their home, and that is enough.

Apply: Visit the Blessed Sacrament this week and say to the Lord: you are here, and I believe it.

2. Martha's faith

  • Lord, if you had been here.
  • I know he will rise on the last day.
  • You are the Messiah, the Son of God.
“For I know that my Redeemer lives, and that at the last he will stand upon the earth; and after my skin has been thus destroyed, then in my flesh I shall see God.” Job 19:25–26
“She said to him, “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.”” John 11:27

Apply: Bring to Jesus, in honest words like Martha's, one loss or sorrow in your life, and then say: I believe.

  • Martha welcomed him into her home.
  • Mary sat at his feet.
  • Only one thing is needed.

Apply: Put aside ten minutes each day this week, with no distraction, to listen to a Gospel passage.

Full homily

Dear friends, the first reading ends with a great scene: the tent is finished, and the glory of the Lord fills it, so that even Moses cannot go in. God has come to live among his people. And the Psalm sings what every heart wants: How lovely is your dwelling place, O Lord of hosts (Psalm 84:1). Where is God's dwelling place today? In church, yes. But also in a home like the one in Bethany, where Jesus was welcomed.

Today the Church remembers three friends of Jesus: Martha, Mary and Lazarus. We know them as a household. In Luke, Martha welcomed him into her home, and was busy with serving, while Mary sat at his feet to listen. Jesus said: Martha, Martha, you are worried and distracted by many things; there is need of only one thing. Mary has chosen the better part (Luke 10:41–42). He was not scolding the service; he was warning against the worry. We can serve him in a hundred ways, but he also wants us to sit.

In John's Gospel Martha is the one who goes out to meet Jesus when Lazarus has died. She says what we all say: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died (John 11:21). It is an honest prayer, with a little reproach in it. And Jesus does not rebuke her. He lifts her faith: I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live. Do you believe this? And she answers with one of the greatest confessions in the Gospels: Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world (John 11:27).

What a woman! She serves, she argues, she believes. And her brother Lazarus, who never speaks a word in the Gospels, is called out of the tomb. Dear friends, in our homes there are Marthas, Marys and Lazaruses: those who serve, those who pray, and those who say little but are living signs of what Jesus can do. Let us welcome Jesus in. Let us serve with love, listen with attention, and believe in the one who is the resurrection and the life. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus had friends in a village called Bethany: Martha, Mary and their brother Lazarus. When Jesus visited, Martha cooked and cleaned. Mary sat down next to Jesus and listened to him. Jesus said that listening is very special. And when Lazarus died, Jesus cried, because he loved him. Then he called: Lazarus, come out! And Lazarus came alive again! Jesus is stronger than death. Today let us help at home, and let us also sit quietly and listen to Jesus.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you were a guest in the home of Martha, Mary and Lazarus, and you wept at the grave of your friend. Be a guest in our homes, and make them places of hospitality, prayer and peace. Teach us Martha's honest faith, Mary's attentive listening and Lazarus's silent witness. You are the resurrection and the life: strengthen our faith and bring our dead into your light. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
