Word for the day

“But I say to you, Do not resist an evildoer. But if anyone strikes you on the right cheek, turn the other also;” Matthew 5:39

Reflection

St Paul writes to the Corinthians as a co-worker of God, and he speaks with urgency: “As we work together with him, we urge you also not to accept the grace of God in vain.” He quotes the prophet Isaiah, “At an acceptable time I have listened to you, and on a day of salvation I have helped you,” and then he adds his own commentary, like a man pointing at a clock: “See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” Grace is not something to be saved for later. Today is the day. Then Paul describes what his ministry has cost him, so that no one can say he preached for gain: “through great endurance, in afflictions, hardships, calamities, beatings, imprisonments, riots, labors, sleepless nights, hunger.” But the list does not end in complaint. It turns into a string of glorious paradoxes: “We are treated as impostors, and yet are true; as unknown, and yet are well known; as dying, and see, we are alive; as punished, and yet not killed; as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything.” This is the life of someone who belongs to the crucified and risen Christ. The world sees weakness and loss; God sees a man who possesses everything. The Psalm gives the reason for this joy: “O sing to the Lord a new song, for he has done marvelous things.”

In the Gospel we continue the Sermon on the Mount, and Jesus quotes one of the oldest laws of the ancient world: “You have heard that it was said, ‘An eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth.’” We often think of this law as cruel, but in its time (Exodus 21:24) it was a limit on revenge: no more than an eye for an eye, not a life for an eye, not a whole family for one insult. Jesus now goes much further. “But I say to you, Do not resist an evildoer. But if anyone strikes you on the right cheek, turn the other also.” A blow on the right cheek, given by a right hand, is a backhanded slap: an insult more than an injury. Jesus says: do not answer insult with insult. If someone sues you for your coat, give your cloak as well; “if anyone forces you to go one mile, go also the second mile.” Roman soldiers could force a local man to carry their baggage for a mile, as Simon of Cyrene was forced to carry the cross. Jesus says: surprise him by going further than he demands. These are not rules for being weak. They are a way of breaking the chain of revenge with a stronger force, the force of goodness. The Church does not read this as a command to let the innocent be harmed: protecting the weak and legitimate defence remain duties of love (Catechism 2263–2265), and no one should remain in a home where they are being abused. But in the daily insults, quarrels and injustices of family, office and neighbourhood, the disciple of Jesus refuses to pay back evil for evil.

Homily outline

Theme: “If anyone forces you to go one mile, go also the second mile.”

The day of salvation is today, the paradoxes of the apostle, and the goodness that breaks the chain of revenge.

1. Now is the acceptable time

  • Co-workers with God.
  • Do not receive grace in vain.
  • Today, not tomorrow.
“Thus says the Lord: In a time of favor I have answered you, on a day of salvation I have helped you;” Isaiah 49:8
“See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” 2 Corinthians 6:2

Apply: Do this week the good thing you have been postponing: a confession, a reconciliation, a visit to the sick.

2. Having nothing, possessing everything

  • The cost of the ministry.
  • Sorrowful yet always rejoicing.
  • Christ, the true wealth.
“Though the fig tree does not blossom, and no fruit is on the vines; though the produce of the olive fails, and the fields yield no food; though the flock is cut off from the fold, and there is no herd in the stalls, yet I will rejoice in the Lord; I will exult in the God of my salvation.” Habakkuk 3:17–18
“as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing everything.” 2 Corinthians 6:10

Apply: Thank God this week for one loss or hardship through which he has made you richer in faith.

3. The second mile

  • An eye for an eye: a limit on revenge.
  • Turn the other cheek.
  • Overcome evil with good.
“If your enemies are hungry, give them bread to eat; and if they are thirsty, give them water to drink;” Proverbs 25:21
“Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.” Romans 12:21

Apply: Answer one insult or unfair demand this week with an unexpected act of kindness.

Grace is offered today: the apostle who has Christ possesses everything, even in poverty and pain.

Jesus breaks the chain of revenge with a stronger force, the goodness that goes the second mile.

Take-home resolution: Each day this week, do one thing more than you were asked, with love.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul says today: see, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation! (2 Corinthians 6:2). We often say: later I will go to confession, later I will pray more, later I will make peace with my brother. Paul says: now. God's grace is offered today. Tomorrow is not promised to us.

Then Paul tells us what his life as an apostle was like: beatings, imprisonments, sleepless nights, hunger. And yet he says: we are sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; poor, yet making many rich; having nothing, and yet possessing everything (2 Corinthians 6:10). How is this possible? Because Paul has Christ, and the one who has Christ has everything. Many of our grandparents lived this way: they had little money, but they had faith, and they were rich.

In the Gospel Jesus asks something very difficult: do not resist an evildoer. But if anyone strikes you on the right cheek, turn the other also (Matthew 5:39). Jesus does not mean that we should allow the weak to be harmed, or that a wife should stay with a husband who beats her. The Church teaches that we must protect the innocent. But in the small insults of daily life, the harsh word, the unfair blame, the rude neighbour, Jesus asks us not to hit back.

And he asks even more: if anyone forces you to go one mile, go also the second mile (Matthew 5:41). Do more good than is demanded. This is how a family, an office, a parish is healed. Revenge makes the circle of evil bigger; goodness breaks it. Let us ask Jesus today for this grace, and let us use it now, for now is the day of salvation. Amen.

For children

Children, what do you do when your brother or sister hits you? Many of us want to hit back! Jesus says: do not hit back. Be kind instead. And if someone asks you to help, help even more than they ask. If Amma asks you to carry one bag, carry two! This is called going the second mile. It makes Jesus very happy, and it makes your home a happier place.

Prayer

Lord God, now is the acceptable time and today is the day of salvation: do not let us receive your grace in vain. Give us the spirit of St Paul, who was sorrowful yet always rejoicing, poor yet making many rich, having nothing and yet possessing everything. Lord Jesus, you did not answer insult with insult, and on the cross you prayed for those who struck you. Teach us not to pay back evil for evil, to turn the other cheek to the small insults of each day, and to go the second mile in love. Amen.
