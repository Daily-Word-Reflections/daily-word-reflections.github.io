Word for the day

“When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved standing beside her, he said to his mother, “Woman, here is your son.” Then he said to the disciple, “Here is your mother.”” John 19:26–27

Reflection

The day after Pentecost the Church turns to the one who was in the upper room when the Spirit came, and calls her by a name that sums up her place in God's plan: Mother of the Church. The first reading takes us back to the beginning, to the garden after the first sin. The Lord God calls: “Where are you?” Adam hides, afraid; he blames the woman, and the woman blames the serpent. Then God speaks to the serpent the first promise of salvation in the Bible, which the Fathers called the Protoevangelium, the first Gospel: “I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head, and you will strike his heel.” A woman and her offspring will stand against the power of evil, and the offspring will crush its head. The reading ends with a name: “The man named his wife Eve, because she was the mother of all living.” Christian tradition has seen in Mary the New Eve. St Irenaeus wrote (in substance) that the knot of Eve's disobedience was untied by Mary's obedience; what the virgin Eve bound by unbelief, the Virgin Mary loosed by faith. Eve became the mother of all the living in the order of nature; Mary, by her faith and her share in her Son's work, becomes the mother of all who live in grace. If Acts 1:12–14 is read instead, we see the apostles after the Ascension “constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus”: the Church at prayer, with the Mother in its midst. The Psalm sings of Zion, the mother city where every people is born: “Glorious things are spoken of you, O city of God,” and “All my springs are in you.”

The Gospel brings us to Calvary. “Standing near the cross of Jesus were his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene.” In the hour of his death Jesus looks at his mother and at the disciple whom he loved: “Woman, here is your son.” Then to the disciple: “Here is your mother.” “And from that hour the disciple took her into his own home.” He calls her “Woman,” as he did at Cana, the name that recalls the woman of Genesis. The beloved disciple is not named, so that every disciple can stand in his place. Jesus does not only provide for his widowed mother; from the cross he gives her as mother to all whom he loves, and gives them to her as children. Then, “knowing that all was now finished,” he says “I am thirsty,” and “It is finished,” and “gave up his spirit.” Finally a soldier pierces his side, “and at once blood and water came out.” The Fathers saw in that blood and water the sacraments of the Eucharist and Baptism, and the Church herself born from the side of Christ, as Eve was formed from the side of Adam. At the foot of the cross, Mary stands at the birth of the Church; in the upper room she prays at its Pentecost. St Paul VI solemnly declared her Mother of the Church on 21 November 1964, at the Second Vatican Council, on the day the Council approved its teaching on the Church, Lumen Gentium, whose last chapter is about her. St Augustine had written (in substance): Mary is the mother of the members of Christ, because by her love she cooperated so that the faithful might be born in the Church.

Theme: “Here is your mother.”

The day after Pentecost: the woman of the first promise, the Mother given from the cross, and the Mother praying with the newborn Church.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we celebrated Pentecost, the birthday of the Church. Today, the very next day, the Church turns to a mother. Every birth has a mother, and the Church does too. Since 2018 the Monday after Pentecost has been the memorial of the Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of the Church.

The first reading takes us to the very beginning. After the first sin, God calls Adam: where are you? (Genesis 3:9). Adam hides and blames. But in the middle of that sad scene God makes a promise: I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head (Genesis 3:15). Long before Bethlehem, God already had a woman and her Son in mind. The Fathers called Mary the New Eve: what Eve lost by disobedience, Mary restored by her yes.

In the Gospel we stand at the cross. Jesus is dying. And with almost his last breath, he thinks of his mother and of his friend. Woman, here is your son. Here is your mother. And from that hour the disciple took her into his own home (John 19:26–27). The disciple has no name in the Gospel, so that you can put your name there. Jesus gives his Mother to you. And then from his pierced side flow blood and water, the sacraments by which the Church is born and fed. Mary was there.

And after the Ascension, Mary was in the upper room, praying with the apostles until the Spirit came (Acts 1:14). She is the Mother who stays with the Church in prayer. Today Ordinary Time begins again. Ordinary days, ordinary work, ordinary family life. Let us take Mary into our homes for these ordinary days, as John did. At noon today we pray the Angelus again; let us pray it with love. And let us listen to her one instruction, given at Cana: do whatever he tells you. Holy Mary, Mother of the Church, pray for us. Amen.

For children

Children, when Jesus was on the cross, his mother Mary was standing near him with his friend John. Jesus said to John: here is your mother! Jesus gave his own Mummy to John, and to you and me! Mary is the Mother of the whole Church. Whenever you are sad or scared, you can talk to her, and she will take you to Jesus. Let us say: Holy Mary, my Mother, pray for me!

Prayer

O God, Father of mercies, whose Only Begotten Son, as he hung upon the Cross, chose the Blessed Virgin Mary, his Mother, to be our Mother also, grant, we pray, that with her loving help your Church may be more fruitful day by day and, exulting in the holiness of her children, may draw to her embrace all the families of the peoples. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, from the cross you gave us your Mother; like the beloved disciple, we take her into our homes. Holy Mary, Mother of the Church, who prayed with the apostles for the Holy Spirit, pray for us now as we begin again the ordinary days of the year, that we may do whatever your Son tells us. Amen.
