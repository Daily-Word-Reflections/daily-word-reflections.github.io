Word for the day

“When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved standing beside her, he said to his mother, “Woman, here is your son.” Then he said to the disciple, “Here is your mother.”” John 19:26–27

Reflection

The day after Pentecost the Church turns to the one who was in the upper room when the Spirit came, and calls her by a name that sums up her place in God's plan: Mother of the Church. The first reading takes us back to the beginning, to the garden after the first sin. The Lord God calls: “Where are you?” Adam hides, afraid; he blames the woman, and the woman blames the serpent. Then God speaks to the serpent the first promise of salvation in the Bible, which the Fathers called the Protoevangelium, the first Gospel: “I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head, and you will strike his heel.” A woman and her offspring will stand against the power of evil, and the offspring will crush its head. The reading ends with a name: “The man named his wife Eve, because she was the mother of all living.” Christian tradition has seen in Mary the New Eve. St Irenaeus wrote (in substance) that the knot of Eve's disobedience was untied by Mary's obedience; what the virgin Eve bound by unbelief, the Virgin Mary loosed by faith. Eve became the mother of all the living in the order of nature; Mary, by her faith and her share in her Son's work, becomes the mother of all who live in grace. If Acts 1:12–14 is read instead, we see the apostles after the Ascension “constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus”: the Church at prayer, with the Mother in its midst. The Psalm sings of Zion, the mother city where every people is born: “Glorious things are spoken of you, O city of God,” and “All my springs are in you.”

The Gospel brings us to Calvary. “Standing near the cross of Jesus were his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene.” In the hour of his death Jesus looks at his mother and at the disciple whom he loved: “Woman, here is your son.” Then to the disciple: “Here is your mother.” “And from that hour the disciple took her into his own home.” He calls her “Woman,” as he did at Cana, the name that recalls the woman of Genesis. The beloved disciple is not named, so that every disciple can stand in his place. Jesus does not only provide for his widowed mother; from the cross he gives her as mother to all whom he loves, and gives them to her as children. Then, “knowing that all was now finished,” he says “I am thirsty,” and “It is finished,” and “gave up his spirit.” Finally a soldier pierces his side, “and at once blood and water came out.” The Fathers saw in that blood and water the sacraments of the Eucharist and Baptism, and the Church herself born from the side of Christ, as Eve was formed from the side of Adam. At the foot of the cross, Mary stands at the birth of the Church; in the upper room she prays at its Pentecost. St Paul VI solemnly declared her Mother of the Church on 21 November 1964, at the Second Vatican Council, on the day the Council approved its teaching on the Church, Lumen Gentium, whose last chapter is about her. St Augustine had written (in substance): Mary is the mother of the members of Christ, because by her love she cooperated so that the faithful might be born in the Church.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we celebrated Pentecost, the birthday of the Church. Today, the very next day, the Church turns to a mother. Every birth has a mother, and the Church does too. Since 2018 the Monday after Pentecost has been the memorial of the Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of the Church.

The first reading takes us to the very beginning. After the first sin, God calls Adam: where are you? (Genesis 3:9). Adam hides and blames. But in the middle of that sad scene God makes a promise: I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head (Genesis 3:15). Long before Bethlehem, God already had a woman and her Son in mind. The Fathers called Mary the New Eve: what Eve lost by disobedience, Mary restored by her yes.

In the Gospel we stand at the cross. Jesus is dying. And with almost his last breath, he thinks of his mother and of his friend. Woman, here is your son. Here is your mother. And from that hour the disciple took her into his own home (John 19:26–27). The disciple has no name in the Gospel, so that you can put your name there. Jesus gives his Mother to you. And then from his pierced side flow blood and water, the sacraments by which the Church is born and fed. Mary was there.

And after the Ascension, Mary was in the upper room, praying with the apostles until the Spirit came (Acts 1:14). She is the Mother who stays with the Church in prayer. Today Ordinary Time begins again. Ordinary days, ordinary work, ordinary family life. Let us take Mary into our homes for these ordinary days, as John did. At noon today we pray the Angelus again; let us pray it with love. And let us listen to her one instruction, given at Cana: do whatever he tells you. Holy Mary, Mother of the Church, pray for us. Amen.

For children

Children, when Jesus was on the cross, his mother Mary was standing near him with his friend John. Jesus said to John: here is your mother! Jesus gave his own Mummy to John, and to you and me! Mary is the Mother of the whole Church. Whenever you are sad or scared, you can talk to her, and she will take you to Jesus. Let us say: Holy Mary, my Mother, pray for me!

Prayer

O God, Father of mercies, whose Only Begotten Son, as he hung upon the Cross, chose the Blessed Virgin Mary, his Mother, to be our Mother also, grant, we pray, that with her loving help your Church may be more fruitful day by day and, exulting in the holiness of her children, may draw to her embrace all the families of the peoples. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, from the cross you gave us your Mother; like the beloved disciple, we take her into our homes. Holy Mary, Mother of the Church, who prayed with the apostles for the Holy Spirit, pray for us now as we begin again the ordinary days of the year, that we may do whatever your Son tells us. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

On the day after Pentecost the Roman Rite honours the Blessed Virgin Mary, Mother of the Church. The readings take us to two moments: the garden, where after the first sin God promises enmity between the serpent and the woman and her offspring, and the Cross, where Jesus gives his mother to the beloved disciple and the disciple to his mother. The Lectionary also allows Acts 1:12–14, where the apostles pray “together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus” before Pentecost.

Psalm 87 sings of Zion, the city of God, as the mother of the peoples: “This one was born there.” The Church, the new Jerusalem, is our mother, and Mary is the mother of the Church.

Genesis 3:9–15, 20 · I will put enmity between you and the woman

Setting.

The man and the woman have eaten from the tree, their eyes have been opened, and they have hidden themselves from God among the trees of the garden (3:1–8). Now God comes to find them. The Lectionary leaves out vv. 16–19, the consequences for the woman and the man, and adds v. 20, where the man names his wife Eve.

Background. This is part of the second account of creation (2:4b–3:24). It tells in the language of images a real event at the beginning of human history, the first sin, as the Catechism teaches (CCC 390). The serpent is the figure of the tempter, whom the Book of Revelation calls “that ancient serpent, who is called the Devil and Satan” (Revelation 12:9).

Structure.

  • vv. 9–10: “Where are you?” I was afraid and hid.
  • vv. 11–13: God questions; the man blames the woman, the woman blames the serpent.
  • v. 14: the curse on the serpent.
  • v. 15: enmity between the serpent and the woman, his offspring and hers.
  • v. 20: Eve, mother of all living.

Key words.

  • ’ayyekkāh, where are you?: God’s first question to fallen man. Not because God does not know, but to call the man out of hiding. It is the question of a Father seeking his child.
  • ’êbāh, enmity: A lasting hostility. God himself puts it: the human race will not be left in friendship with evil.
  • zera‘, offspring, seed: It can mean descendants in general or one descendant. Christian tradition reads it of Christ, the offspring of the woman who defeats the serpent.
  • ḥawwāh, Eve: The name sounds like the Hebrew word for “living”. Eve is “the mother of all living”. The Fathers called Mary the new Eve, mother of all who live in Christ.

Verse by verse.

“But the Lord God called to the man, and said to him, “Where are you?”” (3:9)

Sin makes us hide; God comes looking. The whole of salvation history begins with this search.

“The man said, “The woman whom you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit from the tree, and I ate.”” (3:12)

The first fruit of sin is broken relationship: the man blames the woman, and even God who gave her. The unity of the first couple is wounded.

“I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head, and you will strike his heel.” (3:15)

Even before the punishment of the man and woman, God speaks a promise. The Hebrew says “he” (or “it”), meaning the offspring; the old Latin Vulgate read “she”, and so many paintings show Mary crushing the serpent. The Church’s faith does not rest on that reading: the victory belongs to Christ, and Mary shares in it as his mother, wholly on his side against the serpent.

“The man named his wife Eve, because she was the mother of all living.” (3:20)

After the sentence of death, the man gives his wife a name of life. Hope is not lost.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism calls Genesis 3:15 the “Protoevangelium”, the first announcement of the Messiah and Redeemer, of a battle between the serpent and the Woman, and of the final victory of a descendant of hers. Many Fathers and Doctors of the Church see the Woman as Mary, the mother of Christ, the new Eve (CCC 410–411). St Irenaeus taught that the knot of Eve’s disobedience was loosed by Mary’s obedience; what the virgin Eve bound by unbelief, the Virgin Mary loosed by faith. The Council quotes him (Lumen Gentium 56), and it recalls that the promise of victory over the serpent foreshadows Mary (Lumen Gentium 55). Blessed Pius IX cited this verse in defining the Immaculate Conception in 1854.

John 19:25–34 · Woman, here is your son. Here is your mother

Setting.

Jesus has been crucified, and the soldiers have divided his clothes (19:23–24). Only John tells that the mother of Jesus stood near the Cross. In John’s Gospel she appears only twice, at Cana (2:1–12) and at Calvary, and she is never named; she is “the mother of Jesus”. At Cana Jesus said to her, “Woman, ... My hour has not yet come” (2:4). Now the hour has come (13:1).

Structure.

  • v. 25: the women near the Cross.
  • vv. 26–27: “Woman, here is your son.” “Here is your mother.” The disciple takes her into his own home.
  • vv. 28–30: “I am thirsty”; the sour wine; “It is finished”; he gives up his spirit.
  • vv. 31–33: the legs of Jesus are not broken.
  • v. 34: the soldier pierces his side; blood and water flow.

Key words.

  • gynai, woman: A respectful form of address, used also at Cana (2:4). It recalls “the woman” of Genesis 3:15 and the mother of the new humanity.
  • eis ta idia, into his own home: Literally “into his own”: among the things that were his. The disciple welcomes Mary into his life as a treasured gift.
  • tetelestai, it is finished: Accomplished, brought to its goal. The work the Father gave the Son is complete (17:4).
  • paredōken to pneuma, gave up his spirit: Literally “handed over the spirit”. He dies, and his death already looks towards the gift of the Spirit to the Church.

Verse by verse.

“standing near the cross of Jesus were his mother, and his mother’s sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene.” (19:25)

They stand. Mary does not flee. She who said “Here am I, the servant of the Lord” (Luke 1:38) remains faithful at the hour of the Cross.

“When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved standing beside her, he said to his mother, “Woman, here is your son.”” (19:26)

The beloved disciple is not named. The Church sees in him every disciple whom Jesus loves. From the Cross Jesus gives his mother a new motherhood.

“Then he said to the disciple, “Here is your mother.” And from that hour the disciple took her into his own home.” (19:27)

This is more than a son providing for his widowed mother. It is the gift of the hour: Mary becomes mother of the disciples, and the disciple receives her.

“When Jesus had received the wine, he said, “It is finished.” Then he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.” (19:30)

He bows his head first, then hands over his spirit: in John he lays down his life of his own accord (10:18).

“Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.” (19:34)

John insists that he saw it (19:35). The Fathers saw in the blood and water the sacraments of the Eucharist and Baptism, and the Church born from the side of Christ, as Eve was formed from the side of Adam.

How the Church reads it.

The Council teaches that “from the side of Christ as he slept the sleep of death upon the Cross there came forth the wondrous sacrament of the whole Church” (Sacrosanctum Concilium 5; CCC 766). Lumen Gentium says that Mary, standing at the Cross, was given by the dying Jesus as mother to his disciple (Lumen Gentium 58; CCC 964), and, quoting St Augustine, that she is truly mother of the members of Christ, because she cooperated by her love so that the faithful might be born in the Church (Lumen Gentium 53; CCC 963). St Paul VI proclaimed Mary Mother of the Church on 21 November 1964, at the Council. Saint John Paul II wrote that the disciple’s taking Mary “into his own” is the model for every Christian who entrusts himself to her (Redemptoris Mater 45).

Questions people ask

Why does Jesus call his mother “Woman”?

It is not cold or disrespectful. In Greek it was a courteous form of address. In John it has a deeper meaning: Mary at Cana and at the Cross is “the woman” whose offspring defeats the serpent (Genesis 3:15), the new Eve, the mother of the new people of God. Jesus speaks to her not only as her son, but as Redeemer, giving her a mission.

Does calling Mary “Mother of the Church” put her in the place of Christ?

No. The Council teaches that there is one mediator, Christ, and that Mary’s maternal role does not obscure or diminish his unique mediation but shows its power; it flows from his merits and depends on them entirely (Lumen Gentium 60). Mary is the first and most excellent member of the Church, and at the same time its mother (Lumen Gentium 53). She always leads us to her Son: “Do whatever he tells you” (John 2:5).

For the Latin reader

In 2018 Pope Francis inscribed this memorial in the General Roman Calendar for the Monday after Pentecost (decree Ecclesia Mater of the Congregation for Divine Worship, 11 February 2018), so that the Church, born at Pentecost, might honour the Mother who prayed with the apostles for the Spirit. It is an obligatory memorial. With it the Church in the Roman Rite resumes Ordinary Time, in the seventh week this year; the vestments are white for the memorial.
