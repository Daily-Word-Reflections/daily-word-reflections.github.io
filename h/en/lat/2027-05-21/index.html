Word for the day

“So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate.” Mark 10:8–9

Reflection

Ben Sira writes one of the finest pages on friendship in all literature. He begins with the pleasant side: “Pleasant speech multiplies friends, and a gracious tongue multiplies courtesies.” Be kind, and many will be kind to you. But then he becomes a careful realist. “Let those who are friendly with you be many, but let your advisers be one in a thousand. When you gain friends, gain them through testing, and do not trust them hastily.” He describes the fair-weather friend with a sharp pen: there are friends “who sit at your table, but they will not stand by you in time of trouble,” and when you are brought low, “they turn against you, and hide themselves from you.” And then comes the praise of the true friend: “Faithful friends are a sturdy shelter: whoever finds one has found a treasure. Faithful friends are beyond price; no amount can balance their worth. Faithful friends are life-saving medicine; and those who fear the Lord will find them.” The secret of good friendship, he says, is the fear of the Lord: “as they are, so are their neighbors also.” Those who belong to God become faithful, and they attract faithful friends. On the night before he died Jesus would say to his disciples: “I have called you friends.” He is the friend who stays in the time of trouble.

In the Gospel the Pharisees test Jesus: “Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife?” In his time there was a lively debate among Jewish teachers about the grounds for divorce, some strict and some very lenient; but in practice it was the husband who could send his wife away. Jesus goes behind the debate to the beginning: “from the beginning of creation, ‘God made them male and female.’ ‘For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.’ So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate.” The permission of Moses was given “because of your hardness of heart”; the plan of God is a covenant of faithful love that lasts for life. Then, in the house, Jesus says plainly that remarriage after divorce is adultery, for wife and husband alike, which in that world was itself a defence of women. The Church holds this teaching faithfully (CCC 1614–1615, 1640), and with the same faithfulness she accompanies with tenderness those whose marriages have broken, never treating them as outsiders (Amoris Laetitia 243). Marriage, like friendship, is God's work, and both are made strong by the fear of the Lord. Today we honour St Christopher Magallanes, a parish priest of Jalisco in Mexico, arrested exactly a hundred years ago today, on his way to celebrate Mass, and shot without trial with Fr Agustín Caloca on 25 May 1927. He had opposed armed revolt and preached peace; before he died he forgave his executioners. He was a faithful friend of Christ to the end.

Homily outline

Theme: “What God has joined together, let no one separate.”

The treasure of faithful friends, marriage as God made it from the beginning, and the faithfulness of the martyrs.

1. Faithful friends are a treasure

  • Friends at the table and friends in trouble.
  • Test before you trust.
  • The fear of the Lord makes friendship firm.
“Faithful friends are a sturdy shelter: whoever finds one has found a treasure.” Sirach 6:14
“I do not call you servants any longer, because the servant does not know what the master is doing; but I have called you friends, because I have made known to you everything that I have heard from my Father.” John 15:15

Apply: Visit or call one friend in difficulty this week and stay with them for a while.

2. From the beginning

  • Behind the debate to God's plan.
  • Male and female, one flesh.
  • A covenant for life.
“Therefore a man leaves his father and his mother and clings to his wife, and they become one flesh.” Genesis 2:24
“So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate.” Mark 10:8–9

Apply: If you are married, renew your vows privately in prayer with your spouse this week; if not, pray for one couple by name each day.

3. A new heart and a faithful love

  • Hardness of heart.
  • Christ's grace makes faithfulness possible.
  • The martyrs: faithful unto death.
“A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you; and I will remove from your body the heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh.” Ezekiel 36:26
“No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends.” John 15:13

Apply: Ask St Christopher Magallanes for the grace to forgive one person who has hurt you.

Friendship and marriage are God's works, made firm by the fear of the Lord and by the grace of Christ.

The martyrs, like St Christopher Magallanes, show the deepest faithfulness: to give one's life for the Friend who gave his for us.

Take-home resolution: Be a sturdy shelter for one person this week, and pray daily for the marriages you know.

Full homily

Dear friends, Ben Sira says something we all know from experience. When you are doing well, many people are your friends. But there are friends who sit at your table, but they will not stand by you in time of trouble (Sirach 6:10). When the hospital bill comes, or the business fails, the table empties.

And then he praises the true friend: faithful friends are a sturdy shelter: whoever finds one has found a treasure (Sirach 6:14). A faithful friend is life-saving medicine. Where do we find such friends? Ben Sira answers: those who fear the Lord will find them. When God is at the centre, friendship becomes faithful.

In the Gospel Jesus speaks of the closest friendship of all, marriage. The Pharisees want to discuss when divorce is allowed. Jesus takes them back to the beginning: God made them male and female, and the two become one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate (Mark 10:9). This is not a heavy rule; it is God's dream for love. And Christ gives the grace to live it. To those who are hurting from broken marriages, the Church does not close the door; she walks with them.

Today we remember St Christopher Magallanes, a parish priest in Mexico, arrested a hundred years ago today on his way to celebrate Mass, and shot four days later. He forgave his killers and prayed that his blood would bring peace to his country. He was a faithful friend of Jesus to the end. Let us be faithful friends, faithful husbands and wives, and faithful disciples. Amen.

For children

Children, a true friend is someone who stays with you when you are sad, not only when you have sweets to share! Jesus is that kind of friend. He also says that when a mother and father get married, God joins them together. Let us pray for our parents today and be good friends to everyone.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, faithful friend, you call us friends and never desert us in the time of trouble. Give us faithful friends and make us faithful friends to others. Bless the marriages of our families; renew in husbands and wives the love that you joined together from the beginning, and console with your tenderness all whose marriages have been wounded. Through the prayers of St Christopher Magallanes and his companions, who loved you even unto death, give us hearts of flesh and steadfast love. Amen.
