Word for the day

“Jesus said to them, “Have you never read in the scriptures: ‘The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone; this was the Lord's doing, and it is amazing in our eyes’?” Matthew 21:42

Reflection

On the Fridays of Lent the Church sets before us the mystery of the beloved son who is rejected. Joseph is his father's favourite, “the son of his old age,” and Jacob makes him a long robe with sleeves. His brothers hate him and “could not speak peaceably to him.” When Jacob sends him to see how they are, they see him coming from far off and say: “Here comes this dreamer. Come now, let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits.” Reuben saves his life; Judah suggests a profit instead: they sell him to traders “for twenty pieces of silver,” and he is taken down to Egypt. Envy has done its work. But the Psalm sings what the brothers could not see: God “had sent a man ahead of them, Joseph, who was sold as a slave.” The one they threw into the pit would one day feed them in a famine and forgive them. Years later Joseph would say: “Even though you intended to do harm to me, God intended it for good.”

In the Gospel Jesus tells the story of a vineyard owner who plants, fences, builds a tower and leases it to tenants. At harvest he sends servants for his share; the tenants beat one, kill another, stone another. Last of all he sends his son: “They will respect my son.” But the tenants say: “This is the heir; come, let us kill him and get his inheritance.” They throw him out of the vineyard and kill him. The chief priests and Pharisees realise he is speaking about them. Jesus is telling his own story, a few days before it happens: he will be sold for silver like Joseph, taken outside the city and killed. And yet, “The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone; this was the Lord's doing.” God turns the worst that sin can do into the foundation of our salvation. On this Lenten Friday we ask: do I welcome the Son, or do I want the vineyard for myself? And what fruit am I giving to the Lord of the vineyard?

Homily outline

Theme: “The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.”

The beloved son sold by envy, the son thrown out of the vineyard, and the God who turns evil into good.

1. The beloved son sold

  • A father's love.
  • The brothers' envy.
  • Twenty pieces of silver.
“They said to one another, “Here comes this dreamer. Come now, let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits; then we shall say that a wild animal has devoured him, and we shall see what will become of his dreams.”” Genesis 37:19–20
““The patriarchs, jealous of Joseph, sold him into Egypt; but God was with him,” Acts 7:9

Apply: Bring your envy to confession this Lent, and pray daily for the one you envy.

2. The son thrown out

  • The vineyard and the tenants.
  • Servants beaten and killed.
  • The heir killed outside.
“he had sent a man ahead of them, Joseph, who was sold as a slave.” Psalm 105:17
“But when the tenants saw the son, they said to themselves, ‘This is the heir; come, let us kill him and get his inheritance.’” Matthew 21:38

Apply: Make the Way of the Cross this Friday, staying a moment longer at the second station, where Jesus takes up the cross.

3. The rejected stone

  • The Lord's doing.
  • Evil turned to good.
  • Fruit in due season.
“Even though you intended to do harm to me, God intended it for good, in order to preserve a numerous people, as he is doing today.” Genesis 50:20
“We know that all things work together for good for those who love God, who are called according to his purpose.” Romans 8:28

Apply: Offer one rejection or hurt you carry, and ask God to bring good from it.

Joseph sold for silver is a figure of Christ betrayed.

The rejected Son is the cornerstone of our salvation.

Take-home resolution: Pray the Way of the Cross on the Fridays of Lent.

Full homily

Dear friends, Israel loved Joseph more than any other of his children, because he was the son of his old age (Genesis 37:3). His brothers hated him. When they saw him coming they said: here comes this dreamer. Come now, let us kill him (Genesis 37:19–20). In the end they sold him for twenty pieces of silver.

Envy is a quiet sin, but it can lead to terrible things. It cannot bear another's good. Yet God was with Joseph. The pit led to Egypt, Egypt to the palace, and the palace to bread for his brothers in the famine. What they meant for evil, God meant for good.

In the Gospel the vineyard owner sends his own son, saying: they will respect my son. But the tenants say: this is the heir; come, let us kill him and get his inheritance (Matthew 21:38). Jesus is telling his own Passion. Like Joseph, he will be sold for silver and handed over.

And yet: the stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone; this was the Lord's doing (Matthew 21:42). On this Lenten Friday let us stand at the cross and give the Lord the fruit he seeks: a heart without envy, hands that share, and lives that welcome his Son. Amen.

For children

Children, Joseph's brothers were jealous and sold him far away. But God took care of Joseph and turned it all into good. Jesus was also rejected, but God made him our Saviour. When you feel jealous, ask Jesus to give you a happy heart!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, beloved Son of the Father, you were sold and thrown out and killed, yet you became the cornerstone of our salvation. Free us from envy and greed, make us fruitful tenants of your vineyard, and turn all our sufferings to good. Amen.
