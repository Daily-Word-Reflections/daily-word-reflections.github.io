Readings

  • First Reading Zephaniah 3:14–18 (or Romans 12:9–16) · The King of Israel, the Lord, is in your midst
  • Responsorial Psalm Isaiah 12:2–3, 4, 5–6 · Great in your midst is the Holy One of Israel
  • Gospel Luke 1:39–56 · Why has this happened to me, that the mother of my Lord comes to me?

Word for the day

“And blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” Luke 1:45

Reflection

On the last day of the month of Mary the Church follows a young woman on a journey. The prophet Zephaniah, writing in a dark time for Jerusalem, suddenly breaks into a song of joy addressed to “daughter Zion”: “Sing aloud, O daughter Zion; shout, O Israel! Rejoice and exult with all your heart, O daughter Jerusalem!” The reason for the joy is a presence: “The king of Israel, the LORD, is in your midst; you shall fear disaster no more.” And again: “Do not fear, O Zion; do not let your hands grow weak. The LORD, your God, is in your midst, a warrior who gives victory; he will rejoice over you with gladness, he will renew you in his love; he will exult over you with loud singing as on a day of festival.” The Hebrew for “in your midst” can also mean “within you,” “in your womb.” The Church has always heard in these words the voice of the angel to Mary: “Greetings, favored one! The Lord is with you” (Luke 1:28), a greeting that in Greek begins with the word “Rejoice,” and then: do not be afraid. Mary is the true daughter Zion, and the Lord is now, literally, within her. The responsorial psalm, the song of Isaiah 12, carries the same joy: “Shout aloud and sing for joy, O royal Zion, for great in your midst is the Holy One of Israel.” Where the second option is read, St Paul's words in Romans 12 sound like a portrait of what Mary does in today's Gospel: “Let love be genuine,” “extend hospitality to strangers,” “Rejoice with those who rejoice,” “do not be haughty, but associate with the lowly.”

Then the Gospel. Mary has just heard the angel's message, and has heard too that her elderly relative Elizabeth, “who was said to be barren,” is in her sixth month. Her first response to the gift of God is not to stay at home and wonder, but to go and serve: “In those days Mary set out and went with haste to a Judean town in the hill country.” It was a journey of perhaps four days on foot, from Nazareth in the north to the hills near Jerusalem, where tradition places the village of Ein Karem. Mary carries the Son of God within her, hidden and very small, and she brings him to another home. St Luke tells the story with words that echo the Old Testament account of the Ark of the Covenant being brought into the hill country of Judah (2 Samuel 6). There, David asked in awe, “How can the ark of the LORD come into my care?”; here Elizabeth asks, “And why has this happened to me, that the mother of my Lord comes to me?” There, David leaped and danced before the ark; here, “the child leaped in her womb.” There, the ark stayed three months in the house of Obed-edom and brought blessing; here, “Mary remained with her about three months.” Mary is the new Ark, carrying not the tablets of the Law but the Word made flesh.

Elizabeth, “filled with the Holy Spirit,” speaks the words that the Church repeats millions of times a day in the Hail Mary: “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb.” She calls Mary “the mother of my Lord,” the first time in St Luke's Gospel that a human voice calls Jesus Lord, and the basis of the Church's faith that Mary is truly the Mother of God (Catechism 495). And she praises Mary's faith: “blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” Then Mary sings. The Magnificat is woven out of the Psalms and the song of Hannah (1 Samuel 2), the prayer of a young woman who knew the Scriptures by heart. “My soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior, for he has looked with favor on the lowliness of his servant.” She does not boast of herself; she boasts of God, who “has done great things for me.” And then the song widens to the whole of history: God “has brought down the powerful from their thrones, and lifted up the lowly; he has filled the hungry with good things, and sent the rich away empty.” The God who chose a poor girl from Nazareth is the God who takes the side of the poor. Every evening, at Vespers, the Church sings this song with Mary. On this last day of May we may ask her for two gifts: her haste to serve, and her joy in God.

Theme: “Blessed is she who believed.”

The joy of daughter Zion, the haste of love, the faith that Elizabeth praised, and the song of the lowly lifted up.

Apply: Begin each day this week with the Angelus or a Hail Mary, remembering that the Lord is with you too.

  • Four days on foot.

“In those days Mary set out and went with haste to a Judean town in the hill country,” Luke 1:39

  • Filled with the Holy Spirit.

“And blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” Luke 1:45

4. My soul magnifies the Lord

  • The song of Hannah renewed.
  • The lowly lifted up.
  • The hungry filled.

“And Mary said, “My soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior,” Luke 1:46–47

Apply: Give food or help to one hungry person or family this week, so that Mary's song comes true through you.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today we close the month of May with a beautiful journey. A young woman from Nazareth, who has just said yes to God, sets out on foot over the hills. She is carrying the Son of God within her. Nobody can see him yet. But wherever she goes, he goes.

The prophet Zephaniah had sung long before: sing aloud, O daughter Zion! The Lord, your God, is in your midst (Zephaniah 3:14, 17). The Hebrew words can mean in your womb. In Mary the prophecy is fulfilled literally. God is not only near his people; he is living inside a young girl from a small village. That is why Mary's whole life is joy.

And what does she do with this joy? St Luke says: Mary set out and went with haste (Luke 1:39). She does not stay at home thinking about her privilege. She hears that her old relative Elizabeth is expecting a baby, and she goes to help. The first thing Mary does with Jesus is to carry him to someone in need. That is the pattern for every Christian: the one who receives Jesus goes out to serve.

When Mary greets her, Elizabeth's child leaps for joy, and Elizabeth is filled with the Holy Spirit. She cries out: blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb. Why has this happened to me, that the mother of my Lord comes to me? (Luke 1:42–43). Every time we pray the Hail Mary, we repeat Elizabeth's words. And she adds: blessed is she who believed. Mary's greatness is first her faith.

Then Mary sings: my soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior (Luke 1:46–47). She sings of a God who looks at the lowly, who brings down the powerful and lifts up the humble, who fills the hungry with good things. Mary is not proud. She is happy because God is great and God is good to the poor. If we want to honour Mary, we must also care for the lowly and the hungry.

Today let us thank Mary for this month of May, for every Rosary prayed in our homes. And let us ask her for her haste: when we see someone in need, let us not delay. Let us visit the sick, the old, the lonely, the expectant mother. Let us carry Jesus to them, as Mary did. And then, with her, our souls too will magnify the Lord. Amen.

For children

Children, after the angel told Mary she would be the mother of Jesus, she heard that her cousin Elizabeth was going to have a baby too. Did Mary say, I am too important now? No! She ran over the hills to help Elizabeth. And when Mary said hello, the baby inside Elizabeth jumped for joy, because baby Jesus was inside Mary!

Today is the last day of May, Mary's month. Let us be like Mary. Is there someone at home you can help today, quickly and with a smile? Then you are bringing Jesus to them, just like Mary did.

Prayer

Almighty ever-living God, who, while the Blessed Virgin Mary was carrying your Son in her womb, inspired her to visit Elizabeth, grant us, we pray, that, faithful to the promptings of the Spirit, we may magnify your greatness with the Virgin Mary at all times. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Holy Mary, Mother of God, you went with haste to serve, carrying Christ within you. Teach us your joy and your readiness. As this month of May ends, take our families under your care, and help us to bring Jesus to every home we enter. Amen.
