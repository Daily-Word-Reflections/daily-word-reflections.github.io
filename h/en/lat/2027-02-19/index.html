Word for the day

“leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift.” Matthew 5:24

Reflection

The exiles in Babylon had a proverb: “The parents have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge.” We are paying for our ancestors' sins, they said; nothing we do can change our fate. Ezekiel answers with God's own word: every person stands before God in his own present choice. “If the wicked turn away from all their sins that they have committed… they shall surely live; they shall not die. None of the transgressions that they have committed shall be remembered against them.” And then the heart of the chapter: “Have I any pleasure in the death of the wicked, says the Lord GOD, and not rather that they should turn from their ways and live?” The warning cuts the other way too: the righteous who turn to evil cannot hide behind their past goodness. Neither our past sins nor our past virtues decide our future; what matters is the turning of the heart now. That is why Lent is always hopeful: it is never too late to turn and live. The Psalm prays it from the depths: “If you, O LORD, should mark iniquities, Lord, who could stand? But there is forgiveness with you.”

Jesus asks for a righteousness that “exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees”. Not a longer list of rules, but a law written deeper in the heart. “You have heard that it was said… ‘You shall not murder’… But I say to you that if you are angry with a brother or sister, you will be liable to judgment.” Murder begins long before the knife, in contempt, insult and anger nursed in secret. Then Jesus says something astonishing about worship: “So when you are offering your gift at the altar, if you remember that your brother or sister has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift.” God would rather wait for our gift than receive it from a divided heart. On this Lenten Friday, when we abstain from meat, the better fast is to abstain from anger and to go first to the one we have hurt.

Homily outline

Theme: “First be reconciled to your brother or sister.”

The God who wants us to live, the root of anger, and the gift that waits for peace.

1. Turn and live

  • Not trapped by the past.
  • No pleasure in death.
  • Never too late.
“Have I any pleasure in the death of the wicked, says the Lord GOD, and not rather that they should turn from their ways and live?” Ezekiel 18:23
“This is right and is acceptable in the sight of God our Savior, who desires everyone to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.” 1 Timothy 2:3–4

Apply: Pray today for someone you have written off as unable to change.

2. A deeper righteousness

  • More than the scribes.
  • Anger is the root.
  • Insult and contempt.
“He has told you, O mortal, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?” Micah 6:8
“For I tell you, unless your righteousness exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.” Matthew 5:20

Apply: Refuse every insult, even in thought, for one whole day.

3. Leave your gift

  • At the altar you remember.
  • Go first.
  • Then come and offer.
“But there is forgiveness with you, so that you may be revered.” Psalm 130:4
“leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift.” Matthew 5:24

Apply: Visit, call or write to someone with whom you need to be reconciled before Sunday Mass.

No one is beyond the call to turn and live.

God waits for our gift until our hearts are at peace.

Take-home resolution: Before Easter, go first to one person with whom you are not at peace.

Full homily

Dear friends, the exiles said: we are paying for our parents' sins; nothing we do matters. Ezekiel answers with God's word: if the wicked turn away from all their sins… they shall surely live; they shall not die (Ezekiel 18:21).

God asks: have I any pleasure in the death of the wicked… and not rather that they should turn from their ways and live? (Ezekiel 18:23). No one is too far gone. Lent is always a time of hope.

Jesus asks us for a deeper righteousness. Murder begins with anger and insult. So when you are offering your gift at the altar, if you remember that your brother or sister has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled (Matthew 5:23–24).

Today we abstain from meat. Let us also abstain from anger, and go first to someone we have hurt. That is the gift God most wants from us this Friday. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says: if you have hurt someone, go and make peace first. Is there a brother, sister or friend you need to say sorry to today? Go and say it, and God will be very happy!

Prayer

Lord God, you take no pleasure in the death of sinners but desire that they turn and live. Turn our hearts to you this Lent. Uproot from us the anger that kills love, and give us courage to go first to those we have hurt, so that our gifts at the altar may be pleasing to you. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
