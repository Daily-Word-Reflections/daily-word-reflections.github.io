Word for the day

“As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” John 17:21

Reflection

Paul has been arrested in the Temple in Jerusalem, and the Roman tribune, who “wanted to find out what Paul was being accused of,” brings him before the council. Paul looks at the assembly and sees two groups. The Sadducees, the priestly party, held that there is no resurrection, no angel, no spirit; the Pharisees believed in all three. So Paul cries out: “Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. I am on trial concerning the hope of the resurrection of the dead.” This is not a trick. It is the heart of his message: Jesus is risen, and in him the hope of Israel for the resurrection has begun to come true. The council divides, and some scribes of the Pharisees even say, “We find nothing wrong with this man.” Luke is careful: the quarrel is a debate within Israel about Israel's own hope, not a story of one people against another. The meeting ends in uproar, and the soldiers drag Paul to the barracks. Then, in the night, comes the real word of the day: “the Lord stood near him and said, ‘Keep up your courage! For just as you have testified for me in Jerusalem, so you must bear witness also in Rome.’” The Psalm puts that night into a prayer: “I keep the Lord always before me; because he is at my right hand, I shall not be moved.” And it ends with the hope Paul preached: “You show me the path of life. In your presence there is fullness of joy.”

In the Gospel Jesus lifts his eyes beyond the Upper Room, beyond the apostles, to us: “I ask not only on behalf of these, but also on behalf of those who will believe in me through their word, that they may all be one.” Every Christian who has ever believed through the preaching of the apostles was in that prayer. The unity he asks for is not a mere agreement; it is a share in the life of the Holy Trinity: “As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us.” And it has a purpose: “so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” Divided Christians make the Gospel hard to believe; Christians who love one another make it shine. Today the Church also remembers Our Lady of Fatima. On 13 May 1917, during the First World War, three shepherd children of Portugal, Lucia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto, saw a Lady at the Cova da Iria who asked them to pray the Rosary every day for peace and to offer prayers and sacrifices for sinners. The Church approved the apparitions in 1930. On 13 May 1981 St John Paul II was shot in St Peter's Square; he believed (in substance) that a mother's hand had guided the bullet, and a year later he went to Fatima to give thanks. Francisco and Jacinta were canonised there by Pope Francis on 13 May 2017. Mary's message at Fatima is the Gospel's own: prayer, conversion, and the peace that comes when hearts are made one in her Son.

Homily outline

Theme: “That they may all be one.”

The Lord who stands by Paul in the night, the hope of the resurrection, and the prayer of Jesus for us, with Our Lady of Fatima.

1. Keep up your courage

  • Arrested and alone.
  • The Lord stands near.
  • From Jerusalem to Rome.
“I hereby command you: Be strong and courageous; do not be frightened or dismayed, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go.” Joshua 1:9
“That night the Lord stood near him and said, “Keep up your courage! For just as you have testified for me in Jerusalem, so you must bear witness also in Rome.”” Acts 23:11

Apply: Write down the fear that weighs on you most, and each night this week hand it to the Lord with the words: Keep up my courage, Lord.

2. The hope of the resurrection

  • Pharisees and Sadducees.
  • A debate within Israel's hope.
  • The path of life.
“For you do not give me up to Sheol, or let your faithful one see the Pit.” Psalm 16:10
“Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. I am on trial concerning the hope of the resurrection of the dead.” Acts 23:6

Apply: Visit or phone someone who is grieving this week, and share with them the hope of the resurrection.

3. That they may all be one

  • Jesus prays for us.
  • Unity in the life of the Trinity.
  • Mary at Fatima: prayer and peace.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” John 17:21

Apply: Take one step towards reconciliation in your family or parish before Pentecost, and pray a decade of the Rosary for the unity of Christians.

The risen Lord stands near his witnesses in the night and sends them on.

He prayed for us that we may be one, so that the world may believe; Mary at Fatima calls us to the prayer that makes that unity possible.

Take-home resolution: Pray a decade of the Rosary each day until Pentecost for peace and for the unity of Christians.

Full homily

Dear friends, imagine Paul in the barracks of Jerusalem that night. He has been beaten, arrested, shouted at in the council. He is alone. And then the Lord stood near him and said, Keep up your courage! For just as you have testified for me in Jerusalem, so you must bear witness also in Rome (Acts 23:11). The risen Jesus does not take the danger away. He stands beside his servant in it.

Why was Paul on trial? He said it himself: I am on trial concerning the hope of the resurrection of the dead (Acts 23:6). That is our hope too. Death does not have the last word. The Psalm sings it: you show me the path of life; in your presence there is fullness of joy (Psalm 16:11). When we are afraid, we can say with the psalmist: I keep the Lord always before me; I shall not be moved.

In the Gospel, Jesus prays for us by name, so to speak: not only for these, but also for those who will believe in me through their word, that they may all be one (John 17:20–21). You and I were in that prayer on the night before he died. And he asks for a unity like that of the Father and the Son, so that the world may believe. Our quarrels in families and parishes are not small things; they hide Jesus from the world. Our love makes him visible.

Today we also remember Our Lady of Fatima. To three shepherd children in a time of war she asked for simple things: pray the Rosary every day, pray for sinners, pray for peace. Let us do what she asked. As we wait for Pentecost, let us gather with Mary in prayer, so that the Holy Spirit may make us one and give us courage to bear witness, in Jerusalem, in Rome, and wherever the Lord sends us. Amen.

For children

Children, Paul was in big trouble and all alone. But that night Jesus came and stood beside him and said: be brave! Jesus is beside you too when you are afraid. Jesus also prayed that all his friends would be one big loving family. And at Fatima, Mother Mary asked three children to pray the Rosary for peace. Can you say one Hail Mary for peace today?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, risen from the dead, you stood beside Paul in the night and said: Keep up your courage. Stand beside us in our fears and keep us steady, for you are at our right hand. You prayed to the Father for all who would believe through the apostles' word: make us one, as you and the Father are one, so that the world may believe. Through the prayers of Our Lady of Fatima, Mother of mercy, give us hearts converted, faithful in prayer and eager for peace. Come, Holy Spirit. Amen.
