Word for the day

“Jesus said to him, “If it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? Follow me!”” John 21:22

Reflection

For fifty days the Church has read the Acts of the Apostles, from the Ascension at Jerusalem to the shipwreck at Malta. Today we reach the last page, and it ends in Rome. Paul has arrived as a prisoner, but a strange kind of prisoner: “Paul was allowed to live by himself, with the soldier who was guarding him.” Three days later he calls together the leaders of the Jewish community of Rome. He speaks to them as “brothers,” and he insists that he has “done nothing against our people or the customs of our ancestors,” and that he has “no charge to bring against my nation.” Then he says why he wears a chain: “it is for the sake of the hope of Israel that I am bound with this chain.” Paul never stopped being a son of Israel; the risen Messiah is, for him, the fulfilment of Israel's own hope, not its rejection. And the book ends with a picture rather than a conclusion: “He lived there two whole years at his own expense and welcomed all who came to him, proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness and without hindrance.” Luke does not tell us how Paul's trial ended, or how he died. The last word of Acts in Greek is akōlytōs, “without hindrance.” The apostle is in chains, but the word of God is not. The story of the Church is left open, for us to continue. The Psalm gives the reason for that boldness: “The Lord is in his holy temple; the Lord's throne is in heaven,” and “the upright shall behold his face.”

The Gospel of John ends in the same open way. On the shore of the lake the risen Jesus has just asked Peter three times, “Do you love me?” and told him how he would one day glorify God by his death. Peter turns, sees the beloved disciple following them, and asks a very human question: “Lord, what about him?” Jesus answers gently but firmly: “If it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? Follow me!” Each disciple has his own road. Peter will die a martyr; John, tradition tells us, will live long and die in peace. Comparison is a waste of time; what matters is following. Then the evangelist steps forward: “This is the disciple who is testifying to these things and has written them, and we know that his testimony is true.” The Gospel rests on the witness of one who reclined next to Jesus at the supper and stood at the foot of the cross with Mary. And it ends with a smile: “if every one of them were written down, I suppose that the world itself could not contain the books that would be written.” Tonight, at the Vigil of Pentecost, the Church will hear Jesus cry out: “Let anyone who is thirsty come to me.” The last weekday of Easter ends where the novena began, in the upper room, waiting with Mary for the gift that will write the rest of the story in us.

Homily outline

Theme: “What is that to you? Follow me!”

The last page of Acts and the last page of John: an apostle unchained in spirit, and a disciple called to follow on his own road.

1. For the hope of Israel

  • A prisoner in Rome.
  • Brothers, not enemies.
  • The Messiah, fulfilment of Israel's hope.
“For surely I know the plans I have for you, says the Lord, plans for your welfare and not for harm, to give you a future with hope.” Jeremiah 29:11
“For this reason therefore I have asked to see you and speak with you, since it is for the sake of the hope of Israel that I am bound with this chain.” Acts 28:20

Apply: Pray this week for the Jewish people and for good relations between Christians and Jews, remembering that our hope was first theirs.

2. Without hindrance

  • Two whole years in a rented house.
  • Welcoming all who came.
  • The word that cannot be chained.
“So shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty, but it shall accomplish that which I purpose, and succeed in the thing for which I sent it.” Isaiah 55:11
“proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness and without hindrance.” Acts 28:31

Apply: Welcome one person into your home or your time this week, and let them see your faith.

3. What is that to you? Follow me!

  • Lord, what about him?
  • Each disciple's own road.
  • A true witness who has written.
“Where you go, I will go; where you lodge, I will lodge; your people shall be my people, and your God my God.” Ruth 1:16
“Jesus said to him, “If it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? Follow me!”” John 21:22

Apply: Name one person you often compare yourself with, and pray for them by name each day until next Sunday.

Acts and John both end with an open door: the story of Christ's witnesses is not finished.

The Spirit who comes at Pentecost will write the next lines in us, if we follow Jesus on our own road and bear true witness.

Take-home resolution: Join the Vigil of Pentecost or pray tonight for the gifts of the Holy Spirit, and stop comparing; follow.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we come to the last page of two books. Since Easter we have been reading the Acts of the Apostles and the Gospel of John. Both end today, and both end in a surprising way: not with a full stop, but with an open door.

Acts ends in Rome. Paul is a prisoner, chained to a soldier, but he is not silent. He calls the Jewish leaders of the city his brothers, and he tells them: it is for the sake of the hope of Israel that I am bound with this chain (Acts 28:20). Then for two whole years he welcomed all who came to him, proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness and without hindrance (Acts 28:31). We do not hear how his trial ended. Luke leaves the story open, because it is not finished. We are the next chapter.

John's Gospel ends on the shore of the lake. Peter has just been told how he would die, and he looks at John and asks: Lord, what about him? How often we do the same! Why does he have it easier? Why did she get that gift and not me? Jesus answers: what is that to you? Follow me! (John 21:22). Each of us has a road. Our task is not to measure the roads of others, but to walk our own behind Jesus.

Tonight, at the Vigil of Pentecost, the Church will pray with the apostles and with Mary for the gift of the Holy Spirit. Let us bring to that prayer our chains and our comparisons. The Spirit who made Paul bold in chains and made John a true witness will make us bold and true as well. The world could not contain all the books about Jesus; let the Holy Spirit write a few more lines in our lives. Come, Holy Spirit. Amen.

For children

Children, today two books in the Bible come to their last page. In the first one, St Paul is a prisoner in Rome, but he still tells everyone about Jesus. In the second, Peter asks Jesus: what about John? And Jesus says: don't worry about him, you follow me! Jesus has a special path just for you. Tomorrow is Pentecost, the birthday of the Church! Let us say: Come, Holy Spirit!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you told Peter: What is that to you? Follow me! Deliver us from comparing ourselves with others, and give us the grace to follow you on the road you have chosen for each of us. Like Paul in Rome, let us proclaim your kingdom with boldness, even when we are bound; like the beloved disciple, let us bear true witness to what we have seen of your love. As Easter draws to its close, we wait with Mary and the apostles: send us your Holy Spirit, the Advocate, the giver of life. Come, Holy Spirit. Amen.
