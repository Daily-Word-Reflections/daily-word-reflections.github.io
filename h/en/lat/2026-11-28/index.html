Word for the day

“Be alert at all times, praying that you may have the strength to escape all these things that will take place, and to stand before the Son of Man.” Luke 21:36

Reflection

The liturgical year ends with the last page of the Bible. The angel shows John “the river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb”, and the tree of life whose leaves are “for the healing of the nations”. The tree lost in Eden is given back. “There will be no more night… for the Lord God will be their light.” And the final promise: “See, I am coming soon!”

Jesus' last words in Luke's discourse are practical: guard your hearts from being “weighed down with dissipation and drunkenness and the worries of this life”. Be alert, praying. These are perfect words for the threshold of Advent. Tonight the Church begins a new year with the prayer of the first Christians: Marana tha, “Come, Lord Jesus!”

Homily outline

Theme: “Come, Lord Jesus.”

The year ends with the river of life and the tree of healing; be alert in prayer; Advent begins.

1. The river and the tree

  • The river flows from the throne.
  • The tree of life returns.
  • Its leaves heal the nations.
“On the banks, on both sides of the river, there will grow all kinds of trees for food. Their leaves will not wither nor their fruit fail, but they will bear fresh fruit every month, because the water for them flows from the sanctuary. Their fruit will be for food, and their leaves for healing.” Ezekiel 47:12
“Then the angel showed me the river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb through the middle of the street of the city. On either side of the river is the tree of life with its twelve kinds of fruit, producing its fruit each month; and the leaves of the tree are for the healing of the nations.” Revelation 22:1–2

Apply: Thank God for one healing grace this year.

2. Be alert, praying

  • Guard against heaviness of heart.
  • Worries can crowd out God.
  • Prayer gives strength to stand.
“O come, let us worship and bow down, let us kneel before the LORD, our Maker! For he is our God, and we are the people of his pasture, and the sheep of his hand. O that today you would listen to his voice!” Psalm 95:6–7
“Be on guard so that your hearts are not weighed down with dissipation and drunkenness and the worries of this life, and that day does not catch you unexpectedly, like a trap. For it will come upon all who live on the face of the whole earth. Be alert at all times, praying that you may have the strength to escape all these things that will take place, and to stand before the Son of Man.” Luke 21:34–36

Apply: Choose one Advent fast.

3. No more night

  • God himself will be the light.
  • The promise: I am coming soon.
  • Advent begins tonight.
“The sun shall no longer be your light by day, nor for brightness shall the moon give light to you by night; but the LORD will be your everlasting light, and your God will be your glory.” Isaiah 60:19
“And there will be no more night; they need no light of lamp or sun, for the Lord God will be their light, and they will reign forever and ever.” Revelation 22:5

Apply: Light the first Advent candle tomorrow with your family.

The year closes in light.

Marana tha: Come, Lord Jesus!

Take-home resolution: Plan your family's Advent prayer tonight.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Church's year ends today with the last page of the Bible. The angel shows John the river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb, and the tree of life whose leaves are for the healing of the nations (Revelation 22:1–2). The tree lost in Eden is given back.

There will be no more night; they need no light of lamp or sun, for the Lord God will be their light (Revelation 22:5). And the last promise: see, I am coming soon (Revelation 22:7). The whole of history moves toward that light.

Jesus' last words in Luke's discourse are practical: be on guard so that your hearts are not weighed down with dissipation and drunkenness and the worries of this life. Be alert at all times, praying (Luke 21:34–36). These are perfect words for the threshold of Advent.

This evening, with First Vespers, the Church begins a new year, praying the prayer of the first Christians: Marana tha, Come, Lord Jesus. Prepare an Advent wreath for your home today, and choose one Advent fast to lighten your heart. Amen.

For children

Children, tomorrow Advent begins! Help your family prepare an Advent wreath and light the first candle together, saying: Come, Lord Jesus!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you are the light that never sets. Keep my heart free from heaviness and worry, make me alert in prayer, and as a new year of grace begins, teach me to pray with your Church: Marana tha, come, Lord Jesus! Amen.
