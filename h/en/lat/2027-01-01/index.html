Word for the day

“But Mary treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart.” Luke 2:19

Reflection

The Church begins the civil year not with fireworks but with a mother. On the eighth day of Christmas she proclaims the oldest Marian title of all: Mary is Theotokos, Mother of God. The Council of Ephesus (431) defended this title against those who wished to call her only the mother of the man Jesus. The Church answered: the child she bore is one Person, the eternal Son, true God and true man; so she is truly the Mother of God (CCC 495). To honour Mary is to confess who Jesus is.

St Paul gives the shortest Christmas creed in the New Testament: “when the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the law.” God's eternity enters time through the yes of one woman. And the purpose is our adoption: “so that we might receive adoption as children.” Because of Mary's Son, the Spirit cries in our hearts, “Abba! Father!” The first reading puts the priestly blessing of Aaron on our lips for the new year: “The LORD bless you and keep you.” In Christ that blessing has a face.

In the Gospel the shepherds find “Mary and Joseph, and the child lying in the manger.” Everyone is amazed, “But Mary treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart.” On the eighth day the child is circumcised and receives the name Jesus, “the name given by the angel”. The year opens with a Name that means “the Lord saves”, and with a mother who keeps God's deeds in her heart. St Paul VI made this day the World Day of Peace; Mary, Queen of Peace, teaches us to receive the Prince of Peace.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, a happy and holy new year to you all. The Church begins the year by looking at a mother and her child. On the eighth day of Christmas we celebrate Mary, the Holy Mother of God.

Why such a great title? Because of who her Son is. The child she bore is not a man in whom God lived for a while; he is the eternal Son of God who became man in her womb. One Person, truly God and truly man. That is why the Council of Ephesus in 431 declared that Mary is Theotokos, Mother of God. When we honour Mary, we are really confessing our faith in Jesus.

St Paul says that when the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman (Galatians 4:4). Time reached its fullness not in an empire or a palace but in a young woman of Nazareth who said yes. And the purpose of it all is that we might receive adoption as children. Because Mary's Son became our brother, we can call God Abba, Father.

In the Gospel the shepherds hurry to Bethlehem and tell everyone what the angels had said. All who heard were amazed. But Mary treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart (Luke 2:19). Amazement passes; pondering stays. Mary teaches us not to rush from one event to the next, but to keep God's deeds in our heart and seek their meaning.

On the eighth day the child receives his name: Jesus, the Lord saves. The first reading gives us the blessing that God told Aaron to place on the people: the Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make his face shine upon you; the Lord give you peace (Numbers 6:24–26). In Jesus, God's face has shone upon us; in Jesus, God's peace has a name.

So let us begin this year with Mary. Let us ponder each day what God is doing. Let us call on the holy name of Jesus. And let us be people of peace, for today is also the World Day of Peace. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us this year. Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, a blessed new year to all of you. On the eighth day after his birth the Child was circumcised and was called Jesus, the name given by the angel before he was conceived (Luke 2:21). The name means the Lord saves. God chose it himself.

In the Bible a name is a mission. The angel told Joseph: you are to name him Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins (Matthew 1:21). Every time we say the name of Jesus, we confess what he came to do.

Paul tells us that because Jesus humbled himself to the cross, God gave him the name that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend (Philippians 2:9–10). That is why we bow our heads at his name.

Peter, standing before the council, said: there is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven by which we must be saved (Acts 4:12). We do not know what this year will bring. But we know the name in which we will face it.

So let us begin the year as the Church begins it: in the name of Jesus. Let it be the first word on our lips in the morning and the last at night. And may the Lord bless us and keep us in his name throughout this year. Amen.

Dear friends, today the Church greets Mary with her greatest title: Mother of God. In the year 431 the bishops gathered at Ephesus defended this title, because the Child she bore is not a man who later became God, but the eternal Son of God made man. Honouring Mary protects the truth about Jesus.

Paul says: when the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman (Galatians 4:4). God did not come among us from nowhere. He came through a mother, through a family, through a people.

When the shepherds told what they had seen, Mary treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart (Luke 2:19). She did not understand everything, but she kept it and turned it over in prayer. That is a good way to live a year.

Paul adds: because you are children, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, Abba, Father (Galatians 4:6). Through Mary's Son we become children of God.

So let us begin the year with her. Let us ask Mary, Mother of God, to keep our families, and let us learn from her to ponder each day what God has done. And may the Lord bless us and keep us, and give us peace. Amen.

Who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen.
