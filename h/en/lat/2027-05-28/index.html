Word for the day

“Whenever you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against anyone; so that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses.” Mark 11:25

Reflection

Ben Sira now begins the longest section of his book, a hymn in praise of the ancestors of Israel, from Enoch and Noah to the high priest Simon of his own day. He opens with a famous line: “Let us now sing the praises of famous men, our ancestors in their generations.” He names kings, counsellors, prophets, musicians and wise teachers. But then he does something tender. He remembers those whom nobody remembers: “But of others there is no memory; they have perished as though they had never existed; they have become as though they had never been born.” And he insists that they too count before God: “But these also were godly men, whose righteous deeds have not been forgotten; their wealth will remain with their descendants, and their inheritance with their children's children.” Their goodness lives on in their families: “Their descendants stand by the covenants; their children also, for their sake. Their offspring will continue forever, and their glory will never be blotted out.” Every family in our parishes has such people: a grandmother who taught the rosary, a father who worked hard and never cheated, a teacher who lived the faith quietly. No book records their names, but their goodness is not forgotten, and we are living on their inheritance. The Psalm sings with joy: “the LORD takes pleasure in his people; he adorns the humble with victory.”

The Gospel is one of the most dramatic in St Mark. Jesus enters Jerusalem and the Temple, looks around at everything, and leaves for Bethany. The next morning he sees a fig tree “in leaf,” goes to look for fruit and finds “nothing but leaves, for it was not the season for figs.” He says, “May no one ever eat fruit from you again.” Then he enters the Temple and drives out the sellers and buyers, overturns the tables of the money changers and quotes two prophets: “Is it not written, ‘My house shall be called a house of prayer for all the nations’? But you have made it a den of robbers.” The next morning the disciples see the fig tree “withered away to its roots.” Mark places the cleansing of the Temple between the two halves of the fig tree story, like a filling in a sandwich, so that each explains the other. Jesus is acting like the prophets of old, who used signs to wake people up. A tree full of leaves but without fruit is a picture of worship full of activity but without prayer, justice and faith. This is not a judgment on the Jewish people; Jesus loved the Temple, called it “my house,” and the Church teaches that he honoured it (Catechism 584). It is a warning to every place of worship and every heart, including our own parishes. Then Jesus tells the disciples what real fruit looks like: “Have faith in God.” Faith that moves mountains, prayer that trusts, and above all forgiveness: “Whenever you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against anyone; so that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses.” A house of prayer is a house of forgiveness.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whenever you stand praying, forgive.”

The forgotten godly, a fig tree with leaves only, and a house of prayer and forgiveness.

1. Not forgotten

  • Famous men and forgotten ones.
  • Goodness that lives in children.
  • Our inheritance of faith.
“But these also were godly men, whose righteous deeds have not been forgotten;” Sirach 44:10
“Therefore, since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us also lay aside every weight and the sin that clings so closely, and let us run with perseverance the race that is set before us,” Hebrews 12:1

Apply: Visit or call an elderly relative this week and thank them for the faith they handed on.

2. Leaves without fruit

  • A tree that looks good from afar.
  • Tables overturned.
  • A warning for every heart.
“They are like trees planted by streams of water, which yield their fruit in its season, and their leaves do not wither. In all that they do, they prosper.” Psalm 1:3
“My Father is glorified by this, that you bear much fruit and become my disciples.” John 15:8

Apply: Choose one religious practice you do out of habit and do it this week with your whole heart.

3. A house of prayer and forgiveness

  • For all the nations.
  • Have faith in God.
  • Forgive when you pray.
“for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples.” Isaiah 56:7
“Whenever you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against anyone; so that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses.” Mark 11:25

Apply: Before Sunday Mass, be reconciled with one person, by a word, a call or at least a prayer for them.

God does not forget the quiet goodness of those whom history forgets.

Jesus looks for fruit in his house: faith in God, true prayer and a forgiving heart.

Take-home resolution: Each evening this week, forgive before you pray.

Full homily

Dear friends, Ben Sira praises the great heroes of Israel's history. But he also remembers the forgotten ones: of others there is no memory. And he says: these also were godly men, whose righteous deeds have not been forgotten (Sirach 44:9–10). God does not forget. The grandmother who prayed the rosary every evening, the father who worked honestly all his life: they are not in the history books, but they are in God's book, and their goodness lives in us.

In the Gospel Jesus is hungry and sees a fig tree full of leaves. From a distance it looks wonderful. But when he comes close he finds nothing but leaves (Mark 11:13). Then he goes into the Temple and finds it busy with buying and selling. He drives out the traders and says: my house shall be called a house of prayer for all the nations (Mark 11:17). The fig tree is a picture: much activity, many leaves, but no fruit.

This is a warning for us, not for others. A parish can be very busy, with feasts, programmes, collections and meetings, and still have little real prayer. A Christian can say many prayers and still have a hard heart. Jesus is looking for fruit. And he tells us what fruit he wants: have faith in God (Mark 11:22). Trust that he hears. Pray with confidence.

And then he says the most important thing: whenever you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against anyone; so that your Father in heaven may also forgive you (Mark 11:25). The best fruit of prayer is a forgiving heart. Today let us thank God for the forgotten saints in our families, and let us bring to him one person we need to forgive. Then our hearts will be a house of prayer. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus once saw a tree with lots of green leaves, but when he looked for fruit, there was none! Then he went to God's house, the Temple, and people were busy buying and selling instead of praying. Jesus said: God's house is for praying! He also said: when you pray, forgive anyone who has hurt you. Tonight before you pray, forgive your brother, your sister or your friend. That is a beautiful fruit for Jesus!

Prayer

Lord God, you take delight in your people and you do not forget the righteous deeds of those whom the world forgets. We thank you for the godly men and women who handed on the faith to us. Lord Jesus, you came to your Father's house looking for fruit; do not find our hearts full of leaves only. Make our churches houses of prayer for all the nations, strengthen our faith in God, and teach us to forgive whenever we stand to pray, so that our Father in heaven may forgive us. Amen.
