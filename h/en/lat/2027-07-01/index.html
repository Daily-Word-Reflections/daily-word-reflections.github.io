Word for the day

“When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, “Take heart, son; your sins are forgiven.”” Matthew 9:2

Reflection

Today's first reading is one of the most demanding chapters of the Bible. “After these things God tested Abraham.” The old man has waited twenty-five years for Isaac, has sent Ishmael away at God's command, and now hears the voice again: “Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering.” Notice how slowly the command tightens: your son, your only son, Isaac, whom you love. Each word presses on the father's heart. The surrounding peoples did sacrifice their children, and the Bible tells this story to show the opposite: the God of Abraham does not want the death of a child. The angel stops the knife, and Israel will never offer her sons. Yet the test is real, and Abraham's answer is a model of faith. He rises early in the morning. He walks for three days. He says to his servants, “we will worship, and then we will come back to you.” He believes, as the Letter to the Hebrews explains, that “God is able even to raise someone from the dead” (Hebrews 11:19). The most moving words are Isaac's: “The fire and the wood are here, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?” and Abraham's reply: “God himself will provide the lamb for a burnt offering, my son.” The Hebrew name he gives the mountain, Yahweh-yireh, means “The Lord will provide.” The Church has always heard in this story a shadow of the Father who “did not withhold his own Son, but gave him up for all of us” (Romans 8:32): the beloved son who carries the wood, the mountain in the land of Moriah, the three days, the lamb that God himself provides. Our Jewish elder brothers and sisters read this chapter, the Akedah, with deep reverence at the New Year, and we honour their reading as we offer ours. The Psalm gives the right answer to a test passed: “Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to your name give glory.”

Matthew's Gospel now brings us home to Capernaum, “his own town.” Some people carry a paralysed man to Jesus on a bed, and the first thing Matthew tells us is that Jesus sees something: “When Jesus saw their faith.” The faith of the friends carries the sick man, and Jesus answers with a sentence that nobody asked for: “Take heart, son; your sins are forgiven.” The man had come for his legs; Jesus first heals his heart. Some scribes think, “This man is blaspheming,” and from their own point of view they are right: only God can forgive sins. Jesus does not deny it. He reads their thoughts and asks which is easier, to say “Your sins are forgiven” or “Stand up and walk.” Anyone can say the first, since no one can check it; the second can be seen. So he proves the invisible by the visible: “so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins,” he says to the paralytic, “Stand up, take your bed and go to your home.” And the man goes home carrying the bed that carried him. The crowds “glorified God, who had given such authority to human beings.” The last words are in the plural, and the Church has always understood why: on Easter evening Jesus breathed on his apostles and said, “If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them” (John 20:23). This is why we confess our sins to a priest: the one who forgave the paralytic in Capernaum forgives us through the ministry of his Church (Catechism 1441–1442). And the lamb that God provided on Moriah is the One of whom John the Baptist said, “Here is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!”

Homily outline

Theme: “God himself will provide the lamb.”

The test on Moriah, the Lamb whom God gives, and the friends whose faith carries the paralysed man to the One who forgives.

1. The test on Moriah

  • Your son, your only son, whom you love.
  • Three days of walking in faith.
  • God is able to raise the dead.
“Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering and laid it on his son Isaac, and he himself carried the fire and the knife. So the two of them walked on together.” Genesis 22:6
“By faith Abraham, when put to the test, offered up Isaac.” Hebrews 11:17

Apply: Name one thing God asks of you that you do not understand, and say to him this week, “Here I am.”

2. The Lord will provide

  • Isaac's question.
  • Abraham's answer.
  • The Lamb of God.
“Abraham said, “God himself will provide the lamb for a burnt offering, my son.” So the two of them walked on together.” Genesis 22:8
“The next day he saw Jesus coming toward him and declared, “Here is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” John 1:29

Apply: At the Lamb of God at Mass, say in your heart the name of one person you wish to entrust to him.

3. Your sins are forgiven

  • When Jesus saw their faith.
  • The heart before the legs.
  • Authority given to human beings.
“who forgives all your iniquity, who heals all your diseases,” Psalm 103:3
“When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, “Take heart, son; your sins are forgiven.”” Matthew 9:2

Apply: Bring someone to Jesus this week: by prayer, an invitation to Mass, or a lift to confession.

God tests Abraham, stops the knife, and promises that in his offspring all nations will be blessed; the Father later gives his own Son as the Lamb.

Jesus forgives the paralysed man because of his friends' faith, and shows that the Son of Man has authority to forgive sins.

Take-home resolution: Go to confession this month, and carry one person to God in prayer every day this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the first reading takes us up the mountain with Abraham and Isaac. It is a hard story, and we should not hurry past it. God asks Abraham for his son, his only son, whom he loves. Abraham does not argue. He rises early. He walks for three days. And on the way a boy asks the question every child would ask: where is the lamb? And the father who has no answer except trust says: God himself will provide the lamb, my son (Genesis 22:8).

At the last moment, the angel calls: Abraham, Abraham! Do not lay your hand on the boy (Genesis 22:12). The God of Abraham does not want children sacrificed. He wanted Abraham's heart, and Abraham gave it. And the story does not end with a test passed. It ends with a promise: in your offspring all the nations of the earth will gain blessing (Genesis 22:18). That offspring is Christ. Centuries later another Father would not withhold his own Son, but gave him up for all of us (Romans 8:32), and John would point to Jesus and say: Here is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29). God did provide the lamb.

In the Gospel the lamb is already at work, in a small town by the lake. Friends bring a paralysed man, and Matthew says: when Jesus saw their faith (Matthew 9:2). Think of that. The friends say nothing in the story, and yet Jesus hears them. Their faith carried a man who could not walk. Who in your life has carried you? A mother, a grandmother, a teacher, a friend who prayed? And whom are you carrying?

Then Jesus says the unexpected words: Take heart, son; your sins are forgiven (Matthew 9:2). The paralytic's real burden was not his legs. Jesus heals the heart first, and then, to show that he has the right, he heals the body. And the crowds glorify God who had given such authority to human beings. Dear friends, that is the sacrament of Reconciliation. This month, the month of the Precious Blood, let us make the time. Let the Lamb of God lift the burden off our hearts, and then let us go home, like the paralytic, carrying the bed that carried us.

For children

Children, Abraham loved his son Isaac. One day God asked Abraham to trust him in a very hard way. Abraham got up early and walked to a mountain. Isaac asked: where is the lamb? Abraham said: God will provide the lamb. And God did! He stopped Abraham and gave a ram. God never wants a child to be hurt, and he always keeps his promise. In the Gospel, some friends carried a man who could not walk to Jesus. Jesus said: your sins are forgiven, and he made the man walk! Let us be good friends who help carry others.

Prayer

God of Abraham, you asked everything of your friend and you gave everything back to him. Give us a faith that trusts you on the mountain we do not understand, and teach us that you yourself provide the lamb. Lord Jesus, Lamb of God, who forgave the paralysed man and gave your Church the power to forgive, heal our hearts before our bodies, and make us friends who carry one another to you. Through your Precious Blood, wash away our sins and bring us home. Amen.
