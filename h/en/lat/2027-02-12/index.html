Word for the day

“Is not this the fast that I choose: to loose the bonds of injustice, to undo the thongs of the yoke, to let the oppressed go free, and to break every yoke?” Isaiah 58:6

Reflection

Isaiah is told to shout like a trumpet at a people who seem very religious. They seek God daily, they fast, they bow their heads, and they complain: “Why do we fast, but you do not see? Why humble ourselves, but you do not notice?” God's answer is blunt: “Look, you serve your own interest on your fast day, and oppress all your workers.” They fast and quarrel; they bow like a bulrush and lie on sackcloth, yet nothing changes in their treatment of others. Then the Lord describes the fast he wants: to loose the bonds of injustice, to let the oppressed go free, “to share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house; when you see the naked, to cover them, and not to hide yourself from your own kin.” Fasting that does not lead to justice and mercy is an empty ritual.

Then comes the promise: “Then your light shall break forth like the dawn, and your healing shall spring up quickly… Then you shall call, and the LORD will answer; you shall cry for help, and he will say, Here I am.” In the Gospel the disciples of John ask why Jesus' disciples do not fast. Jesus answers with a wedding image: “The wedding guests cannot mourn as long as the bridegroom is with them, can they? The days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast.” Jesus is the Bridegroom of Israel, the God who wedded his people. Christian fasting is not gloomy self-punishment; it is the longing of the bride for the Bridegroom, whom we await, and a love that makes room for the poor. On this first Friday of Lent we abstain from meat and remember the day the Bridegroom was taken away on the cross.

Homily outline

Theme: “Is not this the fast that I choose?”

The fast God wants: justice, mercy and longing for the Bridegroom.

1. Fasting God does not see

  • Daily seeking, no change.
  • Serving our own interest.
  • Oppressing workers.
“Look, you fast only to quarrel and to fight and to strike with a wicked fist. Such fasting as you do today will not make your voice heard on high.” Isaiah 58:4
“Those who say, “I love God,” and hate their brothers or sisters, are liars;” 1 John 4:20

Apply: Examine how you treat those who work for you or under you.

2. The fast God chooses

  • Loose the bonds.
  • Share your bread.
  • Do not hide from your own kin.
“Is it not to share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house; when you see the naked, to cover them, and not to hide yourself from your own kin?” Isaiah 58:7
“for I was hungry and you gave me food, I was thirsty and you gave me something to drink, I was a stranger and you welcomed me,” Matthew 25:35

Apply: Give what you save by fasting to the poor this week.

3. The Bridegroom

  • Wedding guests do not mourn.
  • The Bridegroom taken away.
  • Fasting as longing.
“And I will take you for my wife forever; I will take you for my wife in righteousness and in justice, in steadfast love, and in mercy.” Hosea 2:19
“And Jesus said to them, “The wedding guests cannot mourn as long as the bridegroom is with them, can they? The days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast.” Matthew 9:15

Apply: When hunger comes today, turn it into a short prayer: Come, Lord Jesus.

True fasting breaks yokes and shares bread.

We fast in love for the Bridegroom and his poor.

Take-home resolution: Keep the Fridays of Lent with abstinence and one act of sharing.

Full homily

Dear friends, God tells Isaiah: shout out, do not hold back! The people fast and complain that God does not notice. God replies: you serve your own interest on your fast day, and oppress all your workers (Isaiah 58:3).

Then God describes the fast he chooses: to loose the bonds of injustice, to let the oppressed go free, to share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house (Isaiah 58:6–7). Fasting without mercy is empty.

And the promise: then your light shall break forth like the dawn; you shall call, and the LORD will answer, here I am (Isaiah 58:8–9). In the Gospel Jesus says his disciples will fast when the bridegroom is taken away (Matthew 9:15). We fast because we long for him.

On this first Friday of Lent, let our abstinence be love: for Jesus the Bridegroom, who was taken away on the cross, and for the poor in whom he waits for us. Amen.

For children

Children, God says the best kind of fasting is sharing. If we give up a sweet this Lent, let us use it to help someone who is hungry!

Prayer

Lord God, you do not want empty fasting but hearts that set the oppressed free and share bread with the hungry. Make our Lenten fast a work of justice and love, a longing for your Son, the Bridegroom, and a light that breaks forth like the dawn. Amen.
