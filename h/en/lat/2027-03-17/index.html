Word for the day

“Then Jesus said to the Jews who had believed in him, “If you continue in my word, you are truly my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will make you free.”” John 8:31–32

Reflection

King Nebuchadnezzar has set up a golden statue, and everyone must fall down before it when the music plays. Three young Jews, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, refuse. Brought before the furious king, they give an answer that has strengthened believers ever since: “If our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the furnace of blazing fire and out of your hand, O king, let him deliver us. But if not, be it known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods.” But if not: their faithfulness does not depend on being rescued. The furnace is heated seven times hotter and they are thrown in bound. Then the king leaps up in amazement: “I see four men unbound, walking in the middle of the fire, and they are not hurt; and the fourth has the appearance of a god.” The Fathers saw in the fourth figure a foreshadowing of the Son of God, who comes into our fire. Instead of the usual Psalm, the Church today sings part of the hymn of the three young men from the Greek text of Daniel: “Blessed are you, O Lord, God of our ancestors, and to be praised and highly exalted forever.”

In the Gospel Jesus speaks of another kind of freedom: “If you continue in my word, you are truly my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will make you free.” His hearers protest that as children of Abraham they have never been slaves. Jesus answers: “everyone who commits sin is a slave to sin… So if the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed.” The debate in this chapter is sharp, and it reflects the painful arguments between the early Christians and the synagogue of John's day; it is never a licence for contempt of the Jewish people, from whom, as Jesus says elsewhere, salvation comes (John 4:22). Today the Church remembers St Patrick (about 385 to about 461). Captured as a boy in Britain and enslaved in Ireland, he learned to pray on the hillsides while tending sheep. He escaped, became a priest and a bishop, and went back freely to the land of his slavery to bring it the Gospel. In his Confession he writes (in substance) that he was like a stone lying deep in the mud until the Mighty One lifted him up. Patrick knew both kinds of slavery and both kinds of freedom. Like the three young men, he served God whether or not he was spared, and the truth made him free.

Theme: “If the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed.”

“But Peter and the apostles answered, “We must obey God rather than any human authority.” Acts 5:29

Full homily

Dear friends, three young men stand before a furious king. Bow to the golden statue, or burn. They answer: if our God is able to deliver us, let him deliver us. But if not, we will not serve your gods (Daniel 3:17–18). Their faith does not depend on a rescue. They will be faithful either way.

They are thrown into the furnace, and the king sees four men walking in the fire, unbound and unharmed, and the fourth looks like a son of the gods. God does not always keep us out of the fire. He comes into it with us.

Jesus speaks of freedom: if you continue in my word, you will know the truth, and the truth will make you free. Everyone who commits sin is a slave to sin. So if the Son makes you free, you will be free indeed (John 8:31–36). The worst slavery is not chains on the hands, but sin in the heart.

St Patrick knew both. Enslaved as a boy in Ireland, he found God while tending sheep in the cold. Freed, he chose to return as a bishop to the land of his slavery, to make its people free in Christ. Let us ask today for faith that does not bargain and for the freedom only the Son can give. Amen.

For children

Children, three young men would not bow down to a golden statue, because they loved God. They were thrown into a fire, but God was with them, and they came out without even the smell of smoke! St Patrick was a slave as a boy, but he became a bishop and taught Ireland about Jesus. Say today: Christ with me, Christ before me, Christ behind me!

Prayer

Lord God of our ancestors, blessed are you and highly exalted for ever. Give us the faith of the three young men, who served you whether or not they were rescued, and walk with us in every fire. Lord Jesus, make us free from the slavery of sin. Through the prayers of St Patrick, who returned in love to the land of his captivity, make us bold witnesses of the Gospel. Amen.
