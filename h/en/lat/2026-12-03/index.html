Word for the day

“Everyone then who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock.” Matthew 7:24

Reflection

Jesus ends the Sermon on the Mount with a warning and a picture. Not everyone who says “Lord, Lord” will enter the Kingdom, but the one who does the Father's will. Two houses face the same rain, floods and wind; one stands because it is founded on rock. Isaiah gives the foundation's name: “Trust in the LORD forever, for in the LORD GOD you have an everlasting rock.” Advent is a time to check the foundations.

St Francis Xavier (1506–1552), Jesuit, one of the first companions of St Ignatius, arrived in Goa in 1542 and preached along the coasts of South India, among the Paravas of the Fishery Coast, in Travancore, then in Malacca, the Moluccas and Japan. He died on the island of Shangchuan, within sight of China. His body rests in the Basilica of Bom Jesus in Goa. He built on rock: “Lord, I am here; what do you want me to do?”

Full homily

Dear friends, today we celebrate St Francis Xavier, patron of the missions, who preached along the coasts of South India in the sixteenth century. The Advent readings speak of building on solid ground.

Isaiah sings of a strong city with walls and ramparts: trust in the Lord for ever, for in the Lord God you have an everlasting rock (Isaiah 26:4). The proud city falls, but the poor and the needy walk securely on the rock.

Jesus ends the Sermon on the Mount with a parable. Not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father. The one who hears my words and acts on them is like a wise man who built his house on rock. The rain fell, the floods came, the winds blew, but the house did not fall (Matthew 7:21, 24–25).

Every Kerala family knows what floods can do. In 2018 we saw houses on weak foundations collapse, while others stood. Jesus says the difference is not in hearing but in doing. Many people hear the Gospel; fewer build their lives on it.

Francis Xavier built on the rock. He heard the words of Jesus, what does it profit a man to gain the whole world and lose his soul, and he acted on them. He left a brilliant career in Paris, and spent himself for the poor fishermen of the Coromandel and Travancore coasts, teaching them the faith in their own language with songs and prayers.

This Advent, let us check our foundations. Is my faith only words, Lord, Lord, or is it action? Let us choose one word of Jesus and put it into practice this week. And let us pray for missionaries, that like St Francis Xavier they may build the Church on the rock of Christ. Amen.

For children

Children, a house on rock does not fall in a storm. When you obey Jesus, you are building on rock. St Francis Xavier did that!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, everlasting Rock, let me not only call you “Lord” but do your will. Build my life on your word, and through the prayers of St Francis Xavier, make me a missionary wherever I am. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Isaiah sings of a strong city whose walls are God's own saving work, and he gives the reason it stands: “in the Lord God you have an everlasting rock.” Jesus ends the Sermon on the Mount with two houses in one storm, and the rock now has a practical meaning: hearing his words and doing them. Advent is a good time to look at the foundations.

Isaiah 26:1–6 · The everlasting rock

Setting.

This song belongs to the same section as yesterday's feast (Isaiah 24–27). Two cities stand side by side there: the proud city that falls and the city of those who trust. Ancient people felt safe behind high walls. The song turns this upside down: the height falls, and the poor walk over it.

Structure.

  • vv. 1–2: the strong city and its open gates.
  • vv. 3–4: trust in the Lord, the rock.
  • vv. 5–6: the lofty city laid low; the poor walk on it.

Key words.

  • ʿîr ʿōz, strong city: A city with strength, but its walls here are God's “victory” or salvation (v. 1). Its security does not come from stone.
  • bāṭaḥ, to trust: To lean on someone and feel safe. The word appears in both v. 3 and v. 4: trust is the only foundation the poet recommends.
  • ṣûr ʿôlāmîm, everlasting rock: Ṣûr is a great mass of rock, the mountain refuge of the land. Moses calls God “the Rock” in his song (Deuteronomy 32:4).

Verse by verse.

“Open the gates, so that the righteous nation that keeps faith may enter in.” (26:2)

The gates are open, not shut. What qualifies a person to enter is faithfulness, not birth or strength. Psalm 118, today's psalm, sings the same: “Open to me the gates of righteousness” (118:19).

“Trust in the Lord forever, for in the Lord God you have an everlasting rock.” (26:4)

This is the centre of the reading and the bridge to the Gospel.

“The foot tramples it, the feet of the poor, the steps of the needy.” (26:6)

God reverses the order of things, as Mary will sing: “He has brought down the powerful from their thrones, and lifted up the lowly” (Luke 1:52).

How the Church reads it.

The Fathers and the liturgy read the rock as Christ, following St Paul: “the rock was Christ” (1 Corinthians 10:4). The Lectionary puts this song beside the Gospel of the two houses for that reason. Advent asks whether our security rests on walls of our own or on him.

Matthew 7:21, 24–27 · The house on the rock

Setting.

These are the closing words of the Sermon on the Mount (chapters 5–7). The reading leaves out vv. 22–23, on those who did great things in Jesus' name and yet never knew him. In the land of Israel a dry valley can turn in an hour into a violent stream after the winter rain. Everyone listening would know how the house on the sand ends.

Structure.

  • v. 21: not saying, but doing the Father's will.
  • vv. 24–25: the wise builder: same storm, house stands.
  • vv. 26–27: the foolish builder: same storm, great fall.

Key words.

  • Kyrie, Kyrie, Lord, Lord: A correct confession and a solemn title. But Jesus says it is not enough to say the right words.
  • phronimos, wise: Sensible, practical, knowing what the moment needs. The same word describes the wise bridesmaids (25:2). Its opposite is mōros, foolish.
  • themelioō, to found: The house “had been founded on rock” (v. 25). What matters happens before the storm, in the digging that no one sees.

Verse by verse.

“Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father in heaven.” (7:21)

Jesus speaks here with an astonishing authority: he himself is the “Lord” of the last day. And he points beyond himself to “my Father”.

“Everyone then who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock.” (7:24)

“These words of mine” take the place that the words of the Law hold in Israel's life. St James says the same: “be doers of the word, and not merely hearers” (James 1:22).

“The rain fell, the floods came, and the winds blew and beat on that house, but it did not fall, because it had been founded on rock.” (7:25)

The rain falls on both houses. Faith is not a promise of no storms. The difference appears only when the weather comes.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches from v. 21 that prayer means disposing the heart to do the Father's will (CCC 2611), and that one enters the Kingdom not by speaking words but by doing that will (CCC 2826). St Augustine closes his commentary on the Sermon on the Mount with these two houses: to hear and not to do is to build without a foundation (in substance).

Questions people ask

Does this Gospel mean that we are saved by works and not by faith?

No. Jesus does not set faith against action; he sets empty words against a real life. St Paul speaks of “faith working through love” (Galatians 5:6). Grace comes first and is a gift, and it builds the house; the doing is what faith looks like when it is alive.

For the Latin reader

St Francis Xavier (1506–1552) was one of the first companions of St Ignatius of Loyola. He landed in Goa in 1542 and preached among the fishing people of the Fishery Coast and in Travancore, before going on to Malacca, the Moluccas and Japan. He died on the island of Shangchuan, off the coast of China, and his body lies in the Basilica of Bom Jesus in Goa. The Church honours him, with St Thérèse of Lisieux, as patron of the missions. He is a witness of today's Gospel for the Latin Catholics of India: a man who heard the words and acted on them.

For study and teaching

  • If a storm came tomorrow, what in my life would stand? What would I find is built on sand?
  • St Francis Xavier gave up a promising career at the University of Paris to go to the ends of the known world. What word of Christ am I hearing without yet acting on it?
