Word for the day

“Then one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, praising God with a loud voice. He prostrated himself at Jesus' feet and thanked him. And he was a Samaritan.” Luke 17:15–16

Reflection

Ten lepers cry out from a distance, “Jesus, Master, have mercy on us!” Jesus sends them to the priests, and on the way they are cleansed. All ten are healed; only one comes back, praising God aloud and falling at Jesus' feet. He is a Samaritan, a foreigner. Jesus tells him: “Your faith has made you well,” literally “has saved you”. Ten received healing; one received salvation, because he turned back to the Healer.

Paul tells Titus that God “saved us, not because of any works of righteousness that we had done, but according to his mercy, through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit”. Baptism is our cleansing; gratitude is our turning back. St Martin of Tours, a soldier who shared his cloak with a beggar and later became a monk and bishop, lived out that gratitude in mercy toward the poor.

Full homily

Dear friends, ten lepers stood at a distance and cried out: Jesus, Master, have mercy on us. Jesus sent them to the priests, and on the way they were cleansed (Luke 17:12–14). All ten were healed. Only one came back, praising God with a loud voice and falling at Jesus' feet, and he was a Samaritan, a foreigner.

Jesus asks: were not ten made clean? Where are the other nine? And to the one who returned he says: your faith has made you well (Luke 17:17–19), or, as the word also means, has saved you. Ten received healing; one received salvation, because he came back to the Healer.

Paul reminds Titus that God saved us not because of any works of righteousness that we had done, but according to his mercy, through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit (Titus 3:5). Everything begins with mercy, and mercy received calls for thanks.

Today we remember St Martin of Tours, the soldier who shared his cloak with a beggar and saw Christ wearing it that night. Gratitude turned into generosity. Let us keep a list this week: three thanks each night, and one act of mercy each day. Amen.

For children

Children, only one leper said thank you to Jesus. Let us be like him! Tonight, tell Jesus three things you are thankful for.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you have cleansed me in the waters of Baptism. Let me not be like the nine who forgot, but turn back each day to thank you, and like St Martin share what I have with the poor. Amen.
