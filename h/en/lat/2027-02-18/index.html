Word for the day

““Ask, and it will be given you; search, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened for you.” Matthew 7:7

Reflection

Queen Esther stands at the edge of a terrible choice. A royal decree has ordered the destruction of her whole people, the Jews of Persia, and only she can plead with the king. But to enter his presence uninvited is to risk death. Before she goes, she fasts, lays aside her royal robes, covers her head with ashes, and lies on the ground in prayer: “O my Lord, you only are our king; help me, who am alone and have no helper but you, for my danger is in my hand.” She remembers how God chose Israel from among all the nations and kept every promise. Then she asks for courage and the right words: “Remember, O Lord; make yourself known in this time of our affliction, and give me courage… Put eloquent speech in my mouth before the lion.” This is how the saints pray when everything depends on God: humbly, remembering his faithfulness, and asking boldly. Her prayer is answered; the king's heart is turned, and her people are saved.

In the Gospel Jesus encourages exactly this kind of prayer. “Ask, and it will be given you; search, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened for you.” Three verbs, each a little more persistent than the last. Then he appeals to every parent's heart: “Is there anyone among you who, if your child asks for bread, will give a stone?… If you then, who are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who ask him!” God is not a reluctant official who must be pestered; he is a Father who loves to give. And the passage ends with the Golden Rule: “In everything do to others as you would have them do to you; for this is the law and the prophets.” The one who receives generously from the Father learns to give generously to others. Lenten prayer and Lenten charity are one movement of the heart.

Full homily

Dear friends, Queen Esther knew her people were about to be destroyed. Before going to the king, she lay on the ground in prayer: O my Lord, you only are our king; help me, who am alone and have no helper but you (Esther C:14).

She did not pretend to be strong. She remembered God's faithfulness and asked for courage and the right words. And God turned the king's heart. Prayer from the depths is never wasted.

Jesus says: ask, and it will be given you; search, and you will find; knock, and the door will be opened for you (Matthew 7:7). If you then, who are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who ask him! (Matthew 7:11).

And Jesus adds: in everything do to others as you would have them do to you (Matthew 7:12). Let us ask the Father with trust today, and then give to others what we hope to receive. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says: ask God for what you need, and he will give you good things, because he is your Father. Tonight, ask God for something for someone else, and trust him.

Prayer

Lord our King, you heard Queen Esther when she was alone and had no helper but you. Hear us when we call on you in our need. Teach us to ask, to search and to knock with the trust of children, and to give to others the goodness we long to receive from you. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
