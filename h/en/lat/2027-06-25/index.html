Word for the day

“There was a leper who came to him and knelt before him, saying, “Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean.” He stretched out his hand and touched him, saying, “I do choose. Be made clean!”” Matthew 8:2–3

Reflection

Twenty-four years have passed since Abram left Haran at the word of the Lord. He is now ninety-nine years old, and the son he was promised has still not been born. Then God appears to him again and gives him a name for himself: “I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless.” The Hebrew is El Shaddai, the God who is enough. The command is simple and searching: live your whole life before my face. Then God renews the covenant and seals it in two ways. First, a new name: Abram, “exalted father,” becomes Abraham, “father of a multitude” (Genesis 17:5), and Sarai becomes Sarah, “princess.” In the Bible a new name means a new mission. Second, a sign in the flesh: “This is my covenant, which you shall keep, between me and you and your offspring after you: Every male among you shall be circumcised.” The covenant is not only an idea in the mind; it is carried in the body, in the family, from generation to generation. Then comes the promise that seems impossible: “I will bless her, and moreover I will give you a son by her.” Abraham falls on his face, and laughs. “Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? Can Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?” He even suggests an easier plan: “O that Ishmael might live in your sight!” God does not reject Ishmael; he blesses him and promises to make him a great nation. But the covenant will pass through the child of the impossible: “your wife Sarah shall bear you a son, and you shall name him Isaac.” Isaac means “he laughs.” Abraham's laugh of disbelief will become the laugh of joy at his son's birth. St Paul will say that Abraham “grew strong in his faith as he gave glory to God, being fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised” (Romans 4:20–21). The Psalm paints the blessing of a home that walks before the Lord: “Happy is everyone who fears the LORD, who walks in his ways,” with children “like olive shoots around your table.”

The Sermon on the Mount is over. “When Jesus had come down from the mountain, great crowds followed him.” St Matthew now shows us that the one who taught with authority also acts with authority: chapters 8 and 9 are a chain of ten mighty works. The first is a meeting with a man nobody wanted to meet. The Law of Moses required a person with a leprous disease to wear torn clothes, cover his lip and cry out “Unclean, unclean,” and to “live alone” outside the camp (Leviticus 13:45–46). The disease was feared; the loneliness was perhaps worse. This man breaks the distance. He comes, kneels, and says one of the humblest prayers in the Gospel: “Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean.” He does not doubt that Jesus can; he only leaves it to Jesus' will. And Jesus does what no one had done for years: “He stretched out his hand and touched him.” By the Law, touching a leper made a person unclean. Here the opposite happens: the clean hand of Jesus does not become unclean; the man becomes clean. “I do choose. Be made clean!” Then Jesus sends him to the priest, to offer “the gift that Moses commanded” (Leviticus 14). Jesus does not despise the Law; he fulfils it, and gives the man back his place in the worshipping community. Abraham laughed because the promise seemed too great; the leper knelt because he trusted it. Both show us the God who wants to bless: I will establish my covenant, says God to Abraham; I do choose, says Jesus to the leper. In this month of the Sacred Heart, the outstretched hand of Jesus is a picture of his heart: he wants us clean, whole and at home.

Homily outline

Theme: “I do choose. Be made clean!”

A new name for Abraham, a laugh turned to joy, and the hand of Jesus that touches the untouchable.

1. A new name, a new mission

  • God Almighty, the God who is enough.
  • Abram becomes Abraham, Sarai becomes Sarah.
  • A covenant carried in body and family.
“No longer shall your name be Abram, but your name shall be Abraham; for I have made you the ancestor of a multitude of nations.” Genesis 17:5
“And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to the promise.” Galatians 3:29

Apply: Remember your baptismal name this week and the saint who bears it; ask what mission God gave you with that name.

2. The laugh of Abraham

  • A hundred years and ninety.
  • An easier plan: Ishmael.
  • Isaac, “he laughs.”
“Then Abraham fell on his face and laughed, and said to himself, “Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? Can Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?”” Genesis 17:17
“No distrust made him waver concerning the promise of God, but he grew strong in his faith as he gave glory to God, being fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised.” Romans 4:20–21

Apply: Bring to God in prayer one hope you have given up on, and thank him in advance for his faithfulness.

3. I do choose

  • He shall live alone.
  • Lord, if you choose.
  • The touch that makes clean.
“He shall remain unclean as long as he has the disease; he is unclean. He shall live alone; his dwelling shall be outside the camp.” Leviticus 13:46
“He stretched out his hand and touched him, saying, “I do choose. Be made clean!” Immediately his leprosy was cleansed.” Matthew 8:3

Apply: This week go to confession and let the Lord say to you: I do choose. Be made clean.

God renews his covenant with Abraham and promises the impossible son, turning the laugh of doubt into the laugh of joy.

Jesus touches the leper whom the world kept at a distance, and shows the heart of God who wants to make us clean.

Take-home resolution: Each day this week reach out to one person whom others leave alone.

Full homily

Dear friends, Abraham is ninety-nine years old. For twenty-four years he has waited for the son God promised. Then God speaks again: I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless (Genesis 17:1). God gives him a new name, Abraham, father of a multitude. And he gives Sarai a new name too, Sarah. A new name means a new beginning, even at ninety-nine.

And what does Abraham do when God promises a son by Sarah? He falls on his face and laughs. Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? (Genesis 17:17). We understand him. We too sometimes laugh quietly at God's promises, or we offer God an easier plan, like Abraham offering Ishmael. But God says: your wife Sarah shall bear you a son, and you shall name him Isaac. Isaac means he laughs. God turns the laugh of doubt into the laugh of joy.

In the Gospel Jesus comes down from the mountain, and a leper comes to him. By the Law he had to live alone, outside the village, crying unclean. Nobody touched him. He kneels and says: Lord, if you choose, you can make me clean (Matthew 8:2). What a beautiful prayer. He does not command Jesus. He trusts both his power and his will.

And Jesus stretches out his hand and touches him. I do choose. Be made clean! (Matthew 8:3). The hand that touched the leper is the hand of the Sacred Heart, whom we honour this month. He chooses to heal, to cleanse, to bring us back. Today let us bring him the thing we have stopped hoping for, and let us stretch out our own hands to someone nobody touches. Amen.

For children

Children, a long time ago Abraham and Sarah were very, very old, and God said: you will have a baby boy! Abraham laughed. A baby at a hundred years? But God kept his promise, and the baby was called Isaac, which means he laughs. In the Gospel a sick man came to Jesus. Nobody wanted to go near him. But Jesus touched him and made him well. Jesus loves to touch and heal the ones nobody wants. Let us be kind to the child who is left alone at school!

Prayer

God Almighty, you called Abraham to walk before you and be blameless, and you kept your promise when it seemed too late. Give us a faith that does not waver, that grows strong as it gives you glory. Lord Jesus, you came down from the mountain and stretched out your hand to the leper whom everyone avoided; stretch out your hand to us today. Where we are unclean, make us clean; where we are lonely, bring us home; and make our hands like yours, ready to touch those whom others will not touch. Sacred Heart of Jesus, we trust in you. Amen.
