Word for the day

“For John came to you in the way of righteousness and you did not believe him, but the tax collectors and the prostitutes believed him; and even after you saw it, you did not change your minds and believe him.” Matthew 21:32

Reflection

Zephaniah condemns a proud city that “has listened to no voice” and promises a different future: God will purify the lips of the peoples, and “I will leave in the midst of you a people humble and lowly. They shall seek refuge in the name of the LORD.” This humble remnant is the true Israel of Advent: Mary, Joseph, Zechariah, Elizabeth, the shepherds.

Jesus tells of two sons. One refuses to work in the vineyard but later changes his mind and goes; the other says yes but does not go. The religious leaders gave God many words; tax collectors and prostitutes gave him their repentance. “Even after you saw it, you did not change your minds.” Advent asks not for fine words but for a changed heart. It is never too late to turn and go into the vineyard.

Full homily

Dear friends, the prophet Zephaniah speaks of a city that is rebellious and defiled: it has listened to no voice, it has accepted no correction (Zephaniah 3:1–2). Then God promises: I will leave in the midst of you a people humble and lowly. They shall seek refuge in the name of the Lord (Zephaniah 3:12).

God's future belongs to the humble: the anawim, the poor of the Lord. Mary, Joseph, the shepherds, Simeon and Anna belong to this humble remnant who waited for the Messiah.

In the Gospel Jesus tells a parable. A man has two sons. He asks the first to work in the vineyard; he says, I will not, but later changes his mind and goes. He asks the second; he says, I go, sir, but does not go. Which of the two did the will of his father? (Matthew 21:28–31).

Then Jesus says something shocking to the religious leaders: the tax collectors and prostitutes are going into the kingdom of God ahead of you. For John came in the way of righteousness, and they believed him, but you did not (Matthew 21:31–32).

The second son represents those who say yes with their lips but no with their lives. The first son represents sinners who first say no but then repent. Both are in us. We say yes at Mass, then no at home. We refuse God, and then, by grace, we change our minds.

It is never too late to change our minds. This Advent, let us be the son who repents. Let us look honestly at where we have said yes but not gone, and go now. And let us ask to belong to the humble remnant who seek refuge in the Lord. Amen.

For children

Children, one son said no but then obeyed; the other said yes but did not. Jesus wants us to say yes and then really do it!

Prayer

Lord, the poor cry out and you hear them. Make me humble and lowly, change my heart where I have said no to you, and let my yes be more than words. Amen.
