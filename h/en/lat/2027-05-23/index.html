Word for the day

“Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.” Matthew 28:19

Reflection

Last Sunday the Easter season ended with the outpouring of the Holy Spirit. Today the Church pauses and looks at the whole mystery that Easter and Pentecost have revealed: the one God who is Father, Son and Holy Spirit. This is not a puzzle invented by theologians. It is the name into which every one of us was baptised, and the Church calls it “the central mystery of Christian faith and life” (CCC 234). The first reading prepares the ground. Moses, at the end of the desert years, asks Israel to look back over all history: “ask now about former ages, long before your own, ever since the day that God created human beings on the earth; ask from one end of heaven to the other: has anything so great as this ever happened or has its like ever been heard of?” Has any people heard the voice of God speaking out of the fire and lived? Has any god gone to take a nation for himself “by a mighty hand and an outstretched arm”? Israel's God is not a distant idea but the God who speaks and acts, who chooses and rescues. And from that history comes the confession that stands at the root of our faith: “So acknowledge today and take to heart that the Lord is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath; there is no other.” Christians do not believe in three gods. The Trinity is the one God of Israel, revealed in his innermost life.

The Psalm sings of this God who creates by his word and his breath: “By the word of the Lord the heavens were made, and all their host by the breath of his mouth.” The Fathers of the Church loved this verse, for in it they heard, quietly hidden in the Old Testament, the Word and the Spirit of the Father at work in creation. The Lord is the God whose “word is upright,” who “loves righteousness and justice,” whose “steadfast love” fills the earth; and his eye is “on those who fear him, on those who hope in his steadfast love.” So the people can say: “Our soul waits for the Lord; he is our help and shield.” The refrain gathers it up: blessed the people the Lord has chosen to be his own.

St Paul, in the Letter to the Romans, shows that the Trinity is not only a truth to be believed but a life into which we are drawn. “For all who are led by the Spirit of God are children of God. For you did not receive a spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received a spirit of adoption.” The Holy Spirit, given to us in Baptism, does not make us servants of a distant master; he makes us sons and daughters, so that we dare to say what Jesus said in Gethsemane: “Abba! Father!” It is “that very Spirit bearing witness with our spirit that we are children of God, and if children, then heirs, heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ.” Here is the Trinity at prayer within us: the Spirit, in us, crying to the Father, with the voice of the Son. We are not outside the mystery looking in. We have been taken inside it.

The Gospel is the last scene of Matthew. The eleven disciples go to the mountain in Galilee where Jesus had told them to go. “When they saw him, they worshiped him; but some doubted.” Even at the end, faith and hesitation stand side by side, and Jesus does not wait for perfect disciples. He comes near and says: “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.” Notice the singular: in the name, not the names. One God, one name, three Persons. Then comes the promise that closes the whole Gospel: “And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” Matthew's Gospel began with Emmanuel, God with us (1:23); it ends with the same promise. The Trinity is not a God who keeps himself far away. The Father sent the Son; the Son sends us; the Spirit makes us children; and the risen Lord remains with us every day. As St Gregory of Nazianzus put it (in substance), no sooner do I think of the One than I am surrounded by the splendour of the Three; no sooner do I distinguish the Three than I am carried back to the One.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear friends, the first thing most of us learned as children in church was not a prayer but a gesture. Our mother or grandmother took our small hand and made it touch the forehead, the chest, the left shoulder and the right: in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Today the Church celebrates what that gesture means: the Most Holy Trinity.

We cannot fully understand God. If we could, he would be smaller than our minds. But God has not left us in the dark. In the first reading Moses tells Israel: ask from one end of heaven to the other: has anything so great as this ever happened? The God who spoke from the fire, who rescued his people from Egypt, is the one true God. So acknowledge today and take to heart that the Lord is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath; there is no other (Deuteronomy 4:39). We Christians believe in one God, not three.

But Jesus has shown us the inner life of this one God. He calls God his Father. He promises the Holy Spirit. And St Paul tells us that this Spirit has been given to us: you have received a spirit of adoption. When we cry, “Abba! Father!” it is that very Spirit bearing witness with our spirit that we are children of God (Romans 8:15–16). Think of that. The Spirit, inside you, praying to the Father with the words of the Son. You are not outside the Trinity looking in; you have been brought inside.

In the Gospel the risen Jesus sends the disciples: go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit (Matthew 28:19). Notice that some of them doubted. Jesus sent them anyway. He does not wait for perfect people. Neither should we wait until we have perfect faith before we share it.

What does the Trinity mean for our daily life? God is a communion of love: the Father loves the Son, the Son loves the Father, and the Holy Spirit is their love in Person. We are made in that image. A family where husband and wife forgive each other, where children are welcomed, where the old are cared for, is a small picture of God. A parish where people of different backgrounds pray and serve together is a picture of God. Selfishness, division, loneliness by choice, these are not God's image.

And finally, Jesus gives the promise that ends Matthew's Gospel: remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age (Matthew 28:20). In every Mass he keeps that promise, coming to us in his Body and Blood. So today, let us make the sign of the cross slowly, with love, and let us live as children of the Father, brothers and sisters of the Son, and temples of the Holy Spirit. Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, the mystery of the Holy Trinity can seem far above us. But St Paul shows us that the Trinity is not only something to think about; it is a family into which we have been brought. For all who are led by the Spirit of God are children of God (Romans 8:14).

Led by the Spirit. Isaiah remembered how God guided Israel in the desert: the spirit of the LORD gave them rest. Thus you led your people (Isaiah 63:14). The Spirit is not only a gift we received at Baptism; he is a guide who walks with us each day. When we must decide something about work, family, marriage or money, we can ask: Holy Spirit, lead me.

Paul continues: for you did not receive a spirit of slavery to fall back into fear (Romans 8:15). Many Christians live as if God were a harsh master waiting to punish them. But we are not slaves; we are sons and daughters. When Israel stood trapped at the Red Sea, Moses said: do not be afraid, stand firm, and see the deliverance that the LORD will accomplish for you today (Exodus 14:13). Children of the Father can face life without fear.

And then the greatest promise: if children, then heirs, heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ, if, in fact, we suffer with him so that we may also be glorified with him (Romans 8:17). What do we inherit? Not land or money, but God himself, the life of the Trinity for ever. The path to that inheritance passes through suffering with Christ. But God promises: when you pass through the waters, I will be with you (Isaiah 43:2).

So today let us live as heirs, not slaves. Let the Spirit lead you. Hand your fears to the Father. Unite your sufferings with the Son. And when we make the sign of the cross at this Mass, let us remember that we are signed with the name of the family to which we belong. Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Our prayer is a share in the life of the Trinity.

We pray to the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit, and live what we pray.

Take-home resolution: This week I will pray the Glory Be with attention and teach it to a child.

Dear friends, have you ever noticed how the prayers at Mass end? The priest prays to the Father, and then concludes: through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit. Every prayer of the Church has this shape: to the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit. St Paul explains why: for through him both of us have access in one Spirit to the Father (Ephesians 2:18).

We do not pray to a distant God from outside. Through Jesus, and with the Holy Spirit in our hearts, we pray from inside the family of God. Even creation was made this way: by the word of the LORD the heavens were made, and all their host by the breath of his mouth (Psalm 33:6). The Father creates through his Word and by his Breath. At the end of the Eucharistic Prayer, the priest lifts the Body and Blood of Christ and sings: through him, and with him, and in him, O God, almighty Father, in the unity of the Holy Spirit, all glory and honour is yours. And we answer with the great Amen.

Isaiah saw the Lord in the temple, and the seraphim crying: holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory (Isaiah 6:3). John heard the same song in heaven (Revelation 4:8). We sing it at every Mass in the Sanctus. This is prayer that asks for nothing; it simply adores. The Glory Be is the same kind of prayer. We say it so quickly. Let us learn to say it slowly, with love.

Jesus sent his disciples to baptise in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and also: teaching them to obey everything that I have commanded you (Matthew 28:20). Moses had said: keep his statutes and his commandments… for your own well-being and that of your descendants after you (Deuteronomy 4:40). Prayer to the Trinity must become a life of love: in our families, in forgiveness, in care for one another.

So this week, let us listen carefully to the prayers at the Mass. Let us say the Glory Be slowly. And let us teach a child to make the sign of the cross and to pray: Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

For children

Children, let us make the sign of the cross together, slowly: in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen. There is one God, and God is three Persons: the Father who made us, Jesus the Son who saved us, and the Holy Spirit who lives in our hearts.

God is like a family of love that never ends. When you were baptised, God made you part of his family! So every time you make the sign of the cross, you can say: thank you, God, for making me your child.

Prayer

God our Father, you sent into the world the Word of truth and the Spirit of sanctification, and so made known to us your wonderful mystery. Lord Jesus Christ, eternal Son, you have made us children of your Father and you remain with us always, to the end of the age. Holy Spirit, Lord and giver of life, you cry in our hearts “Abba! Father!” and bear witness that we are children of God. Most Holy Trinity, one God, we adore you, we praise you, we thank you. Keep us faithful to the faith of our Baptism, make our families and our parish an image of your communion of love, and send us to make disciples of all nations. With the Blessed Virgin Mary, daughter of the Father, mother of the Son and temple of the Holy Spirit, and with all the saints, we give you glory, now and for ever. Amen.
