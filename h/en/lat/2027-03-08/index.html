Word for the day

“Jesus said to him, “Go; your son will live.” The man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him and started on his way.” John 4:50

Reflection

Laetare Sunday has just lifted our eyes toward Easter, and today Isaiah carries that joy further. God speaks like an artist about to begin his greatest work: “For I am about to create new heavens and a new earth; the former things shall not be remembered or come to mind.” Jerusalem will be a joy and its people a delight; “no more shall the sound of weeping be heard in it, or the cry of distress.” No infant will die after a few days, and the old will live out their years; people will build houses and live in them, plant vineyards and eat their fruit. It is the picture of a world healed of death and of wasted labour. The Psalm answers with the song of someone who has been brought back from the edge of the grave: “O LORD my God, I cried to you for help, and you have healed me.” And it gives Lent one of its loveliest lines: “Weeping may linger for the night, but joy comes with the morning.”

The Gospel shows the new creation beginning in one household. Jesus has come back to Cana, where he changed water into wine. A royal official, probably a servant of Herod Antipas, walks some twenty-five kilometres up from Capernaum, because his son is dying. Jesus seems to answer coldly: “Unless you see signs and wonders you will not believe.” The father does not argue; he simply pleads: “Sir, come down before my little boy dies.” Then Jesus gives him not a sign but a word: “Go; your son will live.” And the man does the hardest thing a worried father can do: he turns round and walks home with nothing in his hands but that word. “The man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him and started on his way.” On the road his servants meet him: the boy is alive, and the fever left him at one in the afternoon, the very hour Jesus spoke. Faith that began as a cry becomes trust in a word, and ends as the faith of a whole household. Today we also remember St John of God (1495–1550), a Portuguese soldier and bookseller who, converted by a sermon of St John of Avila, spent the rest of his life gathering the sick and the poor of Granada into a hospital and caring for them with his own hands. From his companions grew the Brothers Hospitallers. He believed that every sick person was Christ, and he served them like a father carrying home a child.

Full homily

Dear friends, today God speaks through Isaiah like a builder about to begin: for I am about to create new heavens and a new earth (Isaiah 65:17). No more weeping, no more children dying, no more wasted work. That is where God is taking the world, and that is where Lent is taking us: to Easter, the first morning of the new creation.

In the Gospel a father comes to Jesus because his little boy is dying. Jesus says: unless you see signs and wonders you will not believe. The father does not argue; he only begs: sir, come down before my little boy dies.

And Jesus says only: go; your son will live (John 4:50). Think of that walk home. No sign, no proof, only a word. The man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him and started on his way. On the road the good news meets him: the fever left the boy at the very hour Jesus spoke. That is what faith is: walking on the strength of Christ's word.

Today we remember St John of God, who once lived a wild and wandering life. After his conversion he filled a house in Granada with the sick and the poor and served them with his own hands, because he saw Christ in every one of them. Let us believe the word of Jesus, and let that faith become hands that serve the sick. Amen.

For children

Children, a father's son was very sick. Jesus said: go home, your son will live! The father believed Jesus and went home, and his son was well. When Jesus says something, we can trust him. Let us pray today for a child who is sick.

Prayer

Lord God, you make all things new and turn our mourning into dancing. Give us the faith of the royal official, to believe your Son's word and set out on our way. Through the prayers of St John of God, give the sick tender care and make our hands ready to serve them. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
