Word for the day

“John answered them, “I baptize with water. Among you stands one whom you do not know, the one who is coming after me; I am not worthy to untie the thong of his sandal.”” John 1:26–27

Reflection

The priests and Levites ask John the Baptist, “Who are you?” He answers first with what he is not: not the Messiah, not Elijah, not the prophet. Then he says who he is: “I am the voice of one crying out in the wilderness.” A voice exists for a word. John's whole identity is to point to another: “Among you stands one whom you do not know.” Humility is not thinking little of ourselves; it is knowing that our life is for Christ.

St John warns that whoever denies the Son does not have the Father, and urges: “Let what you heard from the beginning abide in you.” Today we honour two friends who defended that faith from the beginning: St Basil the Great and St Gregory Nazianzen, students together in Athens, monks together, then bishops in Cappadocia. Against the Arians they defended the full divinity of the Son and of the Holy Spirit; the Creed we say on Sundays owes much to them. Gregory wrote of their friendship: we knew only two roads, the one to the church and the one to the schools.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the second day of the new year the Gospel asks a simple question: who are you? The priests and Levites come to John the Baptist and ask him this. John first says what he is not: I am not the Messiah, not Elijah, not the prophet (John 1:20–21).

Then he says who he is: I am the voice of one crying out in the wilderness, make straight the way of the Lord (John 1:23). A voice exists to carry a word. John knows that his whole life is to carry Christ to others. That is true humility: not hating ourselves, but knowing that we live for someone greater.

St John in the first reading tells us to let what we heard from the beginning abide in us (1 John 2:24). Many voices around us offer a new gospel. The Church asks us to stay with the faith of the Apostles.

Today we remember Saints Basil and Gregory, two friends from Cappadocia in present-day Turkey. They studied together in Athens, prayed together as monks, and became bishops. They defended the truth that Jesus is truly God and that the Holy Spirit is truly God. Gregory said that in Athens they knew only two roads: the road to church and the road to school.

Let us ask for John's humility and for holy friendships like theirs. Let our words and our lives say: not me, but Christ. Amen.

For children

Children, John the Baptist said: I am only a voice; Jesus is the important one! Today let us help someone to know Jesus.

Prayer

O God, who were pleased to give light to your Church by the example and teaching of the Bishops Saints Basil and Gregory, grant that in humility we may learn your truth and practise it faithfully in charity. Through our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.
