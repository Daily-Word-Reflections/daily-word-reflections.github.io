Word for the day

“He proclaimed, “The one who is more powerful than I is coming after me; I am not worthy to stoop down and untie the thong of his sandals. I have baptized you with water; but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit.”” Mark 1:7–8

Reflection

Isaiah 40 opens the “Book of Consolation”. After decades of exile God speaks tenderly: “Comfort, O comfort my people… Speak tenderly to Jerusalem.” The punishment is over; a road is to be built in the desert for God to lead his people home; valleys raised, mountains lowered. And the God who comes is a Shepherd who “will gather the lambs in his arms, and carry them in his bosom”.

Mark begins his Gospel with no birth story. Its first words are a title: “The beginning of the good news of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.” Then he quotes Isaiah and Malachi, and John the Baptist appears in the wilderness, proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins. People come from all Judea and Jerusalem, confessing their sins. John points away from himself: “The one who is more powerful than I is coming… he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit.”

Peter's letter explains the delay of the Lord's return: “with the Lord one day is like a thousand years… The Lord is not slow about his promise… but is patient with you, not wanting any to perish, but all to come to repentance.” God's patience is time given for conversion. So: “Since you are waiting for these, strive to be found by him at peace, without spot or blemish.” Advent is the time to straighten the crooked road of our hearts.

Homily outline

Theme: “Prepare the way of the Lord.”

God comforts; repentance prepares the road; John points to Christ who baptises with the Spirit.

1. Comfort my people

  • The exile is over.
  • God speaks tenderly.
  • His first word to sinners is comfort.
“Comfort, O comfort my people, says your God. Speak tenderly to Jerusalem, and cry to her that she has served her term, that her penalty is paid, that she has received from the LORD's hand double for all her sins.” Isaiah 40:1–2
“Come to me, all you that are weary and are carrying heavy burdens, and I will give you rest.” Matthew 11:28

Apply: Speak tenderly to someone who is discouraged.

2. Prepare the way

  • A highway in the desert.
  • Repentance levels pride and fills discouragement.
  • The crowds confessed their sins.
“A voice cries out: “In the wilderness prepare the way of the LORD, make straight in the desert a highway for our God.”” Isaiah 40:3
“John the baptizer appeared in the wilderness, proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins.” Mark 1:4

Apply: Make an Advent Confession.

3. He will baptize with the Holy Spirit

  • John is unworthy to untie his sandals.
  • He points away from himself.
  • Christ gives the Spirit.
“He will feed his flock like a shepherd; he will gather the lambs in his arms, and carry them in his bosom, and gently lead the mother sheep.” Isaiah 40:11
“He proclaimed, “The one who is more powerful than I is coming after me; I am not worthy to stoop down and untie the thong of his sandals. I have baptized you with water; but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit.”” Mark 1:7–8

Apply: Do one good deed without seeking credit.

God's patience is time for conversion.

Prepare the road; the King is coming.

Take-home resolution: Begin the conversion you have postponed.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today the prophet Isaiah begins with a beautiful word: “Comfort, O comfort my people.” After years of exile, God's first word is not anger but tenderness.

And then a voice cries out: “In the wilderness prepare the way of the Lord.” Every valley lifted up, every mountain made low. In ancient times roads were repaired before a king's visit. Isaiah says: God himself is coming; prepare his road!

Mark begins his Gospel with John the Baptist in the desert. People come from everywhere, confessing their sins and being baptised in the Jordan. That is how the road is prepared: not with decorations but with repentance.

What are the mountains in us? Pride, stubbornness, the refusal to forgive. What are the valleys? Discouragement, laziness, the feeling that I can never change. Advent asks us to bring both to God. Confession is the bulldozer that levels the road!

And John points away from himself: “One more powerful than I is coming… he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit.” A true Christian is like John: a finger pointing to Jesus.

St Peter tells us why the Lord seems to delay: “The Lord is not slow about his promise, but is patient with you, not wanting any to perish.” This Advent is God's patience with us. Let us not waste it. Make a good Confession. Comfort someone who is low. And prepare the road for the King.

For children

Children, when an important guest is coming, what do we do? We clean the house!

John the Baptist told the people: Jesus is coming; clean your hearts!

How do we clean our hearts? By saying sorry to God in Confession, and sorry to people we have hurt.

This week, say sorry to someone and make them smile!

Prayer

Almighty and merciful God, may no earthly undertaking hinder those who set out in haste to meet your Son, but may our learning of heavenly wisdom gain us admittance to his company. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
