Word for the day

“Then he went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was obedient to them. His mother treasured all these things in her heart.” Luke 2:51

Reflection

Yesterday the Church contemplated the pierced Heart of the Son; today she turns to the Heart of his Mother, which was pierced too, as Simeon foretold: “a sword will pierce your own soul too” (Luke 2:35). The two feasts stand side by side because the two hearts were never apart. The first reading is a song of the restored Zion, the city God has rescued and adorned: “I will greatly rejoice in the Lord, my whole being shall exult in my God; for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation, he has covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decks himself with a garland, and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels.” The Church has always heard Mary's voice in these words. She is the first of the redeemed, clothed in salvation from the first moment of her existence: that is what Immaculate means. Her heart was never stained by sin, not because she did not need a Saviour, but because the Saviour reached her first, in view of the merits of her Son. And Isaiah ends with an image of growth: “For as the earth brings forth its shoots, and as a garden causes what is sown in it to spring up, so the Lord God will cause righteousness and praise to spring up before all the nations.” Mary's heart is that garden: the Word was sown in it, and it brought forth the Saviour. The responsorial psalm is the song of Hannah, the mother of Samuel: “My heart exults in the Lord; my strength is exalted in my God.” Hannah's song is the elder sister of Mary's Magnificat: “The barren has borne seven … He raises up the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap.” God lifts up the lowly, and Mary is the lowly one he lifted highest.

The Gospel is the only story of Jesus' boyhood in the Gospels. Every year Joseph and Mary go up to Jerusalem for the Passover. When Jesus is twelve, he stays behind in the city without telling them. They travel a day's journey, assuming he is with relatives, and then begin the long search. “After three days they found him in the temple, sitting among the teachers, listening to them and asking them questions.” Mary speaks for both parents: “Child, why have you treated us like this? Look, your father and I have been searching for you in great anxiety.” Here we see the heart of Mary, not calm and untroubled, but anxious, searching, loving. The Immaculate Heart is not a heart that feels nothing; it is a heart that feels everything without sin. Jesus answers with his first recorded words: “Why were you searching for me? Did you not know that I must be in my Father's house?” And Luke adds honestly: “they did not understand what he said to them.” Mary does not understand, yet she does not stop believing. “Then he went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was obedient to them. His mother treasured all these things in her heart.” The Greek word means to keep carefully, to hold together, to ponder over time. Mary's heart is the place where the mysteries of Christ are kept and slowly understood. Before she conceived Christ in her womb, says St Augustine (in substance), she conceived him in her heart by faith. Devotion to her Immaculate Heart grew through St John Eudes in the seventeenth century and was given new urgency at Fatima in 1917; Pius XII consecrated the world to her Immaculate Heart in 1942 and in 1944 extended this feast to the whole Church. To honour her Heart is to learn from her how to keep the Word, how to search for Jesus when we have lost him, and how to stay faithful when we do not understand.

Today the Church could also keep the memorial of St Boniface, the apostle of Germany. Wynfrith, an English Benedictine monk, was sent by Pope Gregory II in 719 and given the name Boniface. He preached in Hesse and Thuringia, felled the sacred oak of Thor at Geismar to show that the old gods had no power, founded monasteries such as Fulda, and became archbishop of Mainz. In old age he went back to the Frisians, and on 5 June 754, near Dokkum, he and his companions were killed by a band of raiders; tradition says he raised a book of the Gospels over his head. The weekday readings, if they are used, end the story of Tobit: Raphael reveals himself, “I am Raphael, one of the seven angels who stand ready and enter before the glory of the Lord,” and teaches that “almsgiving saves from death and purges away every sin”; and in the Gospel Jesus praises the poor widow who gave “everything she had, all she had to live on.” Mary, the widow, and Boniface each gave God everything. And on this World Environment Day, Isaiah's garden reminds us that the earth that brings forth its shoots is God's gift, to be tended with care.

2. A heart that searches

  • The boy who stayed behind.
  • Three days of anxiety.
  • Found in his Father's house.

  • Treasured and pondered.

  • The widow's two coins.
  • Boniface and the Gospel book.

Apply: Consecrate yourself and your family to the Immaculate Heart of Mary, and give one generous gift to someone in need.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we looked at the Heart of Jesus, pierced on the Cross. Today the Church invites us to look at the Heart of his Mother. These two feasts sit side by side because these two hearts were never separated. Simeon told Mary: a sword will pierce your own soul too (Luke 2:35). When the Son's Heart was pierced, the Mother's Heart was pierced with him.

Isaiah gives us a joyful picture: I will greatly rejoice in the Lord … for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation … as a bride adorns herself with her jewels (Isaiah 61:10). We hear Mary in these words. From the first moment of her life God clothed her with salvation and kept her heart pure from every sin. That is why we call her Heart Immaculate. And Hannah's song, which we prayed as the Psalm, is the mother of Mary's own Magnificat: my heart exults in the Lord.

But the Gospel shows us that an Immaculate Heart is not a heart without feelings. Mary loses her twelve-year-old son. For three days she searches, with Joseph, through the crowded city. When they find him, she says: Child, why have you treated us like this? Your father and I have been searching for you in great anxiety (Luke 2:48). Every mother who has waited at night for a child who did not come home knows that anxiety. Mary's Heart feels everything we feel, but without sin, without bitterness, without despair.

Then Jesus says something she does not understand: did you not know that I must be in my Father's house? And Luke says simply: his mother treasured all these things in her heart (Luke 2:51). She did not understand, but she did not throw the words away. She kept them, carried them, turned them over for years, until on Calvary and on Easter morning she understood. That is faith: keeping God's word even when we cannot see where it leads.

Today we could also remember St Boniface, who left England to bring the Gospel to Germany, cut down the oak that people worshipped, and was killed in his old age, raising the Gospel book over his head. And the weekday Gospel tells of a poor widow who gave everything she had. Mary, Boniface and the widow have one thing in common: they kept nothing back from God.

So let us consecrate ourselves today to the Immaculate Heart of Mary. When you lose Jesus, through sin or through busyness, search for him as she did, and you will find him in his Father's house, in the Church and in the Eucharist. When you do not understand what God is doing, keep his word in your heart as she did. And ask her to give you a heart like hers, wholly given to God. Amen.

For children

Children, when Jesus was twelve years old, he got lost! Mary and Joseph searched for him for three whole days. Can you imagine how worried they were? At last they found him in the Temple, talking with the teachers about God.

Mary did not understand everything Jesus said, but she kept it all in her heart like a treasure. Our Mother Mary has a heart full of love. If you ever feel lost or sad, you can talk to her. She will always lead you back to Jesus.

Prayer

O God, who prepared a fit dwelling place for the Holy Spirit in the Heart of the Blessed Virgin Mary, graciously grant that through her intercession we may be a worthy temple of your glory. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Immaculate Heart of Mary, you kept every word of your Son and treasured what you did not yet understand; teach us to search for Jesus when we have lost him, to keep his word, and to give him everything. Through the prayers of St Boniface, make us brave witnesses to the Gospel. Amen.
