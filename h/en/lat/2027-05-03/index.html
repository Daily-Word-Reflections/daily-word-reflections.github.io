Word for the day

“Jesus said to him, “Have I been with you all this time, Philip, and you still do not know me? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, ‘Show us the Father’?”” John 14:9

Reflection

Today the Church keeps the feast of two of the Twelve whom the Gospels mention only briefly: Philip, and James the son of Alphaeus, often called James the Less to distinguish him from James the brother of John. The first reading was chosen because of one line in it. St Paul is reminding the Corinthians of the Gospel he handed on to them, the core of the faith that he “in turn had received”: “that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the scriptures, and that he was buried, and that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the scriptures, and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve.” Then come the other witnesses: more than five hundred at once, “most of whom are still alive”; “then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles. Last of all, as to one untimely born, he appeared also to me.” This is one of the oldest texts in the whole New Testament. Paul wrote it around the year 54, but he is quoting a summary of the faith he had received years earlier, very soon after the Resurrection. The apostles' faith is not a pious idea that grew slowly; it is the testimony of people who saw the risen Lord. The James to whom the Lord appeared is, in Paul's letters, “James the Lord's brother” (Galatians 1:19), the leader of the Church in Jerusalem. The Latin tradition has long identified him with the apostle James the son of Alphaeus, whom we honour today. Scholars discuss whether these are one man or two, and the Church has not settled the question; what is sure is that the liturgy wants us to see in today's James a witness of the Resurrection.

The Psalm gives the reason the apostles still speak to us: “There is no speech, nor are there words; their voice is not heard; yet their voice goes out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world.” The psalmist was speaking of the sun, the moon and the stars, which praise God without words. St Paul applies the verse to the preachers of the Gospel (Romans 10:18). Two quiet men from Galilee, of whom we know very little, have a voice that has reached the ends of the earth, as far as India.

The Gospel gives us Philip at the Last Supper. Philip came from Bethsaida, the town of Andrew and Peter, on the shore of the Lake of Galilee. He was among the very first to be called: Jesus “found Philip and said to him, ‘Follow me’” (John 1:43). At once Philip found his friend Nathanael and told him, “We have found him about whom Moses in the law and also the prophets wrote.” When Nathanael scoffed, Philip did not argue; he said, “Come and see.” At the feeding of the five thousand, Jesus tested Philip: “Where are we to buy bread for these people to eat?” And Philip, practical as ever, did the arithmetic: “Six months' wages would not buy enough bread.” Later, some Greeks came to Philip, perhaps because of his Greek name: “Sir, we wish to see Jesus.” Now, at the supper, Jesus has said, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me. If you know me, you will know my Father also.” And Philip, honest and a little puzzled, speaks for every seeker: “Lord, show us the Father, and we will be satisfied.” The answer of Jesus is gentle and astonishing: “Have I been with you all this time, Philip, and you still do not know me? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.” Philip has been looking past Jesus for God, when God was looking at him through the face of Jesus. The Son is not a messenger who points to a distant Father; he is in the Father and the Father is in him.

Then Jesus makes a promise to all who believe: “the one who believes in me will also do the works that I do and, in fact, will do greater works than these, because I am going to the Father.” Greater works, because after the Ascension the Gospel will spread through the whole world by the apostles, by Philip and James and all the rest. And “I will do whatever you ask in my name.” Tradition says that Philip preached in Asia Minor and died at Hierapolis in Phrygia; in 2011 archaeologists working there reported finding a tomb they believe to be his. James, according to the Jewish historian Josephus, was stoned in Jerusalem around the year 62 on the orders of the high priest; the Christian writer Hegesippus, quoted by Eusebius, tells of his prayer for his killers. The letter of James, which tradition ascribes to the Lord's brother, gives us a James who is utterly practical: “be doers of the word, and not merely hearers.” Philip who brought others to Jesus, James who lived what he believed: two faces of the one apostolic faith that has come down to us.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.”

Two apostles: Philip who asked to see the Father, and James who saw the risen Lord.

1. What I in turn had received

  • The oldest creed.
  • Witnesses, not inventors.
  • Then he appeared to James.
“After two days he will revive us; on the third day he will raise us up, that we may live before him.” Hosea 6:2
“Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles. Last of all, as to one untimely born, he appeared also to me.” 1 Corinthians 15:7–8

Apply: Pray the Apostles' Creed slowly each day this week, pausing at “rose again on the third day.”

2. Their voice goes out through all the earth

  • The heavens speak without words.
  • Paul applies it to the apostles.
  • Galilee to India.
“There is no speech, nor are there words; their voice is not heard; yet their voice goes out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world.” Psalm 19:3–4
“How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!” Romans 10:15

Apply: Thank God by name this week for the missionaries, priests and catechists who brought the faith to your family.

3. Show us the Father

  • Philip the practical one.
  • Come and see.
  • An honest seeker's prayer.
“Moses said, “Show me your glory, I pray.”” Exodus 33:18
“Philip said to him, “Lord, show us the Father, and we will be satisfied.”” John 14:8

Apply: Bring your deepest question about God to Jesus in prayer this week, honestly, as Philip did.

4. Whoever has seen me

  • God's face in Jesus.
  • Greater works after the Ascension.
  • Doers of the word, like James.
““Come,” my heart says, “seek his face!” Your face, Lord, do I seek.” Psalm 27:8
“Jesus said to him, “Have I been with you all this time, Philip, and you still do not know me? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, ‘Show us the Father’?”” John 14:9

Apply: Do one concrete work of mercy this week as a “doer of the word,” and invite one person to “come and see.”

The faith of the Church rests on the testimony of apostles like James, who saw the risen Lord, and their voice still goes out through all the earth.

In Jesus, as Philip learned, we see the Father; and like James we are called to show that faith in deeds.

Take-home resolution: This week, say “come and see” to one person, and do one work of mercy in the name of Jesus.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we celebrate two apostles about whom the Gospels say very little: Philip and James. They were not the famous ones like Peter or John. But through them, and through the Twelve, the faith has come down to us. St Paul says: I handed on to you what I in turn had received, that Christ died for our sins, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared to Cephas, to the Twelve, and then to James (1 Corinthians 15:3–7).

This is the oldest creed of the Church. It is not a beautiful story invented later. It is the testimony of people who saw the risen Lord with their own eyes, and James was one of them. When we say the Creed on Sunday, we are standing with Philip and James and repeating what they saw.

Look at Philip in the Gospel. He was a practical man. When Jesus asked how to feed five thousand people, Philip calculated: six months' wages would not be enough. And at the Last Supper he asks a very honest question: Lord, show us the Father, and we will be satisfied (John 14:8). Many people today ask the same: show me God, and I will believe.

Jesus answers with tenderness and a little sadness: have I been with you all this time, Philip, and you still do not know me? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father (John 14:9). Philip was looking past Jesus for God. But God was there, at the table, in the face of Jesus. If you want to know what God is like, look at Jesus: touching the leper, forgiving the sinful woman, washing feet, dying on the cross, rising again.

And Philip teaches us how to share this. When he found Jesus, he did not give his friend Nathanael a lecture. He said: come and see (John 1:46). That is still the best way. Bring a friend to Mass, to adoration, to a prayer meeting. Let them see Jesus.

St James, the leader of the Church in Jerusalem, reminds us that faith must be lived: be doers of the word, and not merely hearers (James 1:22). Tradition tells us he died praying for those who killed him. Today let us thank God for the apostles, pray for our bishops, their successors, and ask Philip and James to make us witnesses: people who say, come and see, and people whose lives show what they believe. Amen.

For children

Children, Philip asked Jesus: show us God! And Jesus said: when you see me, you see God the Father. So if you want to know what God is like, look at Jesus: he loves children, he helps the sick, he forgives.

Philip brought his friend to Jesus by saying: come and see! Who can you bring to Jesus? Maybe you can invite a friend to come to church or to say a prayer with you.

Prayer

God our Father, you gladden us each year with the feast of the Apostles Philip and James. Through their prayers, grant us a share in the passion and resurrection of your only-begotten Son, so that we may come to see you for ever. Lord Jesus, way, truth and life, you said to Philip, whoever has seen me has seen the Father: open our eyes to see the Father in your face, and make us, like James, doers of the word and not hearers only. As Philip brought others to you, let us say to our friends, come and see; and through the faith handed on to us by the apostles, keep us witnesses of your Resurrection until you come again. Amen.
