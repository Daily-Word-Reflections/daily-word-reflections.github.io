Word for the day

“Then Mary said, “Here am I, the servant of the Lord; let it be with me according to your word.”” Luke 1:38

Reflection

On the last Sunday of Advent the readings bring two houses face to face. King David, settled in his cedar palace, wants to build a house for the ark of God. Through Nathan, God turns the offer around: “Are you the one to build me a house to live in?… the LORD will make you a house.” God promises David a dynasty, an heir whose throne “shall be established forever”. David wanted to give God a house; God gives David a house, a family line, a future.

In Nazareth the angel Gabriel tells Mary that the promise to David is now fulfilled: her son “will be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give to him the throne of his ancestor David. He will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and of his kingdom there will be no end.” The everlasting house God promised is not made of cedar but of flesh: the Son of God will dwell in the womb of a virgin. Mary herself becomes the house of God, the Ark of the New Covenant.

Mary's question, “How can this be, since I am a virgin?”, is not doubt but a request for understanding. The answer is the Holy Spirit and the power of the Most High overshadowing her, the same verb used of the cloud over the tabernacle (Exodus 40:35). The Church professes that Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary (CCC 484–486). And Mary gives her free consent: “Here am I, the servant of the Lord.” Paul calls this “the mystery that was kept secret for long ages but is now disclosed”, made known to all nations to bring about “the obedience of faith”.

Homily outline

Theme: “Let it be with me.”

God builds David a house; the promise is fulfilled in Mary; Mary's yes opens the door to Christmas.

1. Two houses

  • David wants to build God a house.
  • God promises to build David a house.
  • God's gifts exceed our plans.

He will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and of his kingdom there will be no end.” Luke 1:31–33

2. Nothing is impossible with God

  • Mary asks how.
  • The Spirit overshadows her.
  • The virgin conceives the Son of God.
“Is anything too wonderful for the LORD? At the set time I will return to you, in due season, and Sarah shall have a son.” Genesis 18:14

Apply: Bring an “impossible” situation to God in prayer.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, on this last Sunday before Christmas, the readings tell us about two houses.

King David lived in a palace of cedar. He looked at the ark of God in a tent and felt ashamed: “I live in luxury and God lives in a tent. Let me build God a house.” A good thought. But God answers through the prophet Nathan: “You will build me a house? No, I will build you a house.” God promises David a family line whose throne will last forever.

A thousand years later, in a small village called Nazareth, the angel Gabriel greets a young woman: “You will conceive and bear a son… The Lord God will give him the throne of his ancestor David, and of his kingdom there will be no end.” The promise to David comes true, not in a palace but in a girl's humble home.

Mary asks, “How can this be?” Not doubt, but faith seeking to understand. The angel answers: “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.” Just as God's cloud rested on the tent in the desert, God now rests on Mary. She becomes the house of God, the true Ark of the Covenant.

And then her answer: “Here am I, the servant of the Lord; let it be with me according to your word.” With these words, the whole history of salvation turns. St Bernard imagined the whole world holding its breath, waiting for her yes.

Like David, we often want to do great things for God. But God wants first to do great things in us. This Christmas, he asks to make his home in our hearts. Let us make room, like Mary, and say: “Let it be with me according to your word.”

For children

Children, King David wanted to build a big house for God. But God said: I will build a house for you!

Then God chose Mary to be the mother of Jesus. Mary said yes to God.

Jesus wants to live in our hearts too. Can we say yes like Mary?

This week, help set up the crib at home, and leave the manger empty until Christmas night!

Prayer

Pour forth, we beseech you, O Lord, your grace into our hearts, that we, to whom the Incarnation of Christ your Son was made known by the message of an Angel, may by his Passion and Cross be brought to the glory of his Resurrection. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
