Word for the day

“When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil ways, God changed his mind about the calamity that he had said he would bring upon them; and he did not do it.” Jonah 3:10

Reflection

“The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time.” That little phrase is already Gospel. Jonah had run away the first time, and God gave him a second chance. Now he walks into Nineveh, the vast capital of Assyria, Israel's cruel enemy, and preaches the shortest sermon in the Bible: “Forty days more, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!” Then something astonishing happens. “The people of Nineveh believed God; they proclaimed a fast, and everyone, great and small, put on sackcloth.” The king rises from his throne, takes off his robe and sits in ashes. Even the animals are made to fast. The royal decree says: “All shall turn from their evil ways and from the violence that is in their hands. Who knows? God may relent.” And God does. The forty days of Nineveh are the forty days of our Lent: a time when a whole people, from the palace to the cattle shed, turns back to God.

In the Gospel the crowds keep asking Jesus for a sign. He answers that they will get only one: “the sign of Jonah. For just as Jonah became a sign to the people of Nineveh, so the Son of Man will be to this generation.” Jonah came to Nineveh as a man who had been three days in the belly of the fish; Jesus will give the sign of his own death and resurrection on the third day. The pagans of Nineveh repented at a stranger's five-word sermon, and the queen of the South crossed the earth to hear Solomon. “See, something greater than Jonah is here!” The danger is not that God gives too few signs, but that we who hear Christ every Sunday stop being moved. Today the Church also remembers seven merchants of Florence who in 1233 left their businesses to live in prayer and penance on Monte Senario, serving Our Lady of Sorrows: the Servites. Like Nineveh, they turned around completely.

Homily outline

Theme: “See, something greater than Jonah is here!”

The God of second chances, a city that repents from top to bottom, and the sign of the risen Christ.

1. A second time

  • Jonah ran away.
  • God called again.
  • The shortest sermon.
“Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's walk. And he cried out, “Forty days more, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!”” Jonah 3:4
“The Lord is not slow about his promise, as some think of slowness, but is patient with you, not wanting any to perish, but all to come to repentance.” 2 Peter 3:9

Apply: Thank God for one second chance he has given you.

2. Great and small

  • The people believed.
  • The king sat in ashes.
  • Turning hands as well as hearts.
“The sacrifice acceptable to God is a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise.” Psalm 51:17
“Just so, I tell you, there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who need no repentance.” Luke 15:7

Apply: Make a concrete act of restitution for one wrong you have done.

3. Something greater than Jonah

  • Three days in the fish.
  • Three days in the tomb.
  • Do not grow deaf to Christ.
“But the LORD provided a large fish to swallow up Jonah; and Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.” Jonah 1:17
“The people of Nineveh will rise up at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, because they repented at the proclamation of Jonah, and see, something greater than Jonah is here!” Luke 11:32

Apply: Go to confession before the end of February.

God gives second chances to cities and to sinners.

The risen Christ is the sign: let us turn to him.

Take-home resolution: Celebrate the sacrament of Penance this Lent, and bring your family with you.

Full homily

Dear friends, the word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time (Jonah 3:1). That is good news: God does not give up on us after our first failure.

Jonah enters Nineveh and cries: forty days more, and Nineveh shall be overthrown! (Jonah 3:4). And the people of Nineveh believed God; they proclaimed a fast, and everyone, great and small, put on sackcloth (Jonah 3:5). Even the king stood up from his throne and sat in ashes.

God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil ways, and he did not bring the calamity (Jonah 3:10). Repentance is not just feeling sorry; it is turning, with our hands as well as our hearts.

Jesus says the only sign he will give is the sign of Jonah: his death and resurrection. See, something greater than Jonah is here! (Luke 11:32). If the pagans turned at five words, how much more should we turn at the word of Christ. Amen.

For children

Children, the people of Nineveh were doing many bad things. When Jonah told them to change, they all said sorry to God, even the king! And God forgave them. God always forgives us when we are truly sorry.

Prayer

Merciful God, you sent Jonah to Nineveh a second time and you relented when the people turned to you. Give us a second chance and a contrite heart. May we, who have heard one greater than Jonah, turn from our evil ways and live. Through the prayers of the Seven Holy Founders, help us to stand with Mary at the cross of her Son. Amen.
