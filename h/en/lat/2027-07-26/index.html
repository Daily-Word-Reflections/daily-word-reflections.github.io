Word for the day

“Their bodies are buried in peace, but their name lives on generation after generation. The assembly declares their wisdom, and the congregation proclaims their praise.” Sirach 44:14–15

Reflection

Moses comes down from Sinai carrying “the two tablets of the covenant,” written on both sides, “the work of God.” Below him is the noise of a party. Joshua thinks it is the sound of war; Moses knows better: “it is the sound of revelers that I hear.” When he sees the calf and the dancing his anger burns, and he shatters the tablets at the foot of the mountain, a picture of a covenant broken. He burns the calf, grinds it to powder and makes the people drink it, to show that the idol is nothing. Then he faces Aaron, who gives one of the most comic and most tragic excuses in the Bible: “I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf!” Scripture knows our habit of blaming everything but ourselves. The next day Moses does what the great intercessors do. He tells the people the truth, “You have sinned a great sin,” and then he goes back up the mountain: “perhaps I can make atonement for your sin.” His prayer breaks off in the middle of a sentence: “if you will only forgive their sin” and then, “if not, blot me out of the book that you have written.” He puts his own name on the line for a people who have just betrayed God. The Psalm remembers it: “had not Moses, his chosen one, stood in the breach before him, to turn away his wrath from destroying them.” The Church sees in Moses a shadow of Christ, who “always lives to make intercession for them” (Hebrews 7:25).

In the Gospel Jesus tells two short parables, the mustard seed and the yeast. The kingdom of heaven does not arrive with trumpets; it begins small, hidden, and works from within. The seed is “the smallest of all the seeds,” yet it becomes a tree where birds nest; the yeast is hidden in “three measures of flour,” enough for a whole village's bread, “until all of it was leavened.” St Matthew adds that Jesus speaks in parables to fulfil the word of the Psalm: “I will proclaim what has been hidden from the foundation of the world” (Psalm 78:2). Beside these parables the Church today places the memory of two hidden people, Joachim and Anne. The Gospels never name them. Their names come from the Protoevangelium of James, a second-century writing that the Church does not count as Scripture, but whose names the faithful have honoured in East and West for many centuries. What the Church knows with certainty is simple: Mary had parents. They received her as God's gift, taught her the psalms and the promises of Israel, and handed on a faith in which she could answer the angel: “Here am I, the servant of the Lord.” The Church teaches that Mary was preserved from original sin from the first moment of her conception, yet she was born into a human family, nursed, and taught like any child. A mustard seed of faith passed from grandparents to a girl in Nazareth, and from her the Lord of all took flesh. In Kerala the name of Anne is in every parish, in Annamma and Anna and Ann, and the old stories of Joachim and Anne praying for a child echo the stories of Sarah and Hannah, whose prayer we hear in the Book of Samuel: “if only you will look on the misery of your servant, and remember me” (1 Samuel 1:11).

Homily outline

Theme: “A faith that lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice.”

Moses in the breach, the mustard seed, and the hidden grandparents of Jesus: faith is always handed on.

1. Stand in the breach

  • The calf, the broken tablets, Aaron's excuse.
  • Blot me out of the book.
  • Christ always lives to intercede.
“Had not Moses, his chosen one, stood in the breach before him, to turn away his wrath from destroying them.” Psalm 106:23
“Consequently he is able for all time to save those who approach God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them.” Hebrews 7:25

Apply: Name one person far from God, and pray for them every day this week, asking to stand in the breach for them.

2. The mustard seed

  • The smallest of all the seeds.
  • Yeast hidden in three measures of flour.
  • The kingdom works from within.
“On the mountain height of Israel I will plant it, in order that it may produce boughs and bear fruit, and become a noble cedar. Under it every kind of bird will live; in the shade of its branches will nest winged creatures of every kind.” Ezekiel 17:23
“He put before them another parable: “The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed that someone took and sowed in his field; it is the smallest of all the seeds, but when it has grown it is the greatest of shrubs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and make nests in its branches.”” Matthew 13:31–32

Apply: Do one small good thing today that no one will notice, and trust God to make it grow.

3. Faith handed on

  • Joachim and Anne, hidden grandparents.
  • Mary's Here am I.
  • Tell the next generation.
“We will not hide them from their children; we will tell to the coming generation the glorious deeds of the Lord, and his might, and the wonders that he has done.” Psalm 78:4
“I am reminded of your sincere faith, a faith that lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice and now, I am sure, lives in you.” 2 Timothy 1:5

Apply: Tell one young person this week how you came to believe, and thank the one who first taught you to pray.

Moses shows the heart of an intercessor, who puts his own name on the line for a people who failed.

The kingdom grows from small, hidden beginnings, like the faith that Joachim and Anne handed on to Mary and she to the world.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day for someone far from God, and tell a younger person one story of faith from your family.

Full homily

Dear friends, the people of Israel have made a golden calf, and Moses has to come down the mountain with the tablets in his hands. He sees the dancing and breaks the tablets. And Aaron, the priest, says: I threw the gold in the fire, and out came this calf! (Exodus 32:24). How like us. We make a mess and then say it just happened.

But look at what Moses does next. He does not wash his hands of them. He goes back up the mountain and prays: forgive them, but if not, blot me out of the book that you have written (Exodus 32:32). That is a leader who stands in the breach. The Psalm says it: had not Moses, his chosen one, stood in the breach before him, to turn away his wrath (Psalm 106:23). Every one of us has someone who stood in the breach for us: a mother who prayed, a grandfather who fasted, a grandmother who held the rosary.

In the Gospel Jesus says that the kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed, the smallest of all seeds, and like yeast hidden in flour. God works small and hidden. And today the Church remembers two hidden people. We do not find Joachim and Anne in the Gospels. Their names come from an old book. But we know something certain: Mary had parents, and they taught her to pray and to trust the promises of God. How else could a girl in Nazareth say: Here am I, the servant of the Lord? (Luke 1:38). Faith is always handed on, as St Paul tells Timothy: a faith that lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice and now, I am sure, lives in you (2 Timothy 1:5).

Yesterday we kept the World Day for Grandparents and the Elderly, and today we honour the grandparents of Jesus. So let us ask: who planted the seed of faith in me? And whose seed am I planting? Let us thank God for them, pray for them, and, like Moses, stand in the breach for those who have strayed. Amen.

For children

Children, a mustard seed is tiny, but when it is planted it grows into a big tree where birds can make their nests. Jesus says God's kingdom is like that. Today we remember Mary's mother and father, Joachim and Anne. They loved little Mary and taught her to pray, and she grew up to be the Mother of Jesus. So Joachim and Anne were Jesus' grandparents! Thank God for your own grandparents today, and ask them to tell you a story about when they were small.

Prayer

O God of our fathers, who gave Saints Joachim and Anne the grace of bringing forth the Mother of your incarnate Son, grant, through the prayers of both, that we may attain the salvation you have promised to your people. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord God, you hide your kingdom in small seeds and quiet lives. Bless our grandparents and parents, who plant the faith in the heart of a child. Make us faithful to what they handed on, and, like Moses, ready to stand in the breach for those who have strayed. Amen.
