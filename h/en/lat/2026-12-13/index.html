Reflection

The Entrance Antiphon gives this Sunday its name: “Gaudete in Domino semper”, “Rejoice in the Lord always; again I say, rejoice. The Lord is near” (Philippians 4:4–5). Halfway through Advent the violet softens to rose. Joy is not a break from Advent; it is its heart, because the One we await is already near.

Isaiah's servant says, “The spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me to bring good news to the oppressed, to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives.” Jesus will read these very words in the synagogue at Nazareth and say, “Today this scripture has been fulfilled” (Luke 4:21). The response today is Mary's Magnificat: Christian joy is the joy of the humble whom God has lifted up.

John the Evangelist presents the Baptist as a witness: “He himself was not the light, but he came to testify to the light.” When priests and Levites ask who he is, John answers with three “No”s: not the Messiah, not Elijah, not the prophet. He is only “the voice”. Then he says something every Advent needs to hear: “Among you stands one whom you do not know.” Christ is already in our midst, in the Word, the sacraments, the poor, the person beside us; the question is whether we recognise him.

Paul gives the programme for joyful waiting: “Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances… Do not quench the Spirit… Hold fast to what is good.” And he ends with a promise: “The one who calls you is faithful, and he will do this.” Holiness is God's work in us; our joy rests on his faithfulness.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today is Gaudete Sunday, the Sunday of joy. The priest may wear rose vestments, and we light the rose candle. Why? Because the Lord is near.

The prophet Isaiah says: “The spirit of the Lord is upon me; he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor, to bind up the broken-hearted, to proclaim liberty to captives.” Years later Jesus stood in the synagogue of Nazareth, read these words, and said: “Today this scripture has been fulfilled.” Our joy has a name: Jesus.

Instead of a psalm we sing Mary's song: “My soul rejoices in my God.” Mary was poor and humble, and God did great things for her. Christian joy is not the joy of having everything; it is the joy of being loved by God.

In the Gospel people ask John: “Who are you? Are you the Messiah? Elijah? The prophet?” And he says, “No, no, no. I am only a voice.” And then: “Among you stands one whom you do not know.” Jesus was already there, in the crowd, unnoticed.

Is it the same with us? Jesus is among us: in the Eucharist, in the Word, in the poor at our gate, in the family member we take for granted. Advent asks us to open our eyes.

St Paul gives us a simple programme for this week: “Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances.” Try it: every night, thank God for three joys. And remember his promise: “The one who calls you is faithful, and he will do this.” Rejoice: the Lord is near!
