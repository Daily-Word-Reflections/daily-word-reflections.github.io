Word for the day

“When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth; for he will not speak on his own, but will speak whatever he hears, and he will declare to you the things that are to come.” John 16:13

Reflection

Paul has been hurried out of Thessalonica and Beroea and brought, almost alone, to Athens, the most famous city of learning in the ancient world. Walking its streets, he sees temples, statues and altars everywhere. He is invited to speak at the Areopagus, the hill and council where the city's philosophers and judges met. His speech is a model of how to speak of Christ to people of another culture. He does not begin with insults; he begins with respect: “Athenians, I see how extremely religious you are in every way.” He has noticed an altar “To an unknown god,” and he builds on it: “What therefore you worship as unknown, this I proclaim to you.” Then he speaks of the Creator who “made the world and everything in it,” who does not live in shrines made by hands, who gives to all “life and breath and all things,” and who made all nations from one ancestor, “so that they would search for God and perhaps grope for him and find him, though indeed he is not far from each one of us.” He even quotes their own poets: “In him we live and move and have our being,” and “For we too are his offspring.” Only at the end does he name what is new: God has fixed a day of judgment “by a man whom he has appointed, and of this he has given assurance to all by raising him from the dead.”

At the word “resurrection” the meeting breaks up. “When they heard of the resurrection of the dead, some scoffed; but others said, ‘We will hear you again about this.’” It looks like a failure, and Paul soon leaves for Corinth. Yet Luke adds: “some of them joined him and became believers, including Dionysius the Areopagite and a woman named Damaris, and others with them.” Seeds are sown even where the harvest is small. The Psalm calls the whole creation to praise, “kings of the earth and all peoples,” “young men and women alike, old and young together,” for the Lord's “glory is above earth and heaven.” In the Gospel Jesus explains why the Church's understanding of him can grow without changing: “I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now. When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth.” The Spirit does not bring a new Gospel; “he will take what is mine and declare it to you.” He leads the Church deeper into the one truth that is Christ. That is what Paul did at Athens: he found in the Greeks' own searching a door for the truth of Christ. The Second Vatican Council teaches that the Church rejects nothing that is true and holy in other religions, while she proclaims without ceasing Christ, “the way, and the truth, and the life” (Nostra Aetate 2). In a land of many temples and many seekers like India, Paul at the Areopagus is a teacher for us: respectful, clear, and unashamed of the Resurrection.

Homily outline

Theme: “He will guide you into all the truth.”

Paul at the Areopagus: respect, a shared search, the Resurrection, and the Spirit of truth.

1. To an unknown god

  • A city full of altars.
  • Respect before argument.
  • The Creator of all.
“Truly, you are a God who hides himself, O God of Israel, the Savior.” Isaiah 45:15
“For as I went through the city and looked carefully at the objects of your worship, I found among them an altar with the inscription, ‘To an unknown god.’ What therefore you worship as unknown, this I proclaim to you.” Acts 17:23

Apply: Learn one thing this week about the faith of a neighbour of another religion, and listen without arguing.

2. Not far from each one of us

  • Made from one ancestor.
  • Searching and groping.
  • Life and breath and all things.
“When you search for me, you will find me; if you seek me with all your heart,” Jeremiah 29:13
“so that they would search for God and perhaps grope for him and find him, though indeed he is not far from each one of us.” Acts 17:27

Apply: Take a few moments each day this week simply to be aware that you live and move and have your being in God.

3. Some scoffed, some believed

  • The word “resurrection.”
  • We will hear you again.
  • Dionysius and Damaris.
“For from the greatness and beauty of created things comes a corresponding perception of their Creator.” Wisdom 13:5
“But some of them joined him and became believers, including Dionysius the Areopagite and a woman named Damaris, and others with them.” Acts 17:34

Apply: Do not give up on someone who once laughed at your faith; pray for them by name this week.

4. Into all the truth

  • Many things they cannot bear now.
  • The Spirit takes what is Christ's.
  • Growth without change.
“Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.” Psalm 119:105
“When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth; for he will not speak on his own, but will speak whatever he hears, and he will declare to you the things that are to come.” John 16:13

Apply: Read one paragraph of the Catechism each day until Pentecost, asking the Spirit of truth to guide you.

At Athens Paul showed how to proclaim Christ with respect for the search of others and without hiding the Resurrection.

The Spirit of truth continues that work in the Church, leading us and every people deeper into the one truth that is Christ.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day until Pentecost: Come, Spirit of truth, and guide me and my neighbours into all the truth.

Full homily

Dear friends, imagine Paul in Athens: a Jewish tentmaker standing before the philosophers of the most learned city in the world. What does he say? He does not mock them. He says: Athenians, I see how extremely religious you are in every way (Acts 17:22). He has seen an altar to an unknown god, and he says: what you worship as unknown, this I proclaim to you.

Paul shows us how to speak about Jesus to people of other faiths. First, respect. Look for the good that is already there, the search for God, the prayer, the longing. Second, start from what we share: the one Creator who gives life and breath to all, who made every nation, in whom we live and move and have our being (Acts 17:28). God is not far from each one of us.

Third, do not hide what is new. Paul ends with the Resurrection: God has raised Jesus from the dead. And some laughed. Some said: we will hear you again. Most went away. But Dionysius and a woman named Damaris and others believed. We do not measure success by the size of the crowd. Seeds sown in Athens grew into a Church.

In the Gospel Jesus says: when the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth (John 16:13). The Spirit does not give a different Gospel; he takes what belongs to Jesus and makes it clearer. In our country of many temples and many seekers, let us ask the Spirit to give us Paul's respect and Paul's courage, and to guide us, and our neighbours, into the whole truth of the risen Christ. Amen.

For children

Children, Paul went to a big city called Athens, full of statues of gods. He saw an altar that said: to an unknown god. Paul said: I know this God! He made everything, he gives you life, and he is very close to you. His Son Jesus rose from the dead. When we talk to friends who do not know Jesus, let us be kind and polite like Paul, and tell them how good God is.

Prayer

God our Creator, in you we live and move and have our being, and you are not far from each one of us. You let the peoples of the earth search for you and grope for you; reveal to them your Son, whom you raised from the dead. Give us the respect, clarity and courage of St Paul at Athens. Send us the Spirit of truth, who takes what belongs to Jesus and declares it to us, so that we may be led into all the truth and praise you with all creation. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
