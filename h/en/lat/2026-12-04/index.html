Word for the day

“Then he touched their eyes and said, “According to your faith let it be done to you.”” Matthew 9:29

Reflection

Isaiah describes the transformation God will bring: Lebanon becomes a fruitful field, “the deaf shall hear the words of a scroll, and out of their gloom and darkness the eyes of the blind shall see”, the meek find joy, the tyrant disappears. Advent is the season of this hope.

Two blind men follow Jesus crying, “Have mercy on us, Son of David!” They call him by his messianic title, though they cannot see him. In the house Jesus asks them one question: “Do you believe that I am able to do this?” “Yes, Lord.” He touches their eyes: “According to your faith let it be done to you.” Their eyes are opened. Faith sees before the eyes do. St John Damascene, remembered today, defended the veneration of icons: in Christ the invisible God has become visible, and we may look on his face.

Full homily

Dear friends, on this First Friday of December Isaiah promises a reversal: in a very little while Lebanon shall be turned into a fruitful field. On that day the deaf shall hear the words of a scroll, and out of their gloom and darkness the eyes of the blind shall see (Isaiah 29:17–18). The meek shall obtain fresh joy in the Lord.

In the Gospel two blind men follow Jesus, crying out: have mercy on us, Son of David! It is a cry of faith; they call him by his messianic title. Jesus asks them: do you believe that I am able to do this? They say: yes, Lord. He touches their eyes and says: according to your faith let it be done to you (Matthew 9:27–29).

Jesus asks the same question of each of us this Advent: do you believe that I am able to do this? Able to heal your blindness, to change your heart, to restore your family, to forgive your sin?

There is a blindness worse than physical blindness: not seeing God's love, not seeing the needs of others, not seeing our own sins. The two blind men knew they were blind. Many of us do not.

On the First Friday we honour the Sacred Heart of Jesus. From that Heart flows the mercy the blind men begged for. The simplest prayer of the Church, Lord, have mercy, is their prayer.

Today, let us make their prayer ours. Let us name one blindness in our life and ask Jesus to touch it. If possible, let us go to confession and receive Holy Communion in honour of the Sacred Heart. And let us answer his question with faith: yes, Lord, I believe. Amen.

For children

Children, two blind men asked Jesus for help and He opened their eyes. When you pray, believe that Jesus can help you!

Prayer

Son of David, have mercy on me. Touch the eyes of my heart, increase my faith, and be my light and my salvation this Advent. Amen.
