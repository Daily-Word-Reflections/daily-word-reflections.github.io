The Conversion of Saint Paul, Apostle

25 January 2027 · Latin Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading Acts 22:3–16 (or Acts 9:1–22) · Get up, be baptized, and have your sins washed away
  • Responsorial Psalm Psalm 117:1, 2 · Go out to all the world and tell the good news
  • Gospel Mark 16:15–18 · Go into all the world and proclaim the good news

Word for the day

“I asked, ‘What am I to do, Lord?’ The Lord said to me, ‘Get up and go to Damascus; there you will be told everything that has been assigned to you to do.’” Acts 22:10

Reflection

The Church celebrates no other conversion with a feast of its own; Paul's is unique because it shows what grace can do with the fiercest enemy. Paul tells the story himself, standing before a hostile crowd in Jerusalem: “I persecuted this Way up to the point of death.” On the road to Damascus, at noon, “a great light from heaven suddenly shone about me. I fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to me, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?’” He asks: “Who are you, Lord?” And the answer reveals everything: “I am Jesus of Nazareth whom you are persecuting.”

Jesus does not say “my followers” but “me”. Whoever touches the Church touches Christ, for the Church is his Body. Blinded, Paul is led by the hand into Damascus. There Ananias, a devout disciple, comes to him: “Brother Saul, regain your sight!” and tells him: “you will be his witness to all the world of what you have seen and heard. And now why do you delay? Get up, be baptized, and have your sins washed away, calling on his name.” Conversion is not complete without the Church: Paul needs Ananias, Baptism and the community he once persecuted.

The Gospel gives the risen Lord's command: “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” Paul became the great missionary of that command. The Week of Prayer for Christian Unity ends today, because Paul, who once divided by violence, became the apostle who wrote: “There is one body and one Spirit… one Lord, one faith, one baptism.” No one is beyond conversion, and no division is beyond healing.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear friends, today the Church celebrates a conversion. Saul of Tarsus says of himself: I persecuted this Way up to the point of death, binding both men and women and putting them in prison (Acts 22:4).

On the road to Damascus a great light shone and he heard: Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? He asked: who are you, Lord? And the answer: I am Jesus of Nazareth whom you are persecuting (Acts 22:7–8). Jesus does not say my followers; he says me. Christ and his Church are one Body.

Blind, Saul is led into Damascus. There Ananias, a disciple who was afraid of him, calls him brother and says: get up, be baptized, and have your sins washed away, calling on his name (Acts 22:16). Conversion happens within the Church, through a brother, through a sacrament.

The persecutor becomes the apostle. The risen Lord says: go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation (Mark 16:15). Paul carried that command across the Roman world.

Today the Week of Prayer for Christian Unity ends. Paul, who once divided by violence, wrote about one Lord, one faith, one baptism. Let us pray that every division among Christians may be healed.

No one is beyond conversion. Let us ask Paul's question every day: what am I to do, Lord? Amen.

Homily option 2 · Ananias, the disciple who was afraid

Every conversion story has two heroes: the one who is converted, and the one who dares to welcome him. Today we look at Ananias.

1. A frightened disciple

  • The Lord calls Ananias.
  • He knows Saul's reputation.
  • He objects honestly.
📖 Jonah 1:1–3
📖 Acts 9:10–14

Apply: Tell God honestly what you fear about someone.

2. Go, for he is my chosen instrument

  • God sees what we cannot.
  • A persecutor chosen.
  • Ananias obeys.
📖 1 Samuel 16:7
📖 Acts 9:15–16

Apply: Pray for someone you have written off.

3. Brother Saul

  • He lays hands on him.
  • The first word is brother.
  • Scales fall; Baptism follows.
📖 Genesis 33:4
📖 Acts 9:17–18

Apply: Welcome a newcomer or a returning Catholic as brother or sister.

Without Ananias there might have been no Paul.

God still needs disciples brave enough to say brother.

Take-home resolution: This week I will reach out to one person I have been afraid of or have given up on.

Dear friends, today we celebrate the conversion of Paul. But there is another hero in the story, a quiet disciple in Damascus named Ananias.

The Lord said to him: get up and go to the street called Straight, and look for a man of Tarsus named Saul. Ananias answered honestly: Lord, I have heard from many about this man, how much evil he has done to your saints (Acts 9:11, 13). He was afraid, and he had good reason to be.

But the Lord said: go, for he is an instrument whom I have chosen to bring my name before Gentiles and kings (Acts 9:15). God sees what we cannot see. The man everyone feared was the man God had chosen.

So Ananias went, laid his hands on Saul and said: Brother Saul (Acts 9:17). His first word was brother. Something like scales fell from Saul's eyes, and he was baptised.

Who is the Saul in my life? The person I fear, avoid, or have given up on? Perhaps God is sending me, as he sent Ananias, to say: brother, sister. And let us bring that person to the Mass in our prayer today. Amen.

Homily option 3 · What changes in a conversion

Saul did not change his zeal, his intelligence or his energy. He changed his centre. Conversion is not becoming someone else but becoming who God meant.

1. Everything as loss

  • His pedigree.
  • His achievements.
  • Christ the surpassing value.
📖 Jeremiah 9:23–24
📖 Philippians 3:7–8

Apply: Name one thing you are proud of and offer it to Christ.

2. A new creation

  • The old has passed away.
  • Everything has become new.
  • Reconciled to God.
📖 Isaiah 43:18–19
📖 2 Corinthians 5:17

Apply: Make a good confession this week.

3. Christ lives in me

  • Crucified with Christ.
  • The life I now live.
  • Faith in the Son of God who loved me.
📖 Ezekiel 36:26–27
📖 Galatians 2:20

Apply: Pray each morning: Lord, live in me today.

Conversion keeps our gifts but changes our centre.

Paul's zeal became love for Christ and for all peoples.

Take-home resolution: Each morning this week I will pray Galatians 2:20: it is Christ who lives in me.

Dear brothers and sisters, what changed when Saul became Paul? Not his intelligence: he remained a brilliant scholar. Not his energy: he remained tireless. Not his zeal: he remained on fire. What changed was the centre of his life.

He writes: whatever gains I had, these I have come to regard as loss because of Christ (Philippians 3:7). His birth, his learning, his religious record, all the things he had been proud of, he now laid at the feet of Christ.

He discovered that if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation: everything old has passed away; see, everything has become new (2 Corinthians 5:17). This is what happens in every good confession: the old passes, the new begins.

And he could finally say: it is no longer I who live, but it is Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me (Galatians 2:20).

God does not want to destroy our gifts but to set them on the right centre. Let us offer him our pride, make a good confession, and pray each day: Lord, live in me. And receive him in the Mass as Paul's whole life. Amen.

For children

Children, Saul hurt Christians, but Jesus spoke to him in a bright light and changed his heart. He became St Paul!

Jesus can change anyone. Let us pray for someone who does not know Jesus.

Prayer

O God, who taught the whole world through the preaching of the blessed Apostle Paul, draw us, we pray, nearer to you through the example of him whose conversion we celebrate today, and so make us witnesses to your truth in the world. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen.
