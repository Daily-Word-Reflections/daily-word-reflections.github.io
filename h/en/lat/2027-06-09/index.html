Word for the day

“Do not think that I have come to abolish the law or the prophets; I have come not to abolish but to fulfill.” Matthew 5:17

Reflection

In the Second Letter to the Corinthians Paul has to defend his ministry against people who came to Corinth with impressive letters of recommendation. He answers: I need no letters; “You yourselves are our letter, written on our hearts,” “written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts.” Today's reading continues: “Not that we are competent of ourselves to claim anything as coming from us; our competence is from God, who has made us competent to be ministers of a new covenant, not of letter but of spirit; for the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.” Paul is thinking of Jeremiah's promise of a new covenant, when God would write his law on the heart, and of Ezekiel's promise of a new spirit. He compares the giving of the Law on Sinai, which came “in glory so that the people of Israel could not gaze at Moses' face because of the glory of his face,” with the ministry of the Spirit, and concludes: “how much more will the ministry of the Spirit come in glory?” We must read this carefully. Paul, a Jew who loved the Law, does not say the Law was bad. Elsewhere he writes: “the law is holy, and the commandment is holy and just and good” (Romans 7:12). He means that a law written outside us, on stone, can show us what is right but cannot give us the power to do it; it can only condemn the one who fails. The Spirit writes God's will within us and gives life. The glory of Sinai was real; the glory of the Spirit is greater and lasting. The Psalm sings the holiness of the God who spoke to Moses and Aaron and Samuel “in the pillar of cloud” and was “a forgiving God to them.”

The Gospel guards us against a wrong conclusion. If the Spirit gives life and the letter kills, should Christians throw away the commandments? Jesus answers clearly: “Do not think that I have come to abolish the law or the prophets; I have come not to abolish but to fulfill.” He respects even the smallest marks of the Scripture: “not one letter, not one stroke of a letter, will pass from the law until all is accomplished.” The Ten Commandments still stand; Jesus will show in the coming days that he asks for more, not less: not only no murder, but no anger; not only no adultery, but a pure heart. Fulfilment means bringing the Law to its full meaning, as a flower fulfils the bud. Jesus fulfils the Law by living it perfectly, by revealing its heart, which is love, and by giving the Spirit who makes us able to keep it. St Ephrem the Syrian, whom we may remember today, was born around 306 in Nisibis, served the Church there as a deacon and teacher, and spent his last years in Edessa, where he died in 373. He put the whole faith into hymns and taught choirs to sing them; the Syriac Churches call him the Harp of the Spirit. He wrote (in substance) that no one who drinks from the spring of God's word should be sad that he cannot drink it all; let him rejoice that it is inexhaustible. In 1920 Pope Benedict XV declared him a Doctor of the Church. The Kerala Churches of the Syriac tradition still sing his words.

Homily outline

Theme: “The Spirit gives life.”

A letter written on hearts, the holy Law fulfilled and not abolished, and the Spirit who makes us keep it with love.

1. Our competence is from God

  • You are our letter.
  • Not with ink but with the Spirit.
  • Ministers of a new covenant.
“Now go, and I will be with your mouth and teach you what you are to speak.” Exodus 4:12
“Not that we are competent of ourselves to claim anything as coming from us; our competence is from God,” 2 Corinthians 3:5

Apply: Before any task of service this week, pray: Lord, my competence is from you.

2. The letter and the Spirit

  • The Law is holy.
  • Stone tablets and hearts of flesh.
  • The Spirit writes within.
“I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.” Jeremiah 31:33
“So the law is holy, and the commandment is holy and just and good.” Romans 7:12

Apply: Choose one commandment you find hard and pray each day this week for the Spirit to write it on your heart.

3. Not to abolish but to fulfill

  • Not one stroke of a letter.
  • More, not less.
  • The Harp of the Spirit sings it.
“The law of the Lord is perfect, reviving the soul; the decrees of the Lord are sure, making wise the simple;” Psalm 19:7
“Therefore, whoever breaks one of the least of these commandments, and teaches others to do the same, will be called least in the kingdom of heaven; but whoever does them and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven.” Matthew 5:19

Apply: Teach one child, by word or example, one of God's commandments this week.

Paul's ministry is the work of the Spirit, who writes God's holy Law on human hearts and gives life.

Jesus fulfils the Law and the prophets; St Ephrem teaches us to sing that fulfilment with our whole life.

Take-home resolution: Each day this week, keep one commandment from the heart, and thank the Spirit who makes it possible.

Full homily

Dear friends, some preachers in Corinth carried impressive letters of recommendation, and they asked: where are Paul's letters? Paul answers: you are my letter. You yourselves are our letter, written on our hearts (2 Corinthians 3:2), written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God. The best proof of a minister's work is not a certificate but a changed life.

Then Paul says: the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life (2 Corinthians 3:6). He does not mean that God's Law is bad. Paul loved the Law. He means that a law written only on stone, outside us, can tell us what is right, but it cannot give us the strength to do it. When we fail, it can only condemn us. The Holy Spirit writes God's will inside us, in our hearts, and gives us the power to live it. That is the new covenant God promised through the prophet Jeremiah.

But the Gospel warns us not to go to the other extreme. Jesus says: do not think that I have come to abolish the law or the prophets; I have come not to abolish but to fulfill (Matthew 5:17). The commandments are not thrown away. Jesus shows their full meaning and gives us his Spirit to keep them with love. A child obeys his mother first because he must, and later because he loves her. That is the journey from letter to Spirit.

Today the Church remembers St Ephrem, a deacon of the fourth century, whom our Syriac sister Churches in Kerala call Mar Aprem. He turned the faith into songs and taught people to sing it. He is called the Harp of the Holy Spirit. Let us ask him to help us sing God's commandments in our hearts, and to become letters of Christ that our families and neighbours can read. Amen.

For children

Children, imagine your mother writes on a paper: brush your teeth, say your prayers, help your little brother. You can obey the paper because you have to. But Jesus wants something better: he wants the Holy Spirit to write God's rules inside your heart, so that you do good because you love God. Jesus said: I did not come to throw away God's commandments, but to fulfill them. Today we remember St Ephrem, who wrote beautiful songs about Jesus. Sing a hymn to Jesus today!

Prayer

Holy God, you spoke to Moses in the cloud and gave your people the holy Law. Now through Jesus Christ you have made us ministers of a new covenant, not of letter but of Spirit. Write your will on our hearts, so that we keep your commandments not from fear but from love. Lord Jesus, you came not to abolish but to fulfill; bring our lives to their full meaning in you. Through the prayers of St Ephrem, Harp of the Spirit, may our words and songs give you glory. Amen.
