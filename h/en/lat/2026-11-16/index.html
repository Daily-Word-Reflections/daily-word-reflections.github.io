Word for the day

“Jesus said to him, “Receive your sight; your faith has saved you.”” Luke 18:42

Reflection

In the last weeks of the year the first readings come from the Book of Revelation. The risen Christ speaks to the church of Ephesus, praising its hard work and endurance, but with one complaint: “you have abandoned the love you had at first”. A community can be orthodox and busy and still have grown cold. The remedy: remember, repent, and do the works you did at first.

Near Jericho a blind beggar hears that Jesus is passing and cries out, “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!” The crowd tells him to be quiet; he shouts all the more. Jesus stops and asks, “What do you want me to do for you?” “Lord, let me see again.” He receives his sight and follows Jesus, glorifying God. Persistent prayer, a clear request, and a life of following: this is how first love is recovered.

Theme: “Lord, let me see again.”

Cry out, return to first love, ask for sight and follow.

  • He shouted all the more.

  • Remember where you fell from.

“But I have this against you, that you have abandoned the love you had at first. Remember then from what you have fallen; repent, and do the works you did at first.” Revelation 2:4–5

  • Jesus asks what we want.

  • Healing leads to following.

Apply: Ask Jesus plainly for what you need.

Full homily

Dear friends, in these last weeks of the year the Church reads the Book of Revelation. Today the risen Christ speaks to the church of Ephesus. He praises its hard work and endurance, but he has one complaint: you have abandoned the love you had at first (Revelation 2:4). A community can be correct and busy and still have grown cold.

The remedy is threefold: remember from where you have fallen, repent, and do the works you did at first (Revelation 2:5). Remember the joy of your First Communion, your wedding day, your first steps in prayer. Love is renewed by returning to its source.

Near Jericho a blind beggar hears that Jesus is passing by and cries out: Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me! The crowd tells him to be quiet, and he cries out all the more. Jesus asks: what do you want me to do for you? Lord, let me see again (Luke 18:38–41).

Let us make his prayer our own: Lord, let me see. Let me see where my love has cooled, and let me see you again as I saw you at first. And when we see, let us do what the blind man did: follow him, glorifying God. Amen.

For children

Children, the blind man shouted to Jesus and did not stop. Jesus heard him! Pray today: Jesus, help me to see you in everyone.

Prayer

Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me. Open my eyes, rekindle the love I had at first, and let me follow you on the way, glorifying God. Amen.
