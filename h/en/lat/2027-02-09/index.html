Word for the day

“So God created humankind in his image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them.” Genesis 1:27

Reflection

The creation account reaches its summit. After the fish and birds and animals, God says something new: “Let us make humankind in our image, according to our likeness.” Only of the human being is this said. “So God created humankind in his image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them.” Every person, rich or poor, born or unborn, healthy or disabled, carries the image of God. Man and woman together reflect him, and they receive a task: to fill the earth and care for it as God's stewards. Then comes the seventh day: God “rested on the seventh day from all the work that he had done. So God blessed the seventh day and hallowed it.” Our work finds its meaning in rest and worship.

The Psalm marvels: “what are human beings that you are mindful of them…? Yet you have made them a little lower than God, and crowned them with glory and honor.” But the Gospel shows how that glory can be emptied. The Pharisees criticise the disciples for eating without the ritual washing of hands. Jesus answers with Isaiah: “This people honors me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me.” They had let human customs smother God's command, even using the vow of “Corban” to avoid supporting their parents. “You abandon the commandment of God and hold to human tradition.” On the eve of Lent the question is pointed: is my religion a matter of the lips, or of the heart that bears God's image?

Full homily

Dear friends, today the creation story reaches its summit: God said, let us make humankind in our image, according to our likeness (Genesis 1:26). Only of us is this said.

So God created humankind in his image, male and female he created them (Genesis 1:27). Every person bears God's image: the child in the womb, the poor man at the gate, the old woman in her bed. Then God rested on the seventh day and blessed it. Our work finds its meaning in rest and worship.

In the Gospel Jesus quotes Isaiah: this people honours me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me (Mark 7:6). Some had used religious customs to avoid caring for their own parents. Jesus says: you abandon the commandment of God and hold to human tradition (Mark 7:8).

Tomorrow is Ash Wednesday. Lent asks one thing: give God your heart, not only your lips. Let us choose tonight how we will pray, fast and give, and let God restore his image in us. Amen.

For children

Children, God made you like himself! That is why every person is special. This Lent, let us love Jesus with our hearts, not only with our words.

Prayer

God our Creator, you made us in your image and crowned us with glory and honour. Keep us from honouring you with our lips while our hearts are far from you. As Lent begins, turn our hearts back to you and teach us to see your image in every person. Amen.
