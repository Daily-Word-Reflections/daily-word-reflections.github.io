Word for the day

“He woke up and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Peace! Be still!” Then the wind ceased, and there was a dead calm.” Mark 4:39

Reflection

Few readers in Kerala need to be told what a storm at sea means. In these weeks of the monsoon the waves come high on the beaches of Vizhinjam, Chellanam and Ponnani, the small boats are pulled up on the sand, and fishing families watch the sky. Today's readings are full of that same sea. The first comes from the end of the Book of Job. For thirty-seven chapters Job has suffered, argued with his friends and demanded an answer from God. Now at last “the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind.” But God does not explain Job's suffering. He asks Job to look at creation, and in the verses we hear today he speaks of the sea: “Or who shut in the sea with doors when it burst out from the womb?” The sea in the Bible is the image of all that is wild, dark and threatening, the chaos that God tamed at creation. And God speaks of it as a mother speaks of a newborn child: “when I made the clouds its garment, and thick darkness its swaddling band.” The terrifying ocean is, to God, an infant in swaddling clothes. Then he sets its limits: “and prescribed bounds for it, and set bars and doors, and said, ‘Thus far shall you come, and no farther, and here shall your proud waves be stopped.’” Job is not answered with an argument; he is answered with a Presence greater than his storm. The Psalm tells the story of sailors caught in such a storm: “They mounted up to heaven, they went down to the depths; their courage melted away in their calamity.” Then “they cried to the LORD in their trouble, and he brought them out from their distress; he made the storm be still, and the waves of the sea were hushed.”

Mark's Gospel takes us onto the Sea of Galilee at evening. Jesus has spent the day teaching the crowds in parables from a boat. Now he says, “Let us go across to the other side,” to the Gentile shore. The disciples take him “just as he was,” tired from the day. A sudden windstorm comes down on the lake, as often happens there, and “the waves beat into the boat, so that the boat was already being swamped.” Several of these men were fishermen who knew this lake; they are terrified. And Jesus? “He was in the stern, asleep on the cushion.” Only Mark gives us that small detail, the cushion, the kind of thing an eyewitness remembers; the ancient tradition says Mark wrote down Peter's preaching. They wake him with a cry that is half prayer, half reproach: “Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing?” Many of us have prayed that prayer. Jesus rises. “He woke up and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, ‘Peace! Be still!’ Then the wind ceased, and there was a dead calm.” He speaks to the storm as he spoke to the unclean spirit in the synagogue of Capernaum, with a word of command. Then he turns to his disciples: “Why are you afraid? Have you still no faith?” And they are filled with a new and greater fear, the awe that falls on people in the presence of God: “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?” Every Jew who knew Job 38 and Psalm 107 knew the answer. Only the Lord shuts in the sea with doors; only the Lord makes the storm be still. The man asleep on the cushion is the Creator of the sea. Mark does not write the answer; he leaves it for us to say.

St Paul gives us the answer in the second reading, and shows us what follows from it. “For the love of Christ urges us on, because we are convinced that one has died for all; therefore all have died. And he died for all, so that those who live might live no longer for themselves, but for him who died and was raised for them.” The one who calmed the sea is the one who went down into the deepest storm of all, death itself, and rose. He did it for all. So Paul can say: “From now on, therefore, we regard no one from a human point of view.” We can no longer look at anyone only by caste, wealth, language or religion; each one is a person for whom Christ died. “So if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation: everything old has passed away; see, everything has become new!” The God who in the beginning set bounds to the sea is making all things new in Christ. The early Christians loved to see the Church in the boat on the lake: tossed by the waves of history, often afraid, sometimes feeling that the Lord is asleep, but never alone, because he is in the boat. Pope Francis preached on this Gospel on the evening of 27 March 2020, alone in a rain-soaked St Peter's Square during the pandemic, and said that we had discovered that we are all in the same boat. Jesus does not promise us a sea without storms. He promises that he is in the boat, and that the storm has a limit: thus far, and no farther.

Homily outline

Theme: “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?”

The sea that God sets within bounds, the Lord asleep in the boat, the word that calms the storm, and the love that makes us a new creation.

Apply: Write down the storm you are facing and, beneath it, Job 38:11; pray it each evening this week.

  • Just as he was, tired.
  • The waves beat into the boat.

“He who keeps Israel will neither slumber nor sleep.” Psalm 121:4

Apply: When you feel that God is silent, keep praying honestly, as the disciples did; do not stop calling him.

Apply: Make an act of faith in the divinity of Christ this week, at the elevation of the Host: My Lord and my God.

Apply: Do one act of love this week for someone outside your circle, remembering that Christ died for that person too.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, in these monsoon weeks we know what a storm is. The rain beats on the roof, the rivers rise, and on our coasts the fishing boats lie on the sand while the sea roars. Today the Word of God takes us out onto the water, into a storm, and asks us a simple question: who is in the boat with you?

In the first reading God answers Job out of the whirlwind. Job had suffered terribly and asked God why. God does not give him an explanation. He speaks of the sea. Who shut in the sea with doors when it burst out from the womb? (Job 38:8). To us the sea is huge and frightening. To God it is like a newborn baby wrapped in cloth. And God says to it: thus far shall you come, and no farther, and here shall your proud waves be stopped (Job 38:11). Every storm has a limit, and God sets it.

In the Gospel the disciples are on the lake at night. A great windstorm comes, the waves fill the boat, and these strong fishermen are terrified. And Jesus is asleep on a cushion in the stern. They wake him: Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing? (Mark 4:38). Have we not prayed like that? Lord, my child is sick, my husband has no work, my family is breaking: do you not care?

Jesus gets up. He rebukes the wind and says to the sea: Peace! Be still! And there is a dead calm (Mark 4:39). Then he asks them: why are you afraid? Have you still no faith? And they ask each other: who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him? The answer is in the first reading. Only God can command the sea. The man sleeping on the cushion is the Lord of creation. He is truly man, tired enough to sleep in a storm, and truly God, able to calm it with a word.

St Paul tells us what this Lord has done for us: the love of Christ urges us on, because we are convinced that one has died for all (2 Corinthians 5:14). He went down into the greatest storm, death itself, and came out victorious. So if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation. Jesus does not promise that our boat will never meet a storm. He promises that he is in the boat, and that the storm will not have the last word.

Today many of us remember our fathers. A good father is not a man who never faces storms. He is a man who stays in the boat with his family and keeps calling on the Lord. Let us pray for our fathers, for our fishermen at sea, and for everyone in a storm today. And when we are afraid, let us not be ashamed to wake the Lord with our prayer. He hears, and he says to us: Peace! Be still! Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever been frightened by thunder and lightning at night? Jesus' friends were in a boat when a huge storm came, and the waves splashed in. They were very scared. But Jesus was asleep! They woke him up, and Jesus stood up and said to the wind: Peace! Be still! And the storm stopped.

Jesus is stronger than every storm. So when you hear thunder, or when something at school or at home makes you afraid, you can say: Jesus, you are with me. I am not afraid. And today, give your Papa a big hug and say thank you to him.

Prayer

Grant, O Lord, that we may always revere and love your holy name, for you never deprive of your guidance those you set firm on the foundation of your love. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, you slept in the boat and rose to rebuke the wind. When the waves beat into our homes and our hearts, and we cry out that you do not care, wake in us the faith that you are with us in the boat. Say to our storms: Peace! Be still! Protect fishermen and all who travel on the sea, bless our fathers, and let your love urge us on, so that we live no longer for ourselves but for you, who died and were raised for us. Amen.
