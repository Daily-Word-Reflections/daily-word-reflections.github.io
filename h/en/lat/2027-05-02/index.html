Word for the day

“This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you.” John 15:12

Reflection

The first reading takes us to Caesarea, the Roman port city on the coast of Judea, and to the house of a Roman army officer. Cornelius is a centurion, “a devout man who feared God with all his household,” who gave alms and prayed constantly. An angel has told him to send for Peter; Peter, meanwhile, has seen a strange vision on a rooftop in Joppa and has heard a voice: “What God has made clean, you must not call profane.” Now Peter enters the house of a Gentile, something a devout Jew of the time would not normally do. Cornelius falls at his feet to worship him, “But Peter made him get up, saying, ‘Stand up; I am only a mortal.’” Then Peter speaks words that open a new chapter in the history of the Church: “I truly understand that God shows no partiality, but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him.” And before Peter has even finished, God himself settles the question: “the Holy Spirit fell upon all who heard the word.” The Jewish believers who came with Peter are astounded “that the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out even on the Gentiles,” for they hear them “speaking in tongues and extolling God.” It is a Pentecost in a Gentile home. Peter can only draw the conclusion: “Can anyone withhold the water for baptizing these people who have received the Holy Spirit just as we have?” And he orders them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ.

The Psalm sings what Caesarea shows: “O sing to the Lord a new song, for he has done marvelous things.” “The Lord has made known his victory; he has revealed his vindication in the sight of the nations.” The God of Israel “has remembered his steadfast love and faithfulness to the house of Israel,” and now “all the ends of the earth have seen the victory of our God.” The faithfulness of God to his own people does not shut the door to the nations; it is the door through which they come in.

The second reading gives us the shortest and perhaps the deepest definition of God in the whole Bible: “God is love.” St John does not say that God has love, or that God sometimes loves, but that love is what God is. That is why “everyone who loves is born of God and knows God,” and “whoever does not love does not know God.” Then John tells us what kind of love he means, so that we do not confuse it with a feeling: “In this is love, not that we loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the atoning sacrifice for our sins.” Love begins with God, not with us. It is first received, then given. Our love for one another is not the starting point of religion but its fruit.

The Gospel continues the Last Supper discourse, just after the image of the vine heard last Sunday. Jesus now speaks of the source of the vine's life: “As the Father has loved me, so I have loved you; abide in my love.” There is a chain of love: from the Father to the Son, from the Son to the disciples, and from the disciples to one another. “This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you.” The measure is not “as you love yourself” but “as I have loved you,” and within hours he will show what that means: “No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends.” Then comes one of the most moving sentences Jesus ever spoke: “I do not call you servants any longer, because the servant does not know what the master is doing; but I have called you friends.” God's own Son calls us his friends, and he shares with us all that he has heard from the Father. And it is his choice before it is ours: “You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last.” At Caesarea the friendship of Jesus reached people whom many thought could never be his friends. Today, as the Ascension and Pentecost draw near, the same Spirit reminds the Church that no one is outside the reach of that love.

Homily outline

Theme: “I have called you friends.”

A Gentile Pentecost, the God who is love, the measure of the Cross, and the friendship of Christ.

1. God shows no partiality

  • Peter enters a Gentile house.
  • Stand up; I am only a mortal.
  • The Spirit comes before Baptism.
“For the Lord your God is God of gods and Lord of lords, the great God, mighty and awesome, who is not partial and takes no bribe,” Deuteronomy 10:17
“Then Peter began to speak to them: “I truly understand that God shows no partiality, but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him.” Acts 10:34–35

Apply: Notice this week one person you keep at a distance because of their background, and take one step towards them.

2. God is love

  • Not that God has love: God is love.
  • Love begins with him.
  • Our love is the fruit, not the root.
“I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore I have continued my faithfulness to you.” Jeremiah 31:3
“In this is love, not that we loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the atoning sacrifice for our sins.” 1 John 4:10

Apply: Each morning this week, before you ask God for anything, thank him for loving you first.

3. As I have loved you

  • The chain of love.
  • The new measure.
  • Laying down one's life in small things.
“Many waters cannot quench love, neither can floods drown it.” Song of Songs 8:7
“No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends.” John 15:13

Apply: Choose one daily sacrifice for someone in your home this week, and offer it quietly to Jesus.

4. I have called you friends

  • Abraham and Moses, friends of God.
  • No secrets kept back.
  • Chosen to bear fruit that lasts.
“Thus the Lord used to speak to Moses face to face, as one speaks to a friend.” Exodus 33:11
“I do not call you servants any longer, because the servant does not know what the master is doing; but I have called you friends, because I have made known to you everything that I have heard from my Father.” John 15:15

Apply: Spend ten minutes before the Blessed Sacrament or with the Gospel this week, speaking to Jesus as to a friend.

At Caesarea God showed that his love has no borders; in the First Letter of John he showed that love is his very being.

In the Gospel Jesus makes us his friends and sends us out to love as he has loved: this is the fruit that lasts.

Take-home resolution: Love one person this week as Jesus loves you: first, without waiting to be repaid, and at a cost.

Full homily

Dear friends, a few weeks ago we heard Jesus say, I am the vine, you are the branches. Today he tells us what flows through that vine: love. As the Father has loved me, so I have loved you; abide in my love (John 15:9). The Father loves the Son; the Son loves us; and we are asked to love one another with that same love.

In the first reading Peter does something new. He enters the house of Cornelius, a Roman officer, a Gentile. Cornelius falls at his feet, and Peter says: stand up; I am only a mortal (Acts 10:26). Then Peter says: I truly understand that God shows no partiality (Acts 10:34). And while he is still speaking, the Holy Spirit falls on everyone in that house. God did not wait for the Church's committee to decide. He poured out his love first.

St John says it in three words: God is love (1 John 4:8). Not that God has some love, but that love is what God is. And this love began with him: not that we loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son (1 John 4:10). We do not earn God's love by being good. We become good because we are loved.

Then Jesus gives the measure: love one another as I have loved you (John 15:12). Not as much as is convenient, not only those who love us back. As I have loved you. And how did he love? No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends (John 15:13). Every mother who sits up at night with a sick child, every husband who forgives, every young person who stands by a friend in trouble, lives a small part of this love.

And then the most beautiful word: I do not call you servants any longer; I have called you friends (John 15:15). We are not servants waiting for orders. We are friends to whom Jesus has told everything. You did not choose me but I chose you. He chose you. Today. Here.

In this month of May, let us stay close to Mary, who knew this love better than anyone. Next Sunday, in India, we will celebrate the Ascension; then we will wait with Mary for the Holy Spirit. Let us open our hearts, our homes and our parish to everyone, as Peter opened his to Cornelius, and let our love be the fruit that lasts. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, who is your best friend? Jesus says: you are my friends! Not my servants, my friends. He tells us everything his Father told him, and he loves us so much that he gave his life for us on the cross.

And Jesus asks us: love one another as I have loved you. That means being kind even to children who are different from us, who speak another language or who have no friends. Let us say: Jesus, my friend, help me to love like you!

Prayer

Father, you are love, and you loved us first: you sent your Son as the atoning sacrifice for our sins, and in him you show no partiality, but welcome everyone who fears you and does what is right. Lord Jesus, you call us no longer servants but friends; keep us in your love as you abide in the Father's love, and teach us to love one another as you have loved us, even to laying down our lives. Pour out your Holy Spirit on us, as on the household of Cornelius, so that we may sing a new song and bear fruit that will last. With the Blessed Virgin Mary, whom we honour this month, we ask this in your name, who live and reign for ever and ever. Alleluia. Amen.
