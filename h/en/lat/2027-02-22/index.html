The Chair of St Peter, Apostle

  • Gospel Matthew 16:13–19 · You are Peter, and to you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven

Word for the day

“And I tell you, you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it.” Matthew 16:18

Reflection

In the second week of Lent the Church pauses for a feast of white: the Chair of St Peter. A chair seems a strange thing to celebrate. But in the ancient world the cathedra was the seat from which a teacher taught and a bishop presided; Jesus himself speaks of the scribes who “sit on Moses' seat” (Matthew 23:2). Since at least the fourth century the Church of Rome has kept this day in honour of Peter's chair, that is, of the mission Christ gave him to teach, to guide and to hold the Church together in one faith. We do not honour a piece of wood. We honour the Lord, who chose a fisherman and made him a rock, and who still keeps his Church united around Peter's successor.

The Gospel takes us to Caesarea Philippi, far in the north, where a great rock face rose above a shrine to pagan gods. There Jesus asks two questions. First: “Who do people say that the Son of Man is?” The answers are generous but not enough: John the Baptist, Elijah, Jeremiah, a prophet. Then the question that no one can answer for us: “But who do you say that I am?” Simon speaks for all: “You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God.” Jesus tells him that this faith is a gift: “flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father in heaven.” Then he gives Simon a new name. “You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it.” In Aramaic the name and the rock are the same word, Kepha. He gives him the keys of the kingdom, the sign of a steward over the king's house, as Isaiah once said of Eliakim: “he shall open, and no one shall shut.” And he gives the power to bind and to loose.

Peter did not become a rock by his own strength. The same chapter tells how he tried to turn Jesus away from the cross and heard the words “Get behind me, Satan!” Later he denied his Master three times. His firmness comes from the prayer of Jesus: “I have prayed for you that your own faith may not fail; and you, when once you have turned back, strengthen your brothers.” That is why the first reading sounds so humble. The old Apostle writes as “an elder myself and a witness of the sufferings of Christ,” and asks the shepherds to tend the flock “not under compulsion but willingly,” not lording it over others but being examples, until “the chief shepherd appears.” The Psalm names that chief Shepherd: “The LORD is my shepherd, I shall not want.” In Lent this feast reminds us that conversion is never private. We return to God within the one flock, under the one Shepherd, held together by the faith Peter confessed.

Homily outline

Theme: “You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church.”

The question of Jesus, the rock made by grace, the keys of service and shepherds after the heart of Christ.

1. Who do you say that I am?

  • What people say.
  • What I say.
  • Faith as the Father's gift.
“The LORD is my shepherd, I shall not want.” Psalm 23:1
“He said to them, “But who do you say that I am?” Simon Peter answered, “You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God.”” Matthew 16:15–16

Apply: Write down in one sentence who Jesus is for you, and pray it each morning this week.

2. You are Peter

  • A new name.
  • The rock and the gates of Hades.
  • Firm because Christ prayed for him.
“I will place on his shoulder the key of the house of David; he shall open, and no one shall shut; he shall shut, and no one shall open.” Isaiah 22:22
“but I have prayed for you that your own faith may not fail; and you, when once you have turned back, strengthen your brothers.”” Luke 22:32

Apply: Pray daily this week for the Holy Father, that his faith may not fail.

3. The keys of the kingdom

  • A steward, not an owner.
  • Binding and loosing.
  • Mercy in the confessional.
“For thus says the Lord GOD: I myself will search for my sheep, and will seek them out.” Ezekiel 34:11
“I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.”” Matthew 16:19

Apply: Use the keys Christ gave his Church: make a good confession this Lent.

4. Tend the flock of God

  • Willingly, not under compulsion.
  • Examples, not lords.
  • The unfading crown.
“Even though I walk through the darkest valley, I fear no evil; for you are with me; your rod and your staff, they comfort me.” Psalm 23:4
“Do not lord it over those in your charge, but be examples to the flock. And when the chief shepherd appears, you will win the crown of glory that never fades away.” 1 Peter 5:3–4

Apply: Parents, teachers, leaders: serve those in your care by example this week.

The Church stands on Peter's confession of faith, made firm by Christ's prayer.

With Peter's successor we confess: you are the Messiah, the Son of the living God.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day of this week for the Pope, your bishop and your parish priest.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, in the middle of Lent we put on white and celebrate the Chair of St Peter. We do not honour a chair. We honour the mission Christ gave to a fisherman: to teach, to guide and to hold the Church together in one faith.

At Caesarea Philippi Jesus asked: who do people say that the Son of Man is? Many answers, all too small. Then: but who do you say that I am? (Matthew 16:15). This question comes to each of us. No one can answer it for us.

Simon answered: you are the Messiah, the Son of the living God. And Jesus said: flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father in heaven. Faith is a gift. Then he gave him a new name: you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it (Matthew 16:18).

Peter was not strong by nature. He tried to keep Jesus from the cross. He denied him three times. But Jesus prayed for him, that his faith would not fail, and told him: strengthen your brothers. The rock is made by grace.

That is why the first reading is so humble. Peter, an old man, calls himself an elder and a witness of the sufferings of Christ, and asks shepherds to tend the flock willingly, not lording it over others but being examples (1 Peter 5:2–3). Authority in the Church is service.

Today let us pray for our Holy Father, for our bishop, for our priests. And let us answer the question of Jesus with Peter's words, from our hearts: you are the Messiah, the Son of the living God. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus gave Simon a new name: Peter, which means rock! Peter was not always brave, but Jesus made him strong and made him the leader of his Church.

Today the Pope helps the Church like Peter did. Let us say a Hail Mary for the Pope today!

Prayer

Grant, we pray, almighty God, that no tempests may disturb us, for you have set us fast on the rock of the Apostle Peter's confession of faith. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, Son of the living God, you built your Church on Peter. Keep our Holy Father strong in faith and gentle in love; bless our bishop and priests; and make us one flock, faithful to you through this Lent and always. Amen.
