Word for the day

“I tell you that Elijah has already come, and they did not recognize him, but they did to him whatever they pleased.” Matthew 17:12

Reflection

Sirach praises Elijah: “a prophet like fire, and his word burned like a torch”. Taken up in a whirlwind, he is destined to return “to turn the hearts of parents to their children, and to restore the tribes of Jacob” (cf. Malachi 4:5–6). Coming down from the mountain of the Transfiguration, the disciples ask about this. Jesus answers that Elijah has already come, in John the Baptist, “and they did not recognize him”; the Son of Man too will suffer.

Today the Church in the Americas honours Our Lady of Guadalupe, who appeared in 1531 to St Juan Diego near Mexico City, as a young mestiza woman speaking his language, leaving her image on his cloak (tilma). Within a few years millions of indigenous people were baptised. Like Elijah, like John, Mary prepares the way for Christ and turns hearts. Her image shows her pregnant: Advent in a picture, carrying Christ to a whole people.

Full homily

Dear friends, Sirach remembers the prophet Elijah: then Elijah arose, a prophet like fire, and his word burned like a torch (Sirach 48:1). He was taken up in a whirlwind of fire, and the tradition said he would return to turn the hearts of parents to their children (Sirach 48:9–10).

In the Gospel, as they come down from the mountain of the Transfiguration, the disciples ask Jesus about Elijah. Jesus answers: Elijah has already come, and they did not recognise him, but did to him whatever they pleased. Then the disciples understood that he was speaking about John the Baptist (Matthew 17:10–13).

John came in the spirit and power of Elijah. Both were men of fire: they spoke the truth whatever the cost. Both called the people back to God. Both suffered for it.

Today is also the feast of Our Lady of Guadalupe. In 1531 Mary appeared to a poor indigenous man, St Juan Diego, near Mexico City, as a young mother, dressed like his own people, expecting a child (T). Through her, millions came to faith. She is patroness of the Americas and of the unborn.

Elijah and John prepared the way by fiery words; Mary at Guadalupe prepared the way with a mother's tenderness. God uses both fire and gentleness to turn hearts.

Elijah was to turn the hearts of parents to their children. This is a great need today: parents too busy for their children, children too distant from their parents. This Advent, let us turn our hearts to our families: time, attention, forgiveness. And let us ask Our Lady of Guadalupe to protect all mothers and their unborn children. Amen.

For children

Children, Mary appeared to a poor man named Juan Diego and spoke his language. Mary loves every people and every child!

Prayer

Lord God, you sent Elijah and John to prepare your way, and Mary to bring your Son to every people. Turn my heart to you and to my family, and let me recognise your messengers. Amen.
