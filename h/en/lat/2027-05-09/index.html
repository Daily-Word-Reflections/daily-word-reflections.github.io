The Ascension of the Lord

Word for the day

“They said, “Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking up toward heaven? This Jesus, who has been taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw him go into heaven.”” Acts 1:11

Reflection

St Luke wrote two books, and today's first reading is the hinge between them. “In the first book, Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus did and taught from the beginning until the day when he was taken up to heaven.” The Gospel of Luke ends with the Ascension; the Acts of the Apostles begins with it. The Ascension is not an epilogue to the story of Jesus; it is the beginning of the story of the Church. Luke tells us that after his suffering Jesus “presented himself alive to them by many convincing proofs, appearing to them during forty days and speaking about the kingdom of God.” Forty is the number of preparation: the flood, Moses on the mountain, Israel in the desert, Elijah's journey, Jesus' fast. These forty days prepare the apostles for a new way of being with their Lord. And their last question shows how much they still have to learn: “Lord, is this the time when you will restore the kingdom to Israel?” Jesus does not scold them. He redirects them: “It is not for you to know the times or periods … But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.” That one verse is the table of contents of the whole Book of Acts.

Then the moment itself: “as they were watching, he was lifted up, and a cloud took him out of their sight.” The cloud in the Bible is not weather; it is the sign of God's presence. The cloud led Israel through the desert, filled the tabernacle and the Temple, and overshadowed Jesus at the Transfiguration. Daniel saw “one like a human being coming with the clouds of heaven” to receive an everlasting kingdom (Daniel 7:13–14). So Jesus does not go up into the sky as a rocket goes into space; he enters the glory of God, and he takes our human nature, his risen body with its wounds, with him. And immediately two men in white, like the two at the empty tomb, ask the disciples a question that is also for us: “Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking up toward heaven?” The Ascension is not an invitation to stand and stare. The same Jesus “will come in the same way,” and until then there is work to do.

The Psalm is the song of a King's enthronement: “God has gone up with a shout, the Lord with the sound of a trumpet. Sing praises to God, sing praises; sing praises to our King, sing praises.” The Church has sung it on this feast from the earliest centuries. In the second reading Paul prays that the Ephesians may have “the eyes of your heart enlightened” to see what God has done: he “raised him from the dead and seated him at his right hand in the heavenly places, far above all rule and authority and power and dominion.” To sit at the right hand means to share the Father's power and glory. And Paul adds the astonishing consequence: God “has made him the head over all things for the church, which is his body, the fullness of him who fills all in all.” The Head has gone into heaven, but the Body is still here, joined to him. Where the Head has gone, the Body is called to follow. (The alternative reading, Ephesians 4, makes the same point another way: “When he ascended on high … he gave gifts to his people,” apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers, to build up the Body.)

Mark's Gospel, in its closing verses, gives the Ascension in a few strong lines. Jesus sends the eleven: “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” Belief and baptism will bring salvation; signs will accompany those who believe. Then: “the Lord Jesus, after he had spoken to them, was taken up into heaven and sat down at the right hand of God.” And at once, without a pause: “And they went out and proclaimed the good news everywhere, while the Lord worked with them and confirmed the message by the signs that accompanied it.” That is the key to the whole feast. Jesus sits at the right hand of God, and at the same moment he works with his disciples on earth. He is not further away; he is closer, no longer limited to one place, present to every place where the Gospel is proclaimed. These verses (Mark 16:9–20) are missing from some ancient manuscripts, but the Church received them as inspired Scripture, and Trent confirmed the canon with them.

Today is also World Communications Day, kept on the Sunday before Pentecost since 1967, at the wish of the Second Vatican Council. It is fitting. The Lord who ascends gives his Church a message for “the whole creation” and the ends of the earth. In our age the ends of the earth are reached by phone screens and radio, by newspapers and the internet. Every Christian with a mobile phone is in some way an evangelist, for good or ill. And between the Ascension and Pentecost, the Church does what the apostles did: they returned to Jerusalem and devoted themselves to prayer “together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus” (Acts 1:14). We are in those nine days. Let us look up to where Christ has gone, and then look around at the world he sends us to, and pray with his Mother: Come, Holy Spirit.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear friends, have you ever stood at an airport and watched a plane carry away someone you love? You stand at the glass and watch until it is only a dot, and then it disappears into the clouds. You keep looking a little longer. Then someone touches your shoulder: come, let us go home.

That is what happened to the apostles on the Mount of Olives. Jesus blessed them, and as they were watching, he was lifted up, and a cloud took him out of their sight (Acts 1:9). They kept staring. And two men in white touched their shoulders, so to speak: Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking up toward heaven? (Acts 1:11).

First, the Ascension tells us where Jesus has gone. Not to some planet far away. The cloud in the Bible is the sign of God's glory. Jesus has entered the glory of God, and he has taken our human nature with him: his risen body, with his wounds, now sits at the right hand of the Father (Ephesians 1:20). A man like us is in the heart of God. That is our future too. Where the Head has gone, the Body will follow.

Second, the Ascension tells us that Jesus has not left us. St Paul says God made him the head over all things for the church, which is his body (Ephesians 1:22–23). The Head is in heaven; the Body is here. You are that Body. And Mark tells us something wonderful: they went out and proclaimed the good news everywhere, while the Lord worked with them (Mark 16:20). He sits at the right hand of God and at the same time works with us. He is not further away; he is nearer, because he is no longer in one place but with every disciple everywhere.

Third, the Ascension gives us a job. Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation (Mark 16:15). You will be my witnesses to the ends of the earth (Acts 1:8). The angels did not let the apostles stand and stare. Our faith is not only looking up; it is also going out.

Today is World Communications Day. Two thousand years ago the apostles walked on dusty roads. Today the ends of the earth are in your pocket, on your phone. What do you send and forward? Rumours, gossip, anger? Or hope, truth, and the Good News? Every Christian with a phone can be a witness, like St Carlo Acutis, the teenager who used the internet to spread love for the Eucharist.

Finally, the apostles did not rush out at once. Jesus told them to wait for the Holy Spirit. So they went back to Jerusalem and prayed, together with Mary the mother of Jesus (Acts 1:14). We are in those days now. In this Mass the ascended Lord comes down to us in his Body and Blood. Let us receive him, lift up our hearts to where he is, and with Mary pray every day this week: Come, Holy Spirit. Alleluia. Amen.

Homily option 2 · A life worthy of the calling

In the alternative second reading Paul draws a practical lesson from the Ascension: the risen Lord has gone up and given gifts, so we must live in unity and build up his body.

  • One God and Father of all.
  • Unity worth every effort.
📖 Deuteronomy 6:4
📖 Ephesians 4:4–6

Apply: Pray for unity in your family, parish and the whole Church.

3. He ascended and gave gifts

  • The psalm of the ascending King.
  • Gifts for ministry.
  • Building up the body.
📖 Psalm 68:18
📖 Ephesians 4:7–8, 11–12

Apply: Offer your parish one gift of your time or talent.

The ascended Lord pours out gifts so that his body on earth may grow in unity.

We honour his Ascension by living worthy of our calling.

Take-home resolution: This week I will practise patience, pray for unity, and offer my parish one gift.

Dear brothers and sisters, the Ascension can seem far from daily life. Jesus goes up; we stay here. But St Paul draws a very practical lesson from it. Writing from prison, he says: I therefore, the prisoner in the Lord, beg you to lead a life worthy of the calling to which you have been called (Ephesians 4:1).

What does such a life look like? With all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love, making every effort to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace (Ephesians 4:2–3). The prophet Micah had said the same: to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God (Micah 6:8). The ascended Lord is not honoured by staring at the sky but by patience in the kitchen, in the office and in the parish committee.

Paul continues: there is one body and one Spirit… one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all (Ephesians 4:4–6). Israel prayed every day: hear, O Israel, the LORD is our God, the LORD alone (Deuteronomy 6:4). Because God is one, his people must be one. Every quarrel in the Church wounds the body whose Head has ascended into heaven.

Then Paul quotes a psalm about a king going up in triumph: when he ascended on high he made captivity itself a captive; he gave gifts to his people (Ephesians 4:8; Psalm 68:18). The ascended Christ does not leave us empty-handed. The gifts he gave were that some would be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, some pastors and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ (Ephesians 4:11–12). Every Christian has received a gift for others.

So let us celebrate the Ascension by living worthy of our calling. Be patient with one difficult person. Pray and work for unity. Offer your parish one gift: teaching catechism, singing in the choir, visiting the sick, cleaning the church. And at this Mass, receive the ascended Lord who gives himself as the greatest gift of all. Alleluia. Amen.

2. He lives to intercede for us

  • He prays for us now.

📖 Hebrews 7:25

3. He will come in the same way

  • Coming with the clouds.

📖 Acts 1:11

Apply: Put right one thing you would not want him to find undone.

The ascended Christ is our hope: he is in glory, he prays for us, and he will return.

With enlightened hearts we live in that hope.

Take-home resolution: This week I will pray each morning for enlightened eyes and live ready for the Lord's return.

Dear friends, in today's second reading St Paul prays a beautiful prayer for us. He asks that God may give us a spirit of wisdom and revelation, so that, with the eyes of your heart enlightened, you may know what is the hope to which he has called you (Ephesians 1:17–18). The Ascension is about hope, and hope needs eyes of the heart to see it.

Our physical eyes saw the cloud take Jesus away. The eyes of the heart see more. They see that the man Jesus, with his wounded and risen body, now sits at the right hand of the Father. The psalm had foretold it: the LORD says to my lord, sit at my right hand (Psalm 110:1). The psalmist prayed: open my eyes, so that I may behold wondrous things (Psalm 119:18). Let us ask for those eyes today.

What is Jesus doing at the right hand of the Father? The Letter to the Hebrews answers: he is able for all time to save those who approach God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them (Hebrews 7:25). At this very moment Jesus is praying for you. He shows the Father his wounds and presents your name. When we pray through Christ our Lord, as the Church does at the end of her prayers, we join a prayer that is already rising in heaven.

And the angels promised the apostles: this Jesus, who has been taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw him go into heaven (Acts 1:11). Daniel had seen one like a human being coming with the clouds of heaven (Daniel 7:13). Christ will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead. We do not know the day. So the hope of the Ascension is also a call to be ready.

So let us lift the eyes of our hearts. Let us remember that Jesus prays for us. Let us live each day so that we would be glad to see him come. And at this Mass, where the ascended Lord comes to us hidden in bread, let us receive a foretaste of the glory to which he has called us. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, forty days after Easter, Jesus went up to heaven. A cloud hid him. His friends kept looking up at the sky, and two angels said: why are you looking up? Jesus will come back! Now go and tell everybody about him.

Jesus is in heaven with the Father, and he is also with us every day, especially in Holy Communion. Let us look up and say: Jesus, I love you; and then go and be kind to someone!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, King of glory, today you ascended above all the heavens and took your seat at the right hand of the Father. You did not leave us orphans: you took our human nature into the glory of God, and you remain with us, working with your Church and confirming her word. Lift up our hearts to where you are, so that we seek the things that are above; and send us down the mountain into the world, to proclaim the good news to the whole creation. Be the Head of your Body, the Church; give us the eyes of the heart to see the hope to which you have called us; and make all who speak and write and broadcast in our time servants of truth and peace. With Mary your Mother and the apostles we wait for the Holy Spirit whom you promised. Come again in glory, Lord Jesus, as you went into heaven. You live and reign with the Father in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Alleluia. Amen.
