Word for the day

“He sat down, called the twelve, and said to them, “Whoever wants to be first must be last of all and servant of all.”” Mark 9:35

Reflection

After the great feasts of Easter and Pentecost, the Church takes us back to the school of daily life, and the teacher she chooses is Jesus ben Sira, a wise man of Jerusalem who wrote about two hundred years before Christ. His first lesson for anyone who wants to belong to God is honest and a little startling: “My child, when you come to serve the Lord, prepare yourself for testing.” Faith is not an insurance policy against trouble. Ben Sira does not promise an easy road; he promises a faithful God. “Set your heart right and be steadfast, and do not be impetuous in time of calamity. Cling to him and do not depart.” Then comes the image that has comforted believers for centuries: “For gold is tested in the fire, and those found acceptable, in the furnace of humiliation.” The goldsmith does not put gold in the fire to destroy it but to make it pure. Three times the reading repeats “You who fear the Lord”: wait for his mercy, trust in him, hope for good things. And it ends with a challenge to look at history: “Consider the generations of old and see: has anyone trusted in the Lord and been disappointed?” The answer the whole Bible gives is no, “for the Lord is compassionate and merciful; he forgives sins and saves in time of distress.” The Psalm adds its counsel: “Trust in the Lord, and do good.”

In the Gospel Jesus is walking through Galilee, quietly, “for he was teaching his disciples.” For the second time he tells them plainly what lies ahead: “The Son of Man is to be betrayed into human hands, and they will kill him, and three days after being killed, he will rise again.” This is the testing Ben Sira spoke of, and Jesus walks into it first. “But they did not understand what he was saying and were afraid to ask him.” While their Master speaks of the cross, the disciples are busy with another question. At Capernaum he asks them, “What were you arguing about on the way?” Nobody answers, “for on the way they had argued with one another who was the greatest.” Jesus does not scold them. He sits down, as a teacher does, and gives them a new measure of greatness: “Whoever wants to be first must be last of all and servant of all.” Then he takes a little child, in that world a person with no rank or rights, puts the child in the middle of them, and holds the child in his arms: “Whoever welcomes one such child in my name welcomes me.” Today the Church remembers St John I, an elderly pope who in 526 died in a prison at Ravenna after being sent on a difficult mission by a suspicious king. He did not seek the first place; he accepted the furnace, and was found to be gold.

2. Who was the greatest?

  • Jesus speaks of the cross.
  • The disciples speak of rank.
  • A new measure of greatness.

“He sat down, called the twelve, and said to them, “Whoever wants to be first must be last of all and servant of all.”” Mark 9:35

Apply: In one meeting or family discussion this week, let someone else have the last word and the credit.

  • The one with no rank.
  • Placed in the middle.
  • Welcome the child, welcome Christ, welcome the Father.

Apply: Give real time this week to a child or to someone overlooked: listen, play, teach, or simply sit with them.

Full homily

Dear friends, Pentecost is over and the ordinary weeks of the year have begun again. The first word the Church gives us is not a soft one. My child, when you come to serve the Lord, prepare yourself for testing (Sirach 2:1). Some people imagine that once we give our lives to God, everything will go smoothly. Ben Sira, a wise teacher of Jerusalem, says the opposite: get ready.

But he gives us a beautiful picture. Gold is tested in the fire (Sirach 2:5). A goldsmith in Thrissur or Kozhikode heats the gold not to spoil it but to burn away what is not gold. When the fire of trouble comes, God is not destroying us. He is purifying us. And Ben Sira asks: has anyone trusted in the Lord and been disappointed? Look back at your own life, and the lives of your parents. God has never failed those who clung to him.

In the Gospel Jesus tells his disciples for the second time that he will be betrayed, killed and raised. And what are they talking about on the road? Who is the greatest (Mark 9:34). It is almost funny, and it is very human. We do the same: in families, in offices, even in parish committees. Jesus does not shout. He sits down, takes a little child in his arms, and says: whoever wants to be first must be last of all and servant of all.

Today we remember St John I, an old pope who was sent on a hard journey, then thrown into prison by a king who did not trust him, where he died. He did not choose the first place. He accepted the fire and was found to be pure gold. Let us ask for his courage: to cling to the Lord in trial, and to spend our lives serving the little ones. Amen.

For children

Children, the disciples once had a big argument: who is the most important? Jesus took a small child, maybe as small as you, held the child in his arms and said: if you want to be first, help everybody. So in God's eyes the most important person is the one who serves. Can you help someone today without being asked?

Prayer

Lord our God, compassionate and merciful, you test your servants as gold is tested in the fire, and you never forsake those who trust in you. Keep our hearts steadfast in every trial. Lord Jesus, you walked to your cross as the servant of all; cure us of the desire to be the greatest, and teach us to welcome the little ones in your name. Through the prayers of St John I, who suffered in prison for the Church, make us faithful to the end. Amen.
