Word for the day

“By the tender mercy of our God, the dawn from on high will break upon us, to give light to those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace.” Luke 1:78–79

Reflection

On the last morning of Advent the Church sings with Zechariah. Filled with the Holy Spirit, the old priest who had been silent for nine months bursts into the Benedictus, the song the Church prays every morning at Lauds. He blesses God who “has looked favorably on his people and redeemed them” and has “raised up a mighty savior for us in the house of his servant David”, the promise of 2 Samuel 7 now fulfilled.

Then he turns to his own child: “And you, child, will be called the prophet of the Most High; for you will go before the Lord to prepare his ways.” And the song ends with the image of dawn: “the dawn from on high will break upon us, to give light to those who sit in darkness.” Tonight that dawn breaks. Advent ends not with a lecture but with a song of praise and a sunrise.

Full homily

Dear friends, this morning Mass on Christmas Eve is the last Mass of Advent. The first reading recalls God's promise to David: when your days are fulfilled, I will raise up your offspring after you, and I will establish his kingdom. I will be a father to him, and he shall be a son to me. Your throne shall be established for ever (2 Samuel 7:12–16).

In the Gospel Zechariah, filled with the Holy Spirit, sings the Benedictus: blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has looked favourably on his people and redeemed them. He has raised up a mighty saviour for us in the house of his servant David (Luke 1:68–69).

The Church sings this song every morning at Lauds. Today it has special meaning. The promise to David is about to be fulfilled tonight in Bethlehem, the city of David.

Zechariah sings that we are saved so that we might serve him without fear, in holiness and righteousness before him all our days (Luke 1:74–75). Christmas is not only a feast to enjoy; it is a call to holiness, a life of serving God without fear.

He ends: by the tender mercy of our God, the dawn from on high will break upon us, to give light to those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace (Luke 1:78–79). Tonight the dawn breaks.

In the busyness of this day, cooking, cleaning, shopping, travelling, let us keep a moment of quiet. Let us pray the Benedictus, thank God for his faithfulness, and prepare our hearts for the Child who comes tonight. Amen.

For children

Prayer
