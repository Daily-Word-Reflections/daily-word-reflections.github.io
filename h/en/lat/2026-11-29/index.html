Word for the day

“And what I say to you I say to all: Keep awake.” Mark 13:37

Reflection

Advent opens with a cry from the depths. Isaiah's people, back from exile but disappointed, pray: “O that you would tear open the heavens and come down!” They confess their sin honestly, “we have all become like one who is unclean”, and yet they hold on to one certainty: “Yet, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter.” Advent begins not with our achievements but with our longing.

God answered that prayer beyond imagining. He did tear open the heavens: at the Baptism of Jesus Mark uses that very word, the heavens “torn apart” (Mark 1:10). The Church's Advent has a double face: it prepares us to celebrate Christ's first coming at Christmas, and it directs our minds to his second coming at the end of time (Universal Norms on the Liturgical Year 39).

Mark's Gospel, which we will read through this year, gives Jesus' word for the waiting: “Keep awake.” A man goes on a journey and gives each servant his work, and the doorkeeper must stay awake, because the master may come “in the evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or at dawn”. Those four watches are the hours of the Passion night: the Supper in the evening, Gethsemane at midnight, Peter's denial at cockcrow, Jesus before Pilate at dawn. The disciples slept; we are invited to watch.

Paul encourages the Corinthians: you are “not lacking in any spiritual gift as you wait for the revealing of our Lord Jesus Christ… God is faithful.” Advent waiting is not anxious; it is the waiting of people who have already received much and know that the One who is coming is faithful.

Homily outline

Theme: “Keep awake.”

Advent begins with longing, asks us to be reshaped by the Potter, and calls us to watch for Christ.

1. Tear open the heavens

  • A tired people cries to God.
  • They confess their sins honestly.
  • God answered in Jesus.
“O that you would tear open the heavens and come down, so that the mountains would quake at your presence.” Isaiah 64:1
“The one who testifies to these things says, “Surely I am coming soon.” Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!” Revelation 22:20

Apply: Pray “Come, Lord Jesus” at each Advent candle.

2. We are the clay

  • God is our Father.
  • We are clay in the Potter's hands.
  • Advent is for reshaping.
“Yet, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter.” Isaiah 64:8
“Beware, keep alert; for you do not know when the time will come.” Mark 13:33

Apply: Name one habit you want God to reshape.

3. Keep awake

  • Four watches of the night.
  • The disciples slept in Gethsemane.
  • We watch with Christ in prayer.
“For there is still a vision for the appointed time; it speaks of the end, and does not lie. If it seems to tarry, wait for it; it will surely come, it will not delay.” Habakkuk 2:3
“Therefore, keep awake—for you do not know when the master of the house will come, in the evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or at dawn, or else he may find you asleep when he comes suddenly. And what I say to you I say to all: Keep awake.” Mark 13:35–37

Apply: Fix a daily time of prayer for Advent.

Advent is longing, reshaping and watching.

The One who comes is faithful.

Take-home resolution: Make your Advent Confession this week or next.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today we begin a new Church year. We light the first candle on the Advent wreath. A small flame in the dark, but it says: someone is coming.

The first reading is a prayer from people who were tired and disappointed. They had come back from exile, but life was hard and they felt far from God. And they cried out: “O that you would tear open the heavens and come down!” Do we ever pray like that? For our families, for our nation, for the world?

They were honest about their sins. But they held on to one truth: “Yet, O Lord, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are the potter.” That is Advent: we bring our broken clay to the Potter and ask him to shape us again.

And God answered. He did tear open the heavens. He came down at Bethlehem, as a child. And he will come again in glory. Advent prepares us for both comings.

In the Gospel Jesus gives one word, repeated: “Keep awake.” He mentions evening, midnight, cockcrow and dawn. Those were the hours of his own Passion night, when the disciples kept falling asleep. Jesus asks us to stay awake with him: through prayer, through honesty, through charity.

So let us use these four weeks well. Set up the Advent wreath at home and pray together each evening. Make a good Confession early. Choose one habit you want God to reshape. Then Christmas will not be just a holiday, but a meeting with the One who comes.

For children

Children, what is this circle of green with candles? It's an Advent wreath!

Each Sunday we light one more candle. When all four are lit, it will be almost Christmas!

Advent means “coming”. Jesus is coming! We get ready by praying and being kind.

At home, light a candle every evening and say: “Come, Lord Jesus!”

Prayer

Grant your faithful, we pray, almighty God, the resolve to run forth to meet your Christ with righteous deeds at his coming, so that, gathered at his right hand, they may be worthy to possess the heavenly Kingdom. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
