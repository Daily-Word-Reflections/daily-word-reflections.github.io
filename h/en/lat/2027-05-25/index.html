Word for the day

“With every gift show a cheerful face, and dedicate your tithe with gladness. Give to the Most High as he has given to you, and as generously as you can afford.” Sirach 35:11–12

Reflection

Ben Sira, the wise teacher of Jerusalem, wrote about two centuries before Christ, when the Temple sacrifices were offered every day. He loved the Temple, and he does not mock the sacrifices. But he wants his students to understand what makes an offering pleasing to God. He begins with a surprising list: “The one who keeps the law makes many offerings; one who heeds the commandments makes an offering of well-being. The one who returns a kindness offers choice flour, and one who gives alms sacrifices a thank offering.” Kindness repaid, alms given, a commandment kept: each of these is already an offering laid on God's altar. “To keep from wickedness is pleasing to the Lord, and to forsake unrighteousness is an atonement.” Then he turns to the gifts themselves, and his advice is warm and practical: “Do not appear before the Lord empty-handed.” “Be generous when you worship the Lord, and do not stint the first fruits of your hands. With every gift show a cheerful face, and dedicate your tithe with gladness.” The measure is simple: “Give to the Most High as he has given to you, and as generously as you can afford. For the Lord is the one who repays, and he will repay you sevenfold.” And one warning: “Do not offer him a bribe, for he will not accept it.” God cannot be bought. The Psalm says the same thing in God's own voice: “Not for your sacrifices do I rebuke you,” but “offer to God a sacrifice of thanksgiving,” for “those who bring thanksgiving as their sacrifice honor me.”

In the Gospel Peter speaks for all the Twelve. They have just watched a rich young man walk away sad, and heard Jesus say how hard it is for the wealthy to enter the kingdom. Peter blurts out: “Look, we have left everything and followed you.” There is a question hidden in the sentence: and what will we get? Jesus does not scold him. He answers with a promise that is both generous and honest: “there is no one who has left house or brothers or sisters or mother or father or children or fields, for my sake and for the sake of the good news, who will not receive a hundredfold now in this age.” The hundredfold is real: the one who leaves a family for Christ finds brothers and sisters and mothers in every Christian community, houses opened in every town. But Jesus adds two words no salesman would add: “with persecutions.” And then the true reward: “in the age to come eternal life.” Ben Sira said God repays sevenfold; Jesus says a hundredfold. You cannot outgive God. St Bede the Venerable, whom the Church remembers today, gave his whole life from the age of seven to the monastery of Jarrow in northern England. He never travelled far, yet through his books he taught all of Europe. Tradition says that on his last day he finished dictating a passage of St John's Gospel in English and died singing “Glory be to the Father.” That is the cheerful face Ben Sira asks for, kept to the end.

Homily outline

Theme: “Give to the Most High as he has given to you.”

Every good deed an offering, every gift given gladly, and a God who repays a hundredfold.

1. Every good deed is an offering

  • Kindness repaid is choice flour.
  • Alms are a thank offering.
  • Turning from wrong is atonement.
“The one who returns a kindness offers choice flour, and one who gives alms sacrifices a thank offering.” Sirach 35:3–4
“Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God.” Hebrews 13:16

Apply: Tonight, before sleeping, offer to God the good things you did today, however small, as your evening sacrifice.

2. With a cheerful face

  • Not empty-handed.
  • As generously as you can afford.
  • God cannot be bribed.
“Those who bring thanksgiving as their sacrifice honor me; to those who go the right way I will show the salvation of God.” Psalm 50:23
“Each of you must give as you have made up your mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.” 2 Corinthians 9:7

Apply: Decide this week what you will give to the poor or to the parish, and give it gladly, without complaint or display.

3. A hundredfold, with persecutions

  • Peter's question.
  • A new family in Christ.
  • Eternal life in the age to come.
“For the Lord is the one who repays, and he will repay you sevenfold.” Sirach 35:13
“But many who are first will be last, and the last will be first.” Mark 10:31

Apply: Thank God this week for one person in the Church who has been a brother, sister, father or mother to you, and tell that person so.

Ben Sira teaches that the best offering is a life of kindness and alms, given with a cheerful face.

Jesus promises that no one who gives to him will lose: a hundredfold now, with persecutions, and eternal life to come.

Take-home resolution: Give one gift each day this week, gladly and quietly, as an offering to God.

Full homily

Dear friends, the wise man Ben Sira lived at a time when the Temple in Jerusalem was full of sacrifices: lambs, bulls, flour, oil. He loved the Temple. But he tells us that there are other sacrifices too. The one who returns a kindness offers choice flour, and one who gives alms sacrifices a thank offering (Sirach 35:3–4). A kind word to a neighbour, a coin to a beggar, a commandment kept when it is hard: all of these are laid on God's altar.

Then he says something very human: with every gift show a cheerful face (Sirach 35:11). We all know people who give, but with a long face, so that everyone knows how much it cost them. Ben Sira says: do not give like that. Give to the Most High as he has given to you. God did not give us life and family and daily bread with a long face. He gave gladly. Our giving should look like his.

And he warns us: do not offer him a bribe, for he will not accept it (Sirach 35:14). Sometimes we treat God like an officer who must be pleased before he signs our paper. I will give this candle, this offering, if you give me that job. God is not like that. We give because he has already given, not to buy his favour.

In the Gospel Peter asks the question we all ask quietly: Look, we have left everything and followed you. What will we get? Jesus answers: a hundredfold now in this age, with persecutions, and in the age to come eternal life (Mark 10:30). He does not hide the cost. But he promises that nobody who gives to him will be the loser. Today, let us give something gladly, and trust the God who repays sevenfold and a hundredfold. Amen.

For children

Children, imagine your friend gives you a present but makes a grumpy face while giving it. Would you be happy? A wise man in the Bible says: when you give something to God, give it with a smiling face! Jesus told his friends: if you give up something for me, I will give you back a hundred times more. God is the most generous Giver of all. Let us give to him with a big smile!

Prayer

Most High God, everything we have has come from your hand. Teach us to give back to you as you have given to us, generously and with a cheerful face, not to buy your favour but to thank you for it. Lord Jesus, you promised a hundredfold to all who leave everything for your sake; give us courage when following you costs us something, and keep our eyes on the life of the age to come. Through the prayers of St Bede the Venerable, may we serve you gladly to our last breath. Amen.
