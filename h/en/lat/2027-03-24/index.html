Word for the day

“The Lord GOD helps me; therefore I have not been disgraced; therefore I have set my face like flint, and I know that I shall not be put to shame.” Isaiah 50:7

Reflection

The third Song of the Servant is the song of a disciple. “The Lord GOD has given me the tongue of a teacher, that I may know how to sustain the weary with a word. Morning by morning he wakens, wakens my ear to listen as those who are taught.” Before the Servant speaks, he listens; every morning he lets God open his ear. Because he listens, he does not turn back when suffering comes: “I gave my back to those who struck me, and my cheeks to those who pulled out the beard; I did not hide my face from insult and spitting.” His strength is not hardness but trust: “The Lord GOD helps me… therefore I have set my face like flint.” We heard this same reading on Palm Sunday; today it prepares us for the night of Thursday and the day of Friday, when Jesus will be struck, mocked and spat upon, and will not hide his face. The Psalm is the cry of the righteous sufferer whom even his own family treats as a stranger: “It is zeal for your house that has consumed me… for my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink.” The Gospels saw this psalm fulfilled on Calvary (John 2:17; 19:28–29).

The Gospel tells the bargain. Judas goes to the chief priests and asks: “What will you give me if I betray him to you?” They pay him thirty pieces of silver. That was the price of a slave in the Law (Exodus 21:32), and the “lordly price” at which the shepherd of Zechariah was valued (Zechariah 11:12–13). From this bargain the day takes its old name in English, Spy Wednesday. Then the scene moves to preparation: “Where do you want us to make the preparations for you to eat the Passover?” Jesus is not a victim caught by surprise. He says: “My time is near; I will keep the Passover at your house with my disciples.” He walks freely into his hour.

At table he says: “Truly I tell you, one of you will betray me.” And each disciple asks, “Surely not I, Lord?” That is the right question for us too. None of the Eleven was sure of himself; each knew that his heart could fail. Judas also asks, but notice the difference: the others say “Lord”; he says “Rabbi”, teacher. Jesus answers, “You have said so,” leaving the door open even now. The words of Jesus about Judas are solemn: “woe to that one by whom the Son of Man is betrayed!” They are not a curse but a lament, the grief of a friend. The Church does not pretend to know the final state of any soul; she knows that despair was Judas's last and greatest tragedy, and that no betrayal is greater than the mercy of Christ. On this last day before the Triduum, let us ask with the apostles, “Surely not I, Lord?”, and then run to the One who is about to give his life for betrayers, deniers and deserters, which is to say, for all of us.

Homily outline

Theme: “The Lord GOD helps me; therefore I have not been disgraced.”

The listening Servant, the face like flint, the thirty pieces of silver and the humble question of the disciple.

1. The listening Servant

  • The tongue of a teacher.
  • Morning by morning.
  • A word for the weary.
“The Lord GOD has given me the tongue of a teacher, that I may know how to sustain the weary with a word.” Isaiah 50:4
““Come to me, all you that are weary and are carrying heavy burdens, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me; for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls.” Matthew 11:28–29

Apply: Begin each day of the Triduum with ten minutes of silent listening to the Word.

2. The face like flint

  • His back to the smiters.
  • He did not hide his face.
  • Strength from trust, not hardness.
“The Lord GOD helps me; therefore I have not been disgraced; therefore I have set my face like flint, and I know that I shall not be put to shame;” Isaiah 50:7
“looking to Jesus the pioneer and perfecter of our faith, who for the sake of the joy that was set before him endured the cross, disregarding its shame, and has taken his seat at the right hand of the throne of God.” Hebrews 12:2

Apply: When you are insulted this week, keep silence and pray for the one who insulted you.

3. Thirty pieces of silver

  • The price of a slave.
  • The lordly price.
  • For what do I sell him?
“So they weighed out as my wages thirty shekels of silver.” Zechariah 11:12
““What will you give me if I betray him to you?” They paid him thirty pieces of silver.” Matthew 26:15

Apply: Name the price for which you are tempted to betray Jesus, and renounce it in confession.

4. Surely not I, Lord?

  • No one points at another.
  • Lord or Rabbi.
  • Mercy greater than betrayal.
“Insults have broken my heart, so that I am in despair. I looked for pity, but there was none; and for comforters, but I found none.” Psalm 69:20
“And they became greatly distressed and began to say to him one after another, “Surely not I, Lord?”” Matthew 26:22

Apply: Enter the Triduum in humility: ask Jesus to keep you faithful, and trust his mercy if you fall.

The Servant listens, suffers and trusts; he does not turn back.

Judas sold him; we ask humbly, Surely not I, Lord?, and run to his mercy.

Take-home resolution: Prepare the Triduum: confession, the family timetable, and the Good Friday fast.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Servant tells us his secret: the Lord GOD has given me the tongue of a teacher, that I may know how to sustain the weary with a word. Morning by morning he wakens, wakens my ear to listen as those who are taught (Isaiah 50:4). Before he speaks, he listens. And because he listens, he does not turn back: I did not hide my face from insult and spitting (Isaiah 50:6).

In the Gospel Judas goes to the chief priests and asks: what will you give me if I betray him to you? They paid him thirty pieces of silver (Matthew 26:15). The price of a slave. The Son of God, valued at thirty coins.

At the table Jesus says: one of you will betray me. And each disciple asks: surely not I, Lord? (Matthew 26:22). They do not point at each other. Each looks into his own heart. That is the question for us on this last day before the Triduum. For what price would I sell Jesus? For money? For comfort? For the approval of others?

But remember: Jesus did not curse Judas; he grieved over him and even at the table left the door open. No sin is bigger than his mercy. Tomorrow evening the Triduum begins. Let us come to it as the Servant did, with a listening ear and a face set like flint, trusting that the Lord GOD helps us. Amen.

For children

Children, Judas sold Jesus for thirty silver coins. How sad! Jesus is worth more than all the money and toys in the world. Let us tell Jesus today: you are my best friend, and I will never trade you for anything!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Servant who listened morning by morning, you set your face like flint and did not hide it from insult and spitting. You were sold for thirty pieces of silver by a friend. Keep us from selling you for any price. Let us ask humbly, Surely not I, Lord?, and run to your mercy. Bring us with you into these holy days. Amen.
