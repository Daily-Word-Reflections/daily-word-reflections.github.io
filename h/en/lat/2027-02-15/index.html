Word for the day

“And the king will answer them, ‘Truly I tell you, just as you did it to one of the least of these who are members of my family, you did it to me.’” Matthew 25:40

Reflection

The first full week of Lent opens with the great charter of holiness in Leviticus: “You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy.” We might expect holiness to mean temple rituals, but the commandments that follow are startlingly ordinary. Do not steal; do not lie to one another; do not keep back a labourer's wages until morning; do not curse the deaf or put a stumbling block before the blind; do not slander; do not judge unjustly, favouring either the poor or the great. Then the commandments go down into the heart itself: “You shall not hate in your heart anyone of your kin… You shall not take vengeance or bear a grudge… but you shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am the LORD.” Each command is sealed with the Name of God. Holiness is God's own life, and it shows itself in honest scales, prompt wages, careful words and a heart without grudges.

Jesus draws back the curtain on the last day. The Son of Man sits on his throne of glory, and the nations are gathered before him like a flock at evening. The judgment turns on six simple works: food for the hungry, drink for the thirsty, welcome for the stranger, clothing for the naked, care for the sick, visits to prisoners. Both groups are surprised: “Lord, when was it that we saw you hungry?” And the King answers: “Just as you did it to one of the least of these who are members of my family, you did it to me.” The neighbour of Leviticus has a face, and it is Christ's own. Lent is not first about what we give up but about whom we see. The Church calls these the corporal works of mercy, and on the last day they will be the language in which our love is read.

Homily outline

Theme: “Just as you did it to one of the least of these… you did it to me.”

Holiness in daily dealings, a heart without grudges, and Christ hidden in the least.

1. Be holy as God is holy

  • Holiness is God's own life.
  • It shows in wages and words.
  • Every command sealed with his Name.
“Speak to all the congregation of the people of Israel and say to them: You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy.” Leviticus 19:2
“instead, as he who called you is holy, be holy yourselves in all your conduct;” 1 Peter 1:15

Apply: Examine your work and business dealings for honesty this week.

2. No grudge in the heart

  • Do not hate in your heart.
  • Do not take vengeance.
  • Love your neighbour as yourself.
“You shall not take vengeance or bear a grudge against any of your people, but you shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am the LORD.” Leviticus 19:18
“Love does no wrong to a neighbor; therefore, love is the fulfilling of the law.” Romans 13:10

Apply: Name one grudge and bring it to confession this Lent.

3. You did it to me

  • The King on his throne.
  • Six works of mercy.
  • Christ hidden in the least.
“Whoever is kind to the poor lends to the LORD, and will be repaid in full.” Proverbs 19:17
“And the king will answer them, ‘Truly I tell you, just as you did it to one of the least of these who are members of my family, you did it to me.’” Matthew 25:40

Apply: Do one corporal work of mercy before Sunday.

Holiness is love made concrete in honest hands and a forgiving heart.

On the last day our love will be read in the language of mercy.

Take-home resolution: Choose one work of mercy to practise every week of Lent.

Full homily

Dear friends, on this first Monday of Lent God speaks to Moses: you shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy (Leviticus 19:2). And then, surprisingly, he talks about wages, lies, slander and grudges.

Holiness is not far away. It is in paying the labourer on time, in not putting a stumbling block before the blind, in refusing to hate in our heart. The commandment ends: you shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am the LORD (Leviticus 19:18).

In the Gospel Jesus shows us the last day. The King will say: I was hungry and you gave me food, I was a stranger and you welcomed me, I was sick and you took care of me, I was in prison and you visited me (Matthew 25:35–36). Both the blessed and the cursed are surprised. Neither saw him.

Lent is a time to open our eyes. Just as you did it to one of the least of these who are members of my family, you did it to me (Matthew 25:40). Let us look for Christ this week in someone poor, sick or forgotten. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says: when you share your food or visit a sick friend, you are doing it for me! This week, do one kind thing for someone who needs help, and remember that Jesus is hiding there.

Prayer

Holy Father, you call us to be holy as you are holy. Free our hearts from every grudge, make our dealings honest and our words kind, and open our eyes to see your Son in the hungry, the stranger, the sick and the prisoner, so that on the last day we may hear his voice say: Come, you that are blessed. Amen.
