Word for the day

“Each of you must give as you have made up your mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.” 2 Corinthians 9:7

Reflection

St Paul is still speaking about the collection for the poor of Jerusalem, and now he uses the language every farmer understands: “The point is this: the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully.” A farmer who keeps back most of his seed for fear of losing it will have a poor harvest. Giving to the poor is not throwing seed away; it is sowing. Then Paul adds a word about the heart of the giver: “Each of you must give as you have made up your mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.” The Greek word for cheerful is hilaros, from which comes our word “hilarious.” God does not want gifts squeezed out of us by guilt or by public pressure; he wants the gift of a glad heart. And the one who gives in this way will never be the poorer: “God is able to provide you with every blessing in abundance, so that by always having enough of everything, you may share abundantly in every good work.” Paul quotes today's Psalm about the just man: “He scatters abroad, he gives to the poor; his righteousness endures forever.” God, “who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food,” will multiply our seed. And the final fruit of all giving is not only bread for the poor but praise for God: our generosity “will produce thanksgiving to God.”

In the Gospel Jesus speaks of the three great works of Jewish piety, which the Church still practises: almsgiving, prayer and fasting. He does not question these practices; he takes for granted that his disciples will give, pray and fast. He asks only one question: for whom are you doing it? “Beware of practicing your piety before others in order to be seen by them; for then you have no reward from your Father in heaven.” The word “hypocrite” in Greek meant a stage actor, someone playing a part behind a mask. Jesus describes the actor's way: sounding a trumpet when giving alms, praying “at the street corners, so that they may be seen by others,” disfiguring the face while fasting. Three times he says: “Truly I tell you, they have received their reward.” They wanted applause, and they got it, and that is all they will get. Then three times he describes the way of the child of God: “do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing”; “go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret”; “put oil on your head and wash your face.” And three times comes the same promise: “your Father who sees in secret will reward you.” This does not forbid public worship or good example; a few verses earlier Jesus said, “let your light shine before others” (Matthew 5:16). The difference is the direction of the heart: the light is to give glory to the Father, not to ourselves.

Giving as sowing, the cheerful heart God loves, and the hidden life of alms, prayer and fasting.

  • The God who supplies seed to the sower.
  • A harvest of righteousness.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul speaks to us today like a farmer: the one who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and the one who sows bountifully will also reap bountifully (2 Corinthians 9:6). When we give to the poor, we are not losing; we are sowing. And God is the one who gives the harvest. He supplies seed to the sower and bread for food.

Paul also says: God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:7). Not a giver who is forced, not a giver who complains, not a giver who gives only because his name will be read out in church. God loves the one who gives with a glad heart. Sometimes the poorest give most cheerfully, because they know what it is to be in need.

In the Gospel Jesus speaks about our good works: almsgiving, prayer and fasting. He says: do not do them to be seen by others. The hypocrites wanted to be praised, and Jesus says: they have received their reward (Matthew 6:2). If we give only to get a photograph in the newspaper, or pray only so that others think we are holy, we already have our reward: the praise of people, which lasts a moment.

Instead, Jesus invites us to a secret life with the Father: go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you (Matthew 6:6). Let us today do one good thing that nobody will ever know about. Let us give one gift with a cheerful heart. And let us find one quiet moment to pray behind a shut door. The Father sees, and that is enough. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says: when you do something good, you do not have to show everyone. You can do it in secret! Maybe you can make your sister's bed when she is not looking, or put a coin in the box for the poor without telling anyone. Jesus says God sees everything, even secret good deeds, and he is very happy with them. Try one secret good deed today!

Prayer

Generous Father, you supply seed to the sower and bread for food, and you love a cheerful giver. Release our hearts from fear and meanness, so that we may sow bountifully and share abundantly in every good work. Lord Jesus, you saw the hypocrisy of those who wanted to be seen; purify our alms, our prayer and our fasting, so that we may do them for the Father who sees in secret, and our generosity may produce thanksgiving to God. Amen.
