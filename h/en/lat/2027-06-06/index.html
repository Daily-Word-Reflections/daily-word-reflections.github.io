Word for the day

“And looking at those who sat around him, he said, “Here are my mother and my brothers! Whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother.”” Mark 3:34–35

Reflection

After weeks of great feasts the Church returns to the green Sundays of Ordinary Time, the season of growth, and today's readings go back to the very beginning, to the garden. Adam and the woman have eaten from the forbidden tree. They have hidden among the trees. And God comes looking: “But the Lord God called to the man, and said to him, ‘Where are you?’” It is the first question God asks in the Bible, and it is not the question of a policeman but of a father looking for a lost child. God knows where Adam is; Adam needs to know where he is. Adam answers with the first confession of human fear: “I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself.” Sin has made the friend of God afraid of God. Then comes the chain of excuses: “The woman whom you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit from the tree, and I ate.” The man blames the woman and, quietly, God who gave her; the woman blames the serpent: “The serpent tricked me, and I ate.” Nobody says: I did it. Sin divides; it turns people against each other and against God. But God does not abandon them. To the serpent he speaks a curse and, hidden inside it, the first promise of salvation: “I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head, and you will strike his heel.” The Church calls this verse the Protoevangelium, the first Gospel (Catechism 410–411). The war will not last for ever; a descendant of the woman will crush the serpent's head, though he himself will be wounded. Christians have seen in that offspring Christ, wounded on the Cross and victorious, and in the woman, Mary, the new Eve. The Psalm is the cry of everyone who has hidden from God and now calls to him: “Out of the depths I cry to you, O Lord … If you, O Lord, should mark iniquities, Lord, who could stand? But there is forgiveness with you.”

St Paul, in the second reading, speaks from the depths of his own sufferings. He has been afflicted, perplexed, persecuted, struck down. Yet he keeps preaching, because he believes: “just as we have the same spirit of faith that is in accordance with scripture, ‘I believed, and so I spoke,’ we also believe, and so we speak, because we know that the one who raised the Lord Jesus will raise us also with Jesus.” Faith in the Resurrection gives him courage: “So we do not lose heart. Even though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day.” Every ache of growing older, every illness, every loss is the outer tent wearing out, while within, God is building something new. “For this slight momentary affliction is preparing us for an eternal weight of glory beyond all measure, because we look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen.” And then the great hope: “For we know that if the earthly tent we live in is destroyed, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens.” Adam lost the garden; Christ prepares for us a house that will never be lost.

The Gospel shows Jesus surrounded by opposition from two sides. Such crowds gather at the house that “they could not even eat.” His relatives hear what people are saying, “He has gone out of his mind,” and come to take charge of him. The scribes from Jerusalem go much further: “He has Beelzebul, and by the ruler of the demons he casts out demons.” Jesus answers with clear logic: “How can Satan cast out Satan? If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.” Then a short parable: “no one can enter a strong man's house and plunder his property without first tying up the strong man.” Satan is the strong man who has held humanity captive since the garden; Jesus is the stronger one who has come to bind him and set his prisoners loose. That is the promise of Genesis 3:15 coming true before their eyes. Then a solemn warning: all sins and blasphemies can be forgiven, “but whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit can never have forgiveness.” This is not a single careless word but a hardened refusal to recognise God's saving work, calling the Holy Spirit's work the devil's; whoever refuses mercy to the end shuts the door from the inside (Catechism 1864). Finally his mother and his brothers arrive and stand outside. In the language of the time “brothers” meant close relatives; the Church has always taught that Mary remained ever a virgin and that these were cousins or kinsmen (Catechism 500). Jesus looks at those seated around him and says: “Here are my mother and my brothers! Whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother.” He does not reject his mother; he reveals what makes her truly great. No one ever did the will of God more perfectly than Mary, who said, “Here am I, the servant of the Lord” (Luke 1:38). She is his mother twice over, by flesh and by faith. And the door is opened to us: whoever does the will of God belongs to the family of Jesus. Adam hid; the new family of Jesus sits around him, listening.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother.”

God's question in the garden, the first promise of victory, the stronger one who binds the strong man, and the new family of Jesus.

1. Where are you?

  • Hiding among the trees.
  • Afraid of the God who loves us.
  • The chain of blame.
“But the Lord God called to the man, and said to him, “Where are you?”” Genesis 3:9
“For the Son of Man came to seek out and to save the lost.” Luke 19:10

Apply: Make an examination of conscience this week without excuses, and go to confession.

2. The first Gospel

  • Enmity between serpent and woman.
  • He will strike your head.
  • Mary, the new Eve.
“I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head, and you will strike his heel.” Genesis 3:15
“The Son of God was revealed for this purpose, to destroy the works of the devil.” 1 John 3:8

Apply: When temptation comes this week, call on the name of Jesus and ask the prayers of Mary, who crushed the serpent's head with her Son.

3. The stronger one

  • Accused of Beelzebul.
  • A kingdom divided cannot stand.
  • The strong man bound.
“If you, O Lord, should mark iniquities, Lord, who could stand? But there is forgiveness with you, so that you may be revered.” Psalm 130:3–4
“But no one can enter a strong man's house and plunder his property without first tying up the strong man; then indeed the house can be plundered.” Mark 3:27

Apply: Name one habit of sin that holds you captive and bring it to Christ in confession; he is stronger.

4. The family of Jesus

  • His mother and his brothers outside.
  • Those seated around him.
  • Doing the will of God, like Mary.
“I delight to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart.” Psalm 40:8
“Whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother.” Mark 3:35

Apply: Each morning this week ask, Lord, what is your will for me today? and each evening, Did I do it?

God seeks us when we hide and promises from the beginning a victory over the serpent through the offspring of the woman.

Jesus, the stronger one, binds the strong man and gathers a new family of those who do the will of God; Mary is the first of them, and we do not lose heart.

Take-home resolution: Come out of hiding this week: make a good confession and begin each day by asking to do God's will.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, after the great feasts of Pentecost, the Holy Trinity and the Body and Blood of Christ, we come back today to the green Sundays of Ordinary Time. And the first reading takes us back to the very beginning, to a garden, and to a question. Adam and Eve have disobeyed. They hear God walking in the garden, and they hide. And God calls: where are you? (Genesis 3:9).

It is the first question God asks in the whole Bible, and he is still asking it. Not as a policeman, but as a father whose child has run away. God knows where Adam is. But Adam needs to say it. And notice what Adam says: I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid. Sin always does this. It makes us afraid of the God who loves us. And then comes the blame: the woman you gave me, she gave me the fruit. The woman says: the serpent tricked me. Nobody says, I did it. How often in our families do we do the same: it is his fault, it is her fault, it is the government's fault.

But God does not give up on them. Speaking to the serpent, he makes a promise: I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will strike your head (Genesis 3:15). This is the first Good News in the Bible. One day a child of the woman will crush the serpent. That child is Jesus, and the woman, in the fullest sense, is Mary, the new Eve, who said yes where Eve said no.

In the Gospel we see that promise coming true. The scribes say Jesus is working with the devil. Jesus answers: no one can enter a strong man's house and plunder his property without first tying up the strong man (Mark 3:27). Satan is the strong man who has held us captive since the garden. Jesus is the stronger one who has come to tie him up and set us loose. Every exorcism, every confession, every Baptism is a sign that the strong man is bound.

Jesus also warns about the sin against the Holy Spirit, which cannot be forgiven. Do not be frightened by this. It does not mean that God's mercy has a limit. It means that if someone refuses God's mercy to the very end, saying that God's goodness is evil, he shuts the door from the inside. Anyone who is worried that he has committed this sin has not committed it, because he still wants God's mercy.

Finally, his mother and his relatives stand outside, and Jesus says: whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother (Mark 3:35). He is not pushing his mother away. He is praising her! No one did the will of God like Mary: here am I, the servant of the Lord. And he opens the door to us. You and I can be the family of Jesus. So let us answer God's question today: where are you? Here I am, Lord. I come out of hiding. I want to do your will. And as St Paul says, let us not lose heart, for God is preparing for us a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. Amen.

For children

Children, have you ever broken something and then hidden so that nobody would find you? That is what Adam and Eve did after they disobeyed God. They hid in the garden. But God came looking for them and called: where are you? God was not coming to shout at them. He came because he loved them and wanted them back.

Jesus is God's answer to Adam and Eve. He came to beat the devil and bring us back home. And he said that everyone who does what God wants is part of his family. So when you do something wrong, do not hide. Say sorry to God and to your parents, and you will feel his love.

Prayer

O God, from whom all good things come, grant that we, who call on you in our need, may at your prompting discern what is right, and by your guidance do it. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord God, you came looking for Adam when he hid; you still ask each of us, Where are you? Give us the courage to come out of hiding and to answer without excuses. Lord Jesus, stronger than the strong man, set us loose from every bondage of sin. Make us your true family, doing the will of the Father as your Mother did, and keep us from losing heart until we reach the house not made with hands. Amen.
