Word for the day

“Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.” John 17:17

Reflection

Paul is on his last journey to Jerusalem, and he knows what waits for him there: “imprisonment and persecutions.” He does not have time to go back to Ephesus, where he had worked for three years, so he sends for the elders of that church to meet him at the port of Miletus. Today we hear the second half of his farewell, the only speech in Acts that Paul gives to Christian leaders. It is a pastor's last will. “Keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock, of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God that he obtained with the blood of his own Son.” The Church is not their property; it was bought at the price of Christ's blood, and the Holy Spirit placed them in charge. Paul is honest about the danger: “savage wolves will come in among you,” and some “even from your own group will come distorting the truth.” Then he commends them “to God and to the message of his grace,” and points to his own example: “I coveted no one's silver or gold or clothing,” he worked with his own hands, and he quotes a saying of the Lord found in no Gospel: “It is more blessed to give than to receive.” The scene ends on the shore with tears. “He knelt down with them all and prayed. There was much weeping among them all; they embraced Paul and kissed him,” grieving most because they would not see his face again. The Psalm lifts the sadness into praise: “Sing to God, O kingdoms of the earth.” The God who “gives power and strength to his people” will keep the flock when the shepherd has gone.

In the Gospel we hear the heart of the prayer Jesus offered at the Last Supper, a farewell even deeper than Paul's. He is going to the Father, and he prays for those he leaves behind: “Holy Father, protect them in your name that you have given me, so that they may be one, as we are one.” He does not ask for an escape: “I am not asking you to take them out of the world, but I ask you to protect them from the evil one.” Disciples are not meant to hide from the world, but neither are they meant to belong to it. And then the great petition: “Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.” To be sanctified is to be set apart for God, made holy, and sent: “As you have sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world.” The martyrs remembered today were sent in this way. Nereus and Achilleus were soldiers in Rome. Pope St Damasus, in an inscription he wrote for their tomb in the catacomb of Domitilla, says (in substance) that they had served a cruel order, but suddenly believed, threw down their shields and weapons, and gladly bore witness to the triumph of Christ. St Pancras was a boy of about fourteen, put to death in Rome on the Via Aurelia, probably in the persecution of Diocletian around 304. Soldiers and a teenager: the prayer of Jesus, “protect them from the evil one,” was answered in them not by sparing their lives but by keeping their faith.

Homily outline

Theme: “Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.”

Two farewells: Paul on the shore at Miletus, and Jesus at the Last Supper praying for his own.

1. Keep watch over the flock

  • A Church bought with blood.
  • Shepherds placed by the Spirit.
  • Wolves and the word of grace.
“For thus says the Lord God: I myself will search for my sheep, and will seek them out.” Ezekiel 34:11
“Keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock, of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God that he obtained with the blood of his own Son.” Acts 20:28

Apply: This week, pray each day for your parish priest and your bishop by name, and do one thing to help the faith of someone in your care.

2. More blessed to give

  • Paul's working hands.
  • A saying of the Lord kept by Paul.
  • Joy in giving.
“A generous person will be enriched, and one who gives water will get water.” Proverbs 11:25
“In all this I have given you an example that by such work we must support the weak, remembering the words of the Lord Jesus, for he himself said, ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’” Acts 20:35

Apply: Choose one person who cannot repay you, and give them your time or help before Sunday.

3. Sanctify them in the truth

  • Not taken out of the world.
  • Protected from the evil one.
  • Holy and sent, like the martyrs.
“Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.” Psalm 119:105
“Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.” John 17:17

Apply: Until Pentecost, read a few verses of the Gospel each morning before you go out into the world, and ask: Lord, make me holy in your truth today.

Paul's farewell and the prayer of Jesus tell us the same thing: the Church belongs to God, bought by the blood of Christ and kept by the Father's name.

We are left in the world, not to be swallowed by it, but to be holy in the truth and sent, as the martyrs were.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day until Pentecost: Holy Father, keep me in your name; Come, Holy Spirit.

Full homily

Dear friends, have you ever stood at a railway station or an airport to say goodbye to someone you love? Today Paul stands on the shore at Miletus with the elders of Ephesus. He knows that he will not see them again. And his last words are not about himself, but about them and about the Church: keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock (Acts 20:28).

Paul tells them why the flock is so precious. It is the church of God that he obtained with the blood of his own Son. Every parish, every family of believers, has been bought at that price. That is why a shepherd must be watchful, and why every Christian must care for the faith of others. Paul then points to his own hands. He took nothing from them; he worked to support himself and the weak, remembering the word of the Lord Jesus: it is more blessed to give than to receive (Acts 20:35).

In the Gospel Jesus too is saying goodbye, and he prays for us. Holy Father, protect them in your name. I am not asking you to take them out of the world, but I ask you to protect them from the evil one (John 17:15). Jesus does not want us to run away from the office, the college, the market. He wants us there, but belonging to God. Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth (John 17:17).

The martyrs Nereus and Achilleus were soldiers who laid down their weapons for Christ, and Pancras was only a boy of about fourteen. They lived in the world, but they belonged to God, and the Father kept them faithful to the end. In these days before Pentecost, let us ask the Holy Spirit to make us holy in the truth, generous in giving, and faithful shepherds of whoever God has placed in our care. Amen.

For children

Children, St Paul had to say goodbye to his friends, and they all cried and hugged. He told them: remember what Jesus said, it is better to give than to receive. Jesus also prayed for his friends that God would keep them safe from the evil one. Today, try to give something: share a toy, help at home, or say a kind word. Giving makes Jesus smile!

Prayer

Holy Father, your Son prayed for us on the night before he died: keep us in your name and make us one. Protect us from the evil one, and sanctify us in the truth, for your word is truth. Watch over the shepherds of your Church, whom the Holy Spirit has made overseers of the flock bought by the blood of Christ. By the prayers of Sts Nereus, Achilleus and Pancras, who laid down their weapons and their lives for Christ, give us courage to belong to you in the middle of the world. Come, Holy Spirit, as we wait with Mary for Pentecost. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
