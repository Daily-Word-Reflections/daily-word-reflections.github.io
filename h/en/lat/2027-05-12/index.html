Word for the day

“Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.” John 17:17

Reflection

Paul is on his last journey to Jerusalem, and he knows what waits for him there: “imprisonment and persecutions.” He does not have time to go back to Ephesus, where he had worked for three years, so he sends for the elders of that church to meet him at the port of Miletus. Today we hear the second half of his farewell, the only speech in Acts that Paul gives to Christian leaders. It is a pastor's last will. “Keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock, of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God that he obtained with the blood of his own Son.” The Church is not their property; it was bought at the price of Christ's blood, and the Holy Spirit placed them in charge. Paul is honest about the danger: “savage wolves will come in among you,” and some “even from your own group will come distorting the truth.” Then he commends them “to God and to the message of his grace,” and points to his own example: “I coveted no one's silver or gold or clothing,” he worked with his own hands, and he quotes a saying of the Lord found in no Gospel: “It is more blessed to give than to receive.” The scene ends on the shore with tears. “He knelt down with them all and prayed. There was much weeping among them all; they embraced Paul and kissed him,” grieving most because they would not see his face again. The Psalm lifts the sadness into praise: “Sing to God, O kingdoms of the earth.” The God who “gives power and strength to his people” will keep the flock when the shepherd has gone.

In the Gospel we hear the heart of the prayer Jesus offered at the Last Supper, a farewell even deeper than Paul's. He is going to the Father, and he prays for those he leaves behind: “Holy Father, protect them in your name that you have given me, so that they may be one, as we are one.” He does not ask for an escape: “I am not asking you to take them out of the world, but I ask you to protect them from the evil one.” Disciples are not meant to hide from the world, but neither are they meant to belong to it. And then the great petition: “Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.” To be sanctified is to be set apart for God, made holy, and sent: “As you have sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world.” The martyrs remembered today were sent in this way. Nereus and Achilleus were soldiers in Rome. Pope St Damasus, in an inscription he wrote for their tomb in the catacomb of Domitilla, says (in substance) that they had served a cruel order, but suddenly believed, threw down their shields and weapons, and gladly bore witness to the triumph of Christ. St Pancras was a boy of about fourteen, put to death in Rome on the Via Aurelia, probably in the persecution of Diocletian around 304. Soldiers and a teenager: the prayer of Jesus, “protect them from the evil one,” was answered in them not by sparing their lives but by keeping their faith.

Homily outline

Theme: “Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.”

Two farewells: Paul on the shore at Miletus, and Jesus at the Last Supper praying for his own.

1. Keep watch over the flock

  • A Church bought with blood.
  • Shepherds placed by the Spirit.
  • Wolves and the word of grace.

Apply: This week, pray each day for your parish priest and your bishop by name, and do one thing to help the faith of someone in your care.

2. More blessed to give

  • Paul's working hands.
  • A saying of the Lord kept by Paul.
  • Joy in giving.
“A generous person will be enriched, and one who gives water will get water.” Proverbs 11:25

Apply: Choose one person who cannot repay you, and give them your time or help before Sunday.

3. Sanctify them in the truth

  • Not taken out of the world.

“Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.” Psalm 119:105

Paul's farewell and the prayer of Jesus tell us the same thing: the Church belongs to God, bought by the blood of Christ and kept by the Father's name.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day until Pentecost: Holy Father, keep me in your name; Come, Holy Spirit.

Full homily

Dear friends, have you ever stood at a railway station or an airport to say goodbye to someone you love? Today Paul stands on the shore at Miletus with the elders of Ephesus. He knows that he will not see them again. And his last words are not about himself, but about them and about the Church: keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock (Acts 20:28).

Paul tells them why the flock is so precious. It is the church of God that he obtained with the blood of his own Son. Every parish, every family of believers, has been bought at that price. That is why a shepherd must be watchful, and why every Christian must care for the faith of others. Paul then points to his own hands. He took nothing from them; he worked to support himself and the weak, remembering the word of the Lord Jesus: it is more blessed to give than to receive (Acts 20:35).

In the Gospel Jesus too is saying goodbye, and he prays for us. Holy Father, protect them in your name. I am not asking you to take them out of the world, but I ask you to protect them from the evil one (John 17:15). Jesus does not want us to run away from the office, the college, the market. He wants us there, but belonging to God. Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth (John 17:17).

The martyrs Nereus and Achilleus were soldiers who laid down their weapons for Christ, and Pancras was only a boy of about fourteen. They lived in the world, but they belonged to God, and the Father kept them faithful to the end. In these days before Pentecost, let us ask the Holy Spirit to make us holy in the truth, generous in giving, and faithful shepherds of whoever God has placed in our care. Amen.

For children

Children, St Paul had to say goodbye to his friends, and they all cried and hugged. He told them: remember what Jesus said, it is better to give than to receive. Jesus also prayed for his friends that God would keep them safe from the evil one. Today, try to give something: share a toy, help at home, or say a kind word. Giving makes Jesus smile!

Prayer

Holy Father, your Son prayed for us on the night before he died: keep us in your name and make us one. Protect us from the evil one, and sanctify us in the truth, for your word is truth. Watch over the shepherds of your Church, whom the Holy Spirit has made overseers of the flock bought by the blood of Christ. By the prayers of Sts Nereus, Achilleus and Pancras, who laid down their weapons and their lives for Christ, give us courage to belong to you in the middle of the world. Come, Holy Spirit, as we wait with Mary for Pentecost. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Paul finishes his farewell at Miletus: keep watch over yourselves and the flock, for which God paid with the blood of his own Son; wolves will come; I commend you to God and to the message of his grace. Then he kneels, prays with them, and they weep. In the Gospel Jesus prays for his disciples: protect them, keep them one, sanctify them in the truth. Psalm 68 calls the kingdoms of the earth to sing to God. The Church also remembers today the Roman martyrs Nereus and Achilleus, and the young martyr Pancras.

Acts 20:28–38 · Keep watch over the flock

Setting.

This is the second half of Paul’s farewell speech to the elders of Ephesus at Miletus, which began in yesterday’s reading. Having looked back on his own ministry, Paul now hands the care of the church over to them, and warns them of dangers to come.

Structure.

  • v. 28: keep watch over yourselves and the flock bought with God’s own blood.
  • vv. 29–31: wolves from outside, distorted teaching from within; be alert.
  • v. 32: I commend you to God and to the message of his grace.
  • vv. 33–35: Paul’s example: no greed, work with his own hands, help the weak.
  • vv. 36–38: prayer, tears and farewell at the ship.

Key words.

  • episkopoi, overseers: The same men are called “elders” in v. 17. The word gives us “bishop”. Their oversight is not lordship but shepherding: “to shepherd the church of God”.
  • poimainō, to shepherd: The task Jesus gave Peter, “Tend my sheep” (John 21:16), and that 1 Peter 5:2 gives to the elders. The model is Christ the Good Shepherd.
  • peripoieomai, to obtain, acquire: God “obtained” the Church with blood. The Church is not a human association but a people God has made his own at great cost.

Verse by verse.

“Keep watch over yourselves and over all the flock, of which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to shepherd the church of God that he obtained with the blood of his own Son.” (20:28)

The shepherd must first watch over himself. Textual note: manuscripts differ between “church of God” and “church of the Lord”, and the Greek can be read “with his own blood” or “with the blood of his own one”, that is, his own Son; the NRSV chooses the second and gives the other in a note. Either way, the Church is bought at the price of Christ’s blood.

“I know that after I have gone, savage wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock.” (20:29)

Jesus used the same picture of false prophets in sheep’s clothing (Matthew 7:15). The danger comes from outside and also from within (v. 30).

“And now I commend you to God and to the message of his grace,” (20:32)

Paul does not leave them to their own strength. He leaves them to God and to his word, which builds up.

“‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’” (20:35)

A saying of Jesus not found in the four Gospels. It shows that sayings of the Lord were remembered and handed on in the early Church beyond what the Gospels record.

How the Church reads it.

The Second Vatican Council teaches that the bishops, by divine institution, have succeeded the apostles as shepherds of the Church, and that they are placed by the Holy Spirit as true shepherds of souls (Lumen Gentium 20; Christus Dominus 2). Paul’s self-portrait, working with his own hands and caring for the weak, remains the measure of every pastor. St Augustine, preaching on shepherds, said that for the people he was a bishop, but with them he was a Christian (in substance).

John 17:11b–19 · Sanctify them in the truth

Setting.

We continue the prayer of Jesus at the Last Supper. Having prayed that the Father may glorify him, he now prays for the disciples he is leaving in the world. They are in the world, but they do not belong to it, and they are being sent into it.

Structure.

  • v. 11b: Holy Father, protect them, that they may be one.
  • v. 12: I guarded them; none was lost but the one destined to be lost.
  • vv. 13–16: my joy in them; the world hates them; protect them from the evil one.
  • vv. 17–19: sanctify them in the truth; I send them; I sanctify myself for them.

Key words.

  • pater hagie, Holy Father: The only time in the Gospels Jesus addresses God in this way. The holy Father is asked to make the disciples holy.
  • hagiazō, to sanctify, consecrate: To set apart for God and for his service. In the Old Testament priests and sacrifices were consecrated. Jesus consecrates himself (v. 19) as priest and victim, so that the disciples may be consecrated for their mission.
  • tērēsēs ek tou ponērou, protect them from the evil one: The same petition as the Lord’s Prayer, “deliver us from evil”, which can also be read “from the evil one”. Jesus does not ask that they be taken out of the world, but kept safe in it.

Verse by verse.

“Holy Father, protect them in your name that you have given me, so that they may be one, as we are one.” (17:11)

The unity of the disciples is modelled on the unity of the Father and the Son. It is a gift to be prayed for, not only a task to be organized.

“I am not asking you to take them out of the world, but I ask you to protect them from the evil one.” (17:15)

Christian holiness is not escape. The disciples remain in the world, in families, workplaces and villages, and are kept there by God.

“Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth.” (17:17)

The truth that makes holy is the word of God, and in John the Word is a person (1:1, 14). To be sanctified in the truth is to be made holy by Christ.

“And for their sakes I sanctify myself, so that they also may be sanctified in truth.” (17:19)

Jesus consecrates himself as one offers a sacrifice: he gives himself to the Father on the Cross for them. Their holiness flows from his self-offering.

How the Church reads it.

The Church hears this prayer for unity in every work for the unity of Christians. The Second Vatican Council’s decree on ecumenism begins by recalling that Christ prayed that all may be one (Unitatis Redintegratio 2). Pope Benedict XVI, in Jesus of Nazareth, explained that “sanctify” here has a priestly and sacrificial sense: Jesus offers himself, and draws the disciples into his offering (in substance).

Questions people ask

What does “the one destined to be lost” mean? Was Judas forced to betray Jesus?

The Greek says “the son of perdition”, a Semitic way of describing one who goes to ruin. It does not mean that God forced Judas. Scripture foresaw the betrayal (Psalm 41:9, quoted in John 13:18), but Judas acted by his own choice. The Church teaches that God’s foreknowledge does not take away human freedom (CCC 600).

For the Latin reader

Today the Roman calendar offers two optional memorials. Nereus and Achilleus were soldiers in Rome who became Christians and were martyred; the date is uncertain. Pope St Damasus wrote an epitaph for their tomb, saying that they threw down their arms when they believed. Pancras was a youth martyred in Rome, according to tradition at about fourteen years of age, and buried on the Via Aurelia. Like the disciples in today’s Gospel, they lived in the world without belonging to it.

For study and teaching

  • Paul tells the elders first to keep watch over themselves. What part of my own life do I need to watch more carefully?
  • Jesus prays that his disciples may be one. What can I do this week to heal a division in my family, parish or community?
