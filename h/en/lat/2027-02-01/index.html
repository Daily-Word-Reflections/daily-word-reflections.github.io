Word for the day

“But Jesus refused, and said to him, “Go home to your friends, and tell them how much the Lord has done for you, and what mercy he has shown you.”” Mark 5:19

Reflection

Hebrews closes its great roll call of faith almost out of breath: “time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets.” Some, through faith, “shut the mouths of lions, quenched raging fire, escaped the edge of the sword”. Others “were tortured, refusing to accept release, in order to obtain a better resurrection”; they were stoned, sawn in two, homeless, “destitute, persecuted, tormented… of whom the world was not worthy.” Faith does not always look like victory. Some escaped the sword and some died by it, and both were faithful. The chapter ends with a surprise: they “did not receive what was promised, since God had provided something better so that they would not, apart from us, be made perfect.” The saints of the Old Covenant wait to be completed together with us in Christ.

The Gospel takes us across the lake into pagan country, to a man no one could bind. He lived among the tombs, “always howling and bruising himself with stones.” When Jesus asks his name, the answer is terrible: “My name is Legion; for we are many.” Evil divides and multiplies; it tears a person into pieces. Jesus speaks one word and the man is found “sitting there, clothed and in his right mind.” Strangely, the townspeople are afraid and beg Jesus to leave; they prefer their pigs to their healed neighbour. The healed man wants to follow Jesus, but Jesus sends him home: “Go home to your friends, and tell them how much the Lord has done for you, and what mercy he has shown you.” He becomes the first missionary to the Decapolis.

Homily outline

Theme: “Tell them how much the Lord has done for you.”

The faithful who suffered, the man made whole, and the mission that begins at home.

1. The cloud of the faithful

  • Some escaped the sword.
  • Some died by it.
  • Made perfect with us.
“Be strong, and let your heart take courage, all you who wait for the LORD.” Psalm 31:24
“of whom the world was not worthy. They wandered in deserts and mountains, and in caves and holes in the ground.” Hebrews 11:38

Apply: Thank God by name for one person whose faith carried yours.

2. My name is Legion

  • Among the tombs.
  • Evil divides.
  • Clothed and in his right mind.
“he brought them out of darkness and gloom, and broke their bonds asunder.” Psalm 107:14
“They came to Jesus and saw the demoniac sitting there, clothed and in his right mind, the very man who had had the legion; and they were afraid.” Mark 5:15

Apply: Bring what divides you inside to Jesus in confession.

3. Go home and tell

  • He wanted to follow.
  • Jesus sent him home.
  • The first missionary of the Decapolis.
“Come and hear, all you who fear God, and I will tell what he has done for me.” Psalm 66:16
“But Jesus refused, and said to him, “Go home to your friends, and tell them how much the Lord has done for you, and what mercy he has shown you.”” Mark 5:19

Apply: Tell one family member what God has done for you.

Faith is faithful whether it escapes the sword or not.

Jesus makes us whole and sends us home as witnesses.

Take-home resolution: Share one story of God's mercy at the family table this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, Hebrews closes its list of the heroes of faith: time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets (Hebrews 11:32). Some shut the mouths of lions; others were tortured, stoned and killed by the sword.

Faith does not always look like victory. Some escaped and some died, and all were faithful: people of whom the world was not worthy (Hebrews 11:38). And they wait to be made perfect together with us.

In the Gospel Jesus meets a man whom no chain could hold, living among the tombs. His name is Legion, for we are many (Mark 5:9). Evil tears a person into pieces. One word of Jesus, and the man sits clothed and in his right mind (Mark 5:15).

He wants to follow Jesus, but Jesus says: go home to your friends, and tell them how much the Lord has done for you (Mark 5:19). Our first mission field is our own home. Let us tell our families of his mercy. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus made a very sick man well, and then said: go home and tell your family what God has done for you! Tonight, tell your family one good thing God did for you today.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Son of the Most High God, you freed the man whom no chain could hold and sent him home to tell of your mercy. Free us from all that divides and torments us, and make us witnesses at home of what you have done for us. Amen.
