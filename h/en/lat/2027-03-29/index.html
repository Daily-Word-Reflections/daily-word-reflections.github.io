Word for the day

“Suddenly Jesus met them and said, “Greetings!” And they came to him, took hold of his feet, and worshiped him. Then Jesus said to them, “Do not be afraid; go and tell my brothers to go to Galilee; there they will see me.”” Matthew 28:9–10

Reflection

The Church does not celebrate Easter for one day only. For eight days, the Octave, she lives each day as if it were Easter Sunday: the Gloria is sung, the Alleluia rings out, and each day's readings show us a new appearance of the risen Lord. The early Christians called this week the week of the white garments, because the newly baptized wore their white robes every day until the following Sunday. Today Peter stands up on the day of Pentecost with the Eleven and speaks to the crowd in Jerusalem. He speaks of “Jesus of Nazareth, a man attested to you by God with deeds of power, wonders, and signs.” He was handed over and crucified, “But God raised him up, having freed him from death, because it was impossible for him to be held in its power.” Then Peter reads Psalm 16, today's Psalm, as a prophecy: David sang, “you do not give me up to Sheol, or let your faithful one see the Pit.” But David died and was buried, and his tomb is still there. So he was speaking of the Messiah. “This Jesus God raised up, and of that all of us are witnesses.”

Notice what has happened to Peter. A few weeks earlier he was so afraid that he denied Jesus before a servant girl. Now he stands before thousands and speaks with boldness. Nothing explains that change except what he says: he has seen the Lord alive, and he has received the Holy Spirit. The Resurrection is not only proven by an empty tomb; it is proven by transformed lives. Peter's courage, the joy of the martyrs, the faith that spread to the ends of the earth, including the shores of Kerala, all flow from that first morning. The Psalm gives words to this new confidence: “I keep the LORD always before me; because he is at my right hand, I shall not be moved. Therefore my heart is glad, and my soul rejoices; my body also rests secure.”

In the Gospel the women leave the tomb “quickly with fear and great joy” and run to tell the disciples. Fear and joy together: that is often how Easter faith begins. And on the way, “suddenly Jesus met them.” He greets them simply, as a friend would. They take hold of his feet, real feet, and worship him. He says: “Do not be afraid; go and tell my brothers.” He calls the disciples who abandoned him “my brothers.” The Resurrection is reconciliation. Matthew then tells of a different response. Some of the guards report to the chief priests, who pay them to say that the disciples stole the body while they were asleep. Matthew records this because, in his own time, this story was circulating as an explanation of the empty tomb. It is not a judgement on the Jewish people; it is a reminder that every age offers its own ways of explaining the Resurrection away. St Augustine asked with a smile: if the guards were asleep, how did they know who took the body? The truth cannot be bought. The empty tomb stands, the witnesses speak, and the Lord meets us still on the road with his greeting: do not be afraid.

Homily outline

Theme: “Do not be afraid; go and tell my brothers.”

Not abandoned to the grave, fear and great joy, and the truth that cannot be bought.

1. Not abandoned to the grave

  • David's psalm.
  • David's tomb.
  • Christ whom death could not hold.
“For you do not give me up to Sheol, or let your faithful one see the Pit.” Psalm 16:10
“But God raised him up, having freed him from death, because it was impossible for him to be held in its power.” Acts 2:24

Apply: Pray Psalm 16 slowly each morning of this Octave.

2. Fear and great joy

  • The women run.
  • “Greetings!”
  • Holding his feet in worship.
“You show me the path of life. In your presence there is fullness of joy; in your right hand are pleasures forevermore.” Psalm 16:11
“So they left the tomb quickly with fear and great joy, and ran to tell his disciples.” Matthew 28:8

Apply: Spend a few minutes before the Blessed Sacrament this week, worshipping the risen Lord as the women did.

3. The lie and the truth

  • Sleeping witnesses.
  • Money cannot buy the truth.
  • Faith that is not in vain.
“I keep the LORD always before me; because he is at my right hand, I shall not be moved.” Psalm 16:8
“and if Christ has not been raised, then our proclamation has been in vain and your faith has been in vain.” 1 Corinthians 15:14

Apply: When someone mocks the Resurrection, answer gently and with your own witness, without anger.

God raised Jesus; death could not hold him, and his witnesses were transformed.

The risen Lord meets us on the road and sends us to our brothers and sisters.

Take-home resolution: Tell one person each day of this Octave that Christ is risen.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Church keeps Easter for eight whole days, because one day is not enough for so great a joy. Today Peter stands before a great crowd and says: this Jesus God raised up, and of that all of us are witnesses (Acts 2:32).

Think of who is speaking. A few weeks earlier Peter denied Jesus three times. Now he speaks with boldness before thousands. What changed him? He met the risen Lord, and he received the Holy Spirit. The best proof of the Resurrection is a changed life.

Peter quotes David: you do not give me up to Sheol, or let your faithful one see the Pit (Psalm 16:10). David died and his tomb was still there. But Jesus' body did not see corruption. God raised him up, because it was impossible for him to be held in death's power (Acts 2:24).

In the Gospel the women run from the tomb with fear and great joy. And suddenly Jesus meets them: greetings! They hold his feet and worship him. He says: do not be afraid; go and tell my brothers. He calls those who ran away his brothers. Easter is forgiveness.

Some tried to explain it away: the disciples stole the body while we were asleep. St Augustine laughed: if they were asleep, how did they see? People still try to explain away the Resurrection. But the truth cannot be bought.

This week, let us be like the women: running with joy, holding fast to the Lord in the Eucharist, and telling our brothers and sisters: do not be afraid, he is risen. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, the women ran from the tomb so happy, and Jesus met them on the way and said: hello! Do not be afraid!

Jesus says the same to you today: do not be afraid, I am alive and I am with you. Go and tell your friends!

Prayer

O God, who give constant increase to your Church by new offspring, grant that your servants may hold fast in their lives to the Sacrament they have received in faith. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Risen Lord Jesus, you met the women on the road and said: do not be afraid. Meet us today on our roads. Turn our fear into joy, make us bold witnesses like Peter, and call back to you all our brothers and sisters who have wandered away. Alleluia. Amen.
