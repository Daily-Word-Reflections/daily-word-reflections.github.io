Word for the day

“I have said this to you, so that in me you may have peace. In the world you face persecution. But take courage; I have conquered the world!” John 16:33

Reflection

Paul arrives in Ephesus and meets “some disciples,” about twelve of them. Something about them makes him ask a direct question: “Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you became believers?” Their answer is startling: “No, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit.” They had been baptized “into John's baptism,” the baptism of repentance that prepared the way for the Messiah. They were sincere; they had begun the journey; but they had stopped at the door. Paul explains gently: “John baptized with the baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the one who was to come after him, that is, in Jesus.” They are baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus, and “when Paul had laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they spoke in tongues and prophesied.” It is a small Pentecost in Ephesus, like the one in Samaria (Acts 8:14–17) and in the house of Cornelius (Acts 10:44–48). The Church has always seen here the two moments of Christian initiation: Baptism, and the laying on of hands that we call Confirmation.

The question Paul asked those twelve is worth asking ourselves in this week before Pentecost. Many of us were baptized as babies and confirmed as children. We have heard that there is a Holy Spirit; we say it every Sunday in the Creed. But do we live as people who have received him? Do we pray to him, listen to him, expect anything of him? Paul spent three months in the synagogue, “arguing persuasively about the kingdom of God.” The Spirit makes the Church speak. The Psalm sings of the God who rises up and scatters his enemies, “Father of orphans and protector of widows,” who “gives the desolate a home to live in”: that is what the Spirit does in the Church, making a home for the lonely and a family for the orphaned.

In the Gospel the disciples, at the end of the Last Supper, think at last that they understand: “Yes, now you are speaking plainly … by this we believe that you came from God.” Jesus answers with a sad, loving realism: “Do you now believe? The hour is coming, indeed it has come, when you will be scattered, each one to his home, and you will leave me alone.” Within hours they would run away. Yet Jesus is not bitter: “I am not alone because the Father is with me.” And his last words before the great prayer of John 17 are words of courage: “In the world you face persecution. But take courage; I have conquered the world!” He says this before the cross, as if the victory were already won, because it is. St John of Avila, whom the Church remembers today, knew persecution from within the Church itself: as a young priest he was denounced to the Inquisition and spent about a year in prison. There, he said, he learned more of the mystery of Christ than in all his studies. He came out without bitterness, and became the great preacher of Andalusia and a guide of saints.

Full homily

Dear friends, imagine Paul walking into Ephesus and meeting twelve people who say they are believers. He looks at them and asks a simple question: did you receive the Holy Spirit when you became believers? And they answer: no, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit (Acts 19:2).

They were good people. They had been baptized with John's baptism, a baptism of repentance. They had turned from sin. But they had stopped halfway. Paul baptized them in the name of the Lord Jesus, laid his hands on them, and the Holy Spirit came upon them. A little Pentecost in Ephesus.

Now, if Paul walked into our church this morning and asked the same question, what would we say? We would say: yes, of course, we were baptized and confirmed. But do we live like people who have received the Spirit? Do we ever pray to him? Do we ask his help before a hard decision? For many Christians, the Holy Spirit is the forgotten Person of the Trinity.

We are in the days between the Ascension and Pentecost. This is the best time to get to know him. Pray every day: Come, Holy Spirit. Ask him for his gifts: wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge, piety, fear of the Lord. He is not an idea; he is a Person, and he is waiting to be asked.

In the Gospel Jesus knows that his disciples will run away that very night. Yet he says: take courage; I have conquered the world (John 16:33). The Spirit is the one who gives us that courage. The same frightened disciples, after Pentecost, would stand before rulers and say: we cannot keep from speaking.

Today we remember St John of Avila, a Spanish priest who was falsely accused and put in prison. In prison he did not grow bitter; he grew in love for Christ. He came out and became one of the great preachers of his time. That is what the Holy Spirit does. So let us ask him today: Come, Holy Spirit, and give us courage. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, St Paul met some people who had never heard of the Holy Spirit! He prayed with them, and the Holy Spirit came into their hearts.

You received the Holy Spirit when you were baptized. He is your best helper. When you are scared or need to be good, just say: Holy Spirit, help me!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you told your disciples: take courage, I have conquered the world. We confess that we too are often afraid, and that we know the Holy Spirit only a little. Send him upon us as he came on the disciples at Ephesus when Paul laid his hands on them. Renew in us the grace of our Baptism and Confirmation; give us his gifts of wisdom and fortitude; and make us witnesses who speak boldly of the kingdom of God. Through the prayers of St John of Avila, give your Church holy priests who love the Eucharist and preach your Word with fire. In you may we have peace. You live and reign for ever and ever. Alleluia. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

At Ephesus Paul meets about twelve disciples who have received only John’s baptism and have not even heard of the Holy Spirit. They are baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus, Paul lays hands on them, and the Spirit comes. In the Gospel the disciples say they now believe, and Jesus warns them that they will be scattered, yet he is not alone, and he has conquered the world. Psalm 68 sings of God who rises and scatters his enemies, father of orphans and protector of widows.

Acts 19:1–8 · Did you receive the Holy Spirit?

Setting.

Paul reaches Ephesus, the great city of the province of Asia, on his third journey, as he had promised (18:21). He will stay there more than two years (19:10). Apollos has meanwhile gone to Corinth (19:1), after Priscilla and Aquila taught him the Way more accurately (18:26). The disciples Paul now finds seem to be in a situation like that of Apollos before he was taught.

Structure.

  • vv. 1–2: Paul meets disciples and asks about the Holy Spirit.
  • vv. 3–4: John’s baptism of repentance pointed to Jesus.
  • vv. 5–7: baptism in the name of the Lord Jesus, the laying on of hands, and the gift of the Spirit.
  • v. 8: three months of bold preaching in the synagogue about the kingdom of God.

Key words.

  • mathētai, disciples: Luke calls them disciples, but they seem to have been followers of John the Baptist’s teaching who had not yet received Christian baptism.
  • eis to Iōannou baptisma, into John’s baptism: John’s baptism was a baptism of repentance that pointed ahead to “the one who was to come” (v. 4). Christian baptism is baptism into Christ, with the gift of the Spirit.
  • epithentos tou Paulou tas cheiras, when Paul had laid his hands: The same gesture by which Peter and John gave the Spirit to the baptized of Samaria (8:14–17). The laying on of hands is the ancient gesture of the gift of the Spirit.

Verse by verse.

““Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you became believers?” They replied, “No, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit.”” (19:2)

They probably knew that John spoke of one who would baptize with the Holy Spirit (Luke 3:16). What they had not heard is that the Spirit had now been given.

“John baptized with the baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the one who was to come after him, that is, in Jesus.” (19:4)

Paul does not belittle John. John’s whole work pointed to Jesus. To follow John faithfully is to come to Christ.

“When Paul had laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they spoke in tongues and prophesied” (19:6)

This little Pentecost at Ephesus matches the Pentecost at Jerusalem (2:4), the gift at Samaria (8:17) and at Caesarea (10:44–46). In every place the same Spirit is given to the one Church. The “about twelve” may hint at a new beginning, like the twelve tribes and the Twelve.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism, explaining the sacrament of Confirmation, recalls that the apostles imparted the gift of the Spirit to the newly baptized by the laying on of hands, and cites this passage (CCC 1288). It is one root of the Church’s practice of Confirmation, in which the bishop, successor of the apostles, lays hands on the candidates and anoints them with chrism. Baptism and Confirmation belong together as sacraments of Christian initiation, completed by the Eucharist.

John 16:29–33 · Take courage; I have conquered the world

Setting.

These are the last words of the farewell discourse before Jesus’ great prayer to the Father in chapter 17. The disciples think they have understood at last. Jesus loves them, but he knows that in a few hours they will run away.

Structure.

  • vv. 29–30: the disciples: now you speak plainly; now we believe.
  • vv. 31–32: Jesus: do you now believe? You will be scattered, but I am not alone.
  • v. 33: peace in me, persecution in the world, and victory.

Key words.

  • thlipsis, persecution, distress: The NRSV has “persecution”. The word means pressure, affliction of every kind. Jesus does not promise his disciples a world without trouble.
  • tharseite, take courage: A word Jesus speaks in the Gospels to the paralysed man, to the woman with the haemorrhage, and to the disciples on the sea: “Take heart, it is I” (Matthew 14:27). Courage comes from his presence.
  • nenikēka, I have conquered: A perfect tense: the victory is won and remains. John’s first letter echoes it: “this is the victory that conquers the world, our faith” (1 John 5:4).

Verse by verse.

“Jesus answered them, “Do you now believe?” (16:31)

Not a rebuke, but a gentle warning. Their faith is real but still untested. It will be tested tonight.

“you will be scattered, each one to his home, and you will leave me alone. Yet I am not alone because the Father is with me.” (16:32)

Jesus is not left without the Father even in the hour of desertion. The cry on the Cross in Matthew and Mark, “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?”, is the opening of Psalm 22, a psalm that ends in trust and praise.

“In the world you face persecution. But take courage; I have conquered the world!” (16:33)

The last word of the discourse. The world in John means the world that refuses God, not the world God loves (3:16). His victory is won on the Cross, which looks like defeat.

How the Church reads it.

The Church hears these words above all when Christians suffer for their faith. Many Christians in many lands live this verse today; we pray for them and for those who wrong them. The Second Vatican Council teaches that the Church, “like a stranger in a foreign land”, presses forward amid the persecutions of the world and the consolations of God (Lumen Gentium 8, quoting St Augustine). Christ’s peace is not the absence of trouble but his presence in the midst of it.

Questions people ask

Why were these disciples baptized again, when the Church does not repeat baptism?

They had not received Christian baptism at all, only John’s baptism of repentance. John’s baptism was not the sacrament of baptism into Christ. Christian baptism, once validly received, is never repeated, because it marks the soul with an indelible spiritual sign (CCC 1272).

For study and teaching

  • The disciples at Ephesus had not even heard of the Holy Spirit. How often do I think of the Spirit, and what gift of the Spirit do I most need now?
  • Jesus says, “Take courage; I have conquered the world.” Which trouble in my life do I need to place under his victory?
