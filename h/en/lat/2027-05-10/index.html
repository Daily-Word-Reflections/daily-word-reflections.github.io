Word for the day

“I have said this to you, so that in me you may have peace. In the world you face persecution. But take courage; I have conquered the world!” John 16:33

Reflection

Paul arrives in Ephesus and meets “some disciples,” about twelve of them. Something about them makes him ask a direct question: “Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you became believers?” Their answer is startling: “No, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit.” They had been baptized “into John's baptism,” the baptism of repentance that prepared the way for the Messiah. They were sincere; they had begun the journey; but they had stopped at the door. Paul explains gently: “John baptized with the baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the one who was to come after him, that is, in Jesus.” They are baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus, and “when Paul had laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they spoke in tongues and prophesied.” It is a small Pentecost in Ephesus, like the one in Samaria (Acts 8:14–17) and in the house of Cornelius (Acts 10:44–48). The Church has always seen here the two moments of Christian initiation: Baptism, and the laying on of hands that we call Confirmation.

The question Paul asked those twelve is worth asking ourselves in this week before Pentecost. Many of us were baptized as babies and confirmed as children. We have heard that there is a Holy Spirit; we say it every Sunday in the Creed. But do we live as people who have received him? Do we pray to him, listen to him, expect anything of him? Paul spent three months in the synagogue, “arguing persuasively about the kingdom of God.” The Spirit makes the Church speak. The Psalm sings of the God who rises up and scatters his enemies, “Father of orphans and protector of widows,” who “gives the desolate a home to live in”: that is what the Spirit does in the Church, making a home for the lonely and a family for the orphaned.

In the Gospel the disciples, at the end of the Last Supper, think at last that they understand: “Yes, now you are speaking plainly … by this we believe that you came from God.” Jesus answers with a sad, loving realism: “Do you now believe? The hour is coming, indeed it has come, when you will be scattered, each one to his home, and you will leave me alone.” Within hours they would run away. Yet Jesus is not bitter: “I am not alone because the Father is with me.” And his last words before the great prayer of John 17 are words of courage: “In the world you face persecution. But take courage; I have conquered the world!” He says this before the cross, as if the victory were already won, because it is. St John of Avila, whom the Church remembers today, knew persecution from within the Church itself: as a young priest he was denounced to the Inquisition and spent about a year in prison. There, he said, he learned more of the mystery of Christ than in all his studies. He came out without bitterness, and became the great preacher of Andalusia and a guide of saints.

Homily outline

Theme: “Take courage; I have conquered the world!”

Disciples who had not heard of the Holy Spirit, the laying on of hands, and the courage that comes from Christ's victory.

1. We have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit

  • Twelve sincere disciples.
  • Stopped at the door.
  • The forgotten Person of the Trinity.

Apply: Pray each day this week the prayer “Come, Holy Spirit,” and speak to him as a Person, not an idea.

2. Paul laid his hands on them

  • Baptism in the name of Jesus.
  • The laying on of hands.
  • A little Pentecost in Ephesus.
“But Moses said to him, “Are you jealous for my sake? Would that all the Lord’s people were prophets, and that the Lord would put his spirit on them!”” Numbers 11:29
“When Paul had laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they spoke in tongues and prophesied” Acts 19:6

Apply: Thank God for your Confirmation, and pray this week for one young person preparing for it.

3. Take courage

  • Disciples who will scatter.
  • I am not alone.
  • A victory already won.
“I hereby command you: Be strong and courageous; do not be frightened or dismayed, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go.” Joshua 1:9
“I have said this to you, so that in me you may have peace. In the world you face persecution. But take courage; I have conquered the world!” John 16:33

Apply: Face one fear or difficulty this week in the name of Jesus, asking the Spirit for the gift of fortitude.

Like the disciples at Ephesus, we need more than a beginning; we need the Holy Spirit, given in Baptism and Confirmation and renewed in prayer.

Christ has already conquered the world; in him we have peace, and through his Spirit, the courage of St John of Avila and of all the saints.

Take-home resolution: Ask the Holy Spirit each day until Pentecost for one of his seven gifts.

Full homily

Dear friends, imagine Paul walking into Ephesus and meeting twelve people who say they are believers. He looks at them and asks a simple question: did you receive the Holy Spirit when you became believers? And they answer: no, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit (Acts 19:2).

They were good people. They had been baptized with John's baptism, a baptism of repentance. They had turned from sin. But they had stopped halfway. Paul baptized them in the name of the Lord Jesus, laid his hands on them, and the Holy Spirit came upon them. A little Pentecost in Ephesus.

Now, if Paul walked into our church this morning and asked the same question, what would we say? We would say: yes, of course, we were baptized and confirmed. But do we live like people who have received the Spirit? Do we ever pray to him? Do we ask his help before a hard decision? For many Christians, the Holy Spirit is the forgotten Person of the Trinity.

We are in the days between the Ascension and Pentecost. This is the best time to get to know him. Pray every day: Come, Holy Spirit. Ask him for his gifts: wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge, piety, fear of the Lord. He is not an idea; he is a Person, and he is waiting to be asked.

In the Gospel Jesus knows that his disciples will run away that very night. Yet he says: take courage; I have conquered the world (John 16:33). The Spirit is the one who gives us that courage. The same frightened disciples, after Pentecost, would stand before rulers and say: we cannot keep from speaking.

Today we remember St John of Avila, a Spanish priest who was falsely accused and put in prison. In prison he did not grow bitter; he grew in love for Christ. He came out and became one of the great preachers of his time. That is what the Holy Spirit does. So let us ask him today: Come, Holy Spirit, and give us courage. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, St Paul met some people who had never heard of the Holy Spirit! He prayed with them, and the Holy Spirit came into their hearts.

You received the Holy Spirit when you were baptized. He is your best helper. When you are scared or need to be good, just say: Holy Spirit, help me!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you told your disciples: take courage, I have conquered the world. We confess that we too are often afraid, and that we know the Holy Spirit only a little. Send him upon us as he came on the disciples at Ephesus when Paul laid his hands on them. Renew in us the grace of our Baptism and Confirmation; give us his gifts of wisdom and fortitude; and make us witnesses who speak boldly of the kingdom of God. Through the prayers of St John of Avila, give your Church holy priests who love the Eucharist and preach your Word with fire. In you may we have peace. You live and reign for ever and ever. Alleluia. Amen.
