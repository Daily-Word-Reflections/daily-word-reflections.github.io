Word for the day

“Jesus answered them, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.”” John 2:19

Reflection

On the Third Sunday of Lent Year B places two great signs side by side: the Law given on Sinai and the Temple cleansed in Jerusalem. The first reading begins not with a command but with a reminder of grace: “I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery.” Only then come the Ten Words: no other gods, no idols, no misuse of God's name, keep holy the sabbath, honour father and mother, do not kill, commit adultery, steal, bear false witness or covet. The commandments are not chains placed on slaves; they are the way of life for a people already set free. That is why the Psalm can sing that the law of the LORD is “perfect, reviving the soul,” more to be desired than gold, “sweeter also than honey.” The Church's response puts Peter's words on our lips: “Lord, you have the words of everlasting life.”

In the Gospel Jesus goes up to Jerusalem for the Passover and finds the Temple courts turned into a market: oxen, sheep and doves for sale, money changers at their tables. “Making a whip of cords, he drove all of them out of the temple,” poured out the coins and overturned the tables: “Stop making my Father's house a marketplace!” The disciples remember the Psalm: “Zeal for your house will consume me.” This is not a loss of temper; it is a prophetic sign. Malachi had promised that the Lord would suddenly come to his temple to purify it, and now he has come. When the leaders demand a sign, Jesus gives a riddle: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” They think of the stone building, forty-six years in the making. “But he was speaking of the temple of his body.” The true meeting place of God and humanity is no longer a building but the Body of Christ, destroyed on the cross and raised on the third day. Through baptism we are built into that Body; we become, in St Paul's words, God's temple.

St Paul tells us what sign we preach: “Jews demand signs and Greeks desire wisdom, but we proclaim Christ crucified, a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Gentiles, but to those who are the called… Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God.” The cross looks like weakness and folly, yet “God's foolishness is wiser than human wisdom, and God's weakness is stronger than human strength.” Lent is the time to let Jesus cleanse the temple of our hearts: to overturn the tables where we bargain with God, drive out the idols of money, pleasure and pride, and keep his commandments with love. Where there are catechumens, the Church today reads the Year A Gospel of the Samaritan woman at the well and celebrates the First Scrutiny, praying that the elect may be freed from what is weak and sinful and filled with the living water of Christ. Both Gospels lead to the same place: the new temple, from which living water flows, is Jesus himself.

Homily outline

Theme: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.”

The Ten Words of freedom, the zeal of Jesus, the temple of his Body and the power of the cross.

1. The Ten Words of freedom

  • Grace before command.
  • Love of God, love of neighbour.
  • Sweeter than honey.
“I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery; you shall have no other gods before me.” Exodus 20:2–3
““Do not think that I have come to abolish the law or the prophets; I have come not to abolish but to fulfill.” Matthew 5:17

Apply: Pray through one commandment each day this week and ask how to keep it with love.

2. Zeal for your house

  • A market in the house of prayer.
  • The whip of cords.
  • The Lord comes to his temple.
“It is zeal for your house that has consumed me; the insults of those who insult you have fallen on me.” Psalm 69:9
“He told those who were selling the doves, “Take these things out of here! Stop making my Father's house a marketplace!” His disciples remembered that it was written, “Zeal for your house will consume me.”” John 2:16–17

Apply: Name the table in your heart where you bargain with God, and let Jesus overturn it in confession.

3. The temple of his body

  • Destroyed on the cross.
  • Raised on the third day.
  • We, living stones.
“See, I am sending my messenger to prepare the way before me, and the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple.” Malachi 3:1
“Jesus answered them, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.”” John 2:19

Apply: Spend ten minutes before the Blessed Sacrament this week, adoring the true temple.

4. Christ crucified

  • Signs and wisdom.
  • A stumbling block and folly.
  • The power and wisdom of God.
“The law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul; the decrees of the LORD are sure, making wise the simple;” Psalm 19:7
“but we proclaim Christ crucified, a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Gentiles, but to those who are the called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God.” 1 Corinthians 1:23–24

Apply: Make the sign of the cross slowly and thoughtfully each morning, as a confession of faith.

God's commandments are the way of freedom; Jesus cleanses and fulfils.

The crucified and risen Body of Christ is the new temple, and we are built into it.

Take-home resolution: Make an examination of conscience with the Ten Commandments and go to confession before Easter.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today's first reading begins: I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery (Exodus 20:2). Before God gives a single commandment, he reminds Israel: I have set you free. The Ten Commandments are not chains. They are the road map of freedom.

That is why the Psalm can say the law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul, sweeter also than honey. And we answer with Peter: Lord, you have the words of everlasting life.

In the Gospel Jesus goes into the Temple and finds a market. He makes a whip of cords, overturns the tables and says: stop making my Father's house a marketplace! (John 2:16). His disciples remember: zeal for your house will consume me.

Then he says: destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up (John 2:19). He was speaking of the temple of his body. The true meeting place of God and humanity is Jesus himself, crucified and risen. And by baptism we are built into him.

St Paul says: we proclaim Christ crucified, a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Gentiles, but to those who are the called, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God (1 Corinthians 1:23–24). The cross looks weak, but it is stronger than all human strength.

This Lent, let Jesus walk into the temple of your heart. Let him overturn the tables where you bargain with God, drive out the idols, and make your heart a house of prayer. And let us pray for the elect at their scrutinies, that they may come to the living water at Easter. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus went to God's house and found people buying and selling and making noise. He said: this is my Father's house, a place to pray!

Our hearts are God's house too. Let us keep them clean by saying sorry and saying our prayers.

Prayer

O God, author of every mercy and of all goodness, who in fasting, prayer and almsgiving have shown us a remedy for sin, look graciously on this confession of our lowliness, that we, who are bowed down by our conscience, may always be lifted up by your mercy. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, zeal for your Father's house consumed you. Cleanse the temple of our hearts, drive out every idol, write your commandments on our hearts, and give the elect the living water of your Spirit. Amen.
