Word for the day

“For the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.” Mark 10:45

Reflection

The first reading is a prayer, one of the most fervent in the Wisdom books. Ben Sira's people are small and pressed on every side by powerful nations, and he cries out: “Have mercy upon us, O God of all, and put all the nations in fear of you.” But his deepest wish is not revenge. It is that the nations should come to know the true God: “As you have used us to show your holiness to them, so use them to show your glory to us. Then they will know, as we have known, that there is no God but you, O Lord.” Israel's suffering and Israel's rescue are meant to become a lesson for the whole world. Then the prayer grows tender: “Gather all the tribes of Jacob,” “Have mercy, O Lord, on the people called by your name, on Israel, whom you have named your firstborn,” “Have pity on the city of your sanctuary, Jerusalem.” And he ends with a line that the Church can pray at every Mass: “Hear, O Lord, the prayer of your servants, according to your goodwill toward your people, and all who are on the earth will know that you are the Lord, the God of the ages.” The Psalm, from a time of national disaster, prays in the same spirit: “Help us, O God of our salvation, for the glory of your name; deliver us, and forgive our sins, for your name's sake.”

In the Gospel Jesus is walking ahead of his disciples on the road up to Jerusalem, and “they were amazed, and those who followed were afraid.” For the third time he tells them plainly what will happen: he will be handed over, condemned, mocked, flogged and killed, “and after three days he will rise again.” And at that very moment James and John come with a request: “Grant us to sit, one at your right hand and one at your left, in your glory.” They are thinking of thrones; he is thinking of a cross. “You do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup that I drink?” They answer bravely, “We are able,” and in time they will: James will be the first of the Twelve to die for Christ. The other ten are angry, perhaps because they wanted the same seats. So Jesus gathers them all and turns the world upside down: among the nations, rulers “lord it over them,” “but it is not so among you; but whoever wishes to become great among you must be your servant, and whoever wishes to be first among you must be slave of all. For the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.” St Philip Neri, whom we remember today, could have had honours in the Rome of the popes. He refused the cardinal's hat more than once. He spent his life hearing confessions, visiting the sick in the hospitals, walking with young men from church to church, and making people laugh so that pride would have no room. He showed that the greatness Jesus speaks of can be joyful.

Homily outline

Theme: “The Son of Man came not to be served but to serve.”

A prayer that all nations may know God, a request for thrones, and a Master who serves.

1. That all may know you

  • A small people's great prayer.
  • Holiness shown to the nations.
  • Hear the prayer of your servants.
“Hear, O Lord, the prayer of your servants, according to your goodwill toward your people, and all who are on the earth will know that you are the Lord, the God of the ages.” Sirach 36:22
“And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” John 17:3

Apply: Pray each day this week for one nation or people who do not yet know Christ.

2. The cup and the thrones

  • Jesus walks ahead.
  • Right hand and left.
  • “We are able.”
“Help us, O God of our salvation, for the glory of your name; deliver us, and forgive our sins, for your name's sake.” Psalm 79:9
“But Jesus said to them, “You do not know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup that I drink, or be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with?”” Mark 10:38

Apply: When you receive Holy Communion this week, tell the Lord: I am willing to share your cup, give me strength.

3. Not to be served but to serve

  • Rulers who lord it over others.
  • “It is not so among you.”
  • St Philip Neri's joyful service.
“Do not exalt yourself, or you may fall and bring dishonor upon yourself.” Sirach 1:30
“But it is not so among you; but whoever wishes to become great among you must be your servant,” Mark 10:43

Apply: Each day this week do one hidden act of service with joy, as St Philip Neri would.

Ben Sira prays that God's people may make God known to the nations.

Jesus shows how: not by thrones, but by the cup of self-giving and the joy of service.

Take-home resolution: Look each day this week for the lowest place, and take it gladly.

Full homily

Dear friends, Ben Sira prays for his small, suffering people, and his prayer is wider than his own nation. As you have used us to show your holiness to them, he prays, so use them to show your glory to us. Then they will know, as we have known, that there is no God but you, O Lord (Sirach 36:4–5). He wants the whole world to know the true God. That is still the prayer of the Church: that all who are on the earth will know that you are the Lord.

In the Gospel Jesus is walking ahead on the road to Jerusalem. He tells the Twelve for the third time that he will suffer, die and rise. And right then James and John come and ask for the best seats: one at your right hand and one at your left, in your glory (Mark 10:37). We smile at them. But how often we do the same: we follow Jesus, and quietly we hope for a little honour, a good name, a front seat.

Jesus answers gently: are you able to drink the cup that I drink? The cup is his suffering, his self-giving. Then he calls all twelve together and says: whoever wishes to become great among you must be your servant. For the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many (Mark 10:43, 45). In his kingdom, the chair of honour is a servant's stool.

St Philip Neri understood this. In Rome, a city full of honours and titles, he refused to become a cardinal. He spent his days in the confessional, in the hospitals, with poor young men. And he did it with joy, with jokes, with music. He showed that serving is not a sad duty; it is the joy of the Holy Spirit. Let us ask for that joy today, and look for one small chance to serve. Amen.

For children

Children, two of Jesus' friends wanted the best chairs, right next to Jesus! Jesus said: in my kingdom the most important person is the one who helps others. St Philip Neri was like that. He helped poor people and sick people, and he was always laughing and joking. Today, can you help someone at home, and do it with a big smile, like St Philip?

Prayer

O God, who never cease to bestow the glory of holiness on the faithful servants you raise up for yourself, graciously grant that the Holy Spirit may kindle in us that fire with which he wonderfully filled the heart of Saint Philip Neri. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, you came not to be served but to serve. Deliver us from the wish for the first seats, and give us joy in the lowest place, so that all the earth may know that you are Lord. Amen.
