Word for the day

“Truly I tell you, no prophet is accepted in the prophet's hometown.” Luke 4:24

Reflection

Naaman is everything the world admires: commander of the army of Aram, “a great man and in high favor with his master,” a mighty warrior. But the reading adds one short sentence that changes everything: he “suffered from leprosy.” The way to healing begins with the smallest person in the story, a young girl from Israel taken captive in a raid, who serves Naaman's wife. She does not curse her masters; she wishes them well: “If only my lord were with the prophet who is in Samaria!” Naaman sets out with letters, silver, gold and fine garments, expecting a cure worthy of his rank. Elisha does not even come to the door. He sends a messenger with a plain word: “Go, wash in the Jordan seven times.” Naaman is offended. He wanted a ceremony, a great man waving his hand over the spot. Are not the rivers of Damascus better than this muddy stream? Again it is the little ones who save him. His servants say: if he had asked something hard you would have done it; how much more when he only says, “Wash, and be clean.” Naaman goes down, immerses himself seven times, and his flesh becomes “like the flesh of a young boy.” And his heart is healed with his skin: “Now I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel.”

In the Gospel Jesus is in his own synagogue at Nazareth. The people who watched him grow up cannot accept that the carpenter's son speaks for God. Jesus reminds them of two old stories: Elijah was sent to a widow in Zarephath of Sidon, and Elisha cleansed “none of them… except Naaman the Syrian.” God's mercy has always gone out beyond the borders people draw for it, and it is often received by outsiders who know their need while insiders think they have no need. The townspeople are filled with rage and try to throw him from the cliff, “but he passed through the midst of them and went on his way.” The hour of his Passion has not yet come. In Lent the Church reads Naaman's story as a picture of Baptism: the proud man goes down into the water, comes up with the skin of a child, and confesses the one God. It is also a lesson for the baptized. God usually heals us through simple things: water, a word of absolution, bread and wine, a humble servant's advice. Pride wants something grand; grace asks only that we go down into the water.

Homily outline

Theme: “Go, wash, and be clean.”

The little captive girl, the humble waters of the Jordan, and the prophet rejected at home.

1. Grace begins with the little ones

  • A great man with a hidden wound.
  • A captive girl who wishes her master well.
  • Servants who speak sense.
“She said to her mistress, “If only my lord were with the prophet who is in Samaria! He would cure him of his leprosy.”” 2 Kings 5:3
“He has brought down the powerful from their thrones, and lifted up the lowly;” Luke 1:52

Apply: Thank God for one humble person who led you closer to him, and tell them so this week.

2. Go down into the water

  • Pride wants a ceremony.
  • Seven times in a muddy river.
  • The skin of a child.
“So he went down and immersed himself seven times in the Jordan, according to the word of the man of God; his flesh was restored like the flesh of a young boy, and he was clean.” 2 Kings 5:14
“And this is what some of you used to be. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and in the Spirit of our God.” 1 Corinthians 6:11

Apply: Go to confession before Holy Week and receive God's simple, powerful word of pardon.

3. No prophet in his hometown

  • Too familiar to be heard.
  • Mercy beyond our borders.
  • He went on his way.
“As a deer longs for flowing streams, so my soul longs for you, O God. My soul thirsts for God, for the living God.” Psalm 42:1–2
“There were also many lepers in Israel in the time of the prophet Elisha, and none of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian.” Luke 4:27

Apply: Listen this week, with an open heart, to someone whose voice you have stopped hearing.

God heals the proud through humble means and humble people.

The waters of Baptism make us new, and his mercy reaches every nation.

Take-home resolution: Renew your baptismal promises each morning this week with holy water.

Full homily

Dear friends, Naaman had everything: power, wealth, victories, the favour of his king. But he had leprosy. And the first person who points him to healing is a little captive girl from Israel, far from home, who wishes her master well (2 Kings 5:3).

Naaman expected a great ceremony. Elisha did not even come out; he sent word: go, wash in the Jordan seven times. Naaman was angry. Are not the rivers of Damascus better? It was his servants who said: if he had asked something difficult, you would have done it. How much more when he only says, wash, and be clean (2 Kings 5:13).

He went down, and his flesh was restored like the flesh of a young boy, and he was clean (2 Kings 5:14). And he said: now I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. The water healed his skin; humility healed his heart.

In the Gospel the people of Nazareth could not accept Jesus because they knew him too well. He reminded them that Naaman the Syrian was cleansed when many in Israel were not (Luke 4:27). This Lent let us not wait for something grand. God heals us through simple things: water, a word of absolution, bread and wine. Let us go down into the water. Amen.

For children

Children, Naaman was a big, strong general, but he was sick. God told him: go and wash seven times in the river. He thought it was too easy and got angry! But when he obeyed, God healed him. God gave us water too, in our baptism. Let us thank Jesus for our baptism today!

Prayer

Lord God, you healed Naaman the Syrian in the waters of the Jordan and opened his heart to know you, the one God. Heal us of pride, teach us to receive your grace in simple things, and let our thirst for you grow, until we come with joy to the waters of Easter. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
