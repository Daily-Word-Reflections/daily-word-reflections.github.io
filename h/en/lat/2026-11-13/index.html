Word for the day

“Remember Lot's wife. Those who try to make their life secure will lose it, but those who lose their life will keep it.” Luke 17:32–33

Reflection

Jesus compares the day of the Son of Man to the days of Noah and of Lot. People were eating, drinking, marrying, buying, selling, planting, building, all ordinary and good things, until the flood came or fire fell. Their sin was not ordinary life; it was living as if God did not exist. Then the strange command: “Remember Lot's wife.” She was rescued from Sodom but looked back, longing for what she was leaving, and was lost. Jesus asks for an undivided heart.

The Second Letter of John, written to “the elect lady and her children” (probably a local church), joins truth and love: “this is love, that we walk according to his commandments”. Some had gone “beyond” the teaching of Christ, denying that he came in the flesh. John's answer is to abide: “Everyone who does not abide in the teaching of Christ, but goes beyond it, does not have God.” Faithfulness, not novelty, keeps us ready.

Full homily

Dear friends, Jesus compares the day of the Son of Man to the days of Noah and of Lot. People were eating and drinking, marrying, buying and selling, planting and building, until the flood came or fire fell (Luke 17:26–29). None of these things is wrong. Their sin was living as if God did not exist.

Then a strange command: remember Lot's wife (Luke 17:32). She was rescued from Sodom but looked back, longing for what she was leaving, and was lost. Whoever tries to keep his life will lose it, and whoever loses it will keep it.

St John, in his second letter, gives the simple rule for these days: walk in truth and in love, according to the commandment we have had from the beginning, that we love one another (2 John 4–6). Readiness is not anxiety; it is faithfulness in ordinary days.

So let us make a short examen each night this week before sleep: where was God today, where did I forget him, what will I do better tomorrow? Then the day of the Lord will find us looking forward, not back. Amen.

For children

Children, Noah was ready when the rain came because he listened to God. Before you sleep tonight, say: thank you, sorry, and please help me tomorrow.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, keep me ready for your coming. Do not let me live as if you did not exist, nor look back at what I have left for you. Help me to walk in truth and love and to abide in your teaching. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

The Elder warns a Church against teachers who deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. Jesus warns his disciples that the day of the Son of Man will come suddenly, as in the days of Noah and Lot. Both readings ask us to stay with what is true and not to cling to what is passing.

2 John 4–9 · Abide in the teaching

Setting.

The second and third letters of John are the two shortest books of the New Testament. The writer calls himself “the elder”. He writes to “the elect lady and her children” (v. 1), most likely a local Church and its members. Some had left the community and were teaching a false idea of Christ (compare 1 John 2:19).

Structure.

  • vv. 4–6: joy, and the old commandment: love one another, and walk in his commandments.
  • v. 7: deceivers who do not confess Jesus Christ come in the flesh.
  • vv. 8–9: be on guard; whoever abides in the teaching has the Father and the Son.

Key words.

  • en sarki, in the flesh: The deceivers denied that the Son of God truly became man. The faith insists that the Word truly took our flesh.
  • proagōn, going beyond, running ahead: Literally “going ahead”. The false teachers presented themselves as advanced. The Elder says that whoever runs ahead of the teaching of Christ leaves God behind.

Verse by verse.

“And this is love, that we walk according to his commandments” (1:6)

Love and obedience are not two things. To love is to walk in his commandments, and his commandment is to love.

“whoever abides in the teaching has both the Father and the Son.” (1:9)

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism notes that the first heresies denied not so much Christ’s divinity as his true humanity, against which the Church insisted that the Son of God came in the flesh (CCC 465). The Incarnation is the ground of our faith in the sacraments, where Christ still reaches us through bodily signs.

Luke 17:26–37 · Remember Lot’s wife

Setting.

Jesus continues his teaching on the day of the Son of Man (17:22–37). He draws on two stories from Genesis: the flood (Genesis 6–8) and the destruction of Sodom (Genesis 19).

Structure.

  • vv. 26–30: in the days of Noah and of Lot, people went on with ordinary life until judgment came suddenly.
  • vv. 31–33: do not turn back; remember Lot’s wife; whoever tries to secure life will lose it.
  • vv. 34–35: one taken, the other left.
  • v. 37: “Where, Lord?” “Where the corpse is, there the vultures will gather.”
  • ¶ The NRSV has no v. 36. Some manuscripts add “Two will be in the field; one will be taken and the other left” from Matthew 24:40, but the oldest do not have it. The POC numbers the verses without it, so its vv. 36–37 together correspond to NRSV v. 37.

Key words.

  • paralambanesthai, to be taken: Used of being taken along with someone. The saying stresses separation and suddenness, not a secret removal.
  • aetoi, vultures, eagles: The word can mean either. The image is proverbial: the sign of death is plain to all. When the day comes, it will be unmistakable.

Verse by verse.

“Remember Lot’s wife.” (17:32)

She looked back at the burning city and became a pillar of salt (Genesis 19:26). Her heart stayed with what she was leaving.

“Those who try to make their life secure will lose it, but those who lose their life will keep it.” (17:33)

How the Church reads it.

Eating, drinking, marrying and building are not sins. The danger is to live as if they were everything. The Catechism teaches that the Last Judgment will come when Christ returns in glory, and that only the Father knows the day and the hour (CCC 1040). The answer to that uncertainty is not fear but readiness.

Questions people ask

Does Luke 17:34–35 teach a secret “rapture”?

No. Jesus is describing the suddenness of his coming, which will separate people who seem alike outwardly. The Church teaches one glorious coming of Christ at the end (CCC 1038–1040).

For study and teaching

  • What “Sodom” am I tempted to look back at with longing?
  • How can I keep the Incarnation, God truly in the flesh, at the heart of my faith?
