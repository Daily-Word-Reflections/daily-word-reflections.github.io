Word for the day

“But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you, so that you may be children of your Father in heaven; for he makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the righteous and on the unrighteous.” Matthew 5:44–45

Reflection

For several years St Paul organised a collection among his Gentile churches for the poor Christians of Jerusalem (Romans 15:26; 1 Corinthians 16:1–4). It mattered to him greatly: it was a sign that Jews and Gentiles in Christ were one family. In today's reading he tells the Corinthians, who were fairly prosperous, about the churches of Macedonia, Philippi and Thessalonica, who were not: “during a severe ordeal of affliction, their abundant joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity on their part.” Poverty and joy together produced generosity. “They voluntarily gave according to their means, and even beyond their means, begging us earnestly for the privilege of sharing in this ministry to the saints.” The poor begged for the privilege of giving! And Paul gives the secret: “they gave themselves first to the Lord.” When the heart is given, the purse follows. Paul does not command the Corinthians; he invites them to “excel also in this generous undertaking,” and then he points to the source of all giving: “For you know the generous act of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty you might become rich.” The word Paul uses for this “generous act” is charis, grace. The Son of God emptied himself, was born in a manger and died on a cross, and by his poverty we receive the riches of God. The Psalm sings of the same God, “who executes justice for the oppressed; who gives food to the hungry.”

In the Gospel Jesus reaches the summit of the Sermon on the Mount. “You have heard that it was said, ‘You shall love your neighbor and hate your enemy.’” The Law of Moses commanded love of neighbour (Leviticus 19:18); nowhere did it command hatred of enemies, but many concluded that a neighbour meant only one's own people, and that enemies could be hated. Jesus breaks that boundary: “But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you, so that you may be children of your Father in heaven.” And he gives the reason from the sky itself: the Father “makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the righteous and on the unrighteous.” In these monsoon weeks we see it with our own eyes: the rain does not ask whose field it is falling on. To love only those who love us is no more than “the tax collectors” do; to greet only our own is what everyone does. Then comes the great command: “Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect.” The Greek word, teleios, means complete, mature, whole. St Luke gives the same saying as “Be merciful, just as your Father is merciful” (Luke 6:36). The perfection of the Father is not a cold flawlessness; it is mercy without limits. Every Christian, and not only priests and religious, is called to this holiness (Lumen Gentium 40).

Homily outline

Theme: “Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you.”

The generosity of the poor, the poverty of Christ that makes us rich, and the Father's love that falls like rain on all.

1. Rich by his poverty

  • Joy and poverty overflowing in generosity.
  • They gave themselves first.
  • The generous act of our Lord.
“who executes justice for the oppressed; who gives food to the hungry.” Psalm 146:7
“For you know the generous act of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty you might become rich.” 2 Corinthians 8:9

Apply: Give something this week from your own need, not only from what you have left over, and give it with joy.

2. Sun and rain for all

  • Neighbour and enemy.
  • The Father's love does not choose.
  • Pray for persecutors.
“When you come upon your enemy's ox or donkey going astray, you shall bring it back.” Exodus 23:4
“But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you, so that you may be children of your Father in heaven;” Matthew 5:44–45

Apply: Do one practical kindness this week for someone who does not like you.

3. Be perfect as your Father

  • Teleios: complete and whole.
  • Merciful as the Father is merciful.
  • Every Christian is called to holiness.
“You shall be holy, for I the Lord your God am holy.” Leviticus 19:2
“Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect.” Matthew 5:48

Apply: Each evening this week ask: where was I merciful today, and where did I fail to be?

The poor of Macedonia gave beyond their means because they had first given themselves; Christ became poor to make us rich.

The Father's love falls like rain on all; we become his children when we love our enemies and pray for those who hurt us.

Take-home resolution: Pray every day this week, by name, for one person who has hurt you.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul tells a beautiful story about the churches of Macedonia. They were poor and suffering, yet their abundant joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity (2 Corinthians 8:2). They even begged Paul for the privilege of giving! How is that possible? Paul says: they gave themselves first to the Lord (2 Corinthians 8:5). When we belong to God, giving becomes a joy.

And Paul shows us where this generosity comes from: you know the generous act of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty you might become rich (2 Corinthians 8:9). Jesus left the glory of heaven, was born in a stable, and died with nothing. Everything we have of God, we have because he became poor for us.

In the Gospel Jesus asks the hardest thing of all: love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you (Matthew 5:44). Look at the monsoon rain. It falls on the field of the good farmer and of the lazy one, on the church and on the toddy shop. Jesus says: that is how your Father loves. He makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good. If we love only those who love us, what is special about that?

Our Church in India knows such love. Blessed Rani Maria was killed in 1995, and her family forgave her murderer and welcomed him as a brother. That is what Jesus means by: be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect (Matthew 5:48). Not a perfection of never making mistakes, but a perfection of mercy. Let us ask today for this grace: to pray, by name, for one person who has hurt us. Amen.

For children

Children, when it rains, does the rain fall only on good children's houses? No! It falls on everyone. Jesus says God loves everyone like that. And he asks us to love even people who are not nice to us. Is there someone at school who teases you? Tonight, say a little prayer for them: Jesus, bless my friend. That is a very brave and beautiful thing to do.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, though you were rich, yet for our sakes you became poor, so that by your poverty we might become rich. Give us the joy of the churches of Macedonia, who gave beyond their means because they gave themselves first to you. Father in heaven, you make your sun rise on the evil and on the good and send your rain on all; teach us to love our enemies and to pray for those who persecute us, so that we may truly be your children, merciful as you are merciful. Amen.
