Readings

  • First Reading Jeremiah 17:5–10 · Cursed is the one who trusts in mortals; blessed is the one who trusts in the Lord
  • Responsorial Psalm Psalm 1:1–2, 3, 4 and 6 · Happy are they who hope in the Lord

Word for the day

“There was a rich man who was dressed in purple and fine linen and who feasted sumptuously every day. And at his gate lay a poor man named Lazarus, covered with sores,” Luke 16:19–20

Reflection

Jeremiah sets two pictures side by side. The one who trusts in mere mortals and turns his heart from the LORD “shall be like a shrub in the desert,” living in parched places where nothing good comes. But “Blessed are those who trust in the LORD, whose trust is the LORD. They shall be like a tree planted by water, sending out its roots by the stream. It shall not fear when heat comes, and its leaves shall stay green; in the year of drought it is not anxious, and it does not cease to bear fruit.” In Kerala we know such trees by the backwaters: in the hottest May they stay green because their roots reach the water. Then Jeremiah looks inside: the human heart is devious and hard to understand, but “I the LORD test the mind and search the heart, to give to all according to their ways.” Where are the roots of my heart? In money, in people's opinion, in my own strength, or in God?

Jesus answers with a story. A rich man, dressed in purple and fine linen, feasts every day. At his gate lies a poor man, covered with sores, longing for the scraps from the table; only the dogs come to him. The rich man is never said to be cruel. He simply does not see. The poor man has a name, Lazarus, which means God helps; the rich man has none. Both die. Lazarus is carried by the angels to Abraham's side; the rich man finds himself in torment and begs for a drop of water. Abraham reminds him: you received your good things in your lifetime; and between us and you a great chasm has been fixed. The chasm in eternity is the gate he never crossed in time. Then the rich man begs for his five brothers, and Abraham replies: “If they do not listen to Moses and the prophets, neither will they be convinced even if someone rises from the dead.” Lent asks us to open our eyes to the Lazarus at our gate, while there is still time, and to listen to the One who has indeed risen from the dead.

Full homily

Dear friends, Jeremiah shows us two trees. One is a shrub in the desert: the person who trusts only in human strength. The other is a tree planted by water: blessed are those who trust in the LORD, whose trust is the LORD (Jeremiah 17:7). In the year of drought it is not anxious, and it does not cease to bear fruit.

Then Jesus tells of a rich man who feasted sumptuously every day, and at his gate lay a poor man named Lazarus, covered with sores (Luke 16:19–20). The rich man did not beat Lazarus. He did not chase him away. He simply did not see him. That was his sin.

Notice: the poor man has a name, Lazarus, God helps. The rich man has no name. In God's eyes the forgotten are remembered. After death the gate the rich man never crossed becomes a great chasm that cannot be crossed.

And Abraham says: if they do not listen to Moses and the prophets, neither will they be convinced even if someone rises from the dead (Luke 16:31). Someone has risen from the dead: Jesus. Let us listen to him today, open our eyes, and cross the gate to Lazarus while there is time. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told about a rich man who had lots of food but never shared with poor Lazarus at his door. Jesus wants us to see people who need help. Can you share something with someone today?

Prayer

Lord God, you search the heart and know us. Root us in trust in you like a tree beside the water, open our eyes to the Lazarus at our gate, and make us listen to your word while there is time. Through Christ our Lord, who rose from the dead. Amen.
