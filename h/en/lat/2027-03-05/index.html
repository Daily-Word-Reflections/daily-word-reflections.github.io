Word for the day

“Jesus answered, “The first is, ‘Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one; you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.’” Mark 12:29–30

Reflection

The book of Hosea ends with one of the most beautiful invitations in the Bible. The prophet has spent fourteen chapters naming Israel's unfaithfulness, using the painful image of a wife who leaves her husband for other lovers. Now God himself teaches his people the words of their return: “Take words with you and return to the LORD; say to him, ‘Take away all guilt; accept that which is good.’” They are to renounce the false securities they trusted: “Assyria shall not save us; we will not ride upon horses; we will say no more, ‘Our God,’ to the work of our hands.” And then God answers with pure tenderness: “I will heal their disloyalty; I will love them freely, for my anger has turned from them. I will be like the dew to Israel; he shall blossom like the lily, he shall strike root like the forests of Lebanon.” This is what conversion looks like from God's side: not a grudging acceptance, but a garden in bloom. The Psalm echoes the same longing: “O that my people would listen to me… I would feed you with the finest of the wheat, and with honey from the rock I would satisfy you.”

In the Gospel a scribe asks Jesus the question every teacher of the law discussed: “Which commandment is the first of all?” Jesus answers with the Shema, the prayer devout Jews still pray every morning and evening: “Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one; you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.” Then, without being asked, he adds a second: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.” The two cannot be separated. The scribe agrees warmly: this “is much more important than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices.” Jesus looks at him and says: “You are not far from the kingdom of God.” Not far, but not yet inside. What is still missing? Jesus himself: the one who loves the Father with his whole being and loves his neighbour to the point of laying down his life. On this Lenten Friday, at the foot of the Cross, we see the two great commandments joined in one act of love. Return to the Lord, says Hosea; love him with all your heart, says Jesus. Both lead to the same place: the pierced Heart of Christ, from which healing and new life flow like dew.

Homily outline

Theme: “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart.”

The words of return, the dew of God's free love, and the two commandments joined at the Cross.

  • God teaches the words of repentance.
  • Renouncing false securities.
  • Accept that which is good.
“Return, O Israel, to the LORD your God, for you have stumbled because of your iniquity. Take words with you and return to the LORD;” Hosea 14:1–2
“So he set off and went to his father. But while he was still far off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion; he ran and put his arms around him and kissed him.” Luke 15:20

Apply: Write your own short act of contrition and pray it each night until Easter.

2. Like the dew

  • I will love them freely.
  • The lily and the cedar.
  • Grace makes us bloom.
“I will heal their disloyalty; I will love them freely, for my anger has turned from them. I will be like the dew to Israel; he shall blossom like the lily, he shall strike root like the forests of Lebanon.” Hosea 14:4–5
“We love because he first loved us.” 1 John 4:19

Apply: Notice one sign of new growth God has given you this Lent and thank him for it.

3. The first and the second

  • Hear, O Israel.
  • All your heart, soul, mind and strength.
  • Your neighbour as yourself.
“Hear, O Israel: The LORD is our God, the LORD alone. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your might.” Deuteronomy 6:4–5
“Those who say, “I love God,” and hate their brothers or sisters, are liars; for those who do not love a brother or sister whom they have seen, cannot love God whom they have not seen.” 1 John 4:20

Apply: Pray the Way of the Cross today, and at each station name one neighbour to love better.

God himself teaches us to return, and receives us like dew on a dry garden.

Full homily

Dear friends, at the end of the book of Hosea God himself teaches his people how to come back: take words with you and return to the LORD; say to him, take away all guilt; accept that which is good (Hosea 14:2). God does not wait with folded arms. He goes before us and puts the words of repentance in our mouths.

And he answers: I will heal their disloyalty; I will love them freely… I will be like the dew to Israel; he shall blossom like the lily (Hosea 14:4–5). This is how God receives a sinner who returns: not with a cold welcome, but like dew on a dry garden.

In the Gospel a scribe asks: which commandment is the first of all? Jesus answers: hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one; you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength (Mark 12:29–30). And the second: you shall love your neighbor as yourself. They cannot be separated.

Jesus tells the scribe: you are not far from the kingdom of God. Not far, but not yet in. What brings us in is Jesus himself, who on the Cross loved the Father and loved us to the end. On this Friday of Lent let us stand before the Cross, take words with us, and return. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus said the most important thing is to love God with all your heart, and to love other people too. God loves us so much! Today, Friday, let us look at the cross and say: Jesus, I love you with all my heart.

Prayer

Lord our God, you are the one Lord, and you call us to return to you with words of sorrow and hearts full of love. Heal our unfaithfulness, love us freely, be like the dew upon us, and teach us to love you with all our heart and our neighbour as ourselves, after the example of your Son's pierced Heart. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
