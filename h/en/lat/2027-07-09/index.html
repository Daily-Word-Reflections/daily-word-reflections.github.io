Word for the day

“See, I am sending you out like sheep into the midst of wolves; so be wise as serpents and innocent as doves.” Matthew 10:16

Reflection

Jacob is an old man, and the journey he now takes is the hardest of his life. Joseph is alive and has sent wagons, and the whole family sets out for Egypt. But Egypt is not the land of the promise; it is a place where the patriarchs went in times of famine, and Isaac had once been forbidden to go there (Genesis 26:2). So at Beer-sheba, on the southern edge of Canaan, Jacob stops. He does not simply go. “When Israel set out on his journey with all that he had and came to Beer-sheba, he offered sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac.” And God answers: “God spoke to Israel in visions of the night, and said, ‘Jacob, Jacob.’ And he said, ‘Here I am.’” The double call is the call of the great vocations, the call of Abraham on Mount Moriah and of Moses at the burning bush. Then come three promises. “I am God, the God of your father; do not be afraid to go down to Egypt, for I will make of you a great nation there.” That is the first: the promise of a people. “I myself will go down with you to Egypt.” That is the second: God himself will travel with him into the foreign land. “And I will also bring you up again; and Joseph's own hand shall close your eyes.” The third is the promise of a return, and of a gentle death in the arms of a son. So the whole house of Jacob goes down, sons, grandsons, daughters, little ones, flocks, in the wagons that Pharaoh sent; the chapter counts seventy persons, the seed of the people of God. At the end of the road comes the meeting. Israel sends Judah ahead to Goshen, and it is the man who once offered himself in place of Benjamin who leads the way. Then “Joseph made ready his chariot and went up to meet his father Israel in Goshen. He presented himself to him, fell on his neck, and wept on his neck a good while.” The father's first words are among the most moving in all the Bible: “I can die now, having seen for myself that you are still alive.” For more than twenty years he had grieved for a son he thought dead. Now he asks nothing more of life. The Psalm is the song of an old believer who has seen it all: “Trust in the Lord, and do good,” “Take delight in the Lord, and he will give you the desires of your heart,” and the sweet promise that “in the days of famine they have abundance.” It ends: “The salvation of the righteous is from the Lord; he is their refuge in the time of trouble.”

In the Gospel the mission discourse takes a darker turn. Jesus does not promise his messengers an easy road: “See, I am sending you out like sheep into the midst of wolves; so be wise as serpents and innocent as doves.” The disciples must be shrewd, not naive, and pure, not cunning. They will be handed over to councils and flogged in the synagogues; they will be dragged before governors and kings “as a testimony to them and the Gentiles.” Even this is mission: a trial becomes a pulpit. Jesus promises help for exactly those moments: “When they hand you over, do not worry about how you are to speak or what you are to say; for what you are to say will be given to you at that time; for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father speaking through you.” The words about family betrayal are harsh: “Brother will betray brother to death, and a father his child.” In every age, in lands where the Church is persecuted, they have been true. “You will be hated by all because of my name. But the one who endures to the end will be saved.” Jesus also allows the prudent step: “When they persecute you in one town, flee to the next.” The martyr does not go looking for death; he goes on bearing witness, and if it comes, he does not run from it. The saying that closes the passage, “you will not have gone through all the towns of Israel before the Son of Man comes,” is a difficult one, read in several ways in the tradition of the Church; it tells at least that the work of the mission will not be finished before the Lord comes. The Church may keep today the memorial of St Augustine Zhao Rong and his companions, the 120 martyrs of China whom Pope John Paul II canonised in the Jubilee Year 2000. Bishops, priests, religious and lay women and men, Chinese and foreign, they died for the faith in the centuries from 1648 to 1930. Augustine Zhao Rong was a Chinese priest who, according to the usual account, had first been a soldier guarding a captive missionary and was so moved by his faith that he asked for baptism; he died in prison in 1815. Closer to home, India too has her martyrs; St Devasahayam, a layman of the Tamil country who was killed in 1752 for his faith, was canonised in 2022. They show how Jacob's three promises hold good: God gives a people, he goes down with them into every foreign land, and he brings them up again.

Homily outline

Theme: “I myself will go down with you.”

Jacob goes down into Egypt with God's promise; Jesus sends his disciples as sheep among wolves, with the Spirit of the Father to speak in them.

1. Do not be afraid to go down

  • Sacrifice at Beer-sheba.
  • Jacob, Jacob; Here I am.
  • Three promises.
“Then he said, “I am God, the God of your father; do not be afraid to go down to Egypt, for I will make of you a great nation there. I myself will go down with you to Egypt, and I will also bring you up again; and Joseph's own hand shall close your eyes.”” Genesis 46:3–4
“And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” Matthew 28:20

Apply: Before the next frightening task or journey, say aloud: Lord, you go down with me.

2. I can die now

  • Judah leads the way.
  • Joseph wept a good while.
  • A life that has seen faithfulness.
“Israel said to Joseph, “I can die now, having seen for myself that you are still alive.”” Genesis 46:30
“Master, now you are dismissing your servant in peace, according to your word;” Luke 2:29

Apply: Call or visit an elderly person this week and listen to what God's faithfulness has meant in their life.

3. Sheep in the midst of wolves

  • Wise as serpents.
  • Innocent as doves.
  • Even trials are mission.
“The salvation of the righteous is from the Lord; he is their refuge in the time of trouble.” Psalm 37:39
“See, I am sending you out like sheep into the midst of wolves; so be wise as serpents and innocent as doves.” Matthew 10:16

Apply: Choose prudence and purity together in one real decision this week: neither naive nor cunning.

4. It is not you who speak

  • The Spirit of your Father.
  • The martyrs of China and of India.
  • Endure to the end.
“Trust in the Lord, and do good; so you will live in the land, and enjoy security.” Psalm 37:3
“When they hand you over, do not worry about how you are to speak or what you are to say; for what you are to say will be given to you at that time; for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father speaking through you.” Matthew 10:19–20

Apply: Learn the name of one martyr, and pray to them this week for the courage to bear witness.

God calls Jacob by name, promises to go down with him into Egypt and to bring him up again, and so begins the long journey of a people.

Jesus sends his disciples like sheep among wolves and gives them the Spirit of the Father; the martyrs show that the promise holds.

Take-home resolution: Offer one prayer before each important step this week, and pray for persecuted Christians by name.

Full homily

Dear friends, Jacob is old, and he is afraid. He has to leave the land of the promise and go down to Egypt, a foreign country and the land of Pharaoh. And what does he do? He does what believers always do: before he sets out, he stops at Beer-sheba and offers sacrifice. And in the night God calls him: Jacob, Jacob. Here I am. And God says: do not be afraid to go down to Egypt, for I will make of you a great nation there. I myself will go down with you (Genesis 46:3–4).

I myself will go down with you. That is one of the most beautiful sentences in the Bible. God does not wave from the shore. God goes down. Later God will go down in the Incarnation, in the womb of Mary, and down to the grave, and in the Mass he goes down on the altar. Wherever you go, whatever you fear, God goes down with you. And then the meeting of father and son: Joseph wept on his neck a good while, and old Jacob said: I can die now, having seen for myself that you are still alive (Genesis 46:30). A life that has seen God's faithfulness can be ended in peace.

In the Gospel Jesus speaks plainly to the Twelve: I am sending you out like sheep into the midst of wolves (Matthew 10:16). He does not hide it. Many of his followers have been put to death. Today the Church remembers St Augustine Zhao Rong and his companions, the martyrs of China. We think too of St Devasahayam, who gave his life for Christ in the south of our own land. How could they do it? Jesus had promised: it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father speaking through you (Matthew 10:20).

Few of us will be asked to die for the faith. But all of us are asked to witness: at work, at home, in the family. To be wise as serpents and innocent as doves. To say a word for the Lord when it is not easy. Do not worry about how you are to speak. Go, trusting the God who said to Jacob: I myself will go down with you. Amen.

For children

Children, old Jacob was going to a new country, and he was afraid. But at night God called: Jacob, Jacob! And God said: do not be afraid, I will go with you. Later Jacob saw his son Joseph again, and he cried with joy. In the Gospel Jesus tells his friends that it will not always be easy to follow him, but the Holy Spirit will help them to find the right words. Some brave Christians, like the martyrs of China, died because they loved Jesus so much. When you feel afraid, say: Jesus, you are with me.

Prayer

God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, you called Jacob by name at Beer-sheba and promised to go down with him into Egypt and bring him up again. Be with all who set out into the unknown this day, and with all who are afraid. Lord Jesus, you sent your disciples out like sheep among wolves and promised them the Spirit of your Father. Give us the wisdom of serpents and the innocence of doves; give us courage when we are brought before the powerful, and words from the Holy Spirit when we have none. Receive into your glory the martyrs of every land, St Augustine Zhao Rong and his companions, and the martyrs of India, and by their prayers keep us faithful to the end. Amen.
