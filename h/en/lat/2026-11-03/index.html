Word for the day

“At the time for the dinner he sent his slave to say to those who had been invited, ‘Come; for everything is ready now.’” Luke 14:17

Reflection

Paul's hymn is one of the oldest Christian confessions: Christ Jesus, though in the form of God, “emptied himself, taking the form of a slave”, and “humbled himself and became obedient to the point of death—even death on a cross”. Therefore God highly exalted him. The way of God is downward before it is upward. Paul introduces the hymn with a simple command: “Let the same mind be in you that was in Christ Jesus.”

In the Gospel the host's banquet is ready, but the invited guests make excuses: a field, oxen, a marriage. None of these is evil; they simply matter more to the guests than the invitation. So the host sends his servant to the streets and lanes for “the poor, the crippled, the blind, and the lame”. The Eucharist is this banquet. The question is not whether we are worthy, but whether we will come.

Homily outline

Theme: “Come; for everything is ready now.”

The Lord who emptied himself invites all, especially the poor, to his banquet.

1. The Lord who came down

  • Christ did not cling to his glory.
  • He became a servant to the point of the cross.
  • His humility is the pattern of our life.
“See, my servant shall prosper; he shall be exalted and lifted up, and shall be very high.” Isaiah 52:13
“For the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.” Mark 10:45

Apply: Choose the lowest place in one situation today.

2. Everything is ready

  • God's feast is prepared.
  • Good things become excuses when they come first.
  • The Eucharist is the banquet now.
“On this mountain the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a feast of rich food, a feast of well-aged wines, of rich food filled with marrow, of well-aged wines strained clear.” Isaiah 25:6
“At the time for the dinner he sent his slave to say to those who had been invited, ‘Come; for everything is ready now.’” Luke 14:17

Apply: Name one excuse and drop it this week.

3. Bring in the poor

  • The host's anger becomes generosity.
  • The poor and the lame fill the hall.
  • Our parish table must have room for them.
“Ho, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters; and you that have no money, come, buy and eat! Come, buy wine and milk without money and without price.” Isaiah 55:1
“Go out at once into the streets and lanes of the town and bring in the poor, the crippled, the blind, and the lame.” Luke 14:21

Apply: Invite someone who is usually left out.

The humble Lord sets the table for the humble.

Come, and bring others with you.

Take-home resolution: Attend one weekday Mass this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul gives us today one of the oldest hymns of the Church. Christ Jesus, though he was God, did not cling to his glory; he emptied himself, took the form of a slave and became obedient even to death on a cross (Philippians 2:6–8). God's way goes down before it goes up.

Paul does not give us this hymn only to admire it. He begins with a command: let the same mind be in you that was in Christ Jesus (Philippians 2:5). The question for today is simple: where am I clinging to my rights, my place, my image?

In the Gospel a man gives a great banquet. Everything is ready, but the invited guests make excuses: a field, some oxen, a marriage. So the master sends his servant into the streets to bring in the poor, the crippled, the blind and the lame (Luke 14:21). The table of God is full of people the world overlooks.

Every Mass is that banquet. Let us not be among those with excuses. Come to the table this week, even on a weekday if you can, and bring someone with you who feels unwanted. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is God, but he came down to be a servant. He invites everyone to his feast, especially the poor. Let us say yes when he invites us to Mass!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you emptied yourself and became obedient unto death. Give me your mind of humility; let no excuse keep me from your table, and open my heart to the poor whom you invite. Amen.
