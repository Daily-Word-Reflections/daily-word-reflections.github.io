Word for the day

“But Jesus said, “Do not stop him; for no one who does a deed of power in my name will be able soon afterward to speak evil of me. Whoever is not against us is for us.”” Mark 9:39–40

Reflection

Ben Sira speaks of Wisdom as a mother and a teacher: “Wisdom teaches her children and gives help to those who seek her. Whoever loves her loves life, and those who seek her from early morning are filled with joy.” Wisdom here is far more than cleverness. She is the way God's own mind shapes a human life, and to serve her is to serve God: “Those who serve her minister to the Holy One; the Lord loves those who love her.” Then the reading becomes very honest about how Wisdom educates. She does not hand out her secrets on the first day. “For at first she will walk with them on tortuous paths; she will bring fear and dread upon them, and will torment them by her discipline until she trusts them.” Every good teacher, every good parent, knows this. Trust is built through testing, and only then “she will come straight back to them again and gladden them, and will reveal her secrets to them.” The Christian reads this page with new eyes, for St Paul calls Christ crucified “the power of God and the wisdom of God.” The Wisdom that walks with us on crooked paths has a face and a name. The Psalm sings the fruit of that long schooling: “Great peace have those who love your law; nothing can make them stumble.”

The Gospel is short and sharp. John, the son of Zebedee, the one whom Jesus called a “son of thunder,” brings a report that he expects will please his Master: “Teacher, we saw someone casting out demons in your name, and we tried to stop him, because he was not following us.” Notice the words: not following “us.” John is guarding the borders of the group. Jesus answers with surprising openness: “Do not stop him; for no one who does a deed of power in my name will be able soon afterward to speak evil of me. Whoever is not against us is for us.” The same thing had happened to Moses in the desert, when Eldad and Medad prophesied outside the tent of meeting and Joshua wanted them silenced; Moses answered, “Would that all the Lord's people were prophets.” Jesus does not deny that there is one Church, built on the apostles, and at once he warns against causing the little ones to stumble. But he refuses a jealous spirit that cannot rejoice when good is done in his name by others. The Second Vatican Council teaches that many elements of sanctification and truth are found outside the visible structure of the Catholic Church, gifts that belong to the Church of Christ and impel towards unity (Lumen Gentium 8). Wisdom, after all, teaches her children to recognise her work wherever it appears.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whoever is not against us is for us.”

Wisdom who teaches her children, the hard paths of her schooling, and a Lord who will not let jealousy shrink his Kingdom.

  • To love Wisdom is to love life.
  • Seek her from early morning.
  • Christ, the Wisdom of God.

  • Discipline that hurts for a time.
  • Then joy and revealed secrets.

  • John guards the borders.
  • Moses and the two prophets in the camp.
  • Glad at every good done in his name.

Apply: Pray by name this week for another parish, Church or group, and speak well of them to someone.

Wisdom schools us patiently, through hard paths, until our hearts can be trusted with her secrets.

Take-home resolution: Each day this week, thank God for one good work done by people outside your own circle.

Full homily

Dear friends, Ben Sira tells us that Wisdom is like a mother who teaches her children. Whoever loves her loves life, and those who seek her from early morning are filled with joy (Sirach 4:12). Notice: from early morning. The wise person does not wait until the day is spoiled to look for God.

But Wisdom is not a soft teacher. At first she will walk with them on tortuous paths; she will bring fear and dread upon them (Sirach 4:17). She tests us before she trusts us. Every student who has struggled with mathematics, every apprentice who has spent months only sweeping the workshop, knows this. Then, Ben Sira says, she comes straight back and gladdens us, and reveals her secrets. For us Christians this Wisdom has a face: Christ, the power of God and the wisdom of God.

In the Gospel John is proud of himself. We saw a man casting out demons in your name, and we stopped him, because he was not following us. Not following us. How easily we say this too: not our parish, not our association, not our Church. Jesus answers: do not stop him. Whoever is not against us is for us (Mark 9:40).

This does not mean that all ways are the same, or that the Church does not matter. It means that we never become jealous of God's goodness. Wherever the sick are healed, the poor are fed and the name of Jesus is honoured, we rejoice, and we pray that all may come to the fullness of his truth and to the unity he prayed for. Let us ask Wisdom to make our hearts as wide as the heart of Christ. Amen.

For children

Children, once the disciples saw a man doing good things in Jesus' name. But he was not in their group, so they told him to stop! Jesus said: do not stop him. Jesus is happy whenever someone does good. So when another child does something kind, even if they are not your friend, you can be happy too and say: thank you, God!

Prayer

God of wisdom, you teach your children by paths we do not always understand, and you gladden those who persevere. Make us lovers of your wisdom, which is Christ your Son. Heal our hearts of the jealousy that wants to keep goodness for ourselves; teach us to rejoice wherever your name is honoured and your works are done, and lead all who believe in Christ into the unity he prayed for. Amen.
