Word for the day

“Blessed are the eyes that see what you see! For I tell you that many prophets and kings desired to see what you see, but did not see it.” Luke 10:23–24

Reflection

Isaiah sees a shoot coming out of the stump of Jesse, the cut-down family tree of David. On this shoot the Spirit of the Lord rests: wisdom and understanding, counsel and might, knowledge and fear of the Lord. These are the gifts the Church sees fulfilled in Christ and shared with us in Confirmation (CCC 1831). Under his rule the wolf lives with the lamb and “the earth will be full of the knowledge of the LORD”.

In the Gospel Jesus, “rejoicing in the Holy Spirit”, thanks the Father for revealing these things not to the wise and intelligent but to “infants”. Then he tells his disciples that they see what prophets and kings longed to see. Advent invites us to become little enough to receive, and to recognise how blessed we are to know Christ.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, on this first weekday of Advent Isaiah shows us a stump. The great family tree of David has been cut down; it looks dead. Yet the prophet says: a shoot shall come out from the stump of Jesse, and a branch shall grow out of his roots (Isaiah 11:1). Advent begins with hope where everything seems finished.

Upon this shoot the Spirit of the Lord will rest: the spirit of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and might, of knowledge and the fear of the Lord (Isaiah 11:2). The Church sees here the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, which Christ possesses in fullness and shares with us in Confirmation.

And the fruit of this King is peace: the wolf shall live with the lamb, the leopard lie down with the kid, and a little child shall lead them (Isaiah 11:6). It is a picture of creation healed. Old enemies are reconciled. The violence of the strong gives way to the trust of the small.

In the Gospel Jesus rejoices in the Holy Spirit and thanks the Father, because these things have been hidden from the wise and the intelligent and revealed to infants (Luke 10:21). God's secrets are opened not to the proud but to the little ones.

Advent asks us to become little again: to admit our need, to stop pretending to have everything under control, to receive like children. The little child of Isaiah's prophecy leads the wild animals; the little Child of Bethlehem will lead us to peace.

Is there a stump in your life, something that looks finished: a marriage, a vocation, a friendship, your prayer? Bring it to the Lord this Advent. He can make a shoot spring from it. And ask the Holy Spirit to renew his seven gifts in you, so that you may be ready for Christmas. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus was happy when little ones understood God. God loves to show His secrets to children. Thank Him today in a short prayer!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, shoot from the stump of Jesse, let your Spirit rest on me too. Make me little enough to receive what you reveal, and grateful to see what prophets and kings longed to see. Amen.
