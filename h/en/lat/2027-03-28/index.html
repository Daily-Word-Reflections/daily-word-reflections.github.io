Easter Sunday of the Resurrection of the Lord

Word for the day

“Then the other disciple, who reached the tomb first, also went in, and he saw and believed;” John 20:8

Reflection

“This is the day that the LORD has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it.” Today the whole Church sings the Psalm of Easter. The stone the builders rejected, the man condemned and crucified on Friday, “has become the chief cornerstone. This is the LORD's doing; it is marvelous in our eyes.” In the first reading Peter stands in the house of Cornelius, a Roman centurion, and tells the whole Gospel in a few lines: Jesus of Nazareth, anointed with the Holy Spirit and power, “went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with him.” They put him to death by hanging him on a tree, “but God raised him on the third day and allowed him to appear, not to all the people but to us who were chosen by God as witnesses, and who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead.” The Resurrection is not a feeling or a beautiful idea. The Apostles ate and drank with the risen Lord; they touched him; they gave their lives for what they had seen. And Peter ends with an offer for everyone: “everyone who believes in him receives forgiveness of sins through his name.”

St Paul tells us what the Resurrection changes in us. “So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.” Through Baptism our life is “hidden with Christ in God,” and when Christ who is our life is revealed, we too will be revealed with him in glory. Easter is not only something that happened to Jesus; it has happened to us. The alternative reading uses the image of the Passover bread: “Clean out the old yeast so that you may be a new batch… For our paschal lamb, Christ, has been sacrificed. Therefore, let us celebrate the festival… with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.” Then, before the Gospel, the ancient Sequence is sung: “Christians, to the Paschal Victim offer your thankful praises… Death and life have contended in that combat stupendous: the Prince of life, who died, reigns immortal.” And it turns to Mary Magdalene: “Speak, Mary, declaring what you saw, wayfaring,” and she answers: the tomb of Christ, who is living, the glory of Jesus' Resurrection, angelic witnesses, the shroud and the napkin.

The Gospel takes us to the garden early on the first day of the week, “while it was still dark.” Mary Magdalene sees the stone removed and runs to Simon Peter and the beloved disciple: “They have taken the Lord out of the tomb, and we do not know where they have laid him.” Two men run. The younger disciple outruns Peter, bends down and sees the linen wrappings lying there, but waits. Peter arrives and goes in. He sees the linen wrappings lying, and the cloth that had been on Jesus' head “not lying with the linen wrappings but rolled up in a place by itself.” No thief would unwrap a body and fold the cloth. Then the other disciple goes in, “and he saw and believed.” He sees signs, an empty tomb and folded cloths, and he believes in what he does not yet see: the living Lord. That is the way of faith for us too. John adds honestly: “as yet they did not understand the scripture, that he must rise from the dead.” Faith begins with a run, a look and a heart open to God; understanding grows afterwards.

The Resurrection of Jesus is the centre of our faith. He did not return to an ordinary life to die again, as Lazarus did. His true body, the body that was crucified and still bears the wounds, has passed into a new and glorious life that will never end. St Paul says plainly: “if Christ has not been raised, then our proclamation has been in vain and your faith has been in vain” (1 Corinthians 15:14). But Christ has been raised, the first fruits of those who have died. Because he lives, sin does not have the last word, death does not have the last word, and the grave of everyone we love is not the end. This is why we gather every Sunday: every Sunday is the day of the Resurrection, a little Easter. And this is why we receive his Body: the risen Lord himself, truly present, gives us the seed of our own resurrection. At every Mass today the faithful renew their baptismal promises and are sprinkled with Easter water. Christ is risen! He is truly risen! Alleluia.

Homily outline

Theme: “He saw and believed.”

The empty tomb, the witnesses who ate with him, the life hidden with Christ, and Christ our Passover.

1. The empty tomb

  • While it was still dark.
  • Two disciples running.
  • The folded cloth: a sign.
“The stone that the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone. This is the LORD's doing; it is marvelous in our eyes.” Psalm 118:22–23
“Then the other disciple, who reached the tomb first, also went in, and he saw and believed;” John 20:8

Apply: Go to Mass every Sunday of the Easter season as a meeting with the risen Lord, not an obligation.

2. Witnesses who ate and drank with him

  • A real, bodily Resurrection.
  • Chosen witnesses.
  • Forgiveness for all who believe.
“I shall not die, but I shall live, and recount the deeds of the LORD.” Psalm 118:17
“but God raised him on the third day and allowed him to appear, not to all the people but to us who were chosen by God as witnesses, and who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead.” Acts 10:40–41

Apply: Learn one simple way to explain the Resurrection to a friend who asks.

3. Seek the things that are above

  • Raised with Christ in Baptism.
  • A life hidden with Christ in God.
  • Revealed with him in glory.
“he will swallow up death forever. Then the Lord GOD will wipe away the tears from all faces” Isaiah 25:8
“So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God. Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth,” Colossians 3:1–2

Apply: Choose one earthly worry and hand it over to God each morning this week.

4. Christ our Passover

  • The blood on the doorposts.
  • The Lamb sacrificed.
  • The bread of sincerity and truth.
“The blood shall be a sign for you on the houses where you live: when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague shall destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt.” Exodus 12:13
“Clean out the old yeast so that you may be a new batch, as you really are unleavened. For our paschal lamb, Christ, has been sacrificed. Therefore, let us celebrate the festival, not with the old yeast, the yeast of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.” 1 Corinthians 5:7–8

Apply: Clean out one “old yeast” this week: a grudge, a lie or a habit of malice.

Christ is truly risen in his body; the tomb is empty and the witnesses ate with him.

Raised with him in Baptism and fed by him in the Eucharist, we live as an Easter people.

Take-home resolution: Greet everyone this week with “Christ is risen!” and live one concrete act of Easter joy each day.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, Christ is risen! He is truly risen! Alleluia! This is the day that the LORD has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it. The stone that the builders rejected has become the chief cornerstone (Psalm 118:22).

Early in the morning, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene came to the tomb. Perhaps you too have come to Easter in some darkness: a loss, an illness, a sin, a worry. Mary found the stone rolled away. The darkness was already breaking, though she did not yet know it.

Peter and John ran. John looked in and saw the linen wrappings lying there, and the cloth from Jesus' head rolled up by itself. Then he went in, and he saw and believed (John 20:8). He saw an empty tomb, and he believed in a living Lord. That is our faith: we see signs, and we believe in him.

And this faith is not a dream. Peter tells Cornelius: God raised him on the third day and allowed him to appear… to us who were chosen by God as witnesses, and who ate and drank with him after he rose from the dead (Acts 10:40–41). The Apostles ate with him, touched him and died for him. The Resurrection is real.

St Paul says: so if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above (Colossians 3:1). At Baptism we were raised with him. So Easter must show in our lives: forgiveness instead of grudges, hope instead of fear, generosity instead of greed.

Today we will renew our baptismal promises, and then receive the risen Lord in the Eucharist. Let us go home as Easter people: greet one another with Christ is risen, visit someone lonely, and let the Alleluia be our song. Because he lives, death is not the end. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is alive! On Easter morning his friends found his tomb empty. He had risen from the dead!

Today let us say to everyone: Jesus is risen! And let us sing Alleluia, which means: praise the Lord!

Prayer

O God, who on this day, through your Only Begotten Son, have conquered death and unlocked for us the path to eternity, grant, we pray, that we who keep the solemnity of the Lord's Resurrection may, through the renewal brought by your Spirit, rise up in the light of life. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Risen Lord Jesus, you have conquered death and opened heaven. Let the joy of this day fill our homes. Raise us from every tomb of sin and sadness, teach us to seek the things that are above, and keep us faithful witnesses of your Resurrection until we see you face to face. Alleluia. Amen.
