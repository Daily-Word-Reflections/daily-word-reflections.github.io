  • Responsorial Psalm Psalm 103:1–2, 13–14, 17–18a · The steadfast love of the Lord is from everlasting to everlasting on those who fear him

Word for the day

“Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary and brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon, and are not his sisters here with us?” And they took offense at him.” Mark 6:3

Reflection

Hebrews speaks to Christians who were tired of suffering: “In your struggle against sin you have not yet resisted to the point of shedding your blood.” It quotes Proverbs: “My child, do not regard lightly the discipline of the Lord, or lose heart when you are punished by him; for the Lord disciplines those whom he loves.” Hardship is not a sign that God has abandoned us; it can be the training of a Father who is making his children holy. “Now, discipline always seems painful rather than pleasant at the time, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness.” So: “lift your drooping hands and strengthen your weak knees… Pursue peace with everyone, and the holiness without which no one will see the Lord,” and let “no root of bitterness” spring up.

In the Gospel Jesus comes home to Nazareth, and his own people stumble over his ordinariness: “Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary?” They know his trade, his mother and his relatives, and so they think they know him. “And they took offense at him.” Familiarity becomes a wall. Mark says something astonishing: “he could do no deed of power there, except that he laid his hands on a few sick people and cured them. And he was amazed at their unbelief.” God does not force himself on us. The same danger is ours: to think we already know Jesus because we have heard of him since childhood. Today the Church remembers St Blaise, bishop and martyr, whose blessing of throats asks God to protect us from illness and every evil; and St Ansgar, the patient missionary of the North, who preached for decades with little visible success.

Homily outline

Theme: “The Lord disciplines those whom he loves.”

A Father's training, a hometown's unbelief, and faith that sees the Lord afresh.

1. The discipline of a Father

  • Not abandonment but training.
  • Painful now, fruitful later.
  • No root of bitterness.
“for the LORD reproves the one he loves, as a father the son in whom he delights.” Proverbs 3:12
“Now, discipline always seems painful rather than pleasant at the time, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.” Hebrews 12:11

Apply: Offer one present hardship to God as training in holiness.

2. Is not this the carpenter?

  • The hometown crowd.
  • They thought they knew him.
  • Familiarity becomes a wall.
“he had no form or majesty that we should look at him, nothing in his appearance that we should desire him.” Isaiah 53:2
“Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary and brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon, and are not his sisters here with us?” And they took offense at him.” Mark 6:3

Apply: Read the Gospel this week as if for the first time.

3. Amazed at their unbelief

  • No deed of power.
  • God does not force.
  • Faith opens the door.
“As a father has compassion for his children, so the LORD has compassion for those who fear him.” Psalm 103:13
“And he could do no deed of power there, except that he laid his hands on a few sick people and cured them. And he was amazed at their unbelief.” Mark 6:5–6

Apply: Make an act of faith before Communion: Lord, I believe.

God's discipline is a Father's love.

Let us welcome Jesus with faith, not with the familiarity that shuts him out.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day this week: Lord, I believe; help my unbelief.

Full homily

Dear friends, Hebrews tells a tired community: my child, do not regard lightly the discipline of the Lord, or lose heart when you are punished by him; for the Lord disciplines those whom he loves (Hebrews 12:5–6). Our hardships are not signs that God has left us.

Discipline always seems painful rather than pleasant at the time, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness (Hebrews 12:11). So lift your drooping hands, pursue peace, and let no root of bitterness spring up.

In Nazareth the people said: is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary? And they took offense at him (Mark 6:3). They thought they knew him, so they could not receive him. He could do no deed of power there, and he was amazed at their unbelief.

We too can take Jesus for granted because we have known him since childhood. Today, through the prayers of St Blaise, let us ask for healing of body and soul, and ask for new eyes to see the Lord. Amen.

For children

Children, the people of Nazareth said: he is only the carpenter! They did not believe in Jesus. Let us always listen to Jesus as if we are hearing him for the first time.

Prayer

Father of spirits, you train us as your children for our good, that we may share your holiness. Keep us from bitterness and from the unbelief that takes your Son for granted. Through the prayers of St Blaise, protect us from every illness of body and soul. Amen.
