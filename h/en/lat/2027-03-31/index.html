Word for the day

“When he was at the table with them, he took bread, blessed and broke it, and gave it to them. Then their eyes were opened, and they recognized him; and he vanished from their sight.” Luke 24:30–31

Reflection

Peter and John go up to the Temple at three o'clock in the afternoon, the hour of prayer. They still pray as faithful Jews, in the Temple of their fathers. At the gate called Beautiful sits a man lame from birth, carried there every day to beg. He looks at them expecting a coin. Peter fixes his eyes on him: “Look at us.” Then he says the words that have become a motto for the Church's mission: “I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.” Peter takes him by the right hand and raises him up, and at once his feet and ankles are made strong. He jumps up, stands, walks, and enters the Temple with them, “walking and leaping and praising God.” Isaiah had promised that in the day of salvation “the lame shall leap like a deer.” The risen Lord is at work through his Church. Peter has no money, but he has the name of Jesus, and that is worth more than all the gold in the Temple. The Psalm sings: “Let the hearts of those who seek the LORD rejoice.”

The Gospel is one of the most beloved pages of the New Testament: the road to Emmaus. On the evening of Easter Sunday two disciples, one named Cleopas, are walking about seven miles from Jerusalem, talking about everything that has happened. Jesus himself comes near and walks with them, “but their eyes were kept from recognizing him.” He asks what they are discussing, and they stand still, “looking sad.” Their words are heartbreaking: “we had hoped that he was the one to redeem Israel.” Hope in the past tense. They even report the women's news of the empty tomb and the angels, but it has not reached their hearts. Then Jesus speaks: “Oh, how foolish you are, and how slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have declared! Was it not necessary that the Messiah should suffer these things and then enter into his glory?” And beginning with Moses and all the prophets, he interprets to them the things about himself in all the Scriptures. Later they will say: “Were not our hearts burning within us while he was talking to us on the road?”

As they near the village he walks ahead as if he were going on. They urge him: “Stay with us, because it is almost evening.” He goes in. At table “he took bread, blessed and broke it, and gave it to them.” These are the same four actions as at the Last Supper and as at every Mass. “Then their eyes were opened, and they recognized him; and he vanished from their sight.” He is not gone; he is now present in a new way, in the bread broken and given. At once, though it is night, they get up and return to Jerusalem, where they hear: “The Lord has risen indeed, and he has appeared to Simon!” And they tell “how he had been made known to them in the breaking of the bread.” The road to Emmaus has the very shape of the Mass: the Lord walks with us in our sadness, explains the Scriptures in the Liturgy of the Word, makes himself known in the breaking of the bread in the Liturgy of the Eucharist, and sends us back on mission. “Breaking of the bread” was one of the first names Christians gave to the Eucharist (Acts 2:42). Every Sunday the risen Lord does for us exactly what he did at Emmaus.

Homily outline

Theme: “They recognized him in the breaking of the bread.”

What I have I give you, hearts burning on the road, and the Lord known at table.

1. What I have I give you

  • The hour of prayer.
  • No silver or gold.
  • Walking and leaping and praising God.
“then the lame shall leap like a deer, and the tongue of the speechless sing for joy.” Isaiah 35:6
“But Peter said, “I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.”” Acts 3:6

Apply: Give one person today something better than money: your time, your attention, and a prayer in Jesus' name.

2. Hearts burning on the road

  • “We had hoped.”
  • The stranger who explains the Scriptures.
  • Slow of heart, set on fire.
“Your words were found, and I ate them, and your words became to me a joy and the delight of my heart; for I am called by your name, O LORD, God of hosts.” Jeremiah 15:16
“They said to each other, “Were not our hearts burning within us while he was talking to us on the road, while he was opening the scriptures to us?”” Luke 24:32

Apply: Read the Sunday Gospel before going to Mass this week, and listen for the word that sets your heart on fire.

3. Known in the breaking of bread

  • “Stay with us.”
  • Took, blessed, broke, gave.
  • Running back to Jerusalem.
“Seek the LORD and his strength; seek his presence continually.” Psalm 105:4
“Then they told what had happened on the road, and how he had been made known to them in the breaking of the bread.” Luke 24:35

Apply: At your next Communion, say silently: “Stay with me, Lord,” and then go and serve someone that same day.

The risen Lord heals through his Church and walks with us in our disappointments.

He sets our hearts on fire with his word and gives himself in the breaking of the bread.

Take-home resolution: Pray each evening of this Octave: “Stay with us, Lord, because it is almost evening.”

Full homily

Dear friends, Peter and John meet a man who has never walked in his life. He asks for a coin. Peter says: I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk (Acts 3:6). And the man goes into the Temple walking and leaping and praising God.

The Church is not rich in silver and gold; her treasure is the name of Jesus. When we give Christ, people are healed and raised up. What do you have to give today?

In the Gospel two disciples are walking away from Jerusalem, sad. They say: we had hoped (Luke 24:21). Hope in the past tense. Perhaps some of us are walking that road too, away from the Church, away from hope.

But Jesus walks with them, though they do not recognize him. He opens the Scriptures, and their hearts begin to burn. Later they say: were not our hearts burning within us while he was talking to us on the road? (Luke 24:32). That is what happens when we listen to God's word.

They invite him: stay with us, because it is almost evening. At table he takes bread, blesses it, breaks it and gives it to them, and their eyes are opened. They recognize him in the breaking of the bread. This is the Mass: the Word that sets hearts on fire, and the Bread that is the risen Lord himself.

And then they run back to Jerusalem, at night, to tell the others. That is what every Mass does to us: it sends us out. Let us ask the Lord today: stay with us, and send us to give what we have, the name of Jesus. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, two friends of Jesus were sad. Jesus walked with them, but they did not know it was him. At supper he broke the bread, and they knew: it is Jesus!

At Mass the priest breaks the bread too, and Jesus is really there. Let us say: Jesus, stay with us!

Prayer

O God, who gladden us year by year with the solemnity of the Lord's Resurrection, graciously grant that, by celebrating these present festivities, we may be worthy through them to reach eternal joys. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Stay with us, Lord, for it is almost evening. Walk with us when we are sad, open the Scriptures to us until our hearts burn, make yourself known to us in the breaking of the bread, and send us back with joy to tell our brothers and sisters that you are risen. Alleluia. Amen.
