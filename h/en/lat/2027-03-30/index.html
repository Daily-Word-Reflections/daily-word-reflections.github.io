  • Gospel John 20:11–18 · I have seen the Lord

Word for the day

“Jesus said to her, “Mary!” She turned and said to him in Hebrew, “Rabbouni!” (which means Teacher).” John 20:16

Reflection

Peter's first Easter sermon reaches its climax: “God has made him both Lord and Messiah, this Jesus whom you crucified.” The listeners are “cut to the heart” and ask the question every converted heart asks: “Brothers, what should we do?” Peter's answer is the shape of Christian beginnings to this day: “Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ so that your sins may be forgiven; and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.” Repentance, Baptism, forgiveness, the Spirit. And the promise is wide: “for you, for your children, and for all who are far away, everyone whom the Lord our God calls to him.” That day about three thousand were added. Peter is not accusing a nation; he is speaking to the people in front of him, as the Church speaks to every one of us, for all of us by our sins had a part in the Lord's death (Catechism 598). The words “this Jesus whom you crucified” are addressed to me. And the same words carry mercy: the One we crucified is the One who forgives.

This Octave the Church remembers the many who were baptized at Easter all over the world. The Psalm is their song and ours: “Truly the eye of the LORD is on those who fear him, on those who hope in his steadfast love, to deliver their soul from death.” “The earth is full of the steadfast love of the LORD.” The newly baptized have been cut to the heart, have turned to Christ, and now begin their life in the Spirit. In the early Church they received special instructions during this week, called mystagogy, explaining the sacraments they had just received. Every Christian can use this week to rediscover his own Baptism: the water, the oil, the white garment, the candle, the name.

In the Gospel Mary Magdalene stands weeping outside the tomb. She bends down and sees two angels in white sitting where the body of Jesus had lain, one at the head and one at the feet, like the cherubim over the ark of the covenant. “Woman, why are you weeping?” “They have taken away my Lord, and I do not know where they have laid him.” She turns and sees Jesus but does not recognize him; she thinks he is the gardener. In a way he is: the new Adam in the garden of the new creation. He asks: “Whom are you looking for?” Then he says one word: “Mary!” And she knows him. The Good Shepherd calls his own sheep by name, and they know his voice. She cries: “Rabbouni!” and clings to him. Jesus says: “Do not hold on to me, because I have not yet ascended to the Father. But go to my brothers.” Her love must now become mission; he will be present to her in a new way, no longer to be held as before, but to be shared. And so she goes: “I have seen the Lord.” The Church calls her “apostle to the apostles.” Like her, we too are called by name in Baptism, and we too are sent.

Homily outline

Theme: “Mary!” “Rabbouni!”

Cut to the heart and baptized, called by name, and sent to announce: I have seen the Lord.

1. What should we do?

  • Cut to the heart.
  • Repent and be baptized.
  • The promise for all who are far away.
“Truly the eye of the LORD is on those who fear him, on those who hope in his steadfast love,” Psalm 33:18
“Peter said to them, “Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ so that your sins may be forgiven; and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.” Acts 2:38

Apply: Make a good confession during the Easter season if you have not done so, and renew your baptismal promises in prayer.

2. Called by name

  • Weeping at the tomb.
  • The gardener of the new creation.
  • One word: Mary!
“But now thus says the LORD, he who created you, O Jacob, he who formed you, O Israel: Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” Isaiah 43:1
“Jesus said to her, “Mary!” She turned and said to him in Hebrew, “Rabbouni!” (which means Teacher).” John 20:16

Apply: Pray today using your baptismal name: “Lord, here I am, you have called me.”

3. Go to my brothers

  • Holding and letting go.
  • From love to mission.
  • Apostle to the apostles.
“when I found him whom my soul loves. I held him, and would not let him go” Song of Songs 3:4
“Mary Magdalene went and announced to the disciples, “I have seen the Lord”; and she told them that he had said these things to her.” John 20:18

Apply: Share with one person this week how you have met the risen Lord.

The word of the Resurrection cuts to the heart and leads to Baptism and the Spirit.

The risen Lord calls each of us by name and sends us to announce him.

Take-home resolution: Thank God for your Baptism today, and tell someone: I have seen the Lord.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the day of Pentecost the crowd heard Peter's preaching and were cut to the heart. They asked: brothers, what should we do? Peter said: repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ so that your sins may be forgiven; and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit (Acts 2:37–38). That day about three thousand were baptized.

Peter's words, this Jesus whom you crucified, are spoken to each of us. Our sins too nailed him to the cross. But the One we crucified is the One who forgives. That is why the earth is full of the goodness of the Lord.

In the Gospel Mary Magdalene is weeping at the tomb. Angels speak to her; Jesus himself stands before her; but she does not recognize him. She thinks he is the gardener. Sometimes we too are so full of our sorrow that we do not see the Lord standing beside us.

Then Jesus says one word: Mary! (John 20:16). Only one person says our name like that. She turns and cries: Rabbouni! At our Baptism Jesus called us by name too. He still does, in prayer, in his word, in the sacraments.

But Jesus says: do not hold on to me… go to my brothers. Mary must not keep him for herself. She goes and says: I have seen the Lord (John 20:18). She becomes the apostle to the apostles.

Today, let the Lord call you by name. Let his word cut you to the heart and heal you. And then go, like Mary, and tell someone: I have seen the Lord. Alleluia. Amen.

For children

Children, Mary was crying because she thought Jesus was gone. But Jesus said her name, and she knew him and became very happy!

Jesus knows your name too. Close your eyes and hear him say it with love. Then say: Jesus, I love you!

Prayer

O God, who have bestowed on us paschal remedies, endow your people with heavenly gifts, so that, possessed of perfect freedom, they may rejoice in heaven over what gladdens them now on earth. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Risen Lord, you called Mary by name and turned her tears into joy. Call us by name today. Cut us to the heart and draw us to repentance, renew in us the grace of our Baptism, and send us to our brothers and sisters to say: I have seen the Lord. Alleluia. Amen.
