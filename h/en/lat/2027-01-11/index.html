Word for the day

“and saying, “The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent, and believe in the good news.”” Mark 1:15

Reflection

Ordinary Time begins with two great openings. The Letter to the Hebrews starts: “Long ago God spoke to our ancestors in many and various ways by the prophets, but in these last days he has spoken to us by a Son.” The prophets were many voices; the Son is God's final Word, “the reflection of God's glory and the exact imprint of God's very being”, greater than the angels. In these weeks we will read Hebrews slowly, learning who Christ is and what he has done.

Mark's Gospel opens Jesus' preaching with four short phrases: “The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent, and believe in the good news.” Then, walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus sees two pairs of brothers at work and says: “Follow me and I will make you fish for people.” Immediately they leave nets, boat and father. Ordinary Time is not dull time; it is the time of following Jesus day by day in ordinary life.

Full homily

Dear friends, today Ordinary Time begins. It is called ordinary not because it is dull, but because its weeks are numbered in order. It is the time to follow Jesus through his public life.

Hebrews begins with a solemn sentence: long ago God spoke to our ancestors in many and various ways by the prophets, but in these last days he has spoken to us by a Son (Hebrews 1:1–2). The prophets were many voices; the Son is the final Word. He is the reflection of God's glory and the exact imprint of God's very being.

Mark gives us Jesus' first preaching: the time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent, and believe in the good news (Mark 1:15). God's time has arrived. We must turn and trust.

Then Jesus walks by the lake and sees Simon and Andrew casting nets, James and John mending nets. Follow me and I will make you fish for people (Mark 1:17). Immediately they leave their nets. Jesus calls them in the middle of their ordinary work.

In these ordinary weeks, let us listen to the Son and let go of one net that holds us back. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus called fishermen to follow him, and they went right away! Jesus calls you too. How will you follow him today?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, eternal Word of the Father, you call ordinary people at their work. Speak to me in these ordinary days, give me a heart that repents and believes, and help me to leave my nets and follow you. Amen.
