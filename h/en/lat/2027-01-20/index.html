Word for the day

“Then he said to them, “Is it lawful to do good or to do harm on the sabbath, to save life or to kill?” But they were silent.” Mark 3:4

Reflection

Hebrews reflects on the mysterious king Melchizedek, who “brought out bread and wine” and blessed Abraham. He appears in Scripture “without father, without mother, without genealogy”, and so “resembling the Son of God, he remains a priest forever.” Christ is a priest not by family descent like the Levites, but “through the power of an indestructible life”. The Church has always seen in Melchizedek's bread and wine a sign of the Eucharist.

In the synagogue a man with a withered hand stands before Jesus while his critics watch. Jesus asks: “Is it lawful to do good or to do harm on the sabbath, to save life or to kill?” They are silent. Mark says Jesus looked at them “with anger; he was grieved at their hardness of heart”, and healed the man. Hardness of heart grieves God more than any broken rule. Today many in Kerala honour St Sebastian, the soldier who served Christ before the emperor and gave his life rather than deny him, and St Fabian, pope and martyr. Both had hearts that were soft toward God and firm in the truth.

Homily outline

Theme: “Is it lawful to do good or to do harm on the sabbath, to save life or to kill?”

The eternal priest, the hardened heart, the brave martyrs.

1. A priest for ever

  • King Melchizedek.
  • Bread and wine.
  • An indestructible life.
“And King Melchizedek of Salem brought out bread and wine; he was priest of God Most High.” Genesis 14:18
“It is even more obvious when another priest arises, resembling Melchizedek, one who has become a priest, not through a legal requirement concerning physical descent, but through the power of an indestructible life.” Hebrews 7:15–16

Apply: Thank Christ the priest at Mass this week.

2. Hardness of heart

  • The withered hand.
  • Silent critics.
  • Jesus grieves and heals.
“I will give them one heart, and put a new spirit within them; I will remove the heart of stone from their flesh and give them a heart of flesh,” Ezekiel 11:19
“He looked around at them with anger; he was grieved at their hardness of heart and said to the man, “Stretch out your hand.” He stretched it out, and his hand was restored.” Mark 3:5

Apply: Ask God to soften one hard place in your heart.

3. Good soldiers of Christ

  • Sebastian the soldier.
  • Fabian the pope.
  • Courage for truth.
“I hereby command you: Be strong and courageous; do not be frightened or dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.”” Joshua 1:9
“Share in suffering like a good soldier of Christ Jesus.” 2 Timothy 2:3

Apply: Defend someone treated unjustly.

Christ the priest heals hardened hearts.

Sebastian and Fabian show the courage of soft hearts.

Take-home resolution: Choose always to do good and save life this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, Hebrews tells us of Melchizedek, king of Salem, who brought out bread and wine and blessed Abraham. He appears without father, without mother, without genealogy, and so, resembling the Son of God, he remains a priest forever (Hebrews 7:3).

Christ is a priest not by family descent, but through the power of an indestructible life. And the bread and wine of Melchizedek point to the Eucharist.

In the Gospel Jesus asks: is it lawful to do good or to do harm on the sabbath, to save life or to kill? But they were silent (Mark 3:4). He looked at them with anger, grieved at their hardness of heart, and healed the withered hand.

Today we honour St Fabian, a pope who died a martyr, and St Sebastian, the Roman soldier loved across Kerala, who served Christ before the emperor and gave his life rather than deny him. Let us ask for hearts soft toward God and firm in courage. Amen.

For children

Children, St Sebastian was a brave soldier for Jesus. Be brave too: stand up for a friend who is being teased.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, eternal priest like Melchizedek, you were grieved at hardness of heart. Soften my heart, give me courage like St Sebastian, and let me always choose to do good and to save life. Amen.
