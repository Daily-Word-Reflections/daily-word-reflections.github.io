Word for the day

“No slave can serve two masters; for a slave will either hate the one and love the other, or be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and wealth.” Luke 16:13

Reflection

Jesus draws the lesson of yesterday's parable: “Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much.” Money is “a very little” compared with the true riches of the Kingdom, but how we handle it reveals our heart. Then the sharp line: “You cannot serve God and wealth.” Wealth is not evil, but it wants to be a master. The Pharisees, “lovers of money”, ridicule him; Jesus answers that God knows the heart.

Paul, thanking the Philippians for their gift, shows the freedom of one who serves God alone: “I have learned to be content with whatever I have… I can do all things through him who strengthens me.” He has known plenty and hunger, and he is free in both. Their generosity to him is “a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God”, and God will supply their every need.

Homily outline

Theme: “One Master.”

Faithfulness in small things, freedom from money, contentment in Christ.

1. Faithful in little

  • Money is a small thing compared with the Kingdom.
  • Small honesty trains the heart.
  • God entrusts true riches to the faithful.
“Remove far from me falsehood and lying; give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with the food that I need.” Proverbs 30:8
“Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much; and whoever is dishonest in a very little is dishonest also in much.” Luke 16:10

Apply: Be scrupulously honest in one small matter today.

2. One master

  • Wealth wants to rule.
  • Divided hearts serve neither well.
  • Generosity breaks money's hold.
“Put no confidence in extortion, and set no vain hopes on robbery; if riches increase, do not set your heart on them.” Psalm 62:10
“No slave can serve two masters; for a slave will either hate the one and love the other, or be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and wealth.” Luke 16:13

Apply: Give a fixed share this week to the poor.

3. God knows the heart

  • The Pharisees justified themselves before others.
  • God sees beyond appearances.
  • Contentment comes from Christ, not from possessions.
“For the LORD does not see as mortals see; they look on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart.” 1 Samuel 16:7
“So he said to them, “You are those who justify yourselves in the sight of others; but God knows your hearts; for what is prized by human beings is an abomination in the sight of God.”” Luke 16:15

Apply: Thank God tonight for three things you have.

Serve God, and money becomes a servant.

In Christ we have enough.

Take-home resolution: Decide on a regular share of your income for God and the poor.

Full homily

Dear friends, Jesus draws the lesson from yesterday's parable: whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much (Luke 16:10). Money is a very little compared with the true riches of the Kingdom, but the way we handle it shows what is in our heart.

Then he draws a sharp line: you cannot serve God and wealth (Luke 16:13). Wealth is not evil in itself, but it always wants to become a master. The Pharisees, lovers of money, laughed at him; Jesus answered that God knows their hearts.

Paul thanks the Philippians for their gifts and tells them his secret: he has learned to be content with little or with plenty, because he can do all things through him who strengthens him (Philippians 4:12–13). A free heart is not owned by its possessions.

Here is a practical step: decide on a regular share of your income for God and for the poor, and give it first, not from what is left over. It is a small thing, but faithfulness in small things is the training ground for great ones. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says that if we are honest in little things, we will be trusted with big things. Share something you have with someone who needs it today!

Prayer

Lord, you alone are my Master. Free my heart from the love of money, make me faithful in small things, and teach me, like Paul, to be content in every situation, for I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me. Amen.
