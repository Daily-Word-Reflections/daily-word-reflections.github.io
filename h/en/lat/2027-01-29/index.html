Word for the day

“He also said, “The kingdom of God is as if someone would scatter seed on the ground, and would sleep and rise night and day, and the seed would sprout and grow, he does not know how.” Mark 4:26–27

Reflection

Hebrews reminds a tired community of its early courage: “Recall those earlier days when, after you had been enlightened, you endured a hard struggle with sufferings… you cheerfully accepted the plundering of your possessions.” Now they are tempted to give up. “Do not, therefore, abandon that confidence of yours; it brings a great reward. For you need endurance.” Faith is not only a beginning; it is a long faithfulness. “We are not among those who shrink back and so are lost, but among those who have faith and so are saved.”

Jesus gives two small parables that are a medicine for discouragement. A farmer scatters seed, sleeps and rises, “and the seed would sprout and grow, he does not know how.” The earth produces “first the stalk, then the head, then the full grain in the head.” And the mustard seed, “the smallest of all the seeds on earth”, becomes a shrub where the birds nest. God's Kingdom grows quietly, slowly and surely, often while we sleep. Our part is to sow faithfully and to trust; the growth belongs to God.

Full homily

Dear friends, Hebrews speaks to a tired community: recall those earlier days when, after you had been enlightened, you endured a hard struggle with sufferings (Hebrews 10:32). Now they were tempted to give up.

Do not, therefore, abandon that confidence of yours; it brings a great reward. For you need endurance (Hebrews 10:35–36). Faith is a long faithfulness.

Jesus gives two small parables for the discouraged. The seed grows, he does not know how (Mark 4:27). The mustard seed, the smallest of all seeds, becomes a great shrub (Mark 4:31–32). God's Kingdom grows quietly and surely.

Our part is to sow faithfully and to trust. The growth belongs to God. Do not shrink back. Amen.

For children

Children, a tiny mustard seed grows into a big plant! Small kind things we do for Jesus can grow into something big.

Prayer

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Both readings teach patience, each in its own way. Hebrews asks a tired community to look back at the courage of its first days and to look ahead to the Lord who will not delay. Jesus speaks of a seed that grows while the sower sleeps, and of the tiny mustard seed that shelters the birds. What God has begun, God will bring to harvest. The key words are endurance, of itself and smallest.

Hebrews 10:32–39 · Do not abandon your confidence

Setting.

After a severe warning (10:26–31), the author changes his tone and recalls what these Christians have already come through. We do not know for certain who they were or which persecution he means. They seem to have suffered public shame, imprisonment and the loss of goods, though not yet death (see 12:4).

Structure.

  • vv. 32–34: remember: the earlier days of struggle.
  • vv. 35–36: therefore do not abandon your confidence; you need endurance.
  • vv. 37–39: the Scripture and the conclusion: “we are not among those who shrink back”.

Key words.

  • athlēsis, struggle: A word from the athletic contest, found only here in the New Testament. Christian life is pictured as a long contest (compare 12:1).
  • theatrizomenoi, publicly exposed: From the word for theatre: they were made a public spectacle.
  • hypomonē, endurance: Not passive waiting, but staying under a burden and holding on.

Verse by verse.

“But recall those earlier days when, after you had been enlightened, you endured a hard struggle with sufferings,” (10:32)

“Enlightened” is the same word as in 6:4. By the second century St Justin Martyr calls baptism “enlightenment” (First Apology 61). Remembering the grace of our beginnings is a way to stay faithful.

“and you cheerfully accepted the plundering of your possessions, knowing that you yourselves possessed something better and more lasting.” (10:34)

Their joy did not come from pretending that the loss meant nothing. It came from knowing what could not be taken.

“but my righteous one will live by faith. My soul takes no pleasure in anyone who shrinks back.”” (10:38)

Hebrews quotes Habakkuk 2:3–4 in its Greek form, with a phrase from Isaiah 26:20. St Paul uses the same line of Habakkuk in Romans 1:17 and Galatians 3:11. Here it speaks of the faith that perseveres through trial.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that to live, grow and persevere in the faith until the end we must nourish it with the word of God and beg the Lord to increase it (CCC 162). Tertullian said that the blood of Christians is seed (Apology 50, in substance), a saying that fits today's Gospel well. In many lands today there are Christians and others who suffer loss for their conscience. We pray for them, and for those who have wronged them.

Mark 4:26–34 · The seed grows, he does not know how

Setting.

These are the last parables of the discourse by the sea. The first, the seed growing by itself, is found only in Mark. The second, the mustard seed, is also in Matthew 13 and Luke 13. Jesus' hearers could see how small his following was and how powerful his opponents, and these parables speak to that.

Structure.

  • vv. 26–29: the seed that grows by itself, and the harvest.
  • vv. 30–32: the mustard seed and the large shrub.
  • vv. 33–34: a summary of Jesus' way of teaching.

Key words.

  • automatē, of itself: The source of our word “automatic”. It does not mean that the seed needs no sowing or soil. It means that the growth comes from a power the farmer does not control.
  • sinapi, mustard: The mustard seed was a proverbial image of smallness. The plant can grow taller than a person, but it remains a garden shrub, not a cedar. Jesus chooses a humble image on purpose.
  • kataskēnoun, to make nests: Birds nesting in great trees is an Old Testament image for a kingdom that shelters the nations (Ezekiel 17:23; Daniel 4:12).

Verse by verse.

“and would sleep and rise night and day, and the seed would sprout and grow, he does not know how.” (4:27)

The farmer is not lazy. He has sown, and he will reap. But he cannot make the grain grow, and he need not lose sleep over it.

“But when the grain is ripe, at once he goes in with his sickle, because the harvest has come.”” (4:29)

The words echo Joel 3:13, a picture of God's judgment. Patience is not the absence of an end. The harvest will come.

“yet when it is sown it grows up and becomes the greatest of all shrubs, and puts forth large branches, so that the birds of the air can make nests in its shade.”” (4:32)

The smallest beginning, Jesus and a few fishermen in Galilee, is already the Kingdom in seed.

How the Church reads it.

The Second Vatican Council draws on these parables: those who hear the word with faith and join the little flock of Christ have received the Kingdom itself, and then by its own power the seed sprouts and grows until the harvest (Lumen Gentium 5). St Gregory the Great, in his homilies on Ezekiel, applies the parable to the soul: we sow by a good intention, we sleep by resting in hope, and the seed grows beyond what we can see (in substance).

Questions people ask

Is the mustard seed really the smallest seed on earth?

There are smaller seeds. Jesus is not teaching botany. He uses a common saying of his people, for whom the mustard seed was the very picture of something tiny. The point is the contrast between the beginning and the end.

For study and teaching

  • What grace from the beginning of my faith do I need to remember today (Hebrews 10:32)?
  • Where do I need to stop trying to control the growth, and trust that God is at work while I sleep?
