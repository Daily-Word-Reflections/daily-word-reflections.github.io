Word for the day

“Yet even now, says the LORD, return to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning; rend your hearts and not your clothing.” Joel 2:12–13

Reflection

Lent opens with a trumpet. A locust plague had stripped the land of Judah bare, and the prophet Joel saw in it a call to conversion: “Yet even now, says the LORD, return to me with all your heart… rend your hearts and not your clothing.” The tearing of garments was the old sign of grief; God asks for something deeper, a heart torn open to him. And the reason is not fear but his own character: “for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love.” Joel calls everyone, the elders and the infants, the bride and the bridegroom, the priests weeping “between the vestibule and the altar”. Lent is not private self-improvement; it is the whole Church returning together.

St Paul turns the call into a plea: “we entreat you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God.” The ground of our reconciliation is astonishing: “For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.” The sinless Son took our place, so that we might take his. And there is urgency: “See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” Not someday. Now. The ashes we receive are not a mark of gloom but of honesty: we are dust, and to dust we shall return, yet the dust that God loves can be raised to glory.

In the Gospel Jesus names the three great works of Lent: almsgiving, prayer and fasting. He does not abolish them; he purifies them. “Beware of practicing your piety before others in order to be seen by them.” Give alms so quietly that your left hand does not know what your right hand is doing. Pray in your room with the door shut. Fast with your face washed and your head anointed. Three times we hear the promise: “your Father who sees in secret will reward you.” The paradox of today is plain: we go out with a cross of ash on our foreheads, yet Jesus warns us against showing off. The ash is not to be admired; it is a confession that we are sinners in need of mercy, and an invitation to a hidden life of love that only the Father sees.

Homily outline

Theme: “Return to me with all your heart.”

Ash Wednesday: the torn heart, the urgent now, the hidden works, and the dust that God loves.

1. Rend your hearts

  • The trumpet in Zion.
  • Not garments but hearts.
  • A gracious God.
“Return to the LORD, your God, for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love, and relents from punishing.” Joel 2:13
“So he set off and went to his father. But while he was still far off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion; he ran and put his arms around him and kissed him.” Luke 15:20

Apply: Name one sin you will bring to confession this week.

2. Now is the acceptable time

  • Ambassadors for Christ.
  • The great exchange.
  • Not someday, today.
“Create in me a clean heart, O God, and put a new and right spirit within me.” Psalm 51:10
“For he says, “At an acceptable time I have listened to you, and on a day of salvation I have helped you.” See, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation!” 2 Corinthians 6:2

Apply: Do today the act of reconciliation you have been postponing.

3. In secret

  • Alms without trumpets.
  • Prayer behind a shut door.
  • Fasting with a washed face.
“Prayer with fasting is good, but better than both is almsgiving with righteousness.” Tobit 12:8
“But whenever you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you.” Matthew 6:6

Apply: Keep one Lenten practice that no one else will know about.

4. Dust that God loves

  • You are dust.
  • Formed from the ground.
  • Raised to glory.
“By the sweat of your face you shall eat bread until you return to the ground, for out of it you were taken; you are dust, and to dust you shall return.”” Genesis 3:19
“Therefore we have been buried with him by baptism into death, so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.” Romans 6:4

Apply: Each night of Lent, pray: Lord, I am dust; have mercy and make me new.

Lent is the whole Church returning to a gracious Father.

We are dust, but dust in the hands of the God who raises the dead.

Take-home resolution: Write your three Lenten works and keep them daily, in secret.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, Lent begins with a trumpet. The prophet Joel cries out: yet even now, says the LORD, return to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning; rend your hearts and not your clothing (Joel 2:12–13).

Why return? Not because God is harsh, but because he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love (Joel 2:13). And Joel calls everyone: elders and children, the newly married and the priests. Lent is the whole Church coming home together.

Paul pleads with us: we entreat you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God (2 Corinthians 5:20). He who knew no sin was made sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God. And he adds: see, now is the acceptable time; see, now is the day of salvation! Not next year. Today.

Jesus gives us three works: almsgiving, prayer and fasting. But he warns: do not do them to be seen. Give so quietly that your left hand does not know what your right hand is doing; pray in your room with the door shut; fast with a washed face. And your Father who sees in secret will reward you (Matthew 6:4, 6, 18).

In a moment we will receive ashes. The priest will say: repent, and believe in the Gospel, or: remember that you are dust, and to dust you shall return. The ashes are not a decoration. They are an honest confession: we are sinners, we are mortal, and we need God's mercy.

Yet dust in God's hands is never just dust. From dust he formed Adam; from the dust of the tomb he will raise us. Let us begin these forty days with torn-open hearts, hidden love and great hope. Amen.

For children

Children, today we get a cross of ashes on our foreheads. It reminds us that we need Jesus and that we want to love him more.

For Lent, choose one way to pray more, one thing to give up, and one way to help someone, and do it quietly for Jesus.

Prayer

Grant, O Lord, that we may begin with holy fasting this campaign of Christian service, so that, as we take up battle against spiritual evils, we may be armed with weapons of self-restraint. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Father of mercy, we are dust, yet you love us. As we receive these ashes, tear open our hearts, teach us to pray in secret, to fast with joy and to give without counting, and bring us to Easter made new. Amen.
