Word for the day

“Then Jesus took the loaves, and when he had given thanks, he distributed them to those who were seated; so also the fish, as much as they wanted.” John 6:11

Reflection

Elisha is the prophet of the lean years. Just before today's story the Bible says, “there was a famine in the land” (2 Kings 4:38), and now a man arrives from Baal-shalishah with “twenty loaves of barley and fresh ears of grain in his sack.” They are first fruits, the first of the harvest, which the Law set apart for God and his ministers (compare Numbers 18:12–13; Deuteronomy 18:4). In a famine the man could easily have kept them for his own family; he carries them instead to the man of God. Elisha says only: “Give it to the people and let them eat.” His servant does the arithmetic every hungry community knows: “How can I set this before a hundred people?” Elisha repeats the command and gives the reason: “for thus says the Lord, ‘They shall eat and have some left.’” The servant obeys, and it happens “according to the word of the Lord.” Notice what is not asked: nobody is told to pray bread down from the sky. Twenty real loaves are placed in a servant's hands, and the word of the Lord does the rest. The Psalm names the God behind the sign: “The eyes of all look to you, and you give them their food in due season. You open your hand, satisfying the desire of every living thing.”

In the second reading St Paul writes as “the prisoner in the Lord,” and he begs rather than commands: “lead a life worthy of the calling to which you have been called, with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love, making every effort to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.” Then comes the great litany of the “ones”: one body and one Spirit, one hope, “one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all, who is above all and through all and in all.” The Church's unity is not something we build; it is a gift to be guarded. And the Gospel shows where that gift is fed. To the Corinthians Paul writes: “Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread” (1 Corinthians 10:17). The many are made one by the one bread.

For five Sundays the Church now reads John 6, and it opens with the sign. Every detail is chosen. “Now the Passover, the festival of the Jews, was near”: we are to think of the Exodus and the manna. Jesus “went up the mountain and sat down there with his disciples,” like a new Moses. He asks Philip, “Where are we to buy bread for these people to eat?” and John adds, “He said this to test him, for he himself knew what he was going to do.” Philip does the sums: “Six months' wages would not buy enough bread for each of them to get a little.” Then Andrew, who in this Gospel is always bringing someone to Jesus, brings a boy with “five barley loaves and two fish,” and asks the honest question: “But what are they among so many people?” Jesus says, “Make the people sit down.” Then comes the action that every Mass repeats: “Jesus took the loaves, and when he had given thanks, he distributed them.” The Greek for “given thanks” is eucharistesas, the word from which we get Eucharist. Nothing is wasted: “Gather up the fragments left over, so that nothing may be lost,” and twelve baskets are filled. The crowd, seeing the sign, wants “to take him by force to make him king,” but Jesus “withdrew again to the mountain by himself.” He will be king by giving himself, and in the coming Sundays he will say what the bread is: “I am the bread of life” (John 6:35); “the bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh” (John 6:51).

Homily outline

Theme: “Gather up the fragments left over, so that nothing may be lost.”

Elisha's loaves, a boy's lunch and the Bread of Life: God takes our little, gives thanks, and makes us one.

1. They shall eat and have some left

  • A famine, and first fruits brought to the prophet.
  • How can I set this before a hundred people?
  • The word of the Lord is enough.
“He set it before them, they ate, and had some left, according to the word of the Lord.” 2 Kings 4:44
“When they were satisfied, he told his disciples, “Gather up the fragments left over, so that nothing may be lost.”” John 6:12

Apply: Bring one worry about money, food or the future to the Mass today, and place it with the gifts on the altar.

2. What are they among so many?

  • Five barley loaves and two fish.
  • Andrew's honest question.
  • God asks for our little.
“Ho, everyone who thirsts, come to the waters; and you that have no money, come, buy and eat!” Isaiah 55:1
“There is a boy here who has five barley loaves and two fish. But what are they among so many people?” John 6:9

Apply: Choose one small act of service you can offer every day this week, and do not wait until it feels big enough.

3. One bread, one body

  • He took, gave thanks, distributed.
  • The Eucharist makes the Church.
  • Humility, gentleness, patience.
“he rained down on them manna to eat, and gave them the grain of heaven. Mortals ate of the bread of angels; he sent them food in abundance.” Psalm 78:24–25
“There is one body and one Spirit, just as you were called to the one hope of your calling, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all, who is above all and through all and in all.” Ephesians 4:4–6

Apply: Before Communion today, make peace in your heart with anyone you are at odds with, and offer them a word of kindness this week.

The Lord feeds his people from our small offerings: twenty loaves for a hundred, five loaves for five thousand.

Every Mass repeats his actions of taking, giving thanks and giving, and makes us one body in the bond of peace.

Take-home resolution: This week give one gift to someone in need, and visit one grandparent or elderly person.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, it is a hungry crowd. A man brings twenty small barley loaves to the prophet Elisha in a time of famine. Elisha says: give it to the people. And his servant says what we would all say: how can I set this before a hundred people? (2 Kings 4:43). It is the arithmetic of every hungry place. But Elisha says: thus says the Lord, they shall eat and have some left. And they did. The man's first fruits fed a hundred.

In the Gospel there is an even bigger crowd, five thousand, and again Jesus asks a question to test his disciples: where are we to buy bread for these people? Philip does the sums: six months' wages would not be enough. And then Andrew finds a boy. Notice, it is a boy, a child, with five barley loaves and two fish, the poor man's lunch. And Andrew says honestly: but what are they among so many people? (John 6:9). I think every one of us has said that about our own gifts. What is my little time, my little money, my little talent among so many needs?

But look at what Jesus does. He does not ask the crowd for more. He takes what the boy offers, and he gives thanks. Not a complaint that it is too little, but thanks. Then he distributes it, and they all eat as much as they wanted. The boy did not make the miracle. But there would have been no miracle that day without his five loaves. God asks us for our little, and he does the rest.

And then Jesus says: gather up the fragments left over, so that nothing may be lost (John 6:12). In God's house nothing is thrown away, no crumb and no person. Twelve baskets are filled. That is how we should look at the Eucharist, at the food on our tables, and at the old and the weak among us, whom the world is tempted to call leftovers. Today is the World Day for Grandparents and the Elderly. They are not fragments to be discarded; they are the baskets of the faith, and they hold what was handed down to us. Let us visit them and listen.

St Paul tells us what this bread is for. There is one body and one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism. At every Mass Jesus takes, gives thanks, breaks and gives, and from many grains makes one bread, from many persons one Church. So let us leave the altar with humility, gentleness and patience, bearing with one another in love. And when we feel that our gift is too small, let us remember the boy with the barley loaves, and bring it to Jesus. Amen.

For children

Children, a big crowd followed Jesus and they were all hungry. A little boy had five small loaves and two fish. He gave them to Jesus. Jesus said thank you to God, and he shared the food, and everybody ate until they were full! There were even twelve baskets left. Jesus can do great things with the little we give him. What small thing can you share this week? And please give your grandparents a big hug today!

Prayer

O God, protector of those who hope in you, without whom nothing has firmness, nothing is holy, bestow in abundance your mercy upon us and grant that, with you as our ruler and guide, we may use the good things that pass in such a way as to hold fast even now to those that ever endure. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Let us also pray: Lord Jesus, you saw the hungry crowd and had compassion. Take the little we have, our time, our gifts and our poor loaves, give thanks over them and make them enough. Feed us with your own Body and Blood, make us one body, and teach us to gather up the fragments so that nothing and no one may be lost. Bless our grandparents and all the elderly, who have handed on the faith to us. Amen.
