Reflection

Why does the whole Church celebrate the anniversary of one building in Rome? Because the Lateran Basilica, dedicated to Christ the Saviour (and later also to St John the Baptist and St John the Evangelist), is the cathedral of the Bishop of Rome. Its inscription calls it “mother and head of all the churches of the city and of the world”. Today's feast is a sign of our unity with the Pope and of love for the Church.

Ezekiel sees water flowing from the Temple, growing into a river that makes the Dead Sea fresh: “everything will live where the river goes”. In the Gospel Jesus cleanses the Temple and then speaks of “the temple of his body”. The true Temple is Christ himself, and from his pierced side flowed blood and water (John 19:34), the sacraments of the Church.

Paul adds the third step: “Do you not know that you are God's temple and that God's Spirit dwells in you?” (1 Corinthians 3:16). Church buildings are holy because they house the Eucharist and gather the living temple, the people of God. Celebrating a church's dedication reminds us that we too have been consecrated, and that zeal for God's house must begin with our own hearts.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, today the whole Church celebrates the birthday of a church building in Rome: the Basilica of St John Lateran. Why? Because it is the Pope's cathedral, “the mother and head of all churches”. To celebrate it is to celebrate our unity with Peter's successor.

Ezekiel saw a river flowing from the Temple. It started small and became deep, and wherever it flowed, everything lived; even the salty Dead Sea became fresh. That is what the Church is meant to be: a source of life flowing out into the world.

In the Gospel Jesus makes a whip and drives out the sellers. He is not against worship; he is against turning God's house into a market. Then he says something astonishing: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” He was speaking of his body. The true Temple is Jesus himself.

St Paul then says to us: “You are God's temple, and God's Spirit dwells in you.” Every baptised person is a temple. So we must ask: what is being sold in my temple? Greed, impurity, anger, gossip? Jesus wants to cleanse us too, not to punish but to make room for God.

Let us love our churches, keep them holy and silent, and come early to pray. Let us pray for the Pope and our bishop. And let us allow the river of grace to flow through us to others, so that wherever we go, life may grow.

Dear brothers and sisters, why does the whole Church celebrate the dedication of a basilica in Rome? Because St John Lateran is the cathedral of the Bishop of Rome, the Pope. Its inscription calls it the mother and head of all the churches of the city and of the world. Today's feast is a sign of our unity with the Successor of Peter.

But St Paul takes us further. You are God's building, he says, and no one can lay any foundation other than the one that has been laid, Jesus Christ (1 Corinthians 3:9–11). The real temple of God is not made of marble. It is made of people. Each of us is a stone placed by God in His building. Some stones are visible, some hidden in the foundations; all are necessary.

Then Paul asks a startling question: do you not know that you are God's temple and that God's Spirit dwells in you? (1 Corinthians 3:16). Since Baptism, the Holy Spirit lives in us. That gives our bodies a great dignity and calls for respect: for our own body, for the bodies of others, for the poor and the sick. Sin defiles the temple; confession cleanses it.

In the Gospel Jesus enters the Temple and drives out the traders with a whip of cords (John 2:13–17). The disciples remembered the psalm: zeal for your house will consume me. What clutters the temple of our hearts? Greed, resentment, screens that leave no room for prayer, gossip. And what clutters our parishes? Divisions, pride, business without prayer. Let Jesus cleanse His house.

Ezekiel saw water flowing from the Temple, bringing life wherever it went (Ezekiel 47:1–12). When the Church is clean and united, grace flows from it into the world. Today let us pray for the Pope and for unity in the Church, and let us offer ourselves as living stones in the house of God.

Dear brothers and sisters, some people say: God is everywhere, so why do we need churches? I can pray at home or under a tree. It is true that God is everywhere, and that we can pray anywhere. But today's feast reminds us why church buildings matter.

From the beginning God chose to dwell among His people in a special way: the tent of meeting in the desert, then the Temple in Jerusalem. Solomon himself admitted that the highest heaven cannot contain God, much less a house built by human hands, yet he asked God to keep His eyes open toward that house night and day (1 Kings 8:27–30). In Christ, God's dwelling with us became a person: the Word became flesh and lived among us (John 1:14). The church building is a sign of that presence and of the Church, the people gathered by God (CCC 1179–1180).

A Catholic church is first of all the house of the Eucharist. The altar is the place where the sacrifice of the Cross is made present and the table of the Lord to which the people are invited (CCC 1182). The tabernacle holds the Blessed Sacrament, Christ truly present, for the sick and for adoration (CCC 1183). The ambo is the place of the Word; the baptismal font, the place of new birth; the confessional, the place of mercy (CCC 1184–1185). Every part of the building teaches the faith.

When a church is dedicated, the bishop anoints the altar and the walls with chrism, just as a Christian is anointed. That is why we treat the church with respect: we keep silence, dress modestly, genuflect before the tabernacle, and do not use the church as a social hall.

The Lateran Basilica, the Pope's cathedral, reminds us that every parish church is united to the bishop's cathedral, and every cathedral to Rome. And every church on earth points beyond itself to the heavenly Jerusalem. So love your parish church. Visit it during the week, even for five minutes. Kneel before the tabernacle. There, as nowhere else, Christ waits for you.
