Word for the day

“Jesus looked at them and said, “For mortals it is impossible, but not for God; for God all things are possible.”” Mark 10:27

Reflection

Ben Sira today speaks to anyone who has wandered away from God, and his tone is full of hope. “Yet to those who repent he grants a return, and he encourages those who are losing hope.” God does not merely allow a return; he grants it, opens the road, and even comes to meet those whose courage is failing. Then the invitation, in three urgent verbs: “Turn back to the Lord and forsake your sins; pray in his presence and lessen your offense. Return to the Most High and turn away from iniquity.” Ben Sira, who wrote before the full revelation of the resurrection, adds a reason drawn from life itself: the living give thanks, the dead are silent, so “those who are alive and well sing the Lord's praises.” Today, while we have breath, is the time to return. And he ends with a cry of wonder: “How great is the mercy of the Lord, and his forgiveness for those who return to him!” The Psalm gives the experience of one who has made that return: “While I kept silence, my body wasted away … Then I acknowledged my sin to you … and you forgave the guilt of my sin.” Hiding sin makes us sick; confessing it heals. “Happy are those whose transgression is forgiven.”

The Gospel tells of a man who runs up to Jesus and kneels before him: “Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?” He is sincere, eager, respectful. Jesus points him first to God, “No one is good but God alone,” and then to the commandments, especially those about the neighbour. “Teacher, I have kept all these since my youth.” And then comes one of the most tender lines in the Gospels: “Jesus, looking at him, loved him.” Out of that love comes the invitation: “You lack one thing; go, sell what you own, and give the money to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; then come, follow me.” The man “was shocked and went away grieving, for he had many possessions.” He did not lose the love of Jesus; he lost the courage to accept it. Jesus then turns to the disciples: “How hard it will be for those who have wealth to enter the kingdom of God! … It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle.” The disciples are astounded: “Then who can be saved?” And Jesus answers with the sentence that is the hope of every one of us: “For mortals it is impossible, but not for God; for God all things are possible.” Not everyone is called to sell everything, but every disciple is called to hold nothing more tightly than Christ. The rich man went away; Ben Sira tells us the door of return is always open. Today, as many honour Mary, Help of Christians, we ask her to help us let go of whatever keeps us from following her Son, and we pray, as the Church asks, for the Catholics of China.

Full homily

Dear friends, Ben Sira gives us good news today. Yet to those who repent he grants a return, and he encourages those who are losing hope (Sirach 17:24). However far we have wandered, the road back to God is not closed. He does not wait with folded arms; he comes to meet those who are losing courage.

The Psalm tells us what it feels like to keep sin hidden: while I kept silence, my body wasted away. And what happens when we confess: I acknowledged my sin to you, and you forgave the guilt of my sin (Psalm 32:3, 5). The sacrament of Reconciliation is the place where this happens for us.

In the Gospel a good man runs to Jesus and kneels. He has kept the commandments since his youth. And Mark says: Jesus, looking at him, loved him (Mark 10:21). Before any challenge, there is love. Then Jesus asks for the one thing he lacked: sell what you own, give to the poor, and come, follow me. The man went away grieving, for he had many possessions. His things owned him.

Most of us are not asked to sell everything. But all of us are asked: is there anything I hold more tightly than Jesus? Money, a house, a career, a relationship, my own plans? If we feel it is impossible to let go, Jesus answers: for mortals it is impossible, but not for God; for God all things are possible (Mark 10:27). Today many honour Mary, Help of Christians. Let us ask her help to let go and follow her Son, and let us pray for our brothers and sisters in China. Amen.

For children

Children, a rich man asked Jesus how to go to heaven. Jesus looked at him with love and said: share your things with the poor and come with me. But the man went away sad, because he loved his things too much. Jesus wants us to love him more than toys, games or phones. Can you share something with someone today?

Prayer

Merciful Father, how great is your mercy and your forgiveness for those who return to you. Open for us the road home, and encourage us when we are losing hope. Lord Jesus, you looked at the rich man and loved him; look at us with the same love, and give us the courage to let go of whatever we hold more tightly than you. Mary, Help of Christians, help us to follow your Son; and protect the Church in China and all who suffer for their faith. For you, O God, all things are possible. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Ben Sira calls sinners home: the Lord grants a way back to those who repent, and his mercy to those who turn to him is great. In the Gospel a man runs to Jesus, kneels and asks about eternal life; Jesus looks at him with love and asks for everything, and the man goes away grieving. Psalm 32 sings the joy of the one whose sin is forgiven.

Sirach 17:24–29 · Turn back to the Lord

Setting.

After his hymn to God the Creator who gave the covenant (17:1–14), Ben Sira says that God sees all human deeds (17:15–23). Then comes a call to repentance. The Lectionary numbers these verses 17:20–24, following a different division of the text; the NRSV numbers them 17:24–29.

Ben Sira, like the older psalms, pictures the dead in Hades as unable to praise God (vv. 27–28). Later revelation, in the Book of Wisdom, in 2 Maccabees, and above all in Christ’s resurrection, opens the hope of life with God after death.

Structure.

  • v. 24: God grants a way back to those who repent.
  • vv. 25–26: turn back, pray, hate what he abhors.
  • vv. 27–28: the living praise God; the dead do not.
  • v. 29: how great is the Lord’s mercy.

Key words.

  • metanoeō, to repent: A change of mind and heart. Ben Sira uses it for those to whom God grants a way back (v. 24).
  • epanodos, a return: A road home. Repentance is not only sorrow; it is a way that God himself opens.
  • Hadēs, Hades: The Greek name for Sheol, the shadowy place of the dead in the older books of the Old Testament.

Verse by verse.

“Yet to those who repent he grants a return, and he encourages those who are losing hope.” (17:24)

God does not only accept the sinner who comes back; he strengthens the discouraged so that they can come back.

“Who will sing praises to the Most High in Hades in place of the living who give thanks?” (17:27)

Compare Psalm 6:5 and Isaiah 38:18. The point is urgent: now is the time to turn and give praise.

“How great is the mercy of the Lord, and his forgiveness for those who return to him!” (17:29)

The passage ends not with fear but with wonder at mercy.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism describes conversion as the movement of a contrite heart, drawn and moved by grace to respond to the merciful love of God who loved us first (CCC 1428). The way back that Ben Sira praises is opened fully in Christ and given in the sacrament of Penance, which the Fathers called “the second plank” of salvation after the shipwreck of sin (CCC 1446).

Mark 10:17–27 · Jesus, looking at him, loved him

Setting.

Jesus is “setting out on a journey”, on the road to Jerusalem. Matthew calls the man young (19:20) and Luke calls him a ruler (18:18); Mark says only that he ran and knelt, and that he had many possessions.

Many in Israel, as in other cultures, saw wealth as a sign of God’s blessing (compare Deuteronomy 28:1–14). That is why the disciples are so astonished at Jesus’ words.

Structure.

  • vv. 17–18: the question, and Jesus’ answer: no one is good but God.
  • vv. 19–20: the commandments, kept since youth.
  • vv. 21–22: Jesus looks, loves and calls; the man goes away grieving.
  • vv. 23–27: the camel and the needle; for God all things are possible.

Key words.

  • agapaō, to love: “Jesus, looking at him, loved him” (v. 21). Only Mark records this look of love. The hard call that follows comes from love.
  • hysterei, you lack: “You lack one thing.” Not more rules, but the one thing: to follow Jesus with an undivided heart.
  • kamēlos, camel: The largest animal of the land, through the smallest opening. Attempts to soften the image, such as a small gate called “the needle’s eye” or a rope instead of a camel, have no good ancient support. Jesus means what is humanly impossible.

Verse by verse.

“Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?” (10:17)

He asks what he must do. Jesus will show him that eternal life is received from God and lived in following him.

“Jesus, looking at him, loved him and said, “You lack one thing; go, sell what you own, and give the money to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; then come, follow me.”” (10:21)

The call is personal. Jesus does not ask every disciple to sell everything, but he asks every disciple to put nothing before him.

“For mortals it is impossible, but not for God; for God all things are possible.” (10:27)

An echo of Genesis 18:14, “Is anything too wonderful for the Lord?” Salvation is God’s work, and by his grace we respond.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism presents this meeting as a model of the moral life: keeping the commandments is the first step, and following Christ is its fullness (CCC 2052–2053). St John Paul II built the first chapter of his encyclical Veritatis Splendor on this dialogue as Matthew tells it. Clement of Alexandria wrote a work on this passage, “Who is the rich man that is saved?”, teaching that what must be given up is above all the rule of riches over the heart (in substance).

Questions people ask

When Jesus says “No one is good but God alone”, is he denying that he is God?

No. He is leading the man to think about what he has said. If only God is good, and the man rightly calls Jesus good, then he stands before more than a teacher. The same Jesus then asks the man to leave everything and follow him, something only God could rightly ask.

For study and teaching

  • What possession, habit or security would I find hardest to give up if Jesus asked me today?
  • Ben Sira says God encourages those who are losing hope. Whom can I encourage to come back to the Lord this week?
