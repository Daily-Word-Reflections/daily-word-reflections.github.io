Word for the day

“you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.” Mark 12:30

Reflection

The Book of Tobit now reaches its love story. Young Tobias has set out on a long journey for his blind father, with a companion who calls himself Azariah but is really the angel Raphael. As they approach Ecbatana, Raphael tells him about Sarah, the daughter of their kinsman Raguel: “the girl is sensible, brave, and very beautiful, and her father is a good man.” Tobias is afraid, and with reason: seven husbands have died on their wedding night. But Raphael says: “Do not be afraid, for she was set apart for you before the world was made.” At Raguel's house there are tears of recognition and a warm welcome. Raguel is honest about the danger, yet he gives his daughter in marriage with a blessing: “May the Lord of heaven, my child, guide and prosper you both this night and grant you mercy and peace.” A marriage contract is written. Then comes the wedding night. Before anything else, Tobias rises and says to Sarah: “Sister, get up, and let us pray and implore our Lord that he grant us mercy and safety.” Their prayer is one of the most beautiful in the Bible on marriage. It begins with praise: “Blessed are you, O God of our ancestors, and blessed is your name in all generations forever.” It remembers creation: “You made Adam, and for him you made his wife Eve as a helper and support.” And it offers an honest heart: “I now am taking this kinswoman of mine, not because of lust, but with sincerity. Grant that she and I may find mercy and that we may grow old together.” And together they say: “Amen, Amen.” A marriage that begins on its knees is protected from evil. The Psalm blesses such a home: “Your wife will be like a fruitful vine within your house; your children will be like olive shoots around your table.”

In the Gospel a scribe, impressed by Jesus' answers, asks a sincere question: “Which commandment is the first of all?” Jesus answers with the Shema, the prayer every devout Jew says morning and evening: “Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one; you shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind, and with all your strength.” And he joins to it a second commandment from Leviticus 19:18: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.” The scribe agrees warmly and adds that this love is “much more important than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices.” Jesus looks at him and says: “You are not far from the kingdom of God.” Here a Jewish teacher and Jesus stand together on the foundation of Israel's faith, the love of the one God and of neighbour. Tobias and Sarah lived this double love on their wedding night: love of God first, in prayer, and then a love for each other that is sincere and pure. The martyrs of Uganda lived it to the point of fire. Charles Lwanga, head of the royal pages at the court of Kabaka Mwanga II, protected the boys in his care from the king's sinful demands and taught them the faith. When the persecution broke out, he baptised several catechumens the night before their arrest. On 3 June 1886, at Namugongo, he and many companions were burned alive, praying for their persecutors. Charles is said to have told the executioner that it was as if he were pouring water on him. Some of the pages were barely fourteen. With them died Anglican Christians too, witnesses to the same Lord. St Paul VI canonised the twenty-two Catholic martyrs in 1964, and today the Church of Africa, rich in faith, is their harvest.

Theme: “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart.”

A wedding night that begins in prayer, the first and second commandments, and young martyrs who loved God more than life.

  • Sister, get up, and let us pray.
  • Praise, creation, sincerity.

  • Hear, O Israel.
  • With heart, soul, mind and strength.
  • Your neighbour as yourself.
“Hear, O Israel: The Lord is our God, the Lord alone. You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your might.” Deuteronomy 6:4–5

Apply: Pray the Shema slowly each morning this week, then do one deliberate act of love for a neighbour.

3. The martyrs of Uganda

  • Charles Lwanga protects the pages.

  • Namugongo, 3 June 1886.
“Happy is everyone who fears the Lord, who walks in his ways.” Psalm 128:1
“Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God.” Matthew 5:8

Apply: Pray this week for young people in your parish who are pressured to do wrong, and encourage one of them by name.

Tobias and Sarah show us love that puts God first and so loves the other purely; Jesus names that order as the heart of the Law.

Take-home resolution: Put God first each day this week: begin the morning with the Shema and end the evening with a prayer together with your family.

Full homily

Dear friends, today's first reading is a wedding story. Young Tobias comes to the house of his kinsman Raguel and asks to marry Sarah. Everyone knows the danger: seven husbands have died on their wedding night. But the angel Raphael has told Tobias: do not be afraid, she was set apart for you before the world was made. So Raguel gives his daughter with a blessing, and a marriage contract is written.

Then comes the wedding night, and what does Tobias do first? He says to Sarah: sister, get up, and let us pray (Tobit 8:4). Before anything else, the two of them stand before God. They praise him, they remember how he made man and woman, and Tobias says: I take her not because of lust, but with sincerity; grant that she and I may grow old together (Tobit 8:7). And Sarah answers: Amen. What a beautiful beginning for a marriage! Love that begins in prayer is protected from evil.

In the Gospel Jesus tells us that the first commandment is to love God with all our heart, soul, mind and strength, and the second is to love our neighbour as ourselves (Mark 12:30–31). Tobias and Sarah did exactly that. First God, then each other. Our families too need this order. When God is first, love between husband and wife, parents and children, is pure and strong.

Today we also remember St Charles Lwanga and his companions, young Christians in Uganda. The king demanded of the palace boys what was sinful, and Charles protected them. When they refused to give up their faith, they were burned alive at Namugongo in 1886. Some of them were only fourteen. They loved God with all their strength, more than their own lives. Let us ask them to give our young people pure and brave hearts, and let us ask Tobias and Sarah to teach our families to pray together. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know the most important rule in the whole world? Jesus told us: love God with all your heart, and love your neighbour as much as you love yourself. Long ago in Uganda some boys your age loved Jesus so much that they would not do something wrong even when the king ordered them. They were very brave. Today, show your love for God by saying a prayer, and your love for others by being kind.

Prayer

O God, who have made the blood of martyrs the seed of Christians, mercifully grant that the field which is your Church, watered by the blood shed by Saints Charles Lwanga and his companions, may be fertile and always yield you an abundant harvest. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, your Son, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, God, for ever and ever. Amen. Lord, bless all married couples with the prayer of Tobias and Sarah, give our young people pure and brave hearts, and teach us to love you with all our heart and our neighbour as ourselves. Amen.
