Word for the day

“Mary took a pound of costly perfume made of pure nard, anointed Jesus' feet, and wiped them with her hair. The house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume.” John 12:3

Reflection

Holy Week has begun. Yesterday we walked with palms; this week the Church walks with Jesus, day by day, towards the Upper Room, the cross and the empty tomb. On the first three days the readings are quiet and searching. The first readings give us the Songs of the Servant of the Lord from the Book of Isaiah, four poems about a mysterious servant who is gentle, faithful, rejected and finally glorified through suffering. The Gospels take us into the last days of Jesus in Bethany and Jerusalem. Today the first Song begins: “Here is my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my spirit upon him.” This servant does not shout or push his way through the streets. “A bruised reed he will not break, and a dimly burning wick he will not quench.” He is given “as a covenant to the people, a light to the nations, to open the eyes that are blind, to bring out the prisoners from the dungeon.” At the Jordan the Father had said of Jesus, “with you I am well pleased”; St Matthew quotes this very song to describe him (Matthew 12:18–21). The Psalm is the prayer of the Servant as the darkness gathers: “The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear?”

The Gospel is set “six days before the Passover” in Bethany, at the home of Lazarus, whom Jesus had raised from the dead. A dinner is given for him. Martha serves, as she always does; Lazarus sits at table, living proof of the power of Jesus; and Mary does something that fills the whole house. She takes “a pound of costly perfume made of pure nard,” anoints the feet of Jesus and wipes them with her hair. A pound of nard was a fortune, about a year's wages for a labourer. Judas protests in the name of the poor, but St John tells us plainly that he did not care about the poor: he kept the common purse and stole from it. Jesus defends Mary: “Leave her alone. She bought it so that she might keep it for the day of my burial.” Mary understands what the others have not yet grasped: the one she loves is going to die, and love cannot measure its gifts. Her anointing points to the burial of Jesus on Friday, and beyond it to the morning when other women will come with spices and find the tomb empty.

The scene ends with a shadow. Many people come to see Jesus and Lazarus, and many believe; so “the chief priests planned to put Lazarus to death as well.” St John speaks of a small circle of leaders who feared for their authority and for the nation under Rome (John 11:47–50). The Church teaches clearly that the Passion cannot be blamed on the Jewish people as a whole, then or now (Nostra Aetate 4; Catechism 597–598); every sinner, and so each of us, had a part in the sufferings of Christ. On this Monday the question for us is simple: are we Martha, serving; Lazarus, simply being with him; Mary, pouring out what is precious; or Judas, calculating? Holy Week invites us to break our alabaster jar for him: our time, our pride, our tears, our love. And Jesus, the gentle Servant, will not break the bruised reed of our weakness.

Homily outline

Theme: “The house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume.”

The gentle Servant, the extravagant love of Mary, the calculating heart and the light that overcomes darkness.

1. The gentle Servant

  • Chosen and upheld by the Father.
  • No shouting in the streets.
  • The bruised reed and the dimly burning wick.
“a bruised reed he will not break, and a dimly burning wick he will not quench; he will faithfully bring forth justice.” Isaiah 42:3
““Come to me, all you that are weary and are carrying heavy burdens, and I will give you rest.” Matthew 11:28

Apply: Bring your weakest point to Jesus in confession this week and let him breathe on the flickering wick.

2. The fragrance of love

  • Six days before the Passover.
  • A year's wages poured out.
  • Anointed for burial.
“You prepare a table before me in the presence of my enemies; you anoint my head with oil; my cup overflows.” Psalm 23:5
“Mary took a pound of costly perfume made of pure nard, anointed Jesus' feet, and wiped them with her hair. The house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume.” John 12:3

Apply: Give Jesus one costly gift this week: your full presence at the Holy Thursday, Good Friday and Easter Vigil services.

3. The calculating heart

  • Judas and the common purse.
  • Concern for the poor as a mask.
  • True love for the poor flows from love for Jesus.
“I have given you as a covenant to the people, a light to the nations, to open the eyes that are blind, to bring out the prisoners from the dungeon, from the prison those who sit in darkness.” Isaiah 42:6–7
“and live in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God.” Ephesians 5:2

Apply: Set aside what you save during Holy Week and give it quietly to the poor.

4. Light in the darkness

  • The Psalm of the Servant.
  • Whom shall I fear?
  • Wait for the Lord.
“The LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The LORD is the stronghold of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?” Psalm 27:1
“Again Jesus spoke to them, saying, “I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.”” John 8:12

Apply: Pray Psalm 27 each night of Holy Week before you sleep.

The Servant is gentle with our weakness and gives himself completely.

Mary shows us how to love him: without counting, before the cross and beyond it.

Take-home resolution: Break one alabaster jar for Jesus this week, and give him the whole Triduum.

Full homily

Dear friends, Holy Week has begun, and the Church gives us the first Song of the Servant: here is my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights; I have put my spirit upon him (Isaiah 42:1). This is Jesus. He does not shout in the street. A bruised reed he will not break, and a dimly burning wick he will not quench (Isaiah 42:3). If your faith is only a flicker today, he will not blow it out. He will breathe on it.

Six days before the Passover, Jesus comes to Bethany. Martha serves, Lazarus sits at table, and Mary takes a pound of costly perfume made of pure nard, anoints the feet of Jesus and wipes them with her hair. The house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume (John 12:3). That perfume was worth a year's wages. Mary does not calculate. She loves.

Judas does calculate. Why was this not sold and given to the poor? But he did not care about the poor; he was a thief. Jesus answers: leave her alone. She bought it so that she might keep it for the day of my burial (John 12:7). Mary has understood that Jesus is going to die. True love for the poor is born from love for Jesus, not opposed to it.

This week, let us not be spectators. Let us be Mary. Break the jar: give Jesus your time at the services of the Triduum, your sins in confession, your tears, your love. And when the darkness of the Passion comes, pray with the Psalm: the LORD is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? (Psalm 27:1). Amen.

For children

Children, Mary loved Jesus so much that she poured her most precious perfume on his feet, and the whole house smelt lovely. This week, let us give Jesus something precious too: our prayers, our kindness and our hearts!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, gentle Servant of the Father, you did not break the bruised reed or quench the dimly burning wick. As Mary poured out her perfume at your feet, let us pour out our love for you this Holy Week. Free us from the calculating heart, be our light and our salvation, and bring us with you through the cross to the joy of Easter. Amen.
