Word for the day

“Master, now you are dismissing your servant in peace, according to your word; for my eyes have seen your salvation, which you have prepared in the presence of all peoples, a light for revelation to the Gentiles and for glory to your people Israel.” Luke 2:29–32

Reflection

Old Simeon, “righteous and devout, looking forward to the consolation of Israel”, had been promised by the Holy Spirit that he would not die before seeing the Lord's Messiah. Guided by the Spirit he comes into the Temple just as Mary and Joseph bring in the child. He takes Jesus in his arms and sings the Nunc Dimittis, which the Church prays every night at Compline: now I can die in peace, for I have seen your salvation.

Simeon's song is universal: the child is “a light for revelation to the Gentiles” as well as the glory of Israel. But he also speaks of contradiction: the child will be “a sign that will be opposed”, and a sword will pierce Mary's soul. The first reading draws the practical lesson: “whoever says, ‘I abide in him,’ ought to walk just as he walked”; whoever loves a brother or sister lives in the light. St Thomas Becket, archbishop of Canterbury, killed in his cathedral on this day in 1170, gave his life defending the freedom of the Church.

Full homily

Dear friends, on this fifth day of the Christmas Octave St John writes: by this we may be sure that we know him, if we obey his commandments. Whoever says, I abide in him, ought to walk just as he walked (1 John 2:3, 6). Knowing Christ is not only an idea; it is a way of walking.

John continues: whoever says, I am in the light, while hating a brother or sister, is still in the darkness. Whoever loves a brother or sister lives in the light (1 John 2:9–10). Christmas light is tested by love.

In the Gospel Mary and Joseph bring the infant Jesus to the Temple. There the old man Simeon, righteous and devout, who had been waiting for the consolation of Israel, takes the child in his arms and praises God: Master, now you are dismissing your servant in peace, for my eyes have seen your salvation, a light for revelation to the Gentiles (Luke 2:25–32).

Simeon waited his whole life, and his waiting was fulfilled in holding a baby. The Church prays his song every night at Compline, before sleep. It is the prayer of one who is at peace because he has seen Christ.

But Simeon also tells Mary: this child is destined for the falling and the rising of many, and a sword will pierce your own soul too (Luke 2:34–35). The light of Christmas already points to the Cross. Love costs.

Let us ask for Simeon's patience and peace. Let us walk as Jesus walked, loving our brothers and sisters, so that we truly live in the light. And tonight, before sleep, let us pray: now, Lord, let your servant go in peace. Amen.

For children

Children, old Simeon held baby Jesus and was so happy! We hold Jesus in our hearts when we receive Him with love.

Prayer

Lord, now let your servant go in peace; your word has been fulfilled. My eyes have seen the salvation you prepared for all peoples. Let me walk in your light and love my brothers and sisters. Amen.
