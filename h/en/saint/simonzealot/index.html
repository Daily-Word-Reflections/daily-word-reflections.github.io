Mar Shemun Shleeha the Zealot (St Simon the Apostle)

Apostle · Feast: 10 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“All these were constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus, as well as his brothers.” Acts 1:14

Life

Simon is one of the Twelve whom Jesus chose and named apostles. He appears in all four lists of the apostles in the New Testament. Matthew and Mark call him Simon the Cananaean (Matthew 10:4; Mark 3:18), and Luke calls him Simon the Zealot (Luke 6:15; Acts 1:13). Both names say the same thing. Cananaean comes from an Aramaic word that means zealous, and Zealot is its Greek form. It has nothing to do with the land of Canaan or with the town of Cana, although some later writers read it that way.

What his zeal was we do not know for certain. It may simply mean that he was known for burning devotion to God and to the Law of Moses, like the prophet Elijah and the priest Phinehas before him. Many have thought that he once belonged to the party of zealous patriots who longed to throw off Roman rule by force. That party is clearly known to history only some decades later, at the time of the great revolt of the years 66 to 70, so scholars are cautious. What is certain is that Jesus called a man of fiery temper and placed him in the same small company as Matthew, a former tax collector for the occupying power. In Christ the two became brothers.

The Gospels record no word or deed of Simon himself. The last time Scripture names him is in the upper room in Jerusalem after the Ascension, where the apostles waited for the Holy Spirit and were constantly devoting themselves to prayer with Mary the mother of Jesus (Acts 1:13 to 14). His memory in May, in the season between the Resurrection and Pentecost, recalls those days of prayer.

About his later preaching the traditions of the Churches disagree. Latin tradition sends him with St Jude to preach in Persia and says that both were martyred there, and the Latin Church keeps the two together on 28 October. Greek writers speak of journeys to Egypt and North Africa, and some even to Britain. Georgian and Abkhazian tradition places his preaching and death on the eastern shore of the Black Sea, where a church at New Athos bears his name. Some say he was crucified; Western art often shows him with a saw, because a legend says he was sawn in two. None of these accounts can be proved. The Byzantine Churches and the Malankara Panchangam keep his memory on 10 May.

Lessons

Zeal made holy

Jesus did not put out Simon's fire; he gave it a better purpose.

Brothers in Christ

The Zealot and the tax collector learned to pray side by side.

Hidden service

An apostle whose deeds are not written down is still a pillar of the Church.

Waiting in prayer

He prepared for the Spirit together with Mary and the apostles.

Stories

Two names, one meaning (history)

The early translators met an Aramaic nickname, qan'ana, and kept it as Cananaean, while Luke, writing for Greek readers, translated it as Zealot. The two Gospel names for Simon are one name in two languages, and both describe a man on fire for God.

The bridegroom of Cana (legend)

A Byzantine tradition, reading Cananaean as a man of Cana, says that Simon was the bridegroom at the wedding where Jesus turned water into wine, and that after this sign he left everything to follow him. The Gospel does not name the bridegroom, and this story rests on a misreading of the name.

The cave by the Black Sea (tradition)

Christians of Abkhazia show a cave near New Athos where, they say, Simon lived and prayed in his last days before he was put to death. A church in his honour has stood nearby for many centuries.

Homily

1. In the upper room

He prayed with Mary and the apostles for the Spirit (Acts 1:14).

2. Fire turned to love

Zeal that once may have wished to destroy learned to serve.

3. Unknown but faithful

God remembers what history forgets.

Dear friends, Christ is risen! After the Ascension the apostles gathered in the upper room to wait for the Holy Spirit. Scripture tells us that all these were constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus (Acts 1:14). One of the men in that room was Mar Shemun Shleeha, Simon the Zealot, whom we remember today.

We know almost nothing about him, yet his name tells a story. He was a Zealot, a man of fire. Some think he once wanted to drive out the Romans by the sword. Then he met Jesus, and Jesus did not ask him to become cold. He asked him to burn for something greater, for the Kingdom of God, which is won not by the sword but by the Cross.

Think of the company Simon kept. Beside him stood Matthew, who had collected taxes for the Romans. In the world outside they might have been enemies. In the company of Jesus they became brothers, and in the upper room they prayed with one heart. That is what the Holy Spirit still does in the Church. He gathers people who would never choose one another and makes them one family.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask the Lord to purify our zeal, so that our love for the faith never turns into anger against our neighbour. Let us pray for unity in our parishes and families. And let us wait for the Spirit as Simon waited, with Mary, in prayer. Mar Shemun Shleeha, pray for us. Amen.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you chose Mar Shemun the Zealot to be one of your apostles and turned the fire of his heart to the service of your Kingdom. Through his prayers, purify our zeal, make us one with those who differ from us, and keep us faithful in prayer with your holy Mother as we wait for the gifts of the Holy Spirit. Amen.
