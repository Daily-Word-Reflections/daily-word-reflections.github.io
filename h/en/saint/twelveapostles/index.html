The Twelve Apostles

Commemoration of the Twelve, the day after Sts Peter and Paul · Feast: 30 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“And the wall of the city has twelve foundations, and on them are the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb.” Revelation 21:14

Life

Before choosing the Twelve, Jesus went out to the mountain to pray, and he spent the night in prayer to God. When day came he called his disciples and chose twelve of them, whom he also named apostles, that is, men who are sent (Luke 6:12–13). Mark says that he appointed them to be with him, and to be sent out to proclaim the message (Mark 3:14). Their number recalls the twelve tribes of Israel: in them the Lord was gathering the new people of God. The Gospels and the Acts give their names four times (Matthew 10:2–4, Mark 3:16–19, Luke 6:14–16, Acts 1:13): Simon Peter and his brother Andrew, James and John the sons of Zebedee, Philip, Bartholomew, Thomas, Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, also called Judas son of James, Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot, who betrayed him.

For about three years they lived with Jesus. They heard his teaching, saw his works of mercy, and were sent out two by two to preach and heal. They were ordinary men, fishermen, a tax collector, a zealot, and the Gospels do not hide their faults: they argued about who was the greatest, misunderstood the Cross, slept in Gethsemane and fled when he was arrested. At the Last Supper Jesus washed their feet and gave them his Body and Blood, saying, Do this in remembrance of me. After he rose from the dead he showed himself to them, breathed on them, forgave their failure and sent them out as the Father had sent him (John 20:21–22).

After the Ascension the eleven gathered in prayer with Mary, the mother of Jesus, and Matthias was chosen by lot to take the place of Judas (Acts 1:26). At Pentecost the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they began to bear witness to the risen Christ in Jerusalem and then to the ends of the earth. The first Christians devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers (Acts 2:42). The apostles laid hands on others and handed on to them the care of the Churches, and so the bishops are their successors. That is why in the Creed we confess the Church as one, holy, catholic and apostolic.

Ancient traditions tell where each apostle carried the Gospel: Peter and Paul to Rome, James the son of Zebedee, the first of the Twelve to die as a martyr, in Jerusalem (Acts 12:2), John at Ephesus, Andrew in Greece, Thomas to Persia and India, Bartholomew to Armenia, Matthew, Jude and Simon to the East. The St Thomas Christians of Kerala trace their faith to Mar Thoma Shleeha, one of the Twelve. In the Syriac tradition, as in other Eastern Churches, the day after the feast of Sts Peter and Paul is kept as the commemoration of all the Twelve together. The Syro-Malankara Panchangam keeps it on 30 June, the day after the Apostles' Fast (Shleeha Nombu) comes to an end.

Stories

A night on the mountain (history)

Luke alone tells that Jesus spent the whole night before the choice in prayer. The Twelve were not picked by chance or by merit. They came from the prayer of the Son to the Father, and that is why the Church, before ordaining her ministers, also prays (Luke 6:12–16).

The lot falls on Matthias (history)

Peter told the believers that someone who had been with them from the baptism of John to the Ascension must become a witness to the Resurrection in place of Judas. They proposed two men, prayed, Lord, you know everyone's heart, and cast lots. The lot fell on Matthias, and the Twelve were complete again (Acts 1:15–26).

The Teaching of the Twelve Apostles (history)

An early Christian book, perhaps written in the first century, bears the title The Teaching of the Lord through the Twelve Apostles to the Nations, usually called the Didache. It teaches the way of life, Baptism, fasting, prayer and the Eucharist. It does not claim to have been written by the apostles themselves, but it shows how early the Church looked to the Twelve as the source of her teaching.

Homily

1. Twelve foundations

The Church is built on the apostles (Revelation 21:14).

2. Called to be with him

First with Jesus, then sent (Mark 3:14).

3. The words of eternal life

There is no one else to go to (John 6:68).

Dear friends, yesterday we celebrated Sts Peter and Paul, and the Apostles' Fast came to an end. Today the Church gathers all the Twelve together, like a family that has celebrated a feast and now sits down together. In the vision of the Book of Revelation, the holy city has twelve foundations, and on them are the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb (Revelation 21:14). The Church in which we live and pray stands on these twelve men.

Jesus chose them after a night of prayer. Mark says he chose them first of all to be with him, and only then to be sent out (Mark 3:14). This order matters. Before we can do anything for Christ, we must be with him: in prayer, in the Word, in the Holy Qurbana. A Christian who is always busy but never with the Lord soon has nothing to give.

The Twelve were not great men by the world's measure. They quarrelled, they misunderstood, they ran away. Peter denied him; Thomas doubted. Yet when many disciples left Jesus, Peter spoke for them all: Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life (John 6:68). The Lord did not choose them because they were strong. He made them strong, by his patience, by his forgiveness, and by the gift of the Holy Spirit.

We are St Thomas Christians. Our faith did not come to us from books but from one of the Twelve, Mar Thoma Shleeha, who came to our land and gave his life for Christ. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for the apostles and for all who handed on their faith to us. Let us pray for our bishops, their successors, and ask the holy apostles to make us faithful disciples, who stay with the Lord and go wherever he sends us. Amen.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, who after a night of prayer chose the Twelve to be with you and sent them to proclaim your Gospel, grant through the prayers of your holy apostles that we may hold fast to the faith they handed down, stay close to you in prayer and in the Holy Qurbana, and bear witness to you with courage wherever you send us. Amen.
