St James, son of Zebedee (Mar Yaqob Shleeha)

Apostle and martyr, brother of St John · Feast: 25 July

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Are you able to drink the cup that I drink, or be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with?” Mark 10:38

Life

James was a fisherman of Galilee, the son of Zebedee and the elder brother, it seems, of John the Apostle. The two brothers were mending nets in their father's boat by the Sea of Galilee when Jesus called them; they left their father with the hired men and followed him at once (Mark 1:19–20). The Western Church calls him James the Greater to distinguish him from the other apostle James, son of Alphaeus. In the Syriac tradition he is Mar Yaqob Shleeha.

Jesus gave James and John the name Boanerges, which Mark explains as Sons of Thunder (Mark 3:17). With Peter, James was one of the three whom Jesus took with him at special moments: when he raised the daughter of Jairus (Mark 5:37), on the mountain of the Transfiguration (Mark 9:2), and in the garden of Gethsemane, where they could not stay awake (Mark 14:33). James had much to learn. When a Samaritan village would not receive Jesus, he and John asked whether they should call down fire from heaven on it, and Jesus rebuked them (Luke 9:54–55).

Once the two brothers asked to sit at Jesus' right and left in his glory; Matthew says their mother made the request with them (Matthew 20:20–21). Jesus asked if they could drink the cup he would drink, and when they said they could, he told them they would indeed share his cup (Mark 10:38–39). The other ten were angry, and Jesus taught them all that the one who wishes to be great must be the servant of all. James saw the risen Lord with the other apostles and was with them in prayer before Pentecost (Acts 1:13).

James was the first of the Twelve to die for Christ. Around the year 43 or 44, King Herod Agrippa I began to persecute the Church in Jerusalem: “He had James, the brother of John, killed with the sword” (Acts 12:2). So he drank the cup. Later traditions, written down from about the seventh century, say that he had preached in Spain, and that his body was brought to Galicia. In the ninth century a tomb believed to be his was found there, and the shrine of Santiago de Compostela became one of the great pilgrimages of Christendom. He is the patron of Spain and of pilgrims.

Stories

Fire from heaven (history)

On the way to Jerusalem a Samaritan village refused to welcome Jesus. James and John asked, “Lord, do you want us to command fire to come down from heaven and consume them?” But Jesus turned and rebuked them, and they went on to another village (Luke 9:51–56).

The accuser who believed (tradition)

Clement of Alexandria, quoted by Eusebius, tells that the man who brought James to trial was so moved by his witness that he confessed himself a Christian. As they were led out together he asked James to forgive him. James thought for a moment, said “Peace be with you” and kissed him, and both were beheaded.

The field of the star (legend)

A medieval account tells that a hermit named Pelagius saw a light like a star over a field in Galicia, and that the bishop Theodomir found there the tomb of the apostle. Many explain the name Compostela as the field of the star. The discovery of a tomb revered as his in the ninth century is history; the details are legend.

Homily

1. Called from the boat

Jesus calls us in our daily work (Mark 1:19–20).

2. The cup

Glory comes through the Cross (Mark 10:38).

3. The first apostle martyr

James kept his word (Acts 12:2).

Dear friends, today we honour St James, the son of Zebedee, the fisherman whom Jesus called from his father's boat. He and his brother John left the nets and followed. Jesus did not choose perfect men. He called them as they were and made them into apostles over time.

James had a fiery temper. Jesus called him and his brother Sons of Thunder. When a Samaritan village refused to welcome them, they wanted to call down fire on it. Jesus rebuked them. James was also ambitious: he wanted the best seat in the kingdom. Jesus did not send him away. He asked him, “Are you able to drink the cup that I drink?” (Mark 10:38). James said yes, without knowing what he was promising.

Years later he found out. James was the first of the apostles to give his life for Christ. Luke writes it in one short line: King Herod “had James, the brother of John, killed with the sword” (Acts 12:2). The man who once wanted fire to fall on his enemies died forgiving, and the man who wanted the first seat took the first place in martyrdom. That is what Jesus can make of our temper and our ambition, if we stay with him.

Pilgrims still walk for weeks across Europe to his shrine at Compostela. Our whole life is such a pilgrimage. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, Jesus offers us the cup of his Blood, the same cup he promised James. Let us receive it, and ask St James to help us follow the Lord without delay, to serve instead of seeking first place, and to stay faithful to the end. Amen.

Prayer

Almighty God, who called St James to leave his nets and follow your Son, and gave him to be the first of the apostles to drink the cup of his Passion, grant through his prayers that we may follow Jesus without delay, serve rather than seek the first place, and walk as pilgrims of faith to our home in heaven. Amen.
