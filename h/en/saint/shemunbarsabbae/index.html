Word for the day

“I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” John 10:11

Life

Mar Shemun, Simeon in English, was Bishop of Seleucia-Ctesiphon, the twin royal cities on the Tigris in what is now Iraq, then the capital of the Persian Empire of the Sasanian kings. His surname Bar Sabbae means son of the dyers, and may recall the trade of his family. As bishop of the capital he held first place among the bishops of the Church in Persia, the Church of the East, which later writers call its Catholicos. He led his Church in the reign of King Shapur II.

When the Roman Empire under Constantine became friendly to Christianity, the Persian king and his advisers began to suspect the Christians of his own realm of siding with Rome, his great enemy, and war broke out between the two empires. As a matter of royal policy Shapur ordered that the Christians should pay a double tax to pay for the war, and he required Mar Shemun to collect it from his people. According to the Syriac Acts of his martyrdom, Shemun refused. He said that he was a shepherd of his people, not a tax collector, and that he could not lay this burden on the poor.

Shemun was arrested and brought before the king. He was ordered to worship the sun, as the royal religion required, and promised honour if he would obey. He refused, saying that he worshipped the one God, the Creator of the sun. He was put in chains. In prison he encouraged his fellow prisoners, about a hundred clergy, among them bishops and priests, who were put to death before him while he looked on, so that their courage would not fail. He was beheaded last, on Good Friday, the day of the Lord's own death, in about the year 341, though some scholars prefer 344. The place of his death was Karka d-Ledan in the province of Beth Huzaye.

His death began what Syriac Christians remember as the Great Persecution, which lasted for most of the rest of Shapur's long reign, and in which very many Christians of Persia died. Their memory has been kept with love by all the Syriac Churches. The Church of the East, of which Shemun was a chief shepherd, sent bishops to the St Thomas Christians of Kerala for many centuries, so his martyrdom belongs to our own heritage too.

Lessons

The shepherd stays

He would not hand his poor people over to a crushing burden.

One God alone

He gave worship only to the Creator, not to anything created.

Strength for others

In prison he strengthened his companions before himself.

Witness without hatred

He obeyed the king in everything except what belonged to God.

Stories

The old servant of the king (history)

The Greek historian Sozomen tells of Usthazanes, an elderly official who had cared for Shapur as a boy. A Christian, he had bowed to the sun out of fear. When Mar Shemun passed him in chains and turned away in sorrow, Usthazanes repented, confessed Christ before the king and was condemned. He asked only that a herald should announce that he died not as a traitor but as a Christian, and the king allowed it.

Pusai and the old priest (history)

Among those killed with Shemun was an aged priest named Hananiah, who began to tremble before the sword. Pusai, the head of the king's craftsmen, called out to him to close his eyes for a moment and take courage, for soon he would see the light of Christ. Pusai himself was arrested for those words and put to death.

The prayer on Good Friday (tradition)

The Syriac Acts tell that Shemun asked to die on the day of the Lord's Passion, and that in prison on the night before he prayed for his Church, for the king and for those who would put him to death. These words come from the account of his martyrdom, written down with much devotion by later hands.

Homily

1. The good shepherd

He laid down his life for the sheep (John 10:11).

2. Worship belongs to God

He would not give it to any creature.

3. The day of the Passion

He died as his Master died.

Dear friends, today the Church remembers Mar Shemun Bar Sabbae, bishop of the Persian capital, Seleucia-Ctesiphon, and the many who died with him. Jesus said, “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep” (John 10:11). Shemun lived those words to the letter.

When war came between Rome and Persia, the Christians of Persia were suspected of disloyalty. The king's policy laid on them a double tax and ordered their bishop to collect it. Shemun had always been a loyal subject. But he saw his poor people already struggling, and he said no: he was their shepherd, not the king's tax collector. Then he was told to worship the sun, and again he said no: worship belongs to the Creator alone.

In prison he did not think of himself. He saw a hundred of his clergy led out to die before him, and he kept on encouraging them until the last. Then on Good Friday, the day his Master died on the Cross, he too gave his life. He did not curse the king. He prayed for his Church and for those who put him to death. That is the difference between a martyr and a rebel.

Our Malankara Church shares the Syriac heritage of these martyrs, and for many centuries bishops came to the St Thomas Christians of Kerala from his Church of the East. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for Christians who suffer for their faith today, for our bishops, that they may care for the poor as Shemun did, and for ourselves, that we may give worship to God alone. Amen.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, good shepherd who laid down your life for the sheep, you gave Mar Shemun Bar Sabbae and his companions the grace to die for you on the day of your Passion. Through their prayers, grant that our shepherds may care for the poor, that persecuted Christians may stand firm without hatred, and that we may worship you alone all the days of our life. Amen.
