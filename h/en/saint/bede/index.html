St Bede the Venerable

Priest, monk, Doctor of the Church · Feast: 25 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.” Psalm 119:105

Life

Bede was born in 672 or 673 on lands belonging to the monastery of Wearmouth, in the Anglo-Saxon kingdom of Northumbria, in what is now north-east England. Almost all we know of his life comes from a few lines he wrote at the end of his great History, and from a letter by his disciple Cuthbert describing his death. At the age of seven he was given by his family into the care of Abbot Benedict Biscop, and then of Ceolfrith, at the twin monastery of St Peter at Wearmouth and St Paul at Jarrow. He hardly ever left it again.

He was ordained deacon at nineteen and priest at thirty by St John of Beverley, bishop of Hexham. Bede writes that he spent his whole life in the monastery, keeping the discipline of the Rule and the daily singing in church, and that his delight was always to learn, to teach and to write. The monastery had a fine library, built up from books that Benedict Biscop had brought back from Rome, and Bede made full use of it. He wrote commentaries on most of the books of the Bible, drawing on the Fathers, especially Augustine, Jerome, Ambrose and Gregory the Great, as well as homilies for the Sundays and feasts, lives of saints, poems, and books for teaching grammar, the reckoning of time and the natural world.

His work on the reckoning of time, written in 725, helped to spread the custom of counting the years from the birth of Christ, Anno Domini, which Bede used throughout his writing. In 731 he completed the Ecclesiastical History of the English People, the story of how the Gospel came to the peoples of Britain, from the mission sent by Pope St Gregory the Great to the Church of his own day. He gathered documents and letters, named his sources, and took care to say what he knew to be true and what he had heard from others. It earned him the title of father of English history.

Bede died at Jarrow in 735, on the evening before the feast of the Ascension, which by the reckoning of the time fell on 26 May. His disciple Cuthbert tells how, though very ill, he went on teaching and dictating to the last, singing the psalms and the antiphons of the feast. He was buried at Jarrow, and in the eleventh century his relics were carried to Durham, where his tomb is in the Galilee Chapel of the cathedral. Within a century of his death he was being called Venerable. Pope Leo XIII declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1899. He is the only English Doctor of the Church.

Lessons

A whole life in one place

Bede found the whole world in his monastery and its books.

Learning as service

He studied so that others could understand Scripture.

Truth in history

He cared to tell the story honestly and to name his sources.

Work to the end

He kept serving God with his gifts until his last breath.

Stories

The last sentence (history)

Cuthbert writes that on his last day Bede was dictating an English translation of the Gospel of John. In the evening the boy writing for him, Wilberht, said that one sentence was still left. Bede told him to write quickly. When the boy said it was finished, Bede answered that he had spoken truly, it was finished. Then, lying on the floor of his cell, he sang Glory be to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, and died.

The small gifts (history)

On his last afternoon, Cuthbert says, Bede asked for the little things he kept in a box, some pepper, cloths and incense, and shared them among the priests of the monastery, asking them to remember him at Mass and in prayer.

The boy who sang with the abbot (tradition)

An early life of Abbot Ceolfrith tells that when plague struck Jarrow in 686, all who could sing the Office died except the abbot and one small boy. The two of them kept up the singing of the psalms in choir until others could be trained. Many have thought that the boy was Bede, but the account does not name him.

Words of the saint

I pray you, good Jesus, that as you have graciously granted me to drink in with delight the words of your knowledge, so you will mercifully grant me to come at last to you, the fountain of all wisdom, and to stand for ever before your face.
St Bede, closing prayer of the Ecclesiastical History, in substance
I have spent all my life in this monastery, and amid the observance of the Rule and the daily singing in church, it has always been my delight to learn or to teach or to write.
St Bede, Ecclesiastical History 5.24, in substance

Homily

1. A lamp to my feet

Bede lived by the light of the word (Psalm 119:105).

2. Learning, teaching, writing

His study was a work of love for others.

3. It is finished

He served to the very last day (John 19:30).

Dear friends, the psalmist prays, “Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path” (Psalm 119:105). St Bede the Venerable lived by that lamp for more than sixty years. As a child of seven he entered a monastery in the north of England, and he spent the rest of his life there, praying the psalms, studying the Scriptures and teaching the young monks.

Some might think such a life is small. Bede hardly travelled. Yet from his monastery he wrote books that were copied across Europe for centuries. He explained the Bible so that ordinary priests could preach it. He wrote the story of how the faith came to the English, and he wrote it honestly, telling us where he learned each thing. His study was not for his own name. It was a service of love to the Church.

For us, Bede is a model of how to use the gifts God gives. A student, a teacher, a catechist, a parent helping a child with lessons, a priest preparing a homily: all of these can make their work an offering. Bede also reminds us that the Word of God deserves our time. If we give a few minutes each day to reading the Gospel, the lamp will light our path too.

At the end, very ill, Bede was still dictating a translation of the Gospel of John. When the last sentence was written, he said that it was finished, and he died singing the praise of the Trinity. The words remind us of the Lord on the Cross, who said, “It is finished” (John 19:30). At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for those who taught us the faith, let us pray for teachers and students, and let us ask for the grace to serve him faithfully to our last day. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who enlightened your Church with the learning of St Bede the Venerable, grant through his prayers that we may love your word, seek the truth with patience, and serve you with our gifts until the end of our days, so that we may come at last to you, the fountain of all wisdom. Amen.
