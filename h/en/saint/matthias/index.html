St Matthias the Apostle

Apostle, chosen to take the place of Judas among the Twelve · Feast: 14 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last” John 15:16

Life

After the Ascension of the Lord, the apostles returned to Jerusalem and gathered in the upper room with Mary the mother of Jesus, some women and the brothers of the Lord, and they devoted themselves to prayer (Acts 1:12–14). There were about a hundred and twenty believers together. Peter stood up among them and spoke of Judas, who had betrayed the Lord and had come to a terrible end. The Twelve had been chosen by Jesus as the foundation of the new Israel, and the place left empty had to be filled.

Peter laid down what was needed. The one to be chosen must have been with them during the whole time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among them, from the baptism of John until the day he was taken up, and must become a witness with us to his resurrection (Acts 1:21–22). Two men were put forward, Joseph called Barsabbas, also known as Justus, and Matthias. The community prayed: Lord, you know everyone's heart. Show us which one of these two you have chosen (Acts 1:24). Then they cast lots, and the lot fell on Matthias, and he was added to the eleven apostles (Acts 1:26).

This is the only time Matthias is named in the New Testament. From it we learn that he had followed Jesus from the beginning, even though he was not among the Twelve in the Gospels, and that he had seen the risen Lord. The historian Eusebius reports that he was thought to be one of the seventy disciples whom the Lord sent out two by two (Luke 10:1). A few days after his choice came the day of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit came upon the apostles and Matthias began, with the others, to bear witness to Christ.

Of his later life we have only traditions, and they do not agree. Some say he preached in Judea, others in Cappadocia and on the shores of the Caspian Sea, and others in a land the ancient writers called Ethiopia, probably Colchis by the Black Sea. Most say he died a martyr. A tradition holds that St Helena, mother of the emperor Constantine, sent his relics to Trier in Germany, where the Benedictine abbey of St Matthias keeps a shrine in his honour. His feast in the Latin Church was kept on 24 February until 1969, when it was moved to 14 May, in the season of Easter, close to the Ascension and Pentecost, the days of his choosing.

Lessons

Faithful in the hidden years

He followed Jesus for years without being one of the Twelve, and was ready when called.

A witness to the Resurrection

The first requirement for an apostle was to have seen the risen Lord.

Discernment in prayer

The Church prayed first and then trusted God to show his choice.

Grace after betrayal

God filled the place left by Judas; his plan is not defeated by sin.

Stories

Waiting in the upper room (history)

Between the Ascension and Pentecost the disciples did not scatter. They stayed together in prayer with Mary the mother of Jesus. It was in this praying community, and not by any one man's decision, that Matthias was chosen (Acts 1:14–26).

The one not chosen (history)

Joseph Barsabbas, called Justus, had the same long years with Jesus and the same witness, but the lot did not fall on him. Scripture says nothing of his disappointment. An early tradition reported by Eusebius counts him too among the seventy disciples, faithful in a place no one remembered.

The shrine at Trier (tradition)

Medieval accounts tell that the relics of St Matthias were sent from the Holy Land to Trier by St Helena and rediscovered there in 1127. Pilgrims have walked to the abbey of St Matthias for centuries. The accounts of how the relics came there belong to tradition.

Homily

1. He chose you

Every vocation begins with the Lord's choice (John 15:16).

2. Witness to the Resurrection

Matthias had to be a witness with us (Acts 1:22).

3. Prayer before choosing

Lord, you know everyone's heart (Acts 1:24).

Dear friends, St Matthias is the apostle no one expected. He was not called on the shore of Galilee with Peter and Andrew. His name does not appear in the Gospels at all. And yet, when the Church needed someone to take the place of Judas, the Lord had been preparing him for years. He had walked with Jesus from the baptism of John to the day of the Ascension, quietly, without a title.

Many of us are like Matthias. We serve the Lord in ways no one notices, in the family, in the parish, at work. We may think that what matters is to be seen. But Jesus said to his disciples, You did not choose me but I chose you (John 15:16). The Lord sees the hidden years. He uses them to make us ready for the moment when he calls.

Notice how the first Christians made their decision. They did not campaign. They gathered with Mary in prayer, set out clearly what was needed, and then prayed: Lord, you know everyone's heart (Acts 1:24). The first thing they asked was that the new apostle be a witness to the Resurrection. That is still the first thing the Church needs from bishops, priests, parents and catechists: people who know that Christ is alive.

There was also a place left empty by betrayal. Judas had fallen, but the Twelve were not destroyed. God does not abandon his plan because of our sin. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for those who are discerning their vocation, for those who choose leaders in the Church, and for the grace to be faithful in hidden things, so that like St Matthias we may be ready when the Lord calls. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who chose St Matthias to be numbered among the apostles, grant through his prayers that we may follow your Son faithfully in the hidden years, be ready when you call us, and bear witness to the risen Christ with our whole life. Amen.
