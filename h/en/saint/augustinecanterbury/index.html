St Augustine of Canterbury

Bishop, apostle of the English · Feast: 27 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!” Romans 10:15

Life

Augustine was prior of the monastery of St Andrew on the Caelian Hill in Rome, which Pope St Gregory the Great had founded in his own family house. In 596 Gregory chose him to lead a group of about forty monks to preach the Gospel to the Angles and Saxons, who had settled in Britain after the Roman legions left. The Britons who lived there were Christians, but the newcomers in the south and east were still pagan.

On the way, in Gaul, the monks heard frightening reports about the fierce people they were going to, and they lost heart. They sent Augustine back to Rome to ask to be released. Gregory encouraged them, gave Augustine authority over the group as abbot, and sent them on with letters to the bishops and rulers of Gaul. In 597 they landed on the Isle of Thanet in Kent. The king of Kent, Ethelbert, had a Christian wife, Bertha, a Frankish princess, who had brought a bishop with her as chaplain and worshipped in the old church of St Martin outside Canterbury.

Bede tells that Ethelbert met the monks in the open air, fearing that indoors they might use magic. They came carrying a silver cross and an image of the Saviour painted on a board, singing a litany for themselves and for the people. The king listened, allowed them to live and preach in Canterbury, and before long was baptised himself. Augustine went to Gaul to be ordained bishop. Gregory wrote to the Patriarch of Alexandria that more than ten thousand English had been baptised at Christmas. He sent Augustine more monks, books and vessels for the altar, and the pallium as archbishop, with a plan for the Church in England.

Gregory also sent wise advice, preserved by Bede: the temples of the people should not be destroyed but purified and made into churches, and their feasts turned into Christian celebrations, so that the people might come step by step to the truth. Augustine founded the cathedral of Christ Church in Canterbury and the monastery of Sts Peter and Paul outside the walls. He tried to bring the British bishops into common work with him, but they could not agree. In 604 he ordained Mellitus as bishop of London and Justus as bishop of Rochester, and named Laurence as his successor. He died on 26 May, probably in 604, and was buried in the monastery he had founded. He is honoured as the first Archbishop of Canterbury.

Lessons

Courage renewed

Augustine turned back in fear, and Gregory sent him on again.

Patient sowing

The Gospel takes root step by step, not in one leap.

Respect for culture

What was good in the people's life could be purified and kept.

Unity is hard work

He sought, not always with success, to work with other Christians.

Stories

The procession on Thanet (history)

Bede writes that Augustine and his companions came before King Ethelbert carrying a silver cross as their standard and an image of the Lord painted on a board, singing a litany in which they asked God to turn away his anger from the city and from his holy house. As they entered Canterbury they sang Alleluia.

Not Angles but angels (tradition)

Bede records a story told in his time, that before he became pope, Gregory saw fair-haired boys for sale in the market of Rome. When he was told they were Angles, he said that they had the faces of angels and should become fellow heirs with the angels in heaven. From that day he longed to send the Gospel to their people.

The sign of the hermit (tradition)

Bede tells that before a second meeting with Augustine, the British bishops asked a holy hermit whether to accept him. He told them that if Augustine rose to greet them, he was humble and they should listen; if he stayed seated, they need not. Augustine remained seated, and the meeting failed. The story is a reminder that unity needs humility.

Words of the saint

It would be better not to begin a good work than to think of turning back from it once begun.
Pope St Gregory the Great, letter to Augustine and his companions, 596, in Bede's History 1.23, in substance
One who wishes to climb to the top of a mountain goes up step by step, not by leaps.
Pope St Gregory the Great, letter to Abbot Mellitus, 601, in Bede's History 1.30, in substance

Homily

1. Beautiful feet

The Church sends messengers of good news (Romans 10:15).

2. Do not turn back

Gregory renewed Augustine's courage.

3. Step by step

The Gospel grows patiently in every culture.

Dear friends, St Paul writes, “How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!” (Romans 10:15). Today we remember St Augustine of Canterbury, who walked from Rome to the far end of the known world to bring the Gospel to the English. He was not a great hero by nature. Halfway there, he and his monks were afraid and wanted to go home.

What kept them going was the encouragement of Pope Gregory. He wrote that it is better not to begin a good work than to turn back once it has begun. So they went on. Augustine reached Kent, met the king, and patiently began to preach. Within a few years thousands had been baptised, and the Church in England had begun to grow.

Gregory also taught Augustine to be wise. He told him not to destroy everything in the people's way of life, but to purify what was good and offer it to God. The Church in India knows the value of this wisdom. Our faith has taken flesh in our languages, our songs and our customs. The Gospel does not erase a people; it heals and lifts up what God has already planted.

Augustine's life also had its sorrow. He could not reach agreement with the Christians already living in Britain, and the division lasted long. Unity among Christians needs humility on every side. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for missionaries in every land, for those who are tempted to give up a good work, and for the unity of all who believe in Christ. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who by the preaching of St Augustine of Canterbury led the English peoples to the light of the Gospel, grant through his prayers that the work of missionaries may bear lasting fruit, that we may never turn back from the good we have begun, and that all who believe in Christ may be one. Amen.
