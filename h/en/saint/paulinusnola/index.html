St Paulinus of Nola

Bishop · Feast: 22 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“For you know the generous act of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty you might become rich.” 2 Corinthians 8:9

Life

Pontius Meropius Paulinus was born about the year 354 near Bordeaux in Gaul, into a wealthy senatorial family with estates in Gaul, Spain and Italy. He studied under the famous poet and teacher Ausonius, who became his close friend, and he began a brilliant public career. While still young he served as governor of Campania in southern Italy. There, at Nola near Naples, he first visited the tomb of St Felix, a priest honoured as a confessor of the faith, and he came to love that holy place.

Paulinus married Therasia, a devout Christian woman from Spain. About the year 389 he was baptized at Bordeaux by Bishop Delphinus. The couple's only child, a son named Celsus, died a few days after birth. In their grief they resolved to give their lives wholly to God. They began to sell their great estates and give the money to the poor and to the Church, which astonished the Roman nobility. Ausonius wrote letters begging his pupil to return to poetry and public life; Paulinus answered him in verse, gently and firmly, that his heart now belonged to Christ.

At Christmas, about the year 394, the people of Barcelona pressed him to be ordained a priest, and the bishop ordained him. Soon afterwards he and Therasia settled at Nola, beside the tomb of St Felix. There they lived as brother and sister in a small community of prayer and simple work, receiving pilgrims and the poor. Paulinus enlarged the shrine of Felix with new churches, decorated with mosaics and inscriptions, and every year on the feast of St Felix he composed a poem in his honour. He kept up a warm correspondence with many saints of his time, among them Augustine, Jerome and Sulpicius Severus, the biographer of St Martin of Tours.

Around the year 409 the people of Nola chose Paulinus as their bishop. He shepherded them through hard years, when the Goths invaded Italy and sacked Rome in 410 and the countryside of Campania was plundered. He spent what he had to ransom captives and feed the hungry. He died at Nola on 22 June 431. St Augustine and St Jerome both admired him as an example of a rich man who had become poor for Christ. He is also counted among the finest Christian poets of the Latin language.

Stories

Where my treasure is (history)

St Augustine tells in The City of God that when the Goths took Nola, Paulinus, who had made himself poor for Christ, prayed that he might not be tortured for the sake of gold and silver, since the Lord knew where all his treasure was. Augustine uses this prayer as an example of riches stored safely in heaven.

A bishop sold as a slave (legend)

St Gregory the Great, in his Dialogues written about 160 years later, tells that a poor widow came to Paulinus because her son had been carried away to Africa. Having nothing left to give, Paulinus went to Africa and gave himself as a slave in the young man's place, until his captors, learning who he was, sent him home with all the captives of his city. Many historians doubt that the story belongs to this Paulinus, but it shows how his charity was remembered.

The bells of Campania (legend)

A late legend says that Paulinus was the first to use bells in church, and that the Latin words campana and nola for bells come from Campania and from Nola. There is no early evidence for this, but the tradition links his name with the call to prayer.

Homily

1. Rich made poor for others

Christ became poor to make us rich (2 Corinthians 8:9).

2. Treasure in heaven

Where your treasure is, your heart will be (Matthew 6:21).

3. Faithful friendship

Faithful friends are a sturdy shelter (Sirach 6:14).

Dear friends, today we remember St Paulinus of Nola, a man who had everything the world admires. He was rich, gifted, well connected and admired as a poet. He had governed a province while still young. Yet he came to see that all of this could not fill the heart. In the year of his son's death, with his wife Therasia, he turned toward God with his whole life. Sorrow did not make him bitter; it opened his heart to the God who gives eternal life.

Paul tells the Corinthians that though Christ was rich, yet for our sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty we might become rich (2 Corinthians 8:9). Paulinus took these words to heart. He sold his estates and gave to the poor. His friends thought he had lost his mind. But he had found the pearl of great price. Each of us is asked, in our own state of life, how we use what God has given us. Our money, our time and our talents are gifts entrusted to us for others.

Paulinus also teaches us the beauty of Christian marriage and friendship. He and Therasia walked together toward God. He wrote long, warm letters to Augustine, Jerome and Sulpicius Severus, and these friendships strengthened them all. In a time when many are lonely, and when friendships are often shallow, we can ask for friends who help us pray and grow in faith, and we can try to be such friends to others.

As bishop, Paulinus did not run away when the invaders came. He stayed with his people, ransomed captives and fed the hungry. His prayer, told by Augustine, was that the Lord knew where all his treasure was. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St Paulinus to loosen our grip on possessions, to make our homes places of welcome for the poor, and to help us store up treasure in heaven. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who made the bishop St Paulinus of Nola outstanding in his love of poverty and his care for the poor, grant that we who celebrate his memory may follow the example of his charity and, having shared our goods with those in need, may find our treasure in you. Amen.
