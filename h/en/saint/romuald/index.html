St Romuald

Abbot, founder of the Camaldolese hermits · Feast: 19 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Be still, and know that I am God!” Psalm 46:10

Life

Romuald was born at Ravenna about the year 951, into a noble family. His life was written by St Peter Damian some fifteen years after his death, from the memories of his disciples. As a young man he loved hunting and the pleasures of his class, yet in the forests he sometimes felt a longing for a quiet life with God. When he was about twenty, his father Sergius quarrelled with a relative over property and killed him in a duel, and Romuald was present. Shaken, he went to the monastery of Sant'Apollinare in Classe near Ravenna to do forty days of penance for his father's crime. There he decided to become a monk.

The community did not live as strictly as he wished, and his zeal irritated some of the brothers. With the abbot's permission he left to live under the guidance of a hermit named Marinus in the marshes near Venice. Some years later, about 978, Romuald and Marinus accompanied Pietro Orseolo, the doge of Venice, who secretly gave up his office to become a monk, to the abbey of Saint Michael of Cuixà in the Pyrenees. Romuald lived there as a hermit for about ten years, praying, fasting and studying the lives of the desert fathers. Peter Damian tells that when his father Sergius, who had in turn become a monk, wanted to return to the world, Romuald hurried back to Italy to strengthen him in his vocation.

For the rest of his life Romuald travelled through Italy, founding and reforming hermitages and monasteries. The emperor Otto III admired him and made him abbot of Sant'Apollinare in Classe, but Romuald soon gave up the office because he could not lead the monks as he believed God wanted. He sent disciples as missionaries to the Slavs and Hungarians; two of them, Benedict and John, were killed with their companions in Poland in 1003, and St Bruno of Querfurt, who had lived among his hermits, died a martyr in 1009. Romuald himself set out for Hungary hoping to give his life for Christ, but illness forced him to turn back.

Traditionally about the year 1012, at Camaldoli in the mountains of Tuscany, he founded the hermitage that gave its name to the Camaldolese monks. There each hermit had his own cell for prayer and silence, while all lived under the Rule of St Benedict, and lower down the mountain a house received guests and the sick. Romuald thus joined the solitary life of the hermit with the common life of the monastery. He died alone in his cell at Val di Castro on 19 June, by the traditional reckoning in the year 1027. His followers, the Camaldolese Benedictines, still live his way of silence, prayer and hospitality.

Stories

Forty days for his father (tradition)

When his father killed a relative in a duel, the young Romuald went to the monastery of Classe to do penance in his place for forty days. During those days of prayer he decided to give his whole life to God. Peter Damian says that he saw a vision of St Apollinaris in the church, but the vision belongs to the tradition of his life.

The doge becomes a monk (history)

Peter Damian tells that the doge of Venice, Pietro Orseolo, left his palace by night and went with Romuald and Marinus to the abbey of Cuixà, where he lived and died as a humble monk. The Church venerates him as St Peter Orseolo.

The cell like paradise (history)

Bruno of Querfurt preserved a short rule taught by Romuald: sit in your cell as in paradise, watch your thoughts like a fisherman watching for fish, keep to the path of the psalms, and stand before God with fear and trembling, like one who stands before an emperor.

Homily

1. Be still

Silence opens the heart to God (Psalm 46:10).

2. A clean heart

Penance leads to new life (Psalm 51:10).

3. Pray in secret

The Father sees in secret (Matthew 6:6).

Dear friends, St Romuald's conversion began with a family tragedy. His father killed a relative in a quarrel over property, and the young man saw it happen. Instead of running from the pain, Romuald went to a monastery to do penance for his father. Many families today carry wounds of violence, quarrels over land and money, words that cannot be taken back. Romuald shows us that prayer and penance can turn even such wounds into a road to God. The psalmist prays, Create in me a clean heart, O God, and put a new and right spirit within me (Psalm 51:10).

Romuald loved silence. He taught his disciples to sit in their cells as in paradise and to watch their thoughts as a fisherman watches for fish. The Lord says, Be still, and know that I am God! (Psalm 46:10). Our world is full of noise. Phones, screens and constant talk leave little room for God. We do not need to become hermits, but each of us needs some moments of silence every day, in our room, before the Blessed Sacrament, or on a quiet walk.

Jesus said, whenever you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret (Matthew 6:6). Romuald built many such rooms. Yet he did not shut himself away from others. His hermitage at Camaldoli had a house for guests and the sick. True silence makes us more attentive and more loving, not colder. People who pray deeply often become the best listeners and the kindest hosts.

Romuald kept beginning again. He left places where he was honoured, travelled into old age, and died alone in his cell, at peace. By the traditional reckoning, this year marks a thousand years since his death. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St Romuald to teach us the value of silence, to give us the grace of true repentance, and to help us bring God's peace into our families. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who through St Romuald renewed the life of the hermit in your Church, grant that, denying ourselves and following Christ, we may find you in silence and prayer, do penance for our sins and the sins of others, and reach the joy of your kingdom. Through our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.
