Mar Pachomios (St Pachomius of Egypt)

Abbot, father of community monastic life · Feast: 15 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Now the whole group of those who believed were of one heart and soul, and no one claimed private ownership of any possessions, but everything they owned was held in common.” Acts 4:32

Life

Pachomius was born around 292 in Upper Egypt, near Esna on the Nile, to parents who were not Christians. When he was about twenty he was forced into the Roman army during the civil wars of the time. With other young recruits he was held under guard at Thebes. In the evening Christians of the town came and brought them food and drink. Pachomius asked who these people were, and he was told that they were Christians, who did good to all for the sake of the God of heaven. That night he prayed that if God would deliver him, he would serve all people for the rest of his life.

Soon he was released. He went to the village of Chenoboskion, received instruction and was baptised. He served the poor and sick there, and then became the disciple of an old hermit named Palamon, who taught him a hard life of prayer, work and fasting. After some years, while praying at the deserted village of Tabennesi, he understood that God was calling him to gather men to live together in his service.

Around 320 he began a community at Tabennesi. It did not start easily: his first followers would not obey, and he had to send them away. Slowly he learned to lead. His monks lived in houses within one enclosure, prayed together, worked together, ate together, wore the same simple clothes and obeyed a common Rule. Earlier monks had mostly lived alone or in loose groups around a teacher. Pachomius created the common life, which the early Greek sources call koinonia, fellowship. His sister Mary founded a community of women under his guidance.

By the end of his life there were nine monasteries for men and two for women, with several thousand monks and nuns. His Rule was later translated into Latin by St Jerome and influenced monks in the East and the West, including St Basil and St Benedict. He refused ordination as a priest, wishing his monks to remain humble. He died of the plague on 9 May 346. The Byzantine Churches and the Malankara Panchangam keep his memory on 15 May. In the Syro-Malankara Catholic Church, Most Rev. Mathews Mar Pachomios bears his name and keeps his name day today.

Stories

Bread for the recruits (history)

The Lives of Pachomius tell how the young soldier, held captive at Thebes, was fed by local Christians whom he had never met. Their kindness to strangers made him ask about their faith, and that night he made his first prayer to God.

The rule of the angel (legend)

Palladius, in the Lausiac History, records a tradition that an angel appeared to Pachomius and gave him a bronze tablet on which the Rule for his monks was written.

Hiding from the patriarch (history)

When St Athanasius, Patriarch of Alexandria, visited the Thebaid around 329, the local bishop wished to have Pachomius ordained a priest. Pachomius hid among his monks until the patriarch had gone, so that he would not be raised to an honour he felt was not his.

Homily

1. One heart and soul

The first believers shared all things (Acts 4:32).

2. Kindness to strangers

A meal given to prisoners changed a life.

3. Called to fellowship

We do not go to God alone.

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today our Church remembers Mar Pachomios, the father of monks who live together in community. The Acts of the Apostles says that after the Spirit came, the whole group of those who believed were of one heart and soul (Acts 4:32). Pachomios tried to build a whole way of life on that verse.

His story began with a small kindness. As a young man he was dragged into the army and held as a prisoner. In the evening some people came and brought food to the captives. He asked who they were and was told, They are Christians. They did not preach to him. They simply fed strangers. That was enough to lead him to Christ.

Pachomios learned that it is not easy to live together. His first followers would not obey him, and he had to start again. But slowly he built communities where people prayed, worked and ate together in peace. Our families and parishes are also called to be such communities. It takes patience, humility and forgiveness every day.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask the Holy Spirit to make us of one heart and soul. Let us think of one stranger to whom we can show kindness this week. And let us ask Mar Pachomios to pray for our families, our monasteries and all who live the consecrated life. Amen.

Prayer

Lord God, you led Mar Pachomios to Christ through the kindness of strangers and made him a father of many brothers and sisters living in fellowship. Through his prayers, teach us to serve all people, to live in peace in our families and communities, and to be of one heart and soul in you. Amen.
