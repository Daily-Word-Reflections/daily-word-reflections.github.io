St Irenaeus of Lyons

Bishop and martyr, Doctor of the Church (Doctor of Unity) · Feast: 28 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“There is one body and one Spirit, just as you were called to the one hope of your calling, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all, who is above all and through all and in all.” Ephesians 4:4–6

Life

Irenaeus was born in Asia Minor, probably at Smyrna (today Izmir in Turkey), some time between 130 and 140. As a boy he listened to the preaching of St Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna, who had known St John the Apostle and others who had seen the Lord. Irenaeus later wrote that he could still remember where Polycarp sat and how he spoke about John. Through Polycarp he was linked to the apostles by only one living generation, and this shaped his whole understanding of the faith as something received and handed on.

He went to Gaul and became a priest of the Church of Lyons. In 177 the Christians there were sent to Rome with a letter for Pope Eleutherius, and Irenaeus was the bearer, praised in the letter as zealous for the covenant of Christ. While he was away, a terrible persecution broke out at Lyons and Vienne, in which the aged bishop Pothinus and many others died for Christ. When Irenaeus returned, he was chosen bishop in Pothinus' place. He lived among the Celtic peoples of the region, whose language he had to learn, and cared for a Church still bleeding from persecution.

His great work, usually called Against Heresies, in five books, answered the Gnostic teachers who claimed a secret knowledge and despised the body and the created world. Irenaeus showed that there is one God, Creator of heaven and earth, who is the Father of Jesus Christ; that the Son truly became flesh and recapitulated, that is, gathered up and renewed, the whole history of humanity in himself; and that Mary, by her obedience, untied the knot of Eve's disobedience. To know the true faith, he said, look to the tradition of the apostles, kept in the Churches through the succession of bishops, especially in the Church of Rome. A shorter work, the Demonstration of the Apostolic Preaching, was found in an Armenian translation only in 1904.

Irenaeus died about the year 200. Later writers, among them St Gregory of Tours in the sixth century, honour him as a martyr, though the details are not known. Byzantine Churches keep his memory on 23 August, the Latin Church on 28 June. On 21 January 2022 Pope Francis declared him a Doctor of the Church with the title Doctor of Unity, because he was a bridge between East and West and a teacher who worked to keep the one Church united in truth and in peace.

Stories

Memories of Polycarp (history)

In a letter to Florinus, an old friend who had fallen into error, Irenaeus recalled his boyhood in Smyrna: he could describe the place where Polycarp sat to teach, his way of life, his appearance and his talks about John and the others who had seen the Lord. He wrote that he kept these memories not on paper but in his heart. Eusebius preserved this letter in his History of the Church.

A peacemaker true to his name (history)

About the year 190 Pope Victor wished to cut off the Churches of Asia Minor from communion because they kept Easter on a different day. Irenaeus, though he himself kept the Roman custom, wrote to Victor urging peace and reminding him that earlier popes had kept communion with those who differed in this practice. Eusebius remarks that Irenaeus was well named, for his name means peace, and that he was a peacemaker in deed.

The lost tomb (tradition)

Tradition holds that Irenaeus was buried in a crypt at Lyons under the church later named after him, alongside the martyrs of the city. In 1562, during the wars of religion in France, the tomb was destroyed and his relics were scattered. Only a part was later recovered. The Church of Lyons still keeps his memory with great love.

Words of the saint

The glory of God is a living human being; and the life of humanity consists in the vision of God.
St Irenaeus, Against Heresies 4.20.7, in substance
The knot of Eve's disobedience was loosed by the obedience of Mary; what the virgin Eve bound through unbelief, the Virgin Mary set loose through faith.
St Irenaeus, Against Heresies 3.22.4, in substance

Homily

1. One faith, one Church

Unity is God's gift and our task (Ephesians 4:4–6).

2. Hold to what was handed on

Guard the good treasure entrusted to you (2 Timothy 1:14).

3. The glory of God is a living person

God made us to live fully and see him (John 10:10).

Dear friends, today we honour St Irenaeus, bishop of Lyons, whom Pope Francis has named the Doctor of Unity. As a boy in Smyrna he sat at the feet of St Polycarp, who had known the Apostle John. Later he carried the Gospel to Gaul, at the other end of the Roman world. In his own life he joined East and West. He reminds us that the Church is one family across every land and every age, and that the faith we hold today is the same faith the apostles received from the Lord.

Paul writes, There is one body and one Spirit, just as you were called to the one hope of your calling, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all (Ephesians 4:4–6). In the time of Irenaeus many teachers offered secret doctrines and new gospels. Irenaeus did not answer with clever novelties. He pointed to what had been handed down openly from the apostles through the bishops in every Church. We too live in a time of many voices. The safest path is to remain in the communion of the Church and to learn the faith she has always taught.

Irenaeus also defended the goodness of creation and of the human body. The false teachers said that matter was evil and that only spirit mattered. Irenaeus answered that the one God made heaven and earth, and that the Son of God truly took our flesh. He wrote that the glory of God is a living human being. Our bodies, our work, our families and the world around us are not obstacles to holiness. They are the place where God's glory is meant to shine.

Finally, Irenaeus was a peacemaker. When the Church was in danger of division over the date of Easter, he wrote to the Pope asking for patience and charity. He was faithful to Rome and also gentle with those who differed in custom. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St Irenaeus to make us lovers of the truth and builders of unity, in our families, in our parishes and among all the Churches of Christ. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who called the bishop St Irenaeus to confirm true doctrine and the peace of the Church, grant, we pray, through his intercession, that, being renewed in faith and charity, we may always be intent on fostering unity and concord, and may come at last to see your glory. Amen.
