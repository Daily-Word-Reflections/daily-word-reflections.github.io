St Fidelis of Sigmaringen

Priest and martyr, Capuchin friar · Feast: 24 April

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Be faithful until death, and I will give you the crown of life.” Revelation 2:10

Life

Mark Roy was born in 1577 or 1578 at Sigmaringen, in the south of present-day Germany. He studied philosophy and law at the University of Freiburg im Breisgau and became a doctor of law. For some years he travelled through Europe as tutor to a group of young noblemen, and on these journeys he went often to Mass and visited the sick and the poor. He then practised law at Ensisheim in Alsace, where he was known as the poor man's lawyer because he took up the cases of those who could not pay.

The dishonesty he saw in the courts troubled him deeply, and he left the law to give himself to God. In 1612 he entered the Capuchin friars at Freiburg, taking the name Fidelis, which means faithful. He was ordained a priest and gave his property to the seminary and to the poor. He served as guardian of several friaries, preached with great zeal, prayed long before the Blessed Sacrament and was known for his care for the sick. When an epidemic struck the soldiers at Feldkirch, he nursed them and many were brought back to God.

In 1622 he was sent with other Capuchins to preach in the Grisons, the region of eastern Switzerland called Graubünden, where many people had joined the Reformed churches. It was a time of war, and Austrian troops occupied part of the region, so that religion and politics were bitterly mixed. Fidelis knew the danger. He preached and wrote with conviction, and many returned to the Catholic faith. That same year the Holy See founded the Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith, and his mission came under its care.

On 24 April 1622 he preached at Seewis in the Prättigau valley. After the sermon, as he set out on the road, he was attacked by a band of armed men and killed. Tradition says that he knelt and prayed for those who were killing him. He was the first martyr of the new Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith. He was canonised by Pope Benedict XIV in 1746. His relics are honoured at Feldkirch and in the cathedral of Chur.

Lessons

Justice for the poor

As a lawyer he served those who could not pay.

Faithful to his name

Stories

The poor man's lawyer (history)

At Ensisheim he refused to use trickery in the courts and took the cases of the poor without asking a fee. When he saw how often others did the opposite, he decided to seek a life wholly given to God.

A shot in the church (tradition)

At Seewis, on the day of his death, a shot is said to have been fired at him in the church while he was preaching, and it missed. He finished his sermon and then set out on the road where he was killed.

Soon to be food for worms (tradition)

In the days before his death, it is said, he signed his letters with words saying that he would soon be food for worms. He had prepared himself in prayer and confession for what he expected.

Words of the saint

Woe to me if I prove a half-hearted soldier in the service of my thorn-crowned Captain.
Attributed to St Fidelis of Sigmaringen, in substance

Homily

1. Faithful until death

The crown of life is promised (Revelation 2:10).

2. The honest lawyer

Work done justly is part of holiness.

3. Truth and love together

He preached firmly and prayed for his attackers.

Dear friends, the name Fidelis means faithful. The young lawyer Mark Roy took that name when he became a Capuchin friar, and he lived up to it. Before he was a friar he was already faithful in small things. In the courts of Alsace he was called the poor man's lawyer, because he would not cheat and he served those who could not pay.

Many of us work in offices, shops, courts and fields where dishonesty seems normal. Fidelis shows that holiness begins there, in honest work. When he could no longer bear the corruption he saw, he gave himself entirely to God. Not all of us are called to leave our work, but all of us are called to be faithful in it.

He was sent to preach in a land torn by war and by divisions among Christians. He knew he might be killed. He preached the Catholic faith with conviction, and he also nursed sick soldiers and cared for the poor. When death came on the road from Seewis, tradition tells us that he knelt and prayed for those who struck him. The risen Lord says, Be faithful until death, and I will give you the crown of life (Revelation 2:10).

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for Christian unity, that all who believe in Christ may one day be one. Let us examine our honesty at work. And let us ask St Fidelis to make us faithful, not only in great moments but every day. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who gave St Fidelis of Sigmaringen the grace to preach the faith and to lay down his life for it, grant through his prayers that we may be faithful to you until death, love justice for the poor, and forgive those who wrong us. Amen.
