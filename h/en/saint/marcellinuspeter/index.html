Sts Marcellinus and Peter

Martyrs of Rome, a priest and an exorcist · Feast: 2 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul” Matthew 10:28

Life

Marcellinus was a priest of the Church of Rome and Peter was an exorcist, one of the minor ministers of the Roman clergy who prayed over the sick and the troubled and helped prepare those coming to baptism. They lived at the beginning of the fourth century, when the emperor Diocletian launched the last and fiercest persecution of the Roman Empire, ordering churches to be pulled down, the Scriptures to be burned and Christians to offer sacrifice to the gods. Both men were arrested and put to death in Rome, probably about the year 304.

What we know of their death comes from Pope St Damasus, who wrote an epitaph for their tomb some sixty years later. Damasus says that as a boy he heard the story from the executioner himself. The two were led out of the city to a wooded place so that their burial would remain unknown. They were made to clear the ground for their own grave with their hands and were beheaded there in secret. Damasus adds that a devout woman named Lucilla later learned where they lay and carried their bodies to a cemetery on the Via Labicana, at a place called At the Two Laurels.

Their tomb soon became a place of pilgrimage. After the Church received peace, the emperor Constantine built a large basilica over the cemetery, and next to it rose the great round mausoleum where his mother St Helena was buried. The catacomb of Sts Marcellinus and Peter, with its many painted chambers showing banquets, Bible scenes and Christ among the apostles, can still be visited today. Their names were placed in the Roman Canon, the first Eucharistic Prayer of the Roman Rite, where the priest still remembers them together, Marcellinus and Peter, among the martyrs.

A later account of their passion adds many details, such as the conversion of their jailer and his family, which come from popular devotion rather than from history. In the ninth century Einhard, the biographer of Charlemagne, brought relics of the two martyrs from Rome to Germany and built a church for them at Seligenstadt, where they are still venerated. The Church keeps their memory on 2 June as two humble ministers who gave their lives for Christ without fame and almost without witnesses.

Stories

The executioner's story (history)

Pope Damasus writes that as a boy he heard from the executioner how the two martyrs cleared their own grave in a thicket and were beheaded there. Damasus had the verses carved on their tomb, and the story survives because the man who killed them told it.

The jailer's daughter (legend)

The later passion says that in prison Peter healed the sick daughter of the jailer Artemius, and that Artemius, his wife and his daughter were baptized by Marcellinus and later died as martyrs. These details are not found in the early sources and are a pious legend.

The journey to Seligenstadt (history)

In 827 relics of Sts Marcellinus and Peter were brought from Rome to Germany for Einhard, who wrote an account of their translation and of the cures reported at their shrine. He built a basilica for them at Seligenstadt on the river Main.

Homily

1. No fear of those who kill the body

The soul belongs to God (Matthew 10:28).

2. A cloud of witnesses

The martyrs surround us and encourage us (Hebrews 12:1).

3. In the hand of God

No torment can touch the righteous (Wisdom 3:1).

Dear friends, today we remember two men about whom we know very little. Marcellinus was a priest. Peter was an exorcist, a minister who prayed over the sick and helped prepare people for baptism. They were not bishops or great writers. They were ordinary servants of the Church of Rome. Yet when the persecution came, they did not run away from Christ.

Their killers wanted them forgotten. They were taken to a lonely wood, made to clear the ground for their own grave and beheaded in secret. But the Lord had said, Do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul (Matthew 10:28). The persecutors could take their lives, but not their faith. And God did not let them be forgotten. Pope Damasus heard the story as a boy from the executioner himself, and he carved it on their tomb.

Every time a priest prays the Roman Canon, he still names Marcellinus and Peter. That is a beautiful thing. The Church at the altar remembers two humble ministers. The writer to the Hebrews says that we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses (Hebrews 12:1). When we gather for the Eucharist, we are never alone. The martyrs pray with us and for us.

Most of us will live and die without anyone writing about us. Much of our faithfulness is hidden: a mother praying at night, a man keeping his word at work, a sick person offering pain to God. Marcellinus and Peter teach us that hidden faithfulness is precious, because God sees in secret. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask them to make us faithful in small things and firm in great trials, and to help us forgive those who hurt us. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who surround us with the glorious witness of your martyrs Marcellinus and Peter, grant through their prayers that we may serve you humbly and faithfully, remain steadfast when no one sees us but you, and one day rejoice with them in the joy of your kingdom. Through our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.
