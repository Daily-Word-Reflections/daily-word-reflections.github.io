Sts Peter and Paul, Apostles

The two pillars of the Church, martyrs in Rome · Feast: 29 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith.” 2 Timothy 4:7

Life

From very early times the Church of Rome has honoured St Peter and St Paul together on one day. A Roman list of feasts drawn up in the year 354 already notes their memory on 29 June. They were very different men: Peter a fisherman of Galilee, impulsive and warm hearted, whom the Lord made the rock and shepherd of his flock; Paul a learned Pharisee from Tarsus, who had persecuted the Church before the risen Christ called him to be the apostle of the nations. They had even disagreed openly at Antioch (Galatians 2:11). Yet both gave their lives for Christ in the same city, and the Church sees in them two pillars on which, by God's grace, she stands.

The New Testament does not describe their deaths, but it points toward them. After the Resurrection the Lord told Peter that when he was old he would stretch out his hands and another would lead him where he did not wish to go, and John explains that this indicated the kind of death by which he would glorify God (John 21:18–19). Paul, writing to Timothy near the end of his life, said that he was already being poured out as a libation and that the time of his departure had come (2 Timothy 4:6). The Acts of the Apostles closes with Paul in Rome under guard, preaching the kingdom of God with all boldness (Acts 28:30–31).

About the year 96 St Clement of Rome wrote to the Corinthians of Peter and Paul as the greatest and most righteous pillars of the Church, who had borne witness and suffered among the Romans. St Ignatius of Antioch, on his way to martyrdom, told the Roman Christians that he did not give them orders as Peter and Paul had done. Ancient tradition holds that both died in the persecution under the Emperor Nero, which followed the great fire of Rome in 64: Peter was crucified near the Vatican hill, and Paul, a Roman citizen, was beheaded on the road to Ostia. The exact year of each death is not known.

About the year 200 a Roman priest named Gaius wrote that he could show the trophies, that is, the monuments, of the apostles: on the Vatican hill and on the Ostian Way. Over these places the Emperor Constantine built great basilicas. Excavations under St Peter's Basilica in the twentieth century uncovered a tomb monument of the second century, and in 1968 Pope St Paul VI announced that bones found there had been identified, in his judgement convincingly, as those of St Peter. On this solemnity the Pope blesses the pallium for new metropolitan archbishops as a sign of their communion with the Successor of Peter. In the Malankara tradition the feast closes the Apostles' Fast.

Lessons

Two different men, one faith

God built his Church on a fisherman and a scholar, each with his own gifts.

Grace that heals failure

Peter who denied and Paul who persecuted both became witnesses through mercy.

Faithful to the end

Both finished the race and gave their lives for Christ in Rome.

Unity with Peter

The Church remains one around the successor of Peter and the faith of the apostles.

Stories

The trophies of the apostles (history)

Eusebius preserves the words of Gaius, a Roman priest writing about the year 200, who challenged an opponent: if you will go to the Vatican or to the Ostian Way, you will find the trophies of those who founded this Church. This is one of the earliest witnesses to the places where the two apostles were honoured.

Head downward (tradition)

Origen, as quoted by Eusebius, records that Peter, when he came to Rome, was crucified head downward, having asked to suffer in this way. Later writers explained that he did not count himself worthy to die in the same manner as his Lord. The detail comes from early tradition, not from Scripture.

The three fountains (legend)

A medieval legend tells that when Paul was beheaded outside Rome, his head struck the ground three times, and at each place a spring of water burst forth. The Abbey of the Three Fountains (Tre Fontane) now stands on the traditional site of his martyrdom. The springs belong to legend, but the place has been venerated since ancient times.

Words of the saint

Through jealousy and strife Paul showed the way to the prize of endurance; Peter, having borne his witness, went to his appointed place of glory.
St Clement of Rome, Letter to the Corinthians 5, in substance
One day is given to the feast of two apostles, but they too were one. Though they suffered on different days, they were one.
St Augustine, Sermon 295, in substance

Homily

1. Called in weakness

God chooses the weak to show his power (2 Corinthians 12:9).

2. Shepherd and missionary

Peter feeds the flock; Paul carries the Gospel to the nations (John 21:17; Romans 15:20).

3. Keeping the faith

Paul finished the race and kept the faith (2 Timothy 4:7).

Dear friends, today the whole Church celebrates the two great apostles, Peter and Paul. They were not alike. Peter was a fisherman from Galilee, Paul a learned teacher from Tarsus. Peter walked with Jesus for three years; Paul met him only on the road as the risen Lord. They even quarrelled at Antioch. Yet the Church honours them on one day, because in Rome both poured out their blood for the same Lord. Our parishes and families are also made of very different people. Christ does not ask us to be the same, but to be one in him.

Both men knew failure. Peter denied the Lord three times on the night of the Passion. Paul had once dragged believers to prison. Neither became an apostle because he was perfect. They became apostles because they were forgiven. Paul heard the Lord say, My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness (2 Corinthians 12:9). When we feel unworthy, we can remember these two pillars, and trust that God can build on our weakness too.

At the end, both were faithful. The Lord had told Peter that another would lead him where he did not wish to go, and Peter followed his Master even to a cross. Paul wrote from prison, I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith (2 Timothy 4:7). The Christian life is not a short sprint but a long race. There are days of joy and days of trial. What matters is to keep running, to keep believing, and to finish in the Lord.

Peter and Paul also remind us that we belong to one Church built on the apostles. Peter's successor still serves the unity of the whole Church, and Paul's missionary zeal reaches every land, including our own. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for the Holy Father and for all our bishops, and let us ask Sts Peter and Paul to give us their faith, their courage and their love, so that we too may keep the faith to the end. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who on the solemnity of the Apostles Peter and Paul give us the noble and holy joy of this day, grant that your Church may in all things follow the teaching of those through whom she received the beginnings of right religion, and that, strengthened by their prayers, we may keep the faith to the end and share their crown. Amen.
