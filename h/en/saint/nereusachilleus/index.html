Sts Nereus and Achilleus

Martyrs, soldiers who laid down their arms for Christ · Feast: 12 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Share in suffering like a good soldier of Christ Jesus.” 2 Timothy 2:3

Life

Nereus and Achilleus were Roman soldiers who became Christians and died as martyrs. We know very little about their lives, and the little we know comes from a few lines written about them some decades after their death by Pope St Damasus I, who was Bishop of Rome from 366 to 384 and who loved to honour the tombs of the martyrs with verses carved in marble. Their death is usually placed in one of the persecutions of the third or early fourth century, perhaps that of Diocletian, but the date is not certain.

According to the inscription of Damasus, the two had served in the army and had carried out the cruel orders of a tyrant, ready to do what they were told out of fear. Then, suddenly converted, they left the camp, threw away their shields, their armour and their weapons stained with blood, and confessed Christ, rejoicing to bear the triumph of Christ. Damasus ends by asking the reader to believe what glory Christ can work. The original stone was broken long ago, but its text was copied by early pilgrims, and fragments of it were found in the nineteenth century.

The martyrs were buried in the catacomb of Domitilla on the Via Ardeatina, south of Rome, on land that had belonged to a Christian family related to the emperors. Toward the end of the fourth century a basilica was built there, half underground, over their tombs. In it the archaeologist Giovanni Battista de Rossi found in 1874 a small column carved with the scene of a soldier being beheaded and the name Acilleus, one of the oldest images of a martyrdom in Rome.

Later centuries wrote longer Acts about them, which make them servants of Flavia Domitilla, a relative of the emperor Domitian, and say that they were exiled with her and beheaded at Terracina. These Acts are legend rather than history. Pope St Gregory the Great preached a homily in their basilica on their feast, and today their relics rest in a church in Rome that bears their names. The Church keeps their memory on 12 May.

Stories

The poem of Damasus (history)

Pope Damasus wrote that the two soldiers threw away their shields and bloody weapons and left the camp of the tyrant to confess Christ. His verses, copied by pilgrims who visited the catacombs, are almost the only record of their lives.

The column of Achilleus (history)

In the ruined basilica in the catacomb of Domitilla a small marble column was found, showing a soldier with his hands bound behind him and an executioner raising a sword, with the name of Achilleus carved above. It is one of the oldest pictures of a martyrdom in Rome.

Gregory at the tomb (history)

Pope Gregory the Great once preached at their tomb. He told the people that these saints despised the world while it was flourishing, and that the world, now falling into ruin, should be easier for us to leave behind.

Words of the saint

They threw away their shields, their armour and their weapons stained with blood, and confessing Christ they rejoiced to bear his triumph.
Pope St Damasus I, epitaph for Sts Nereus and Achilleus, in substance
These saints, at whose tomb we are standing, despised the world when it flourished.
Pope St Gregory the Great, Homily 28 on the Gospels, in substance

Homily

1. A good soldier of Christ

Paul uses the soldier as an image of the disciple (2 Timothy 2:3).

2. Leaving the camp

They refused to go on doing what was wrong.

3. Few words, great witness

A short epitaph has kept their memory.

Dear friends, we know almost nothing about Sts Nereus and Achilleus. No letters, no sermons, no long life story. What we have is a short poem carved in stone by Pope Damasus, who wanted the people of Rome to remember them. It tells us they were soldiers who had obeyed cruel orders, and who one day threw down their weapons, left the camp and confessed Christ.

Their story speaks to everyone who has ever been told to do something wrong because it was an order, a custom or the way things are done. In an office, in a business, in politics or even at home, we may hear: just do what you are told. These two soldiers discovered that there is a higher obedience. Peter and the apostles said it before them: we must obey God rather than any human authority (Acts 5:29).

Paul tells Timothy, Share in suffering like a good soldier of Christ Jesus (2 Timothy 2:3). The disciple is a soldier, but a soldier of a different kind. His weapons are truth, faith and the word of God. Nereus and Achilleus exchanged the sword that kills for the Cross that gives life, and they gave their own blood rather than shed the blood of others.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for soldiers and police, for all who must obey orders, and for those whose conscience is under pressure. Let us ask for the grace of a sudden conversion when we are walking the wrong road, and for courage to lay down whatever weapon we use against others, whether it is a sharp tongue or a hard heart. Sts Nereus and Achilleus, pray for us. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who gave the holy martyrs Nereus and Achilleus the grace to lay down the weapons of this world and confess your Son, grant through their prayers that we may turn from every evil we are asked to do, follow our conscience with courage, and serve you as good soldiers of Christ Jesus. Amen.
