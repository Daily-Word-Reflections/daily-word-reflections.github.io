Mar Ishaq Malpan of Antioch (St Isaac of Antioch)

Priest, Malpan and poet of the Syriac Church · Feast: 13 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly; teach and admonish one another in all wisdom; and with gratitude in your hearts sing psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs to God.” Colossians 3:16

Life

Mar Ishaq of Antioch is one of the great poets of the Syriac Church, a writer of memre, long metrical homilies meant to be chanted. Nearly two hundred memre have come down under his name, on prayer, fasting, repentance, the monastic life, the Incarnation, the Eucharist and the events of his time. They are still read and valued by Syriac Christians.

His life is hard to trace, and this must be said plainly. Later Syriac writers already noticed that the writings under the name Isaac may come from more than one man. Scholars usually speak of Isaac of Amid in the early fifth century, of Isaac the Great of Antioch in the fifth century, and of Isaac of Edessa in the sixth. The poems were later gathered under one name, and it is not certain which of them belong to which Isaac. He is not the same person as St Isaac of Nineveh, the Syriac monk of the seventh century.

The Latin writer Gennadius of Marseille, writing late in the fifth century, says that Isaac, a priest of the Church of Antioch, wrote much in Syriac, composed a lament in verse on the ruin of Antioch, and died in the reign of the emperors Leo and Majorian, that is, around 460. Syriac tradition adds that he travelled to Rome and lived as a monk near Antioch. These details cannot all be checked.

His best known poem tells of a parrot in Antioch that had learned to sing the Trisagion, the hymn Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, in the streets, and of the arguments it caused among the people about how the hymn should be sung. The Malankara Panchangam honours him with the title Malpan, teacher, and keeps his memory on 13 May.

Stories

The singing parrot (history)

The memra on the parrot of Antioch, one of the longest Syriac poems, tells of a bird that sang the Trisagion in the marketplace. Its song stirred up a city already divided over how the hymn should be sung, and the poet uses the event to speak about the faith and about peace.

Many Isaacs (history)

The great Syriac scholar Jacob of Edessa, around the year 700, already wrote about the different writers named Isaac. The question he raised has not been settled even today.

The journey to Rome (tradition)

Syriac tradition says that Isaac went to Rome as a young man to see the tombs of the apostles and then returned to the East. The story is not confirmed by early witnesses.

Homily

1. Sing to God

Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly (Colossians 3:16).

2. Teaching through song

What we sing we remember.

3. Known to God

His life is hidden, but his praise remains.

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today our Church remembers Mar Ishaq Malpan of Antioch, a poet and teacher of the Syriac Church. St Paul writes, Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly; teach and admonish one another in all wisdom; and with gratitude in your hearts sing psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs to God (Colossians 3:16). Isaac did exactly this. He taught the faith in song.

Our Syriac tradition loves to pray in song. Many of us learned the faith not first from books but from the hymns we heard as children in our parish. Isaac's memre were chanted so that ordinary people could carry the truths of faith in their memory and in their hearts.

We do not know much about Isaac's life. Scholars even think that poems from more than one writer have been gathered under his name. That should not trouble us. The Church remembers him because his words have helped people to pray for fifteen hundred years. God knows every hidden servant, even when history forgets the details.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us sing the hymns of our liturgy with attention and love. Let us teach them to our children. And let us ask Mar Ishaq to help us turn our whole life into a song of praise. Amen.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you gave Mar Ishaq Malpan the gift of praising you in song and of teaching your people the way of prayer. Through his prayers, let your word dwell richly in us, teach us to sing your praises with grateful hearts, and pour out on us the living water of your Holy Spirit. Amen.
