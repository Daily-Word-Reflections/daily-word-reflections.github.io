Sts Gervasius and Protasius

Martyrs of Milan, whose relics were found by St Ambrose in 386 · Feast: 19 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slaughtered for the word of God and for the testimony they had given” Revelation 6:9

Life

Gervasius and Protasius were martyrs of the Church of Milan in the early centuries. Of their lives and deaths almost nothing certain is known: not their dates, not the persecution in which they died, not the manner of their death. We know them because of what happened to their bodies long afterwards, in the time of St Ambrose, Bishop of Milan, and because two great witnesses, Ambrose himself and St Augustine, wrote about it.

In June 386 Ambrose had built a new basilica in Milan, the one that today bears his own name. The people asked him to consecrate it with relics of martyrs, as he had done for the Roman Basilica, the church of the Apostles. Ambrose wrote to his sister Marcellina that he felt a kind of burning presentiment, and he had the ground opened before the screen of the shrine of Sts Felix and Nabor. There two bodies were found, of men of great stature, with signs of a violent death and much blood. Augustine, who was then in Milan, says in his Confessions that God showed Ambrose in a vision where the bodies of the martyrs lay hidden.

These were stormy days for the Catholics of Milan. The empress Justina, mother of the young emperor Valentinian II, favoured the Arians, who denied that the Son is true God, and she had demanded a basilica of the city for their worship. Ambrose and his people had kept watch in the church day and night, singing hymns and psalms. The finding of the martyrs came as a strengthening of the faithful. The bodies were carried in procession to the new basilica, and as they passed, a blind man of the city named Severus touched the cloth that covered them and recovered his sight. Ambrose speaks of it in his letter and in a sermon, and Augustine mentions it more than once, in the Confessions, in The City of God and in his sermons.

Ambrose laid the martyrs beneath the altar of his new basilica, in the place he had prepared for his own grave. He did not lose that place for long: when he died in 397 he was buried beside them, and the three rest together to this day in the crypt of the Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio (Saint Ambrose) in Milan. The cult of the two martyrs spread quickly through Italy, Gaul and Africa, and many churches took their names. The Church of Milan has always honoured them, and they are remembered on 19 June.

Stories

The finding of the martyrs (history)

Ambrose tells Marcellina that the clergy were at first afraid to dig, but he went ahead, and the bodies were found just as he had sensed. The people came in crowds for two days to see them, and the martyrs were then carried to the new basilica with great joy.

The blind butcher (history)

Severus, a butcher by trade who had been blind for years, heard the noise of the procession, asked to be led to it, and touched the cloth laid over the bodies. His sight came back. Ambrose says the man was well known in Milan, and Augustine, who lived in the city at the time, writes that he too knew of it.

Twin brothers (legend)

A later account of their martyrdom, written long after Ambrose, says that Gervasius and Protasius were twin sons of St Vitalis and St Valeria, and that they died at Milan in the time of Nero. Historians regard this account as a pious composition of uncertain worth.

Homily

1. Under the altar

The martyrs share the sacrifice of the Lamb (Revelation 6:9).

2. God remembers

Those whom history forgets are precious to him (Psalm 116:15).

3. Comfort in trial

God strengthens his people when they need it most.

Dear friends, today we remember two martyrs whose story almost no one knew. Gervasius and Protasius gave their lives for Christ in Milan, perhaps in the second or third century, and then for a long time they were forgotten. We do not know their words or how they died. But God knew where they lay, and in his time he made them known again.

In the year 386 the Catholics of Milan were under heavy pressure. The court favoured the Arians, who denied that the Son is true God, and wanted one of the city's churches for them. St Ambrose and his people kept watch in the church, praying and singing hymns. In those days Ambrose found the bodies of the two martyrs, and a blind man who touched the cloth over them received his sight. St Augustine, not yet baptised, was in Milan at the time and later wrote of it. God gave his tired people a sign that he had not abandoned them.

Ambrose placed the martyrs under the altar of his new church. The Book of Revelation says, I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slaughtered for the word of God and for the testimony they had given (Revelation 6:9). Every altar in our churches carries this meaning. Christ offers himself upon it, and those who gave their lives for him are gathered close to him. When we come to the altar, we come into the company of the martyrs.

Many of us live hidden lives, and many of our sacrifices are never seen. These two martyrs tell us that nothing offered to God is lost. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us place our hidden sufferings beside the sacrifice of Christ. Let us pray for Christians who are under pressure for their faith today, and ask Sts Gervasius and Protasius to keep us faithful when the way is hard. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who gave strength to your Church in Milan through the finding of the holy martyrs Gervasius and Protasius, grant through their prayers that we may hold fast to the true faith in your Son, find our place close to his altar, and, when trials come, receive the comfort you give to those who trust in you. Amen.
