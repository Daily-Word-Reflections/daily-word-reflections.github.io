Mar Barthulmai Shleeha (St Bartholomew the Apostle)

Apostle, one of the Twelve, honoured as the Nathanael of John's Gospel · Feast: 11 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Here is truly an Israelite in whom there is no deceit!” John 1:47

Life

Bartholomew is named in all four lists of the Twelve (Matthew 10:3, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:14, Acts 1:13), and in the first three he stands beside Philip. His name is Aramaic, Bar-Tolmai, son of Tolmai, and so it is a family name rather than a first name. The Gospels record no word or deed of his under that name. Since the early Middle Ages, in both East and West, he has been commonly identified with Nathanael of Cana in Galilee (John 21:2), whom Philip brought to Jesus. The Gospel of John never names Bartholomew and the other Gospels never name Nathanael, and in John too Nathanael is linked with Philip. The identification is not certain, but it is old and widely held.

The call of Nathanael is one of the most human scenes in the Gospels. Philip told him that they had found the one of whom Moses and the prophets wrote, Jesus son of Joseph from Nazareth. Nathanael answered, Can anything good come out of Nazareth? Philip did not argue; he said, Come and see (John 1:45–46). When Jesus saw him coming he said, Here is truly an Israelite in whom there is no deceit! Nathanael asked how he knew him, and Jesus said that he had seen him under the fig tree before Philip called him. Then Nathanael confessed: Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are the King of Israel! (John 1:49). Jesus promised him greater things: he would see heaven opened and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of Man (John 1:51).

Nathanael is named again after the Resurrection. He was one of the seven disciples who went fishing on the Sea of Tiberias with Peter, Thomas and the sons of Zebedee, caught nothing all night, and at dawn saw the risen Lord on the shore and ate the breakfast he had prepared for them (John 21:1–14). As one of the Twelve, Bartholomew was also with the apostles in the upper room in prayer with Mary, the mother of Jesus, and received the Holy Spirit at Pentecost (Acts 1:13–14, Acts 2:1–4).

Of his mission we have only traditions. The historian Eusebius writes that when Pantaenus of Alexandria went to India late in the second century, he found there the Gospel of Matthew in Hebrew letters, left by Bartholomew, one of the apostles. Which land the ancients meant by India here is not certain; some think of Arabia or Ethiopia, others of the west coast of India. The Armenian Church honours Bartholomew, together with Thaddaeus, as the first to bring the Gospel to Armenia, and an old tradition says he was flayed and put to death at Albanopolis in Armenia. His relics are said to have been taken to the island of Lipari, then to Benevento, and later, in part, to Rome, where the Church of St Bartholomew on the Tiber Island keeps them. The Syro-Malankara Panchangam remembers him on 11 June, together with Mar Barnabas.

Stories

Under the fig tree (history)

When Jesus told Nathanael, I saw you under the fig tree before Philip called you, Nathanael was so moved that he at once confessed him as Son of God and King of Israel. The Gospel does not say what happened under the fig tree. Later Jewish writings speak of teachers who studied the Law under a fig tree, and many interpreters think Jesus spoke of a hidden moment of prayer known only to God (John 1:48–49).

The Gospel left in India (tradition)

Eusebius tells that Pantaenus, the teacher of the school at Alexandria, went as a missionary to the peoples of the East as far as India, and found Christians there who had a copy of Matthew's Gospel in Hebrew letters, which Bartholomew had left them. St Jerome repeats the account. It is a precious early witness, though the place cannot be fixed.

The flayed apostle (legend)

An ancient tradition, kept especially in Armenia, says that Bartholomew converted a king named Polymius, and that the king's brother had him flayed alive and then beheaded. For this reason Christian art often shows him holding a knife, and Michelangelo painted him in the Sistine Chapel holding his own skin. The details of the story come from later accounts that cannot be checked.

Homily

1. Come and see

Philip invited, he did not argue (John 1:46).

2. No deceit

Jesus loves an honest heart (John 1:47).

3. Greater things

Heaven opened over the Son of Man (John 1:51).

Dear friends, today our Church remembers Mar Barthulmai Shleeha, St Bartholomew, one of the Twelve. In the Gospel of John he appears under the name Nathanael, and his first words about Jesus are not very kind: Can anything good come out of Nazareth? He was a sincere man, and sincere people sometimes say exactly what they think.

Philip did not argue with him. He said only, Come and see (John 1:46). This is still the best way to bring someone to Christ. We cannot argue people into faith. But we can invite them to come and see: to see how Christians live, how we forgive, how we pray, how we care for the poor. Our lives are the invitation.

When Jesus saw Nathanael coming, he did not scold him for his words. He said, Here is truly an Israelite in whom there is no deceit! (John 1:47). The Lord loves an honest heart, even an honest heart full of questions. Then he told Nathanael that he had seen him under the fig tree. Nathanael understood that he had been known and loved before he knew it, and he cried out, Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are the King of Israel!

Tradition says that Bartholomew carried the Gospel far, perhaps even to India, and gave his life for it. The man who once doubted that anything good could come from Nazareth spent his life telling the nations that the best of all things had come from there. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, Christ sees each of us as he saw Nathanael. Let us come to him with honest hearts, and ask Mar Barthulmai Shleeha to make us people who invite others to come and see. Amen.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, who saw Mar Barthulmai Shleeha under the fig tree and called him to be your apostle, grant through his prayers that we may come and see you with honest hearts, confess you as the Son of God and King, and carry your Gospel faithfully to those who have not yet met you. Amen.
