St Rita of Cascia

Wife, mother, widow and Augustinian nun, patron of difficult causes · Feast: 22 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good.” Romans 12:21

Life

Rita was born around 1381 at Roccaporena, a mountain village near Cascia in Umbria, in central Italy. Her baptismal name was Margherita. The accounts written after her death, which mix memory with devotion, say that her parents were elderly and known in the district as peacemakers, and that from girlhood she longed for the cloister. In obedience to her parents she was married young, probably at about twelve to sixteen years of age, to a man of the village called Paolo Mancini.

The tradition remembers Paolo as a hard and quick-tempered man, caught up in the bitter feuds of the time. Rita bore his moods with patience and prayer, and in the course of some eighteen years he changed and became gentler. They had two sons. Then Paolo was murdered in an ambush, a victim of the family vendettas that troubled the valley. Rita forgave the killers. She hid the bloodstained shirt so that her sons would not be stirred to revenge, but the boys, now young men, were already talking of it. Rita prayed that God would take them rather than let them commit murder. Within a year both fell ill and died, and she nursed them to the end.

Alone now, Rita asked to enter the monastery of St Mary Magdalene in Cascia, a community of Augustinian nuns. She was refused, perhaps because she was a widow, perhaps because the nuns feared being drawn into the feud. The tradition says that she first worked to reconcile her husband's family with his killers, and only when peace was made was she received, around 1417. For about forty years she lived the common life of prayer, work and obedience according to the Rule of St Augustine, caring for sick sisters and praying for sinners and for the people of the valley.

Around 1442, after hearing a sermon on the Passion, Rita is said to have received a wound on her forehead, as if from a thorn of the crown of Christ. It remained with her until death and caused her much pain. She died on 22 May 1457, though some accounts give 1447. Her body, never buried in the ground, is kept in the Basilica of St Rita in Cascia. Pope Urban VIII beatified her in 1627 and Pope Leo XIII canonised her on 24 May 1900. She is invoked in difficult and seemingly hopeless situations, and roses are blessed on her feast in many places.

Patience in the home

Rita's quiet love helped to change a hard heart.

Stories

The dry stick (legend)

The early accounts say that the abbess, to test Rita's obedience, told her to water a dry stick in the garden every day. She did so without complaint, and in time the stick sprouted. A vine still grows in the cloister at Cascia that pilgrims are told came from it.

The rose in winter (legend)

When Rita was dying, a cousin visited her in the winter and asked whether she wanted anything from home. She asked for a rose from the garden at Roccaporena. The cousin went, found a single rose blooming in the snow and brought it to her. This is the origin of the blessing of roses on her feast.

The thorn (tradition)

On Good Friday, the tradition says, Rita heard a preacher speak of the crown of thorns and asked to share a little of the Lord's suffering. A thorn seemed to pierce her forehead, leaving a wound that the sisters saw for the rest of her life. The wound is recorded in the earliest descriptions of her body.

Homily

1. Overcome evil with good

Rita answered violence with forgiveness (Romans 12:21).

2. Peace in the family

She would not let hatred pass to her sons.

3. Hope when all seems lost

God's grace reaches even the hardest case.

Dear friends, St Paul tells us, “Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good” (Romans 12:21). St Rita of Cascia lived those words in a village torn by feuds, in a marriage that was not easy, and in a sorrow that would have broken many hearts.

Rita did not choose her husband. She was married young, and the man she married was rough and violent. She did not run away from her cross, nor did she simply suffer in silence. She prayed, she was patient, she showed kindness, and over the years her husband became gentler. Many wives and mothers among us know what it is to carry a heavy home. Rita tells them that their patient love is not wasted.

Then her husband was killed. In that valley, the custom was clear: blood must be paid with blood. Rita broke the custom. She forgave the murderers, and she feared more for the souls of her sons than for their lives. Later she made peace between the families before she entered the convent. How many of our families are divided by a quarrel over land, money or an old insult. Rita asks us to be the ones who stop the cycle.

That is why people call her the saint of impossible cases. She herself knew situations that seemed hopeless, and she found that nothing is impossible for God. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us bring to the Lord the hard situations in our homes, let us pray for those who cannot forgive, and let us ask, through St Rita, for the courage to overcome evil with good. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who gave St Rita the grace to love her enemies and to bear patiently the sorrows of her home, grant through her prayers that we may forgive from the heart, bring peace where there is division, and trust in your mercy even when our troubles seem beyond all hope. Amen.
