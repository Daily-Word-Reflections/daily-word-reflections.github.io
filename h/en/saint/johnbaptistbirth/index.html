The Nativity of St John the Baptist (Yohannan Mamdana)

Forerunner of the Lord, prophet of the Most High · Feast: 24 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“And you, child, will be called the prophet of the Most High; for you will go before the Lord to prepare his ways.” Luke 1:76

Life

The Church usually keeps the feast of a saint on the day of his death, his birthday into heaven. Only three births are celebrated in the liturgy: the birth of the Lord, the birth of the Blessed Virgin Mary, and the birth of John the Baptist. John is honoured in this way because his birth was itself part of the history of salvation. Luke tells it alongside the birth of Jesus, so that the two births stand side by side as promise and fulfilment, the voice and the Word.

Elizabeth, who had been barren, gave birth to a son in her old age, and her neighbours and relatives rejoiced with her, for the Lord had shown his great mercy (Luke 1:57–58). On the eighth day they came to circumcise the child and wanted to name him Zechariah after his father. Elizabeth said he was to be called John. When they asked his father, who had been unable to speak since the angel's message, he wrote on a tablet, His name is John. At once his mouth was opened and he began to praise God (Luke 1:59–64). The name means God is gracious.

Filled with the Holy Spirit, Zechariah sang the hymn the Church calls the Benedictus, prayed each morning in the Latin liturgy of the hours. He blessed God for raising up a mighty saviour and told his little son that he would go before the Lord to prepare his ways, to give knowledge of salvation to his people by the forgiveness of their sins (Luke 1:67–79). All who heard these things wondered what this child would become, for the hand of the Lord was with him. The child grew and became strong in spirit, and he was in the wilderness until the day he appeared publicly to Israel (Luke 1:66, 80).

In the Syriac tradition John is called Yohannan Mamdana, John the Baptizer, and he is honoured among the greatest of the saints. The feast is kept on 24 June, six months before Christmas, as Luke tells that Elizabeth was in her sixth month when the angel came to Mary (Luke 1:36). St Augustine noted that after this date the days begin to grow shorter, while after Christmas they grow longer, and he saw in this a sign of John's own words: He must increase, but I must decrease (John 3:30).

Stories

His name is John (history)

When the relatives wanted to name the child after his father, Elizabeth insisted on the name given by the angel. Zechariah, still mute, asked for a writing tablet and wrote, His name is John. Everyone was amazed, his tongue was set loose, fear came over all their neighbours, and these things were talked about throughout the hill country of Judea (Luke 1:59–66).

The voice and the Word (tradition)

In a sermon for this feast St Augustine reflected that John is the voice and Christ is the Word. A word is first in the heart of the speaker; the voice carries it to the hearer and then passes away, while the word remains. So John came to carry the Word to the world and then to step aside, saying that he must decrease.

The hymn that named the notes (history)

In the eighth century a Latin hymn for this feast, Ut queant laxis, was written, traditionally ascribed to Paul the Deacon, asking St John, who had loosened his father's tongue, to loosen the lips of the singers. About the year 1025 the monk Guido of Arezzo used the first syllables of its lines, ut, re, mi, fa, sol, la, to teach singers the notes of the scale. The names of the musical notes used around the world thus come from a hymn to St John the Baptist.

Homily

1. His name is John

God's grace comes before all our plans (Luke 1:63).

2. What will this child become?

Every child carries a mystery of God's call (Luke 1:66).

3. The joy of the forerunner

He must increase, I must decrease (John 3:30).

Dear friends, today the whole Church rejoices at the birth of John the Baptist. We usually honour the saints on the day of their death, when they enter heaven. But we celebrate the birth of John, as we celebrate the birth of the Lord and of his Mother, because his birth was a sign that God was about to fulfil his promises. The long silence of prophecy was ending. An old couple held in their arms a child who would prepare the way of the Saviour.

The relatives wanted to call the child Zechariah, as was the custom. But God had already chosen his name: John, which means God is gracious. Zechariah wrote, His name is John (Luke 1:63), and his tongue was set loose. His obedience opened his mouth in praise. When we stop holding on to our own plans and accept what God has chosen, our hearts too are set loose to praise him. Many parents today can learn from this: our children belong first to God, and he has a name and a purpose for each of them.

The neighbours asked, What then will this child become? (Luke 1:66). It is a question we should ask over every child brought to the font of baptism. Each child is a mystery of grace. John grew up in the wilderness, in silence and prayer, until the day came for his mission. Our young people also need times of silence and prayer, away from noise and screens, so that they can hear the voice of God and discover their calling.

John's greatness lay in his humility. He did not draw people to himself but pointed them to Jesus, saying, Here is the Lamb of God. He said, He must increase, but I must decrease (John 3:30). At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St John the Baptist to make us humble witnesses who prepare the way of the Lord in our homes and parishes, and who rejoice when Christ, and not we ourselves, is seen and loved. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who raised up St John the Baptist to make ready a nation fit for Christ the Lord, give your people the joy of spiritual grace, and direct the hearts of all the faithful into the way of salvation and peace, that, like John, we may prepare the way of the Lord and point others to the Lamb of God. Amen.
