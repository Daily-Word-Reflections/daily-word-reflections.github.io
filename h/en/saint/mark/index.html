St Mark the Evangelist

Evangelist, companion of Peter and Paul · Feast: 25 April

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“The beginning of the good news of Jesus Christ, the Son of God.” Mark 1:1

Life

Mark is the John Mark of the Acts of the Apostles. His mother was a Mary who had a house in Jerusalem, where the believers gathered to pray; when Peter was led out of prison by an angel, it was to her door that he went (Acts 12:12). Paul calls Mark the cousin of Barnabas (Colossians 4:10). So he grew up close to the first community of Jerusalem, among people who had known the Lord.

Barnabas and Saul took Mark with them to Antioch and then on their first missionary journey, to Cyprus. But at Perga, in Asia Minor, Mark left them and went back to Jerusalem (Acts 13:13). We are not told why. When the next journey was being planned, Paul refused to take him again, and the disagreement was so sharp that Barnabas and Paul parted: Barnabas sailed with Mark to Cyprus (Acts 15:37–39). The story does not end there. Years later Paul names Mark among his fellow workers (Philemon 24), and near the end of his life he writes, “Get Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful in my ministry” (2 Timothy 4:11).

Mark was also close to Peter, who calls him “my son” in his first letter, sent from the city he names Babylon, which most understand as Rome (1 Peter 5:13). Early in the second century Papias, bishop of Hierapolis, wrote that Mark had been Peter's interpreter and had set down carefully what he remembered of Peter's preaching about the words and deeds of the Lord. Papias is quoted by the historian Eusebius. The Church has always received the Gospel according to Mark as inspired Scripture. It is the shortest of the four, and many scholars think it was the first to be written, around the time of Nero's persecution in Rome or soon after. It moves quickly, with the word “immediately” again and again, and it leads to the Cross, where a Roman centurion says, “Truly this man was God's Son!” (Mark 15:39).

An ancient tradition, recorded by Eusebius, says that Mark went to Egypt, preached the Gospel there and founded the Church of Alexandria. Later accounts say he died a martyr in that city. The Coptic Churches, Catholic and Orthodox, honour him as their founder and first bishop. In 828 merchants of Venice carried what they believed to be his relics from Alexandria to their city, where the Basilica of St Mark now stands. For Kerala too Alexandria is not a distant name: ships from that port traded with Muziris on our coast in the first centuries, and Eusebius tells that Pantaenus, a teacher of Alexandria, travelled to India to preach (some scholars think his India may have been Arabia or Ethiopia). His symbol is the lion, taken from the living creatures around the throne of God (Revelation 4:7).

Lessons

A second chance

The young man who turned back at Perga became the one Paul asked for at the end.

Serving another's word

Mark gave his gift to passing on Peter's preaching, not his own name.

Straight to the point

His Gospel calls us to follow Jesus now, without delay.

The way of the Cross

Mark shows that we know who Jesus is only at the foot of the Cross.

Stories

Peter at the door (history)

When Herod put Peter in prison, the Church prayed for him earnestly. An angel led him out, and he went to the house of Mary, the mother of John Mark, where many were praying. A servant girl named Rhoda knew his voice, but in her joy ran to tell the others instead of opening the gate, and they would not believe her (Acts 12:12–16).

The young man in the garden (tradition)

Only Mark tells of a young man in Gethsemane, wearing nothing but a linen cloth, who ran away naked when they tried to seize him (Mark 14:51–52). From early times many have thought that Mark was quietly signing his own Gospel with this memory, but the Gospel does not name him.

The lion of Venice (legend)

A medieval account says that the Venetian merchants who took the relics from Alexandria hid them under pork in a basket so that the port officials would not search it. That the relics were brought to Venice in 828 is history; the details of the story have the colour of legend.

Homily

1. The young man who turned back

A failure is not the end of a vocation (Acts 13:13).

2. Useful in the ministry

Paul and Mark were reconciled (2 Timothy 4:11).

3. The good news written down

Mark gave us the Gospel of the Cross (Mark 1:1).

Dear friends, we celebrate St Mark, whose Gospel we hear so often. It begins without any introduction: “The beginning of the good news of Jesus Christ, the Son of God” (Mark 1:1). No childhood stories, no long preface. Mark wants us to meet Jesus quickly and to decide whether we will follow him.

But Mark himself did not always follow quickly. As a young man he set out with Paul and Barnabas on a great mission, and at Perga he turned back and went home. Paul was so disappointed that he would not take him again. Many of us know that feeling: we began well in prayer, in a vocation, in a marriage or in a promise to God, and then we turned back. We think the door is closed.

Scripture shows us that it was not. Barnabas, whose name means son of encouragement, took Mark with him. Peter loved him as a son. And at the end of his life the same Paul wrote, “Get Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful in my ministry” (2 Timothy 4:11). The one who had failed became useful. God does not throw away those who turn back; he waits for them and gives them work to do.

Mark's Gospel leads us to the Cross, where a pagan soldier looks at Jesus dying and says, “Truly this man was God's Son!” (Mark 15:39). In these weeks of the Resurrection we rejoice that the Crucified one lives. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for the Gospel handed down to us through Mark and Peter, let us pray for the Churches of Egypt that honour him as their father, and let us ask for courage to begin again wherever we have turned back. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who gave St Mark the grace to proclaim the good news of your Son in writing, grant through his prayers that we may hear the Gospel with open hearts, rise again when we have failed, and follow Jesus faithfully on the way of the Cross to the joy of the Resurrection. Amen.
