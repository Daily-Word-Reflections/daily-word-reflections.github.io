Life

Anselm was born around 1033 at Aosta, in the Alps of northern Italy. His mother Ermenberga was devout, and his father Gundulf was a hard man. After his mother's death the young Anselm quarrelled with his father and left home. For some years he wandered through Burgundy and France, until he came to the abbey of Bec in Normandy, drawn by the fame of its prior and teacher, Lanfranc. In 1060 he became a monk there.

At Bec he became prior and then, in 1078, abbot. He was a gentle and patient teacher of the monks, and a man of deep prayer. There he wrote the Monologion and the Proslogion, in which he sought to understand by reason what he already believed by faith. His motto was faith seeking understanding. In the Proslogion he speaks of God as that than which nothing greater can be thought. He also wrote prayers and meditations, including tender prayers to the Blessed Virgin Mary, whom he called the mother of all things made new.

In 1093, against his will, he was made Archbishop of Canterbury. He stood firm for the liberty of the Church against King William II and later against King Henry I, and twice he was driven into exile. During the first exile he finished his great work Cur Deus Homo, Why God Became Man, which explains why the Son of God took our nature to make satisfaction for sin. In 1098, at the Council of Bari in Italy, he explained to the Greek bishops present the Latin teaching that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son.

After long struggles an agreement was reached with King Henry in 1107, and Anselm spent his last years in Canterbury. He died there on 21 April 1109, the Wednesday of Holy Week, and was buried in his cathedral. Pope Clement XI declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1720. He is often called the Father of Scholasticism, because he showed how faith and reason can work together in the study of God.

Stories

The crosier in his fist (history)

When King William II, believing himself near death, named Anselm archbishop in 1093, Anselm refused. The bishops present forced the pastoral staff against his tightly closed hand and carried him into a church, while he protested that they were yoking an old sheep to an untamed bull.

The hunted hare (tradition)

His biographer Eadmer tells that once, as Anselm was riding, a hare chased by hunting dogs ran under his horse for safety. Anselm stopped and wept, comparing the hare to a soul hunted by the devil, and he ordered that it be left alone.

Bread from the King's table (tradition)

As a small boy in the mountains, he dreamed that he climbed to the palace of God, who received him kindly and had him given the whitest bread. He believed ever after that he had been fed from heaven.

Homily

1. Give an account of hope

Be ready to explain the faith (1 Peter 3:15).

2. Seek his face

Prayer and study go together (Psalm 27:8).

3. Why God became man

Love led the Son to save us.

Dear friends, St Anselm was a monk who wanted nothing more than to pray and to teach his brothers. Yet he became Archbishop of Canterbury against his will, with the staff pressed into his closed fist. He spent years in exile for the rights of the Church. Through it all he remained what he had always been, a man on his knees before God.

Some people think that faith and thinking are enemies, as if believing means not asking questions. Anselm shows us the opposite. His motto was faith seeking understanding. Because he believed, he wanted to know God better. He began his greatest book with a prayer, asking God to teach him to seek. St Peter asks us to be ready to give an account of the hope that is in us (1 Peter 3:15). Our young people ask hard questions. We must not be afraid of them.

Anselm also asked why God became man. His answer was love: the Son took our nature so that a man who was also God could set right what sin had broken. When we think of that, our study turns to wonder, and our wonder turns to prayer. The psalm says, Your face, Lord, do I seek (Psalm 27:8). That was the desire of his whole life.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, where the same Christ comes to us, let us ask St Anselm for a faith that is not lazy, a mind that keeps learning and a heart that keeps praying. And let us ask the Blessed Virgin Mary, whom he loved so much, to bring us to her Son. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who led St Anselm to seek and to teach the depths of your wisdom, grant through his prayers that our faith may seek understanding and our understanding may deepen our love, so that we may come to see you face to face. Amen.
