Pope St Julius I

Pope, defender of St Athanasius and the faith of Nicaea · Feast: 12 April

Open in the Daily Word app

Life

Julius was a Roman, and he was elected Bishop of Rome in February 337, a few months before the death of the Emperor Constantine. He led the Church for fifteen years, until his death on 12 April 352. These were stormy years. The Council of Nicaea in 325 had confessed that the Son is of one being with the Father, true God from true God. But many bishops of the East, with the support of powerful men at court, worked to undo that teaching, and to remove the bishops who defended it.

The chief of these defenders was St Athanasius, Bishop of Alexandria. He was driven from his see more than once. In 339 he was expelled again and came to Rome, and with him other bishops who had been deposed. Julius received them, examined their case with care, and invited their accusers to come to Rome so that the matter could be judged in a council. They did not come. In 340 or 341 a synod of about fifty bishops met in Rome under Julius and declared Athanasius innocent and in communion with the Church.

Julius then wrote a long letter to the bishops who had gathered at Antioch, which Athanasius later preserved in one of his own works. It is firm and calm. He rebukes them for acting against bishops without consulting the other Churches, and he reminds them that in such cases the custom had been to write first to Rome, so that a just decision could be given from there. The letter is an important early witness to the role of the Bishop of Rome in caring for the unity of all the Churches.

In 343 a council was held at Serdica, today's Sofia in Bulgaria, called with the support of the emperors to settle the dispute. The Eastern bishops withdrew, but those who remained upheld Athanasius and recognised the right of bishops to appeal to the Bishop of Rome. Julius lived to see Athanasius return to Alexandria in 346. The Book of the Popes says that he built churches in Rome, among them a basilica across the Tiber, the forerunner of Santa Maria in Trastevere. He was buried on the Via Aurelia.

Stories

The exile at Rome (history)

Athanasius, driven from Alexandria, reached Rome and stayed there for about three years. Julius welcomed him, and the Roman Church came to know the great defender of Nicaea at first hand. Athanasius never forgot that welcome, and he preserved Julius's letter in his own defence.

The letter to Antioch (history)

When the Eastern bishops complained that Rome had no business reopening their judgment, Julius answered that if there had truly been wrongdoing, the matter should have been referred to all, and especially to Rome, and he asked them to settle the matter in peace and justice. His tone is fatherly, but he does not give way on the truth.

The date of Christmas (legend)

A late story says that Pope Julius fixed the birth of Christ on 25 December. A Roman list of feasts compiled in 354 shows that the Church of Rome was already keeping that date by 336, before he became pope, so the story cannot be relied on.

Words of the saint

Do you not know that the custom has been for word to be written first to us, and then for a just decision to be given from here?
Pope St Julius I, letter to the bishops at Antioch, preserved by St Athanasius, in substance

Homily

1. Strengthen your brothers

The ministry Jesus gave to Peter (Luke 22:32).

2. A refuge for the persecuted

Julius stood by Athanasius.

3. One with the Father

The faith we profess in the Creed.

Dear friends, today we remember Pope St Julius, a bishop of Rome in the fourth century. His name is not famous, but without him the story of the faith might have been very different. At the Last Supper Jesus told Peter, “I have prayed for you that your own faith may not fail; and you, when once you have turned back, strengthen your brothers” (Luke 22:32). Julius, the successor of Peter, did exactly that.

In his time many bishops and courtiers wanted to soften the faith of the Council of Nicaea, which had confessed that the Son is one in being with the Father. St Athanasius of Alexandria refused, and he was driven into exile again and again. When he came to Rome, alone and condemned, Julius did not send him away. He listened, he heard both sides, and with a council of bishops he declared him innocent.

Each time we stand to say the Creed we say that the Son is true God from true God, one in being with the Father. Those words were kept for us at a great cost, by people like Athanasius who suffered and people like Julius who stood by them. Faith is a gift handed down. We must guard it, and we must stand beside those who suffer for the truth instead of leaving them alone.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, we pray for our Holy Father the Pope, that God may keep him firm in faith and that he may strengthen his brothers and sisters. We pray for the unity of all Christians. And let us ask Pope St Julius to make us people who are firm and calm, faithful to the truth and kind to all. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who gave Pope St Julius to your Church to strengthen his brothers and to defend the true faith in your Son, grant through his prayers that we may hold fast to the faith of Nicaea, welcome those who suffer for the truth, and live in the unity of your one Church. Amen.
