St Charles Lwanga and Companions

Martyrs of Uganda · Feast: 3 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.” Matthew 5:10

Life

The Gospel reached the kingdom of Buganda, in what is now Uganda, in the late 1870s. Kabaka Mutesa I welcomed Anglican missionaries of the Church Missionary Society in 1877 and Catholic missionaries of the Society of Missionaries of Africa, called the White Fathers, in 1879, led by Fr Siméon Lourdel, whom the people called Mapeera. Many young men at the royal court listened eagerly. The pages of the king, boys and young men from leading families who served in the royal enclosure, learned to read, prayed together and asked for baptism. Some became catechumens of the Catholic mission and some of the Anglican mission.

In 1884 Mutesa died and his young son Mwanga II became king. At first he seemed friendly to the missions, but soon he grew afraid of foreign power and angry that Christian pages put the law of God above his commands, and in particular that they refused his immoral demands. In October 1885 he had the Anglican bishop James Hannington killed on his way into the country. Joseph Mukasa Balikuddembe, a Catholic who was head of the pages, rebuked the king for this murder and protected the boys in his care. On 15 November 1885 Mwanga had him beheaded and his body burned. He was the first Catholic martyr of Uganda.

Charles Lwanga, who was then about twenty-five, was baptized on the night of Joseph Mukasa's death and took his place in charge of the pages. He guarded them, taught them and kept them firm. In May 1886 the king learned that the pages were still praying and ordered all Christians at court to give up their faith. On the night before they were led away, Charles secretly baptized several catechumens, among them Kizito, the youngest, about fourteen years old. The next morning the king had the Christians separated from the others, and they declared that they would remain Christians until death. They were bound and marched to Namugongo, a place of execution some miles away.

On 3 June 1886, the feast of the Ascension, the prisoners at Namugongo were wrapped in reed mats and burned alive on a great pyre. Charles Lwanga was burned apart from the others, slowly, beginning with his feet. Anglican pages died with the Catholics, and others were killed in the months that followed. Twenty-two Catholic martyrs, who died between 1885 and 1887, were beatified by Pope Benedict XV in 1920 and canonized by Pope St Paul VI on 18 October 1964; in his homily he also remembered the Anglicans who died for the name of Christ. Pius XI named Charles Lwanga patron of African Catholic youth. Paul VI prayed at Namugongo in 1969, and every 3 June great crowds of pilgrims walk there from all over East Africa.

Stories

Baptism by night (history)

On the last night before the arrest, Charles Lwanga gathered the catechumens among the pages and baptized them, so that they would go to death as children of God. Among them was the boy Kizito, whom Charles had been preparing.

Water, not fire (tradition)

Tradition says that as the flames rose round his feet Charles told the executioner that it felt as if he were pouring water on him, and asked him to repent and become a Christian. His last word is remembered as Katonda, My God.

The march to Namugongo (history)

The prisoners were made to walk about sixteen miles to Namugongo. On the road some were killed, among them Pontian Ngondwe and Athanasius Bazzekuketta. The survivors waited several days in prison there, praying together, before they were burned.

Words of the saint

It is as if you are pouring water on me. Please repent and become a Christian like me.

These African martyrs add a new page to the roll of those whom the Church calls victorious, a page both tragic and glorious.

Homily

1. Blessed are the persecuted

The kingdom belongs to those who suffer for what is right (Matthew 5:10).

2. Glorify God in your body

You are not your own (1 Corinthians 6:19-20).

3. Acknowledged before the Father

Jesus stands by those who stand by him (Matthew 10:32).

Dear friends, today we honour young martyrs. Many of the Uganda Martyrs were teenagers. Kizito was about fourteen. They had heard the Gospel only a few years before, yet they understood it better than many who have heard it all their lives. When their king ordered them to give up Christ, they said simply that they would remain Christians until death.

Charles Lwanga was their elder brother in faith. When the king wanted to use the pages for sin, Charles stood between the boys and evil. Paul writes, Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, which you have from God, and that you are not your own? For you were bought with a price; therefore glorify God in your body (1 Corinthians 6:19-20). Today too, many children and young people are exposed to abuse and to pressure. Parents, teachers, priests and all of us must guard the little ones as Charles did, with courage and without counting the cost.

On their last night Charles baptized the catechumens. He knew what was coming, and his first care was that they should belong fully to Christ. Then they walked to Namugongo, praying and singing. Jesus had promised, Everyone therefore who acknowledges me before others, I also will acknowledge before my Father in heaven (Matthew 10:32). They acknowledged him in the fire, and he has acknowledged them before the whole Church.

We remember too that Anglican pages died beside them. The blood shed together at Namugongo calls all Christians to deeper unity. And the Church in Uganda, born from that blood, is today strong and full of life. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for the young people of our parish and our families, that they may love purity and stand firm in faith. Let us ask St Charles Lwanga and his companions to give us their courage and their joy. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who made the blood of martyrs the seed of Christians, grant that, as St Charles Lwanga and his companions remained faithful to Christ even in the fire, so we, through their prayers, may keep our hearts pure, protect the young, stand firm in persecution and bear witness to the Gospel with joy. Through our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.
