St Sophia

Holy woman of the early Church (identity uncertain) · Feast: 25 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy and good fruits, without a trace of partiality or hypocrisy.” James 3:17

Life

The Malankara Panchangam remembers St Sophia on 25 May, but it does not say which holy woman of that name is meant, and we have not found a source that settles it. Several saints named Sophia are honoured in the Churches of East and West. This page therefore says plainly what is known and what is not, rather than guessing. If the Church's own calendar notes give a fuller identification, this page will be corrected.

The best known of them is St Sophia, mother of three daughters named Pistis, Elpis and Agape, that is, Faith, Hope and Love. Her story was told from early times in Rome and throughout the East. According to the account of their martyrdom, Sophia was a Christian widow who came to Rome with her young daughters in the second century, perhaps in the reign of the emperor Hadrian. The girls refused to sacrifice to the gods and were put to death one after another, while their mother encouraged them. Sophia buried them and died beside their grave three days later. The Byzantine Churches keep their memory on 17 September.

This account was written long after the events and has the marks of a story meant to teach, for the names of the daughters are the three great virtues and their mother's name means Wisdom. Historians therefore treat its details as legend, though the memory of martyrs behind it may be real. Other holy women named Sophia include a virgin martyr honoured at Fermo in Italy and a Sophia of Thrace honoured in the Greek Church as a mother who cared for orphans. Any of them could be the saint of this day, and it is possible that the Malankara calendar follows a Syriac tradition that is not well known outside it.

Whoever she was, her name points us to Christ, whom St Paul calls the power of God and the wisdom of God (1 Corinthians 1:24). Sophia is the Greek word for wisdom, and the greatest church of the Christian East, the Hagia Sophia in Constantinople, was dedicated not to a woman but to Christ, the Holy Wisdom of God. On her day the Church invites us to ask for the wisdom that comes from above.

Stories

Faith, Hope and Love (legend)

In the old account of their martyrdom, the three daughters of Sophia were questioned one by one by the emperor. Each refused to deny Christ and was put to death. Their mother stood by them, strengthening them to remain faithful to the end.

The mother at the grave (legend)

The same account tells that Sophia gathered the bodies of her daughters and buried them outside Rome. She stayed by their grave praying, and after three days she died there in peace and was buried with them.

Relics in Alsace (tradition)

In the eighth century, relics said to be those of St Sophia were brought from Rome to the abbey of Eschau near Strasbourg, where she is still venerated with her daughters.

Dear friends, today the Panchangam remembers St Sophia. We must be honest: the calendar does not tell us which Sophia is meant, and there are several holy women of that name. The best known is the mother of three martyred daughters, Faith, Hope and Love, whose story has been told for many centuries, though its details are legend. What we can be sure of is the meaning of her name. Sophia means wisdom.

St James writes, But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of mercy and good fruits, without a trace of partiality or hypocrisy (James 3:17). That is a different wisdom from the cleverness of the world. The world's wisdom asks, How can I get ahead? The wisdom from above asks, How can I make peace? How can I show mercy?

The story of Sophia and her daughters is a story about mothers. A wise mother does not only give her children food and education. She gives them faith, hope and love. Many of us received our faith first from our mothers and grandmothers, at the family prayer in the evening. That is wisdom from above, passed from one heart to another.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask the Lord for the wisdom that is gentle and full of mercy. Let us pray for mothers and grandmothers who hand on the faith. And let us ask all the holy women named Sophia, known and unknown, to pray for us. Amen.

Prayer

Lord God, source of all wisdom, the Church remembers today your servant St Sophia. Grant us the wisdom that comes from above, pure, peaceable and full of mercy. Through the prayers of all your holy women, help mothers to raise their children in faith, hope and love, and lead us all to Christ, your Wisdom made flesh. Amen.
