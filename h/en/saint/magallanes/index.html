St Christopher Magallanes and Companions

Priest and martyrs of Mexico · Feast: 21 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you” Matthew 5:44

Life

Christopher Magallanes Jara was born on 30 July 1869 at Totatiche, in the state of Jalisco in Mexico, into a poor farming family. As a boy he worked as a shepherd in the fields. He entered the seminary of Guadalajara at the age of nineteen and was ordained a priest in 1899. In 1909 he became parish priest of his home town, Totatiche, and served there for the rest of his life.

He was a shepherd in every sense. He opened schools and a small seminary for the boys of the region, encouraged the farmers to work together, and helped to bring water and work to poor villages. He had a special love for the Huichol people, the indigenous people of the mountains, and helped to found a mission among them. Many priests were formed in the seminary he ran, even when it had to move from house to house in secret.

In the 1920s the government of Mexico enforced harsh laws against the Church. From 1926 public worship was suspended, churches were closed and priests were hunted. Some Catholics, known as the Cristeros, took up arms, and the country was torn by a bloody civil war. Father Christopher did not join the armed rising and wrote against violence, but he would not abandon his people. He went on celebrating Mass and the sacraments in hiding and kept his seminary alive.

On 21 May 1927 he was arrested on his way to celebrate Mass at a farm. A young priest of his seminary, Father Agustín Caloca, was arrested too. Without any trial, they were taken to Colotlán and shot on 25 May 1927. Before he died he gave away what little he had to the soldiers, forgave those who were killing him and prayed that his blood might serve the peace of Mexico. Pope St John Paul II beatified him in 1992 and canonised him on 21 May 2000 together with twenty-four companions, most of them diocesan priests and a few young laymen, all martyred in those years.

Lessons

A shepherd who stays

He would not leave his people even when it cost him his life.

Peace, not violence

He rejected the way of arms and forgave his killers.

Forming priests

Stories

The last words (history)

Witnesses reported that before the firing squad he said that he was innocent, that he forgave with all his heart those who were responsible for his death, and that he asked God to let his blood serve the peace of a divided Mexico.

Only a moment (tradition)

When young Father Agustín Caloca was afraid, Father Christopher is reported to have comforted him, saying that it would be only a moment and then heaven. The young priest became calm, and both died together.

The seal of confession (tradition)

Among his companions was Father Mateo Correa Magallanes. Accounts say that after he had heard the confessions of prisoners, he was ordered to reveal what they had told him. He refused and was shot in 1927. He is honoured as a martyr of the seal of confession.

Words of the saint

I die innocent. I forgive with all my heart those responsible for my death, and I ask God that the shedding of my blood may serve the peace of our divided Mexico.
St Christopher Magallanes, his last words, in substance
“No one has greater love than this, to lay down one's life for one's friends.”

Homily

1. Love your enemies

He forgave those who killed him (Matthew 5:44).

2. The good shepherd stays

He would not abandon his flock.

3. Blood for peace

He offered his death for a divided nation.

Dear friends, St Christopher Magallanes was a country priest in Mexico. He grew up herding animals in the fields, and he spent almost all his priestly life in his own home town. He built schools, helped farmers, cared for the indigenous people of the mountains and trained young men to be priests. He was not a man who looked for trouble.

Then came the years of persecution, when the laws of his country closed the churches and made it a crime to be a priest in public. Some Catholics took up arms, and blood was shed on both sides. Father Christopher chose another way. He did not take up arms, but he did not run away either. He kept celebrating Mass and the sacraments in secret, because his people needed them.

When he was arrested and taken to be shot, without any trial, he did not curse his killers. He forgave them with all his heart and prayed that his blood might bring peace to his divided country. This is the Gospel lived to the end: But I say to you, Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you (Matthew 5:44). It is easy to love those who love us. It takes the grace of Christ to pray for those who kill us.

There are Christians today in many countries who cannot worship openly, and priests who stay with their people at great risk. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for the persecuted Church, for the people of Mexico, and for all countries divided by hatred. Let us ask St Christopher Magallanes and his companions to help us forgive those who have hurt us and to work for peace without violence. Amen.

Prayer

Almighty God, who made St Christopher Magallanes and his companions faithful to Christ the King even to the shedding of their blood, grant through their prayers that we may stay close to your people in every trial, forgive those who wrong us, and work for peace in our land. Amen.
