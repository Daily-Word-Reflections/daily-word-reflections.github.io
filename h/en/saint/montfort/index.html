St Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort

Priest, missionary, founder, apostle of devotion to Jesus through Mary · Feast: 28 April

Life

Louis Marie Grignion was born on 31 January 1673 at Montfort-la-Cane, now Montfort-sur-Meu, in Brittany, the eldest son of a large family. He later took the name of his baptismal town instead of his family name. He studied with the Jesuits at Rennes, where his love for the Blessed Virgin Mary and for the poor grew strong. In 1693 he went on foot to Paris to study for the priesthood, and he was ordained on 5 June 1700.

He served as chaplain in the poorhouse hospital of Poitiers, where with Marie-Louise Trichet he began the Daughters of Wisdom, a congregation for the care of the sick and the teaching of poor children. His zeal and his unusual ways brought him opposition, and some bishops forbade him to preach. In 1706 he walked to Rome to ask Pope Clement XI whether he should go to the foreign missions. The Pope told him to work in France and named him an apostolic missionary.

For the last ten years of his life he preached parish missions in Brittany and western France. He renewed baptismal promises with the people, taught them to pray the Rosary, wrote simple hymns for them to sing and set up great crosses. He founded the Company of Mary, a community of missionary priests. He wrote The Love of Eternal Wisdom, The Secret of Mary, The Secret of the Rosary and his best known work, the Treatise on True Devotion to the Blessed Virgin. The treatise lay hidden for more than a century and was found and published in the nineteenth century.

He taught consecration to Jesus Christ, Eternal Wisdom, through the hands of Mary. This is not worship of Mary, who is a creature, but a renewal of our baptismal promises, in which we give ourselves wholly to Christ and ask his Mother to help us live as his disciples. He insisted that all true devotion to Mary leads to Jesus, and that devotion which turned us away from him would be false. Worn out by his labours, he died at Saint-Laurent-sur-Sèvre on 28 April 1716, aged forty-three. Pope Pius XII canonised him in 1947. St John Paul II took his motto, Totus tuus, from Montfort's teaching.

Stories

The coat on the road (tradition)

Tradition tells that on his walk to Paris in 1693 he gave his money to the poor and exchanged his good clothes with a beggar, arriving at the seminary in rags.

The calvary at Pontchâteau (history)

In 1709 and 1710, with thousands of local people, he built a great calvary on a hill at Pontchâteau. Just before its blessing, a royal order commanded that it be pulled down. He accepted the decision in obedience and went on with his missions. The calvary was later rebuilt.

The hidden book (history)

In his treatise he wrote that he foresaw that the book might be hidden in the silence of a chest so that it would not appear. It was in fact found in a chest in 1842, more than a hundred years after his death.

Homily

1. Behold your mother

Jesus gave his Mother to the disciple (John 19:27).

2. All for Christ

Consecration renews our baptism.

3. God alone

Every devotion ends in God.

Dear friends, St Louis de Montfort is known to many of us through the prayer of consecration to Jesus through Mary. Some people misunderstand it. They think it puts Mary in the place of God. Montfort said the opposite, very clearly: if devotion to Mary drew us away from Jesus, it would be false and should be rejected. His motto was God alone.

What then is this consecration? It is a renewal of the promises of our baptism. At baptism we were given to Christ. We renounced sin and Satan and promised to follow him. Montfort teaches us to make that gift again, completely, and to ask Mary, the Mother of Jesus, to help us keep it. She is a creature, wholly God's, and she has no wish except that we do whatever her Son tells us (John 2:5).

From the Cross Jesus said to the disciple, Here is your mother, and the disciple took her into his own home (John 19:27). Montfort did just that. He took Mary into his life, and she made him a missionary of her Son. He walked the roads of France, poor and often rejected, teaching people to pray, to sing, to love the Cross and to live their baptism.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, where Christ gives himself to us wholly, let us give ourselves wholly to him. Let us renew our baptismal promises in our hearts. And let us ask St Louis de Montfort, and the Mother whom he loved, to lead us always to Jesus. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who guided the steps of St Louis Marie Grignion de Montfort on the way of salvation and of the love of Christ, in company with the Blessed Virgin, grant through his prayers that we may give ourselves wholly to your Son, and through his Mother be ever more faithful to the promises of our baptism. Amen.
