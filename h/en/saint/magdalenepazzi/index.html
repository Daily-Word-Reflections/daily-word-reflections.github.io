St Mary Magdalene de' Pazzi

Virgin, Carmelite nun and mystic · Feast: 25 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“As the Father has loved me, so I have loved you; abide in my love.” John 15:9

Life

Caterina Lucrezia de' Pazzi was born in Florence on 2 April 1566, into one of the leading noble families of the city. She was a thoughtful and prayerful child. She received her first Communion at the age of ten, and soon afterwards, according to her biographers, made a private vow of virginity. She was educated for some time by the nuns of a Florentine convent, and her parents, who had other plans for her, at last gave way to her wish to enter religious life.

In 1582 she entered the Carmelite monastery of Santa Maria degli Angeli in Florence and took the name Maria Maddalena. Soon after, she fell seriously ill, and in May 1584 she was allowed to make her profession early, lying on a bed in the chapel because it was feared she would die. She recovered, and in the following weeks she passed through a long series of ecstasies. Her sisters, at the direction of her confessor, took turns to write down the words she spoke in prayer. These records, filling several volumes, are a treasury of her teaching on the love of God, the Blood of Christ, the Holy Spirit and the renewal of the Church.

From 1585 to 1590 she passed through five years of deep spiritual trial, which she called her time of probation, when prayer brought her darkness, temptation and the sense that God had left her. She held on in faith and obedience. In 1586, during her prayer, she dictated letters to the Pope, to cardinals and to bishops, urging them to work for the reform and holiness of the Church. She was later mistress of novices and of the young professed sisters, and she guided them with great tenderness and good sense, though she was demanding about the spirit of their life.

Her last years were marked by long and painful illness, which she accepted as a sharing in the sufferings of Christ for the Church. She died on 25 May 1607, aged forty-one. Her body, found incorrupt, is venerated in the Carmelite monastery at Careggi, near Florence. Pope Urban VIII beatified her in 1626 and Pope Clement IX canonised her in 1669. She is honoured as one of the great mystics of the Carmelite Order.

Stories

Love is not loved (tradition)

Her sisters recalled that at times, filled with the thought of God's love, she would run through the cloister ringing a bell or calling out that Love is not loved, Love is not known. She wanted every soul to know how much God loves.

Profession on a sickbed (history)

When it seemed in 1584 that the young novice would die, the community allowed her to make her vows early. She was carried into the chapel on a bed and professed there. Soon afterwards the ecstasies began that her sisters recorded.

The secretaries (history)

Because she spoke in ecstasy and could not later remember her words, the sisters took them down as quickly as they could, sometimes several writing at once, and then compared notes. Thanks to them we can still read her prayer.

Homily

1. Abide in my love

Love received must be returned (John 15:9).

2. Faith in darkness

Five years of trial did not break her.

3. For the Church

Her prayer and pain were offered for renewal.

Dear friends, Jesus says, “As the Father has loved me, so I have loved you; abide in my love” (John 15:9). St Mary Magdalene de' Pazzi spent her whole life trying to abide in that love and to make it known. Her sisters remembered how she would cry out in the cloister that Love is not loved. She was grieved that people go through life without knowing how much God loves them.

She was born into wealth and could have had every comfort. She chose the hidden life of a Carmelite nun. There God gave her great graces. In prayer she spoke of the Blood of Christ, of the Holy Spirit, of the beauty of the soul in grace. Her sisters wrote down her words so that we can still learn from them.

But she also knew the dark night. For five years prayer brought her no light and no comfort, only temptation and dryness. She did not give up. Many of us know times when prayer feels empty and God seems far away. Mary Magdalene teaches us to stay, to keep praying, to keep loving, even when we feel nothing. Love is shown most truly in fidelity.

She loved the Church deeply and offered her illness for its renewal. She did not ask to escape suffering but to offer it with Christ. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for his love, let us pray for those who are passing through darkness in prayer or in life, and let us ask for the grace to abide in his love all our days. Amen.

Prayer

O God, lover of virginity, who set St Mary Magdalene de' Pazzi on fire with love of you, grant through her prayers that we may return your love with all our heart, remain faithful in times of darkness, and offer our sufferings for the holiness of your Church. Amen.
