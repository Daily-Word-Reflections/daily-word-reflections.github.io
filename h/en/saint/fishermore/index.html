St John Fisher and St Thomas More

Martyrs: a bishop and a lay statesman · Feast: 22 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“We must obey God rather than any human authority.” Acts 5:29

Life

John Fisher was born at Beverley in Yorkshire in 1469 and studied at Cambridge, where he became a priest, a teacher and later chancellor of the university. He was chaplain and confessor to Lady Margaret Beaufort, mother of King Henry VII, and with her help founded colleges and encouraged the study of Greek and the Scriptures. In 1504 he was made bishop of Rochester, the poorest diocese in England, and he stayed there for over thirty years, visiting his parishes, preaching often, caring for the poor and writing in defence of the Catholic faith.

Thomas More was born in London in 1478. He trained as a lawyer, and for a time he thought of becoming a Carthusian monk, but discerned that God was calling him to marriage and public life. He was a devoted husband and father, who gave his daughters as good an education as his son, a rare thing in his day. A friend of the scholar Erasmus, he wrote the book Utopia. He served King Henry VIII in many offices and in 1529 became Lord Chancellor of England, the highest office of state after the king.

When Henry sought to have his marriage to Queen Catherine of Aragon declared null, Fisher defended the marriage openly. In 1534 Parliament passed laws requiring an oath that accepted the new succession and rejected the authority of the Pope, and later declared the king Supreme Head of the Church in England. More had already resigned as Chancellor in 1532. Both men were willing to accept Parliament's right to settle the succession, but neither would swear to an oath that denied the Pope's authority and the validity of the first marriage. In April 1534 they were sent to the Tower of London.

In May 1535 Pope Paul III named Fisher a cardinal. A few weeks later the bishop, old and sick, was tried and condemned, and on 22 June 1535 he was beheaded on Tower Hill. Thomas More was tried in Westminster Hall on 1 July and condemned on false testimony; after the sentence he spoke openly in defence of the unity of the Church. He was beheaded on 6 July 1535, saying that he died the king's good servant, but God's first. Pope Pius XI canonized both martyrs on 19 May 1935, and in 2000 Pope St John Paul II named Thomas More patron of statesmen and politicians.

Lessons

Conscience formed by truth

They followed a conscience shaped by the faith of the Church, not by convenience.

Faithful to the unity of the Church

They gave their lives rather than break communion with the successor of Peter.

Holiness in every vocation

A bishop and a married layman reached the same crown by different roads.

Courage with gentleness

They faced death without bitterness, praying for their judges and their king.

Stories

The New Testament on the scaffold (tradition)

According to an early account of his death, as Fisher walked to the scaffold he opened his New Testament and prayed that the first passage his eyes fell upon would comfort him. It opened at the words of Jesus in John 17:3, that eternal life is to know the only true God and Jesus Christ whom he has sent. He closed the book, saying that this was learning enough for him to the end of his life.

Letters from the Tower (history)

During fifteen months in the Tower, Thomas More wrote letters to his daughter Margaret Roper and a Dialogue of Comfort against Tribulation, a meditation on suffering and trust in God. In one letter he told her that nothing can come but what God wills, and that whatever he wills, however bad it may seem, is truly the best. These writings survive and show his peace of soul.

A jest at the block (tradition)

Early biographers record that More kept his humour to the end. Climbing the shaky scaffold, he asked the lieutenant to help him up and said he would shift for himself coming down. At the block he moved his beard aside, remarking that it at least had committed no treason. His cheerfulness was not lightness but the peace of a man ready to meet God.

I die the king's good servant, but God's first.

Homily

1. Obey God first

Conscience must answer to God above all (Acts 5:29).

2. Faithful to the one Church

The Lord prays that his disciples may be one (John 17:21).

3. Peace in suffering

Nothing can separate us from the love of God (Romans 8:38–39).

Dear friends, today we honour two Englishmen who died within two weeks of each other in the summer of 1535: John Fisher, the bishop of Rochester, and Thomas More, a lawyer, husband and father who had been Lord Chancellor. One was a priest and scholar, the other a statesman and family man. Yet they were united in one faith and one witness. Together they show that holiness is for every vocation, in the sanctuary and in the market place, in the bishop's house and in the family home.

When the apostles were ordered to stop preaching, Peter answered, We must obey God rather than any human authority (Acts 5:29). Fisher and More were loyal subjects. They loved their country and served their king. But when the king demanded an oath that went against their conscience and against the unity of the Church, they could not swear. Their conscience was not a private opinion. It was formed by prayer, by Scripture and by the teaching of the Church. This is a lesson for us, who are often tempted to follow the crowd or the easy path.

Both men faced their trials with remarkable peace. Fisher was old and ill, yet he walked to the scaffold reading the Gospel. More wrote from prison to his daughter that nothing can come but what God wills, and that what God wills is truly the best. They did not hate their enemies. More even hoped to meet his judges merrily in heaven. Their courage came from deep trust in God, built up through years of daily prayer and the sacraments.

Today many Christians in public life, in offices, schools and politics, face pressures to keep silent about their faith. Fathers and mothers must also stand firm for what is right in their homes. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St John Fisher and St Thomas More to give us consciences formed by truth, courage joined with gentleness, and a love for the Church that does not waver in the hour of trial. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who in martyrdom have brought true faith to its highest expression, graciously grant that, strengthened through the intercession of St John Fisher and St Thomas More, we may confirm by the witness of our life that faith which we profess with our lips, and remain always faithful to your Church. Amen.
