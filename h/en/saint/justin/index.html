St Justin Martyr

Martyr, philosopher and defender of the faith · Feast: 1 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Always be ready to make your defense to anyone who demands from you an accounting for the hope that is in you” 1 Peter 3:15

Life

Justin was born about the year 100 at Flavia Neapolis, the city built by the Romans near ancient Shechem in Samaria, where Nablus stands today. His family was pagan, of Greek or Roman stock. He tells us himself that as a young man he wanted above all to know the truth about God. He went from one teacher to another: a Stoic who did not think knowledge of God necessary, a Peripatetic who asked for his fee at once, a Pythagorean who wanted him first to master music, geometry and astronomy. At last he found the Platonists and was delighted, because they spoke of the invisible world and of the vision of God.

In his Dialogue with Trypho, Justin describes how his life changed. Walking alone near the sea, he met an old man who questioned him gently about his ideas and showed him that the philosophers could not give what they promised. The old man spoke to him of the prophets, who were more ancient than all the philosophers, who spoke by the Spirit of God and who announced the coming of Christ. Then he told Justin to pray that the gates of light might be opened to him. Justin writes that a fire was kindled in his soul and a love for the prophets and for the friends of Christ. He also says that the courage of Christians in the face of death had convinced him that they could not be living in wickedness.

Justin became a Christian, probably about the year 130, but he kept the cloak of a philosopher and went on teaching. He came to Rome and opened a school there. About the year 155 he addressed his First Apology to the emperor Antoninus Pius and his sons, asking that Christians should not be condemned for a name alone but judged by their lives. In it he gives the earliest full description of the Christian Sunday: the reading of the memoirs of the apostles and the writings of the prophets, the homily of the one who presides, the prayers, the bread and the wine mixed with water over which thanksgiving is offered, and the gifts for widows, orphans and all in need. He says plainly that this food is not common bread and common drink but the flesh and blood of the incarnate Jesus. He also wrote a Second Apology and the Dialogue with Trypho, a long conversation with a Jewish teacher about the Scriptures, carried on with respect on both sides.

Justin taught that the Word of God, who became man in Jesus Christ, had already sown seeds of truth in every people, so that whatever was rightly said by anyone belongs to Christians. About the year 165, under the emperor Marcus Aurelius, he was arrested in Rome with six companions, among them Chariton, Charito, Euelpistus, Hierax, Paeon and Liberian. The account of their trial before the prefect Junius Rusticus has come down to us. They refused to offer sacrifice to the gods, were scourged and beheaded. The Church honours him as Justin Martyr, the first great Christian philosopher, and his name became part of his title.

Lessons

Seeking the truth

He never stopped searching until he found Christ, the truth in person.

Faith and reason together

He showed that faith does not fear honest thinking.

The Sunday Eucharist

He left us the oldest full picture of the Sunday gathering of Christians.

Courage to the end

He defended the faith in writing and then sealed it with his blood.

Stories

The old man by the sea (history)

Justin himself tells how an old man he met by the sea led him from the philosophers to the prophets and to Christ, and told him to pray for the light of understanding. Some scholars think he shaped the scene as a teacher would, but it is his own account of his conversion.

A letter to the emperor (history)

Justin wrote openly to the emperor Antoninus Pius and his sons, giving his name and city. He explained what Christians believe and how they worship, and asked only for a fair trial. It was a bold act for a man who knew that confessing Christ could cost his life.

Before the prefect (history)

The Acts of his martyrdom record that the prefect Rusticus asked Justin whether he really thought he would go up to heaven after being beheaded. Justin answered that he did not merely think it, but knew it and was fully assured. The words come from an early record of the trial, which has reached us in more than one form.

Words of the saint

Whatever things were rightly said among all men are the property of us Christians.
St Justin, Second Apology 13, in substance
You can kill us, but you cannot do us any real harm.
St Justin, First Apology 2, in substance

Homily

1. A seeker who found

Christ is the true light that enlightens everyone (John 1:9).

2. Ready to give an answer

We must account for the hope that is in us (1 Peter 3:15).

3. The Sunday gathering

The first Christians devoted themselves to the breaking of bread (Acts 2:42).

Dear friends, St Justin was a young man with a restless mind. He went from one school of philosophy to another because he wanted to know God. Some teachers disappointed him, some asked for money, some gave him only clever words. He did not give up. Many young people today are also searching for meaning, on the internet, in books, in friendships. Justin tells them, and us, that the search is good, and that it leads at last to a person, Jesus Christ, the true light, which enlightens everyone (John 1:9).

When Justin found Christ he did not stop thinking. He kept his philosopher's cloak and opened a school in Rome. He wrote to the emperor himself to explain the faith. Peter tells us, Always be ready to make your defense to anyone who demands from you an accounting for the hope that is in you (1 Peter 3:15). Our neighbours, colleagues and children ask us questions about God, about the Church, about suffering. We should not be afraid of their questions. Like Justin, we can answer with knowledge, with gentleness and with respect.

Justin also left us a precious picture of Sunday in the early Church. The Christians gathered, listened to the prophets and the apostles, heard the homily, prayed together, offered bread and wine, and received the body and blood of the Lord. Then they shared with the widows, the orphans and the poor. That is what we are doing now. The first Christians devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers (Acts 2:42). Our Sunday is not a habit but a treasure handed down from the martyrs.

In the end Justin was arrested with his companions and asked to sacrifice to the gods. He refused calmly, and he was beheaded. His reasons were good, but it was his love that made him a martyr. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St Justin to give us a mind that seeks the truth, a heart that loves the Lord, and the courage to bear witness to Christ in our homes and in our work. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who taught St Justin the Martyr to find in the folly of the Cross the surpassing knowledge of Jesus Christ, grant through his prayers that we may seek the truth with an honest heart, give a reason for the hope that is in us, gather faithfully for the Holy Eucharist on the Lord's day, and remain firm in the faith even in times of trial. Amen.
