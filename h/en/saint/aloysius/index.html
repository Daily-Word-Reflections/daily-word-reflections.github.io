St Aloysius Gonzaga

Religious of the Society of Jesus, patron of youth · Feast: 21 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God.” Matthew 5:8

Life

Aloysius (in Italian Luigi) Gonzaga was born on 9 March 1568 at Castiglione delle Stiviere in Lombardy, the eldest son of Ferrante Gonzaga, Marquis of Castiglione, of one of the great princely families of Italy. His father wanted him to be a soldier and a ruler, and took the small boy to military camps, where he learned the rough speech of the soldiers. His mother Marta, a devout woman, taught him to pray. As a boy he was sent with his brother to the court of the Medici in Florence, and there, by his own later account, he made a private promise to God to keep his chastity.

In 1580, at the age of twelve, he received his First Holy Communion from the archbishop of Milan, St Charles Borromeo, who was visiting the region. From 1581 he served as a page at the royal court of Spain in Madrid. Amid the splendour and intrigue of the court he grew more and more drawn to prayer, and he resolved to give up his rights and become a religious of the Society of Jesus. His father opposed him fiercely for several years, but at last gave way.

In November 1585 Aloysius signed over his inheritance to his younger brother Rodolfo and entered the Jesuit novitiate of Sant'Andrea in Rome. He studied philosophy and theology at the Roman College, and St Robert Bellarmine was for a time his spiritual guide. His superiors had to moderate his penances and his long hours of prayer; he obeyed, and learned that holiness lies more in doing God's will than in great austerities.

In 1591 famine and plague struck Rome. The Jesuits opened a hospital, and Aloysius begged in the streets for the sick and served them with his own hands, washing them and making their beds. He caught the disease and, after weeks of illness, died on 21 June 1591, aged twenty three, with the name of Jesus on his lips. Pope Benedict XIII canonized him in 1726 and in 1729 named him patron of young students, and Pope Pius XI in 1926 named him patron of Christian youth. His relics rest in the church of Sant'Ignazio in Rome.

Lessons

Purity of heart

He kept his heart undivided for God amid the pleasures of a royal court.

Leaving all for Christ

He gave up a title and a fortune to follow the Lord in religious life.

Obedience over self-will

He learned to accept the moderation of his superiors rather than choose his own penances.

Love that serves the sick

He gave his life caring for plague victims in the streets of Rome.

Stories

First Communion from a saint (history)

In the summer of 1580 St Charles Borromeo, visiting the diocese of Brescia, met the young Aloysius at Castiglione, spoke with him at length and gave him his First Holy Communion. The meeting of the two saints is recorded by the early biographers.

The sick man on his shoulders (history)

During the plague of 1591 Aloysius found a dying man lying in the street, lifted him onto his shoulders and carried him to the hospital, where he cared for him. Soon afterwards he himself fell ill. His biographers record this as the act of mercy that cost him his life.

If you were to die within the hour (legend)

A well loved story tells that while Aloysius was playing a game with other young men, someone asked what each would do if he knew he would die within the hour. Others spoke of running to church or to confession; Aloysius answered that he would go on playing, because he was doing what was asked of him. The story is old and much repeated, though it is not certain that it comes from his own life.

Homily

1. A pure heart sees God

Purity is a heart given wholly to God (Matthew 5:8).

2. Leaving all to follow

Aloysius chose the treasure that does not fail (Matthew 19:21).

3. Love that lays down life

He served the dying and died serving (John 15:13).

Dear friends, today we honour St Aloysius Gonzaga, a young man who died at twenty three. He was born to wealth and power. His father dreamed of making him a soldier and a prince. Yet from boyhood Aloysius heard another call. In the courts of Florence and Madrid, where many lived for pleasure and position, he kept his heart for God. He shows our young people that holiness is possible in every age, even in the middle of a world that pulls in the opposite direction.

The Lord said, Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God (Matthew 5:8). Purity of heart is more than avoiding sin. It is a heart that is not divided, a heart that wants God above all else. Aloysius guarded his eyes, his words and his friendships so that nothing would come between him and the Lord. Today, when screens and images crowd into our homes, his example is very timely. Parents and young people can ask his help to keep their hearts clean and their love true.

Aloysius also learned obedience. He wanted to fast more and pray longer than his superiors allowed. He submitted, and discovered that doing God's will in small daily duties is greater than the penances we choose for ourselves. Holiness is not a show of strength but a surrender of the will. This is a lesson for all of us, young and old, who sometimes want to serve God in our own way rather than in his.

Finally, Aloysius died of love. When plague struck Rome, he carried the sick on his shoulders and nursed them until he caught the disease himself. The prince who gave up a castle found his true dignity at the bedside of the poor. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St Aloysius to protect our children and young people, to give them pure hearts and generous courage, and to teach us all to love the sick as Christ loves them. Amen.

Prayer

O God, giver of heavenly gifts, who joined in St Aloysius Gonzaga a wonderful innocence of life with the spirit of penance, grant through his merits and prayers that, if we have not followed him in innocence, we may follow him in penance, and serve you with a pure heart in love for the sick and the poor. Amen.
