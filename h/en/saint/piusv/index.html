St Pius V

Pope, Dominican friar · Feast: 30 April

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Proclaim the message; be persistent whether the time is favorable or unfavorable; convince, rebuke, and encourage, with the utmost patience in teaching.” 2 Timothy 4:2

Life

Antonio Ghislieri was born on 17 January 1504 at Bosco, near Alessandria in northern Italy, into a poor family, and as a boy he looked after sheep. At fourteen he entered the Dominican Order, taking the name Michele. He was ordained a priest in 1528 and taught philosophy and theology for many years. He lived the strict life of a friar, fasting, praying and travelling on foot.

He served the Church as an inquisitor in northern Italy and then in Rome, and became a bishop and, in 1557, a cardinal. He was firm and severe in defending the faith, and some of the measures of the Holy Office in his time, shaped by the harsh customs of that age, are not ones the Church would take today. Yet even his opponents admired his personal poverty, his prayer and his honesty. On 7 January 1566 he was elected Pope and took the name Pius.

His great task was to put into practice the decrees of the Council of Trent. In 1566 he published the Roman Catechism for parish priests, in 1568 the reformed Roman Breviary and in 1570 the Roman Missal, which shaped the celebration of Mass in the Latin Church for four hundred years. In 1567 he declared St Thomas Aquinas a Doctor of the Church. He reformed the papal court, insisted that bishops live in their dioceses, cared for the poor of Rome and continued as Pope to live the life of a friar.

In 1571, as the Ottoman fleet advanced in the Mediterranean and Cyprus fell, he formed a Holy League with Spain and Venice. On 7 October 1571 the fleets met at Lepanto, off the coast of Greece. Many thousands died on both sides, and many Christian galley slaves were released. The Pope had asked the faithful to pray the Rosary, and he gave thanks to God and to Mary for the outcome, establishing a feast later called Our Lady of the Rosary. He died in Rome on 1 May 1572. In 1588 his body was laid in a tomb in the basilica of St Mary Major, and he was canonised by Pope Clement XI in 1712.

Lessons

Holiness in high office

As Pope he still prayed, fasted and lived simply.

Renewal by teaching

Catechism, Missal and Breviary fed the faith of the people.

Trust in prayer

In danger he turned the whole Church to prayer.

Humble before history

We honour his holiness without copying every measure of his age.

Stories

The white habit (tradition)

As Pope he kept wearing the white habit of the Dominicans. It is often said that this is why Popes since then have dressed in white.

The open window (tradition)

Tradition tells that on 7 October 1571, while he was at work with his cardinals, he suddenly rose, went to a window and, after a time of silence, told them to stop and thank God, because the Christian fleet had prevailed. News of the battle reached Rome days later.

The crucifix (legend)

A legend tells that enemies put poison on the feet of the crucifix that he used to kiss, and that when he bent to kiss it, the figure of Christ drew back its feet.

Homily

1. Proclaim the message

Whether the time is favorable or unfavorable (2 Timothy 4:2).

2. A friar on the throne

Office did not change his prayer and poverty.

3. The power of prayer

He turned the Church to the Rosary.

Dear friends, St Pius V was a shepherd boy who became a Dominican friar and then Pope. When he was elected, he did not stop being a friar. He kept the white habit, prayed long hours, fasted and gave to the poor. A saint is not someone who has no faults. A saint is someone whom God has made holy, and in this Pope the people of Rome saw a man of prayer.

His work still touches us. After the Council of Trent, the Church needed renewal. He gave priests a catechism to teach from, a breviary to pray, and a missal for the Mass. Paul urges Timothy to proclaim the message, whether the time is favorable or unfavorable, with the utmost patience in teaching (2 Timothy 4:2). Pius V knew that the people are renewed when they are taught the faith and when the liturgy is celebrated with care.

In 1571 Christians in the Mediterranean were afraid, and he formed an alliance to defend them. Before the battle of Lepanto he asked all the faithful to pray the Rosary. We remember that day with sorrow for the many on both sides who died, and with thanks for the prayer of the Church. Our weapon today is the same Rosary, prayed not against people but for peace, for conversion and for the coming of God's kingdom in every heart.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for those who taught us the faith. Let us pray for the Pope and for our bishops. And let us ask St Pius V and Our Lady of the Rosary to help us pray faithfully every day, for our families and for peace in the world. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who in your providence raised up Pope St Pius V to guard the faith and to renew the worship of your Church, grant through his prayers that we may share in your mysteries with living faith and fruitful love, and trust in your help in every danger. Amen.
