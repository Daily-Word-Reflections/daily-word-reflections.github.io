St Peter Chanel

Priest and martyr, Marist missionary, first martyr of Oceania · Feast: 28 April

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Very truly, I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.” John 12:24

Life

Peter Chanel was born on 12 July 1803 at Cuet, in the region of Ain in eastern France, into a poor farming family. As a boy he looked after sheep. The parish priest noticed his intelligence and piety and helped him to study. He entered the seminary, was ordained a priest in 1827 and served as a curate and then as parish priest at Crozet, where he renewed the life of a neglected parish by his visits to the sick and his gentle care.

He had long wanted to be a missionary. In 1831 he joined the newly formed Society of Mary, the Marists, and for some years taught in the seminary at Belley. In 1836 the Holy See entrusted the Marists with the mission of Western Oceania, and on 24 December 1836 Peter Chanel sailed from France with Bishop Jean-Baptiste Pompallier and a small group of priests and brothers.

In November 1837 he was left on the island of Futuna, in the Pacific, with Brother Marie-Nizier Delorme. The king, Niuliki, received them kindly at first. Peter learned the language, cared for the sick and lived simply among the people, who called him the man with the good heart. For more than three years he saw very few converts and suffered from poverty and hunger, but he did not lose heart.

When the king's son Meitala asked to be baptised, the king and some of his followers turned against the mission, seeing it as a threat to their authority and their ancient religion. On 28 April 1841 a group led by Musumusu, the king's son-in-law, came to Peter's hut and killed him with clubs and an axe. Within a few years almost the whole island had become Catholic. He was canonised by Pope Pius XII in 1954. He is the first martyr of Oceania and its patron.

Stories

Planted on the island (history)

Shortly before his death he is reported to have said that it did not matter if he died, because the faith had been planted on the island and would not be lost by his death.

The man with the good heart (tradition)

The people of Futuna remembered him by a name meaning the man with a kind heart, because he nursed their sick and shared what little he had.

The killer's grave (tradition)

Musumusu, who led the attack, is said to have repented and been baptised. Tradition tells that he asked to be buried at the threshold of the church built where Peter died, so that all who entered would walk over him.

Homily

1. The grain of wheat

Death that bears fruit (John 12:24).

2. The good heart

Kindness speaks the Gospel first.

3. Faithful without results

God asks for faithfulness, not success.

Dear friends, St Peter Chanel spent more than three years on a small Pacific island and baptised very few people. He was poor and often hungry. He learned the language, nursed the sick, and was patient. Then he was killed. By the world's measure his mission was a failure. By God's measure it was a seed sown in the earth.

Jesus said that unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain, but if it dies, it bears much fruit (John 12:24). Within a few years of his death almost all of Futuna was Catholic, and the people of the island still honour him. Even the man who killed him is remembered as having come to repentance.

We live in a world that wants results quickly. Parents worry that their children are not practising the faith. Priests and catechists feel their work is wasted. Peter Chanel tells us to keep sowing and to keep loving. The people of Futuna did not first know him by his sermons. They knew him as the man with a good heart.

In this Easter season, at this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for missionaries and for the young Churches of the Pacific. Let us ask God to bless our own small and hidden efforts. And let us ask St Peter Chanel to teach us faithfulness, and to trust the harvest to God. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who for the spreading of your Church gave St Peter Chanel the crown of martyrdom, grant that in these days of Easter joy we may so celebrate the mysteries of Christ's death and resurrection that we bear faithful witness to new life. Amen.
