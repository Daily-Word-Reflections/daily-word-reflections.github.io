St Barnabas the Apostle

Apostle, son of encouragement, companion of Paul · Feast: 11 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“He was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith.” Acts 11:24

Life

Barnabas was a Jew of the tribe of Levi, a native of Cyprus, whose name was Joseph. The apostles gave him the name Barnabas, which Luke explains as son of encouragement (Acts 4:36). He first appears in the Acts of the Apostles as a man of generous heart: he sold a field that belonged to him, then brought the money, and laid it at the apostles' feet (Acts 4:37). He was not one of the Twelve, but Luke calls him an apostle together with Paul (Acts 14:14), and the Church honours him with that title. The Syro-Malabar Church keeps his feast as Mar Barnabas Shleeha.

When Saul of Tarsus came to Jerusalem after his conversion, the disciples were afraid of him and did not believe that he was a disciple. But Barnabas took him, brought him to the apostles, and told them how Saul had seen the Lord on the road and had preached boldly in Damascus (Acts 9:26–27). Later, when the Gospel began to spread among the Greeks at Antioch, the Church in Jerusalem sent Barnabas there. When he came and saw the grace of God, he rejoiced, and he exhorted them all to remain faithful to the Lord with steadfast devotion (Acts 11:23). Then he went to Tarsus to look for Saul and brought him to Antioch. For a whole year they taught there, and it was in Antioch that the disciples were first called Christians (Acts 11:26). When famine struck Judea, Barnabas and Saul carried the gifts of the Antioch Christians to the elders in Jerusalem (Acts 11:29–30).

While the Church at Antioch was worshipping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them (Acts 13:2). With John Mark, Barnabas's cousin (Colossians 4:10), they sailed to Cyprus, and then went on into Asia Minor, preaching first in the synagogues and then to the Gentiles. At Lystra, after a lame man was healed, the crowd wanted to offer sacrifice to them, calling Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes, and the two apostles barely restrained them (Acts 14:8–18). Barnabas and Paul then went up to Jerusalem for the council of the apostles and elders, where they told of the signs and wonders God had done among the Gentiles (Acts 15:12).

Afterwards Paul and Barnabas disagreed about taking Mark with them on a second journey. The disagreement became so sharp that they parted company; Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed away to Cyprus (Acts 15:39). Barnabas's patience with Mark bore fruit, for Paul later wrote that Mark was useful in his ministry (2 Timothy 4:11), and Paul names Barnabas as one who, like himself, worked for his living (1 Corinthians 9:6). Scripture says nothing of his death. Tradition holds that he was martyred at Salamis in Cyprus. In the time of the emperor Zeno, toward the end of the fifth century, a tomb near Salamis was honoured as his, and the Church of Cyprus venerates him as its founder. His name is remembered in the Roman Canon among the apostles and martyrs.

Stories

Vouching for Saul (history)

When the believers in Jerusalem feared Saul, Barnabas took him by the hand, so to speak, and brought him to the apostles. He told them what the Lord had done for Saul, and from that day Saul went in and out among them (Acts 9:26–28).

Zeus and Hermes (history)

At Lystra the crowd took Barnabas for Zeus and Paul for Hermes and brought oxen and garlands to sacrifice to them. The apostles tore their clothes and cried out that they were only human beings, bringing good news that the people should turn to the living God (Acts 14:11–18).

The Gospel on his breast (tradition)

A tradition of the Church of Cyprus says that in the late fifth century the tomb of Barnabas was found near Salamis, with a copy of the Gospel of Matthew resting on his breast. The discovery was used to support the self-governing status of the Church of Cyprus. The account comes from later tradition.

Homily

1. Son of encouragement

A good name earned by a good life (Acts 4:36).

2. He took Saul

Barnabas opened the door for Paul (Acts 9:27).

3. Set apart by the Spirit

Mission begins in prayer and fasting (Acts 13:2).

Dear friends, the apostles gave Joseph of Cyprus a new name. They called him Barnabas, son of encouragement (Acts 4:36). That says much about him. Some people leave us feeling smaller and more discouraged. Others leave us stronger and more hopeful. Barnabas was that second kind of person. He was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith (Acts 11:24). His goodness came from God, and it made him an encourager.

Think of Saul. After his conversion, the Christians in Jerusalem were afraid of him, and with reason, for he had persecuted them. Barnabas took the risk. He brought Saul to the apostles and spoke for him. Without Barnabas, would we have had St Paul? Sometimes God asks us to open a door for someone whom others do not trust: a new member in the parish, a young person with a difficult past, someone who has come back to the Church.

Think too of Mark. When Mark had left them on the first journey, Paul did not want to take him again. Barnabas gave him another chance, even at the cost of parting from Paul. Years later Paul wrote, Get Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful in my ministry (2 Timothy 4:11). The patience of Barnabas helped make Mark an evangelist. How many lives would change if we gave others the second chance that God so often gives us.

Finally, Barnabas was generous. He sold his field and laid the money at the apostles' feet. At Antioch, when he saw the grace of God, he rejoiced. He did not envy the success of others; he was glad. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St Barnabas to make us generous, joyful and encouraging, so that our families and our parish may be places where people grow in faith and find the courage to begin again. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who chose St Barnabas, a good man full of the Holy Spirit and of faith, to bring the Gospel to the nations, grant through his prayers that we may share generously what we have, encourage one another in the faith, give a second chance to those who have failed, and proclaim the good news of Jesus Christ with joy. Amen.
