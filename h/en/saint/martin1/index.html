Pope St Martin I

Pope and martyr · Feast: 13 April

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.” Matthew 5:10

Life

Martin was born at Todi in Umbria, in central Italy, near the end of the sixth century. He was a cleric of the Roman Church known for his learning and goodness, and he had served as the Pope's representative at the imperial court in Constantinople. He was elected Bishop of Rome in 649 and consecrated without waiting for the emperor's approval, although the custom of the time was to wait for it.

The Church was then troubled by a teaching called Monothelitism, which held that in Christ there is only one will. The emperors favoured it as a way of uniting Christians who were divided over the person of Christ, and in 648 the emperor Constans II issued an edict, the Typos, forbidding anyone to speak of one will or two. The Church teaches that Christ, true God and true man, has a divine will and a truly human will, and that his human will is always in perfect harmony with the divine will. Without a human will he would not be fully man.

In October 649 Martin held a council in the Lateran basilica in Rome. With many bishops, and with the help of the monk St Maximus the Confessor, it confirmed the true faith and condemned the error and the imperial edicts that protected it. The emperor took this as rebellion. In June 653 the imperial exarch Theodore Calliopas came to Rome with soldiers and seized Martin, who was ill, in the Lateran. He was taken by sea to Constantinople on a long and painful voyage.

In Constantinople he was kept in prison, tried on a political charge of treason, publicly humiliated and condemned to death. The sentence was changed to exile, and he was sent to Chersonesus in the Crimea. There, short of food and forgotten by many, he died in September 655. He is the last Pope honoured by the Church as a martyr. His faith was confirmed by the Third Council of Constantinople in 680 and 681, which taught the two wills of Christ.

Stories

The guarded Mass (tradition)

An earlier exarch, Olympius, is said to have sent a soldier to kill the Pope as he gave Communion at the basilica of St Mary Major. Tradition says the soldier was struck blind and could not see him, and Olympius abandoned the plan.

Through the streets in chains (history)

In Constantinople he was stripped of his episcopal vestments before the crowd, put in chains and led through the city to prison. Contemporary accounts preserved in Rome record this treatment.

Letters from the Crimea (history)

From exile he wrote to friends that he lacked even bread, and that he was saddened to be forgotten by those he had served. Yet he prayed that God would keep the Roman Church firm in the faith.

Words of the saint

I pray that God, through the prayers of St Peter, will keep his people and their shepherd steadfast in the true faith.
Pope St Martin I, letter from exile, in substance

Homily

1. Persecuted for righteousness

The kingdom belongs to those who suffer for the right (Matthew 5:10).

2. Two wills in Christ

He is fully God and fully man.

3. Faithful when forgotten

God sees what the world forgets.

Dear friends, today we honour a Pope who died in exile at the edge of the known world, hungry and forgotten. Pope Martin I was arrested in his own cathedral when he was ill, carried across the sea, mocked in chains in the streets of Constantinople, and sent to the Crimea to die. What had he done? He had refused to let an emperor change what the Church believes about Jesus Christ.

The question may seem distant from us. Does it matter whether Christ has one will or two? It matters greatly. If Christ has no human will, then he did not truly choose, as one of us, to obey the Father. But in Gethsemane we hear him pray that not his will but the Father's be done (Luke 22:42). There we see a true human will, lovingly given over to God. That is the Saviour Martin defended.

Jesus said that those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake are blessed, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven (Matthew 5:10). Martin's last letters are sad. He had little bread, and those he had served seemed to have forgotten him. Yet he did not curse them. He prayed that God would keep the Church firm in the faith.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for the Holy Father and for all who suffer for the truth. Let us think of those among us who feel forgotten, the old, the sick, those far from home, and visit them. And let us ask Pope St Martin to help us stand by Christ when it costs us something. Amen.

Prayer

Almighty God, you gave Pope St Martin I the strength to stand firm for the truth about your Son and to suffer exile and death. Through his prayers, make us steadfast in the faith, patient in trials, and faithful to the Church, which Christ built on the rock of Peter. Amen.
