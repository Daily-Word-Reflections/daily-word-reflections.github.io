St John Baptist de la Salle

Priest, founder of the Brothers of the Christian Schools, patron of teachers · Feast: 7 April

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Let the little children come to me, and do not stop them; for it is to such as these that the kingdom of heaven belongs.” Matthew 19:14

Life

John Baptist de la Salle was born at Reims in France on 30 April 1651, the eldest child of a well-to-do and devout family. He was given the tonsure young, and at sixteen he was made a canon of Reims cathedral. He studied at the seminary of Saint-Sulpice in Paris, but the death of both his parents in 1671 and 1672 called him home to care for his younger brothers and sisters. He was ordained a priest on 9 April 1678 and later took a doctorate in theology.

In 1679 he met Adrien Nyel, a layman who had come to Reims to open schools for poor boys. La Salle helped him, at first only as a friend and adviser. Seeing that the teachers were poorly trained and poorly formed in their faith, he began to take them into his own home, to share their meals and to train them. His family and friends were shocked. In 1683 he gave up his canonry, and during the famine of 1683 and 1684 he gave away his family fortune to the hungry, so that he would stand beside his teachers as one of the poor.

From this small group grew the Brothers of the Christian Schools, a community of laymen consecrated to God to teach the children of the poor without fees. La Salle and his Brothers taught pupils together in classes rather than one by one, taught reading in French, the mother tongue, before Latin, set up houses to train teachers, opened Sunday classes for young workers and cared for boys in trouble with the law. He wrote a guide for the running of schools, the Conduct of Christian Schools, and meditations to help the Brothers pray over their work.

His work met hard opposition. The writing masters and schoolmasters of Paris took him to court, some Church authorities misunderstood him, and for a time even some of his own Brothers turned against him. He bore it all with patience. In his last years he handed the leadership of the institute to a Brother and lived as one of the community at Saint-Yon, near Rouen, where he died on Good Friday, 7 April 1719. Pope Leo XIII canonised him in 1900, and in 1950 Pope Pius XII named him the patron of all teachers.

Lessons

Teaching is a ministry

He taught that teachers are ministers of God to the children in their care.

The poor come first

His schools were opened for those who could not pay.

Giving up to stand beside

He became poor so that his teachers would not be alone.

Patience under opposition

Lawsuits and misunderstanding did not make him bitter.

Stories

Bread in the famine (history)

During the famine of 1683 and 1684 he sold what he owned and distributed his fortune to the starving people of Reims. When it was gone he begged for his own bread, sharing the lot of his Brothers, who had nothing to fall back on.

The heroic vow (history)

In November 1691, when the young community seemed close to failing, he and two Brothers, Nicolas Vuyart and Gabriel Drolin, vowed to stay together and build up the society even if they should have to beg and live on bread alone.

Had I known (history)

Looking back, he wrote that if he had known at the start where his care for the teachers would lead, he would not have dared to begin. God led him step by step, he said, so that he did not see the whole road at once.

Homily

1. Let the children come

Jesus wanted no child kept from him (Matthew 19:14).

2. Teaching as ministry

The classroom can be a place of grace.

3. Step by step

God led him one day at a time.

Dear friends, St John Baptist de la Salle was a rich young priest with a comfortable future. He was a canon of the cathedral of Reims at sixteen. He did not set out to found anything. He simply agreed to help a man who wanted to open schools for poor boys, and one act of kindness led to another, until he had given away his fortune and his place in society to live with poor teachers.

He later wrote that if he had seen the whole road at the beginning, he would never have begun. That is often how God leads us. He shows us the next step, not the whole journey. When we say yes to the small thing in front of us, he prepares us for the larger thing we could not yet carry.

Jesus said, Let the little children come to me, and do not stop them (Matthew 19:14). La Salle saw that ignorance and poverty were keeping children away from Christ, and he did something about it. He taught his Brothers that teaching is a ministry, and that the teacher must touch the hearts of the pupils and not only fill their heads.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for our teachers and catechists, and for parents, who are the first teachers. Let us remember the children near us who are falling behind because they are poor. And let us ask St John Baptist de la Salle to help us take the next step God shows us, even when we cannot see the end of the road. Amen.

Prayer

O God, you raised up St John Baptist de la Salle to teach the young and to care for the children of the poor. Through his prayers, bless all teachers and catechists, give them patience and love for those they teach, and help us to lead the young to Christ. Amen.
