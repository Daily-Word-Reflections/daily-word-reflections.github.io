The First Martyrs of the Holy Roman Church

Martyrs put to death in Rome under Nero, after the fire of the year 64 · Feast: 30 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Yet if any of you suffers as a Christian, do not consider it a disgrace, but glorify God because you bear this name.” 1 Peter 4:16

Life

On the night of 18 to 19 July in the year 64, a fire broke out near the Circus Maximus in Rome. Driven by the wind, it burned for six days and then flared up again. According to the Roman historian Tacitus, of the fourteen districts of the city three were destroyed completely and seven left with only a few ruined houses. Thousands lost their homes. The emperor Nero opened public buildings and his own gardens to shelter the homeless, but a rumour spread that he himself had wanted the fire, so that he might rebuild Rome to his own taste.

Tacitus, who was no friend of the Christians, writes that to put an end to the rumour Nero fastened the guilt on a group hated for their supposed crimes, whom the people called Christians. He explains that the name came from Christ, who had been put to death in the reign of Tiberius by the procurator Pontius Pilate. Those who confessed were arrested first, and then on their information a vast multitude was convicted, in his words, not so much of setting the fire as of hatred of the human race. The Christians of Rome, still a young and humble community, were treated as public enemies simply because of the name they bore.

The deaths were cruel and public. Tacitus says that some were covered with the skins of wild beasts and torn to pieces by dogs, others were fastened to crosses, and others were set on fire to serve as torches when the daylight failed. Nero offered his own gardens, across the Tiber in the area of the Vatican, for the spectacle, and he mixed with the crowd dressed as a charioteer. Even some of the Romans began to pity the victims, feeling that they were being destroyed not for the public good but to satisfy one man's cruelty. Writing to the Corinthians about thirty years later, Pope St Clement of Rome remembers a great multitude of the elect, women among them, who suffered many outrages and tortures and gave a noble example among us.

The names of these martyrs are not known. According to ancient tradition the apostles Peter and Paul also gave their lives in Rome in the time of Nero, Peter crucified near the place of the gardens and buried on the Vatican hill, where the basilica of St Peter now stands. When the Roman calendar was renewed in 1969, the Church placed the memorial of the First Martyrs of the Holy Roman Church on 30 June, the day after the solemnity of Sts Peter and Paul, so that the disciples might be honoured beside their fathers in the faith. More than a century later Tertullian wrote that the blood of Christians is a seed, and the Church of Rome grew from their sacrifice.

Lessons

Bearing the name

They died for the name of Christian, and Peter says that this is no disgrace.

Unknown, yet known to God

Their names are lost to history, but written in heaven.

Witness without revenge

They answered injustice with faith, not with hatred.

Seed of the Church

From their blood the Church of Rome grew strong.

Stories

The living torches (history)

Tacitus describes how, in Nero's gardens, Christians were bound and set alight to give light when night fell, while games went on and the emperor drove among the crowd. A pagan historian, he had no reason to invent their suffering, and his account is one of the earliest witnesses outside the New Testament to Christ and his followers.

Women of great courage (history)

St Clement of Rome writes that among the martyrs were women who were made to play the parts of figures from pagan stories in the arena, and who, though weak in body, finished the race of faith with firmness and received a noble reward. He speaks of what the Roman Church had seen with its own eyes a generation before.

The obelisk in the square (tradition)

The obelisk that stands today in the middle of St Peter's Square once stood in the circus of Gaius and Nero beside the Vatican hill, the area where, by tradition, many Christians and St Peter himself were put to death. It was moved to its present place in 1586. Pilgrims who see it stand near the ground of the first martyrs.

Homily

1. Suffering as a Christian

No disgrace, but glory to God (1 Peter 4:16).

2. Martyrs without names

God remembers those the world forgets.

3. Seed of the Church

Persecution did not destroy the Church in Rome.

Dear friends, yesterday we celebrated the great apostles Peter and Paul. Today the Church remembers the many disciples who died with them in Rome, in the persecution of the emperor Nero. We do not know a single one of their names. They were ordinary believers: slaves and craftsmen, mothers and young people, members of a small community that met in homes to pray and break the bread.

In the summer of the year 64 a great fire destroyed much of Rome. People said that the emperor himself was to blame. To turn away their anger, Nero blamed the Christians. A Roman historian, Tacitus, tells us that a vast multitude was arrested and put to death in terrible ways, some torn by dogs, some crucified, some burned as torches in the emperor's gardens. They were not guilty of the fire. Their only crime was the name of Christ.

St Peter, who would soon die in the same city, had written: Yet if any of you suffers as a Christian, do not consider it a disgrace, but glorify God because you bear this name (1 Peter 4:16). We bear that name too. Most of us will never face such cruelty. But we may be laughed at, passed over or misjudged because we are Christians. The first martyrs of Rome teach us not to be ashamed, and not to answer hatred with hatred.

Nero thought he could wipe out the Christians. Instead, as Tertullian would say, their blood became seed. Today, on the hill where many of them died, stands the basilica of St Peter. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for Christians who are persecuted in our own time, often unknown like these martyrs. And let us ask the first martyrs of the Holy Roman Church to give us courage to bear the name of Christ with joy. Amen.

Prayer

Lord God, you gave the first Christians of Rome the grace to die for the name of your Son, and from their blood your Church grew strong. Through their prayers, give us courage to bear the name of Christian without shame, strength to answer hatred with love, and faithfulness to the end, through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.
