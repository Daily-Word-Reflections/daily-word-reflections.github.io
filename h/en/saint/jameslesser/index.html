St James, son of Alphaeus (St James the Less)

Apostle, one of the Twelve · Feast: 3 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles.” 1 Corinthians 15:7

Life

James, son of Alphaeus, is named in every list of the Twelve whom the Lord chose to be with him and to be sent out to preach (Matthew 10:3, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:15, Acts 1:13). The Gospels tell us nothing else about him under that name. He is called James the Less, or the younger, to tell him apart from James the son of Zebedee, the brother of John. Mark speaks of a Mary, the mother of James the younger and of Joses, who stood at a distance watching the Lord die on the Cross and who came to the tomb on the morning of the Resurrection (Mark 15:40, Mark 16:1).

The Latin tradition, which this feast follows, has long held that James son of Alphaeus is the same man as James the brother of the Lord, that is, a close relative of Jesus, whom Paul met in Jerusalem (Galatians 1:19). Many scholars and the Eastern Churches count them as two different men, and the Church has not settled the question. What can be said with certainty is that the James who led the Church in Jerusalem was greatly honoured by the first Christians, and that the risen Lord appeared to him, as Paul records in the earliest list of witnesses to the Resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:7).

This James was the head of the mother Church of Jerusalem. When Peter had been led out of prison by an angel he sent word to James (Acts 12:17). At the council held in Jerusalem, when the apostles and elders debated whether believers from the nations must keep the whole law of Moses, Peter spoke first, and then James gave the judgement that they should not trouble those Gentiles who are turning to God, asking only a few things for the sake of peace in mixed communities (Acts 15:13–21). Years later, when Paul came back to Jerusalem at the end of his journeys, he went to see James, and all the elders were present (Acts 21:18). The Letter of James in the New Testament is received by the Church under his name.

The Jewish historian Josephus writes that about the year 62, in the interval between two Roman governors, the high priest Ananus had James the brother of Jesus and some others condemned and stoned, and that many in Jerusalem were troubled by this injustice. Hegesippus, a Christian writer of the second century quoted by Eusebius, adds that James was called the Just, that he was thrown down from the temple and struck with a club, and that he died praying for those who killed him. In the sixth century a church of the Holy Apostles was built in Rome where relics of St Philip and St James were laid, and so the two apostles came to share one feast in the Latin Church.

Lessons

Hidden but chosen

Little is told of him, yet the Lord chose him and sent him.

A witness of the Resurrection

The risen Christ appeared to him, and he gave his life for that truth.

Wisdom for peace

At the council he looked for a way to keep Jews and Gentiles together in one Church.

Faith that acts

The letter under his name teaches that faith must be lived.

Stories

The council of Jerusalem (history)

When the Church was divided over the law of Moses, the apostles and elders met. After Peter had spoken and Paul and Barnabas had told of the signs God had done among the Gentiles, James stood up, recalled the words of the prophets and proposed the decision that the whole Church accepted with joy (Acts 15:6–31).

Knees like a camel's (tradition)

Hegesippus wrote that James prayed so often in the temple for the forgiveness of the people that his knees grew hard like a camel's. The detail comes from an ancient writer, but it is not found in Scripture.

Praying for his killers (tradition)

According to Hegesippus, when James had been thrown down and was being stoned, he knelt and prayed to God to forgive them, as the Lord had prayed on the Cross. Josephus confirms that he was stoned by order of the high priest; the details of his last moments come from Christian tradition.

Homily

1. The risen Lord appeared to James

Our faith rests on witnesses (1 Corinthians 15:7).

2. A shepherd of peace

James kept the Church together (Acts 15:19).

3. Faith with works

Faith must show itself in works (James 2:17).

Dear friends, today we honour St James, one of the Twelve. In the Gospels he hardly says a word. He is a name in a list. Yet the Lord called him by name, kept him close and sent him out. Many of us live quiet lives that no one writes about. James reminds us that being little known is not the same as being little loved. The Lord knows each of his disciples by name.

Paul, writing to the Corinthians, gives the oldest list of those who saw the risen Christ, and there we read that he appeared to James, then to all the apostles (1 Corinthians 15:7). Our faith does not rest on ideas but on witnesses. Men like James saw the Lord alive, and they were ready to die rather than deny it. When we say the Creed, we stand on their testimony.

In Jerusalem, James became a shepherd who held the community together. When believers from the nations were coming into the Church and some wanted to lay on them the whole law of Moses, the Church could have split. James listened, prayed over the Scriptures and proposed a path of peace. Families and parishes today also need people who listen first, who look for what unites, and who do not let quarrels tear the body of Christ.

The letter that bears his name is very practical. It tells us to be doers of the word and not merely hearers, to care for orphans and widows, to control our tongue and to pray for the sick. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us ask St James to make our faith active in love, to give us patience in trials, and to keep our communities united in Christ. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who made St James a witness of the Resurrection of your Son and a shepherd of your Church in Jerusalem, grant through his prayers that we may be doers of your word and not hearers only, live our faith in works of love, and stand firm in the hope of the risen Christ. Amen.
