St Theodosia

Virgin martyr (identity uncertain) · Feast: 29 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” Luke 14:27

Life

The Malankara Panchangam remembers St Theodosia on 29 May, but it does not say which saint of that name is meant, and we have not found a source that settles it. Two holy women named Theodosia are honoured by the Byzantine Churches on or around this day, and either could be intended. This page tells of both and does not pretend to know more than we do. If the Church's own calendar notes give a clear identification, this page will be corrected.

The first is St Theodosia of Tyre, a virgin martyr of the persecution of Diocletian. Her story is told by Eusebius of Caesarea, who lived in Caesarea at the time and wrote about the martyrs of Palestine. On Easter Day in the year 307 or 308, Theodosia, a girl from Tyre not yet eighteen years old, saw Christian prisoners standing before the governor's court in Caesarea. She went up to greet them and asked them to remember her when they came before the Lord. The soldiers seized her and took her to the governor, Urbanus, who had her cruelly tortured. She endured it with joy, and at last she was thrown into the sea.

The second is St Theodosia of Constantinople, a nun of the eighth century. According to her later Life, when the emperor Leo III ordered the icon of Christ above the Chalke Gate of the imperial palace to be taken down, around the year 729 or 730, Theodosia and other women pulled away the ladder of the soldier sent to remove it. She was arrested and put to death for defending the holy image. Historians question some details of this account, which was written long after, but her memory became dear to all who honoured the icons.

Theodosia means gift of God. Both women gave themselves back to God as a gift, one by standing with persecuted Christians, the other by defending the honour of the image of Christ. Whichever of them the Panchangam intends, both followed the word of the Gospel: Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple (Luke 14:27).

Stories

A greeting on Easter Day (history)

Eusebius, who may have seen it, tells that Theodosia greeted the confessors at Caesarea and asked their prayers. For this kindness alone she was arrested and tortured, and she was drowned in the sea.

The ladder at the Chalke Gate (tradition)

Her Life tells that when a soldier climbed a ladder to remove the icon of Christ from the palace gate, Theodosia and her companions shook the ladder and he fell. She was seized and killed for her devotion.

Gift of God (tradition)

Both martyrs bore a name that means gift of God, and Christians have often seen in it the meaning of their lives, offered back to the Giver.

Words of the saint

She asked the prisoners to remember her when they came to the Lord.
Eusebius of Caesarea, The Martyrs of Palestine 7, in substance

Homily

1. Carry the cross

There is no discipleship without it (Luke 14:27).

2. Do not be ashamed

She stood beside the prisoners of Christ.

3. A gift returned

Her name means gift of God, and she gave herself to God.

Dear friends, today our Panchangam remembers St Theodosia. The calendar does not tell us which Theodosia is meant, and we must say so honestly. Two holy women of that name are remembered at this time of year. One was a young girl of Tyre, martyred around 307. The other was a nun of Constantinople who died defending the icon of Christ in the eighth century.

The story of Theodosia of Tyre comes from Eusebius, who lived in that time and place. On Easter Day she saw Christians standing in chains before the court. She was not yet eighteen, but she was not afraid. She went up to them, greeted them, and asked them to pray for her when they came before the Lord. For that greeting she was arrested, tortured and thrown into the sea.

Jesus says in the Gospel, Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple (Luke 14:27). For Theodosia, carrying the cross began with a small act: not looking away from those who suffered for Christ. Today too, many Christians are imprisoned or persecuted for their faith. We can at least not forget them.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for persecuted Christians everywhere. Let us ask for the courage of the young Theodosia, who was not ashamed of her brothers and sisters in chains. And let us remember that our own life is a gift of God, meant to be given back to him. Amen.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you gave your servant St Theodosia the courage to carry the cross and follow you even to death. Through her prayers, give us the courage to stand with those who suffer for your name, to honour you in all that is holy, and to offer our lives back to you as a gift. Amen.
