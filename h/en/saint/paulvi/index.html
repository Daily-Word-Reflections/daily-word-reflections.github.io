Pope St Paul VI

Pope, pilgrim of peace, Pope of the Second Vatican Council · Feast: 29 May

Life

Giovanni Battista Montini was born on 26 September 1897 at Concesio, near Brescia in northern Italy. His father, Giorgio, was a lawyer, journalist and member of parliament, and his mother, Giuditta, was active in Catholic women's work. He was often in poor health as a boy and studied at home for long periods. He was ordained priest on 29 May 1920. After studies in Rome he entered the service of the Holy See, and from 1924 worked in the Secretariat of State. For many years he was also chaplain to the Catholic university students of Italy, helping young people to think and live their faith in a troubled time.

Under Pope Pius XII he became one of the Pope's closest collaborators, and during the Second World War he helped to organise aid for refugees, prisoners and the victims of war. In 1954 he was named Archbishop of Milan, a great industrial city where many workers had drifted from the Church. He visited parishes and factories, built new churches in the suburbs and organised a great mission for the whole city. Pope St John XXIII made him a cardinal in 1958. After John's death, he was elected pope on 21 June 1963 and took the name Paul, after the apostle of the nations.

Paul VI guided the Second Vatican Council through its last three sessions and closed it on 8 December 1965, and then gave himself to putting it into practice, including the reform of the liturgy and the new Roman Missal of 1969. He was the first pope to travel widely by air, and he visited five continents. In January 1964 he went as a pilgrim to the Holy Land and embraced the Ecumenical Patriarch Athenagoras in Jerusalem. In December 1964 he came to Bombay for the International Eucharistic Congress, the first pope ever to visit India. In 1969 he made Archbishop Joseph Parecattil of Ernakulam the first cardinal of the Syro-Malabar Church. He was welcomed by enormous crowds of every religion, celebrated Mass for the Congress, met leaders of other faiths, and appealed to the nations to spend on the hungry what they spent on weapons. In 1965 he spoke at the United Nations in New York and cried, No more war, war never again.

He taught with great care, in encyclicals on the Church, the Eucharist, the development of peoples, the sacredness of married love and human life in Humanae Vitae (1968), and in his letter on evangelisation, Evangelii Nuntiandi (1975). In 1968 he proclaimed the Credo of the People of God, and during the Council he named Mary Mother of the Church. He suffered much from misunderstanding and division in the years after the Council, but he never lost his love for the Church or for the modern world. He died at Castel Gandolfo on 6 August 1978, the feast of the Transfiguration. Pope Francis beatified him in 2014 and canonised him on 14 October 2018. His feast is kept on 29 May, the day of his priestly ordination.

Stories

The embrace in Jerusalem (history)

On 5 and 6 January 1964 Paul VI and the Ecumenical Patriarch Athenagoras met on the Mount of Olives, the first meeting of a pope and a patriarch of Constantinople in more than five hundred years. They embraced and prayed together. On 7 December 1965 they lifted the mutual excommunications of 1054.

The car for the poor (history)

During his visit to Bombay in 1964 the Pope rode through the city in a white car that had been given to him for the journey. When he left India he gave it to Mother Teresa of Calcutta for her work. She raffled it and used the money for her centre for people with leprosy.

The kiss of peace (history)

On 14 December 1975, at a Mass in the Sistine Chapel marking ten years since the lifting of the excommunications, Paul VI knelt and kissed the feet of Metropolitan Meliton, the envoy of the Patriarch of Constantinople. It was a sign of humility that astonished the world.

Homily

1. That they may be one

He worked for the unity Christ prayed for (John 17:21).

2. Pilgrim to India

He came to Bombay as a messenger of the Eucharist and of peace.

3. Witness before words

The world needs Christians who live the Gospel.

Dear friends, on the night before he died, Jesus prayed for his disciples: “As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me” (John 17:21). Pope St Paul VI took that prayer to heart. He guided the Church through the Second Vatican Council and the difficult years after it, and he went out to meet the world as no pope had done before.

He went to Jerusalem and embraced the Patriarch of Constantinople. He went to the United Nations and pleaded for peace. And in December 1964 he came to us, to Bombay, for the Eucharistic Congress, the first pope ever to set foot in India. Millions came to see him, Christians and people of many religions. He spoke with respect of India's ancient search for God and asked the world to share its bread with the hungry. Many of our parents and grandparents remembered that visit all their lives.

Paul VI also suffered. Some thought he changed too much, others that he changed too little. His teaching on human life and married love in Humanae Vitae brought him much criticism. Yet he did not seek to please. He spoke the truth with love and carried his cross quietly. He told the Church that people today believe witnesses more than teachers. Our lives must show what our words say.

He died on the feast of the Transfiguration, after years of faithful service. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for his love for India and for the whole world, let us pray for the unity of all Christians and for peace among nations, and let us ask for the grace to be true witnesses of Christ in our families and in our land. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who entrusted your Church to Pope St Paul VI in a time of great change, grant through his prayers that we may love the Church as he loved her, seek dialogue and peace with all peoples, care for the poor and the hungry, and be faithful witnesses of Christ before the world. Amen.
