Elisha the Prophet

Prophet of Israel, disciple and successor of Elijah · Feast: 14 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“There were also many lepers in Israel in the time of the prophet Elisha, and none of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian.” Luke 4:27

Life

Elisha, whose name means God saves, was a prophet in the northern kingdom of Israel in the ninth century before Christ, through the reigns of several kings from Ahab's sons to Joash. His story is told in the First and Second Books of Kings. On Mount Horeb the Lord told Elijah to anoint Elisha son of Shaphat of Abel-meholah as prophet in his place (1 Kings 19:16). Elijah found him ploughing with twelve yoke of oxen and threw his mantle over him. Elisha asked only to kiss his father and mother goodbye. Then he slaughtered the oxen, boiled their flesh using the gear of the oxen, gave it to the people to eat, and followed Elijah as his servant (1 Kings 19:19–21).

When the time came for Elijah to be taken up, Elisha would not leave him, though Elijah three times told him to stay behind. At the Jordan, Elijah struck the water with his mantle and the two crossed on dry ground. Elijah asked what he could do for him, and Elisha answered, Please let me inherit a double share of your spirit (2 Kings 2:9), the portion of the firstborn son. A chariot of fire and horses of fire separated them, and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven. Elisha cried out, Father, father! The chariots of Israel and its horsemen! He took up the mantle that had fallen, struck the Jordan, and the waters parted again. The prophets at Jericho said, The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha (2 Kings 2:15).

His ministry was full of mercy for ordinary people. He made the bad water of Jericho wholesome. He saved a poor prophet's widow from having her two sons taken as slaves, by making her one jar of oil fill every vessel she could borrow. He promised a son to a woman of Shunem who had welcomed him into her house, and when the boy died he prayed, stretched himself upon the child, and gave him back to his mother alive. He fed a hundred people with twenty barley loaves, and there was some left over (2 Kings 4). He healed Naaman, the commander of the army of Aram, of leprosy when Naaman at last humbled himself and washed seven times in the Jordan, and Naaman confessed that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel (2 Kings 5).

Elisha also stood before kings, and Sirach says that he never trembled before any ruler (Sirach 48:12). When the army of Aram surrounded Dothan, he told his frightened servant, Do not be afraid, for there are more with us than there are with them, and prayed that the young man's eyes might be opened; and he saw the mountain full of horses and chariots of fire (2 Kings 6:16–17). When Elisha fell sick and died, he was buried, and later a dead man thrown into his grave came to life when he touched the prophet's bones (2 Kings 13:20–21). Jesus named him in the synagogue at Nazareth (Luke 4:27). The Fathers saw in him a figure of Christ, who also raised a widow's son, fed the crowds with barley loaves and sent the sick to be made clean. The Syro-Malankara Panchangam remembers him on 14 June, as do other Eastern Churches.

Lessons

Elisha left everything at once and followed God's call.

His miracles were for widows, mothers, the hungry and the sick.

Stories

The burning plough (history)

When Elijah's mantle fell on him, Elisha did not keep the oxen in case things went badly. He killed them, cooked the meat using the gear of the oxen and fed the people. There was no going back. He then became Elijah's servant, and Scripture later remembers him as the one who poured water on the hands of Elijah (1 Kings 19:21, 2 Kings 3:11).

Naaman in the Jordan (history)

Naaman came with horses and chariots and gifts, and was angry when Elisha did not even come out to meet him but only sent word to wash seven times in the Jordan. His servants persuaded him, and his flesh became like the flesh of a young boy. The Fathers, St Ambrose among them, saw in this washing a figure of Baptism (2 Kings 5:9–14).

The horses of fire (history)

At Dothan the king of Aram sent an army by night to seize Elisha. In the morning the servant saw the city surrounded and cried out in fear. Elisha prayed, and the servant saw the hills full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha. The prophet then led the blinded soldiers to the king of Israel, and asked that they be fed and sent home in peace (2 Kings 6:8–23).

Homily

1. The call at the plough

Elisha followed at once (1 Kings 19:21).

2. A double share of the Spirit

He asked for the one gift that matters (2 Kings 2:9).

3. Mercy beyond borders

Naaman the Syrian was healed (Luke 4:27).

Dear friends, today the Church remembers the prophet Elisha. He was a farmer's son, out in the field with twelve yoke of oxen, when the prophet Elijah passed by and threw his mantle over him. Elisha did not ask for time to think. He killed the oxen, cooked the meat, fed the people and followed (1 Kings 19:21). Some calls from God cannot be answered halfway.

When Elijah was about to be taken up, he asked Elisha what he wanted. Elisha could have asked for many things. He asked for one: Please let me inherit a double share of your spirit (2 Kings 2:9). This is a good prayer for each of us. We ask God for health, work, success for our children. These are good things. But the gift that matters most is the Holy Spirit, and Jesus promised that the Father will give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him (Luke 11:13).

Elisha's miracles were almost all for ordinary people in need: a widow in debt, a mother whose child had died, a hundred hungry people, a foreign soldier with leprosy. In the synagogue at Nazareth Jesus reminded his listeners that of all the lepers in Israel, only Naaman the Syrian was healed (Luke 4:27). God's mercy is wider than our borders. And Jesus himself did the same works, more fully: he raised the widow's son at Nain, not far from Shunem, and fed the crowds with barley loaves.

When Elisha's servant saw the enemy army around the city, he was terrified. Elisha said, Do not be afraid, for there are more with us than there are with them (2 Kings 6:16), and the young man's eyes were opened to see the horses and chariots of fire. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, the Lord is with us more surely than any army. Let us ask God, through the prayers of the prophet Elisha, for the gift of his Spirit, for a heart that does not turn back, and for eyes of faith to see that we are never alone. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who filled your prophet Elisha with the spirit of Elijah and made him a sign of your mercy for the poor, the sick and the stranger, grant through his prayers that we may leave behind whatever holds us back from following you, ask above all for your Holy Spirit, and see with the eyes of faith that you are always near. Amen.
