St Pancras

Martyr, boy of Rome · Feast: 12 May

Word for the day

“Let no one despise your youth, but set the believers an example in speech and conduct, in love, in faith, in purity.” 1 Timothy 4:12

Life

Pancras was a young Christian who was put to death in Rome for his faith and buried on the Via Aurelia, on the western side of the city. His martyrdom is usually placed in the persecution of Diocletian, at the beginning of the fourth century. The ancient tradition of the Roman Church remembers him as a boy of about fourteen. His tomb was honoured from early times, and about the year 500 Pope Symmachus built a basilica over it. Rebuilt in the seventh century, the basilica of St Pancras still stands there.

The later account of his martyrdom, written some centuries after his death, says that he was born in Phrygia in Asia Minor, lost his parents as a child and was brought to Rome by his uncle Dionysius. There, it says, he was baptised and, when he refused to offer sacrifice to the gods, was beheaded. This account belongs more to legend than to history, but it shows how the Church loved this boy who would not deny Christ.

His fame spread far beyond Rome. Gregory of Tours, the bishop and historian of the sixth century, wrote that those who swore a false oath at his tomb were punished at once, and so he came to be invoked as a guardian of oaths and of truthfulness. Tradition says that when Pope St Gregory the Great sent St Augustine to England, relics of Pancras went too, and that a church in his honour was built at Canterbury. The Venerable Bede records that Pope Vitalian sent relics of Pancras to King Oswiu of Northumbria in the seventh century.

Many churches in Italy, England and elsewhere are named after him, and in London a whole district and a great railway station still carry his name. In the calendar of the Roman Church his memory is kept on 12 May, the same day as the martyrs Nereus and Achilleus.

Stories

The boy who would not sacrifice (legend)

The later Acts tell that the young Pancras was brought before the authorities and ordered to offer sacrifice to the gods. He refused, saying that he was a Christian, and was beheaded on the Via Aurelia. The details come from a late and legendary account.

The guardian of oaths (tradition)

Gregory of Tours wrote that if anyone swore falsely at the tomb of Pancras, he was struck at once, so that people in Gaul came to take their most solemn oaths there. In this way the boy martyr became a patron of truthfulness.

Relics for the English (history)

Bede tells that Pope Vitalian sent to King Oswiu relics of the apostles Peter and Paul and of the martyrs, among them Pancras. In this way the young martyr of Rome came to be loved by the newly converted English.

Homily

1. Let no one despise your youth

The young can be examples to all (1 Timothy 4:12).

2. Faithful until death

A crown of life is promised (Revelation 2:10).

3. A friend of truth

He is honoured as a guardian of honest words.

Dear friends, St Pancras was only a boy, perhaps fourteen years old, when he gave his life for Christ in Rome. At an age when many of us were thinking about games and school, he had to decide whether to deny the Lord or die. He chose Christ. The Church has remembered him for seventeen centuries, while the names of many powerful men of his time are forgotten.

Paul wrote to young Timothy, Let no one despise your youth, but set the believers an example in speech and conduct, in love, in faith, in purity (1 Timothy 4:12). We sometimes think that faith belongs to grandparents. Pancras shows the opposite. The young can teach the old what it means to be faithful. Let us not underestimate the faith of our children and teenagers.

Pancras also became the patron of oaths and of honest words. People came to his tomb to make solemn promises, believing that a lie told there would not go unpunished. Our world is full of false words, in advertisements, in politics, on the internet and sometimes in our own homes. A Christian should be someone whose yes means yes and whose no means no.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for the young people of our families and our parish, that they may love Christ and not be ashamed of him. Let us pray for young Christians who suffer for their faith in many lands today. And let us ask St Pancras to help us be truthful in every word we speak. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who gave the boy St Pancras the strength to remain faithful to you until death, grant through his prayers that our young people may love Christ with all their heart, that we may always speak the truth, and that we may never be ashamed of our faith. Amen.
