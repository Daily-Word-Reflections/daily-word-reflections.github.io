St Boniface

Bishop and martyr, apostle of Germany · Feast: 5 June

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!” Romans 10:15

Life

Boniface was born in Wessex in England about the year 675 and baptized with the name Winfrid. As a boy he asked to enter a monastery, first at Exeter and then at Nursling near Southampton. He became a learned monk, a teacher of the young and the author of a Latin grammar, and he was ordained priest at about thirty. His brothers respected him and expected him to lead them. But Winfrid felt a call to the peoples across the sea on the European continent, many of them related to the English by language and blood, who had not yet received the Gospel or had received it only in part.

In 716 he sailed to Frisia, in what is now the Netherlands, but war and the hostility of the pagan king Radbod forced him to return. Two years later he set out again, this time for Rome. In 719 Pope Gregory II received him, gave him the name Boniface and sent him to preach to the peoples of Germany. He worked for a time in Frisia with the English bishop St Willibrord and then went into Hesse and Thuringia. In 722 the pope ordained him bishop. Boniface remained closely bound to the Apostolic See all his life, writing often to the popes for advice and keeping the teaching and discipline of Rome.

His biographer Willibald tells that at Geismar in Hesse Boniface cut down a great oak sacred to the god the Romans called Jupiter, while the pagans watched, and that from its wood he built a chapel in honour of St Peter. Many were baptized. In 732 Pope Gregory III made him archbishop. Boniface founded and organized dioceses in Bavaria and central Germany, held councils to reform the Frankish Church, and asked for monks and nuns from England to help him. Among them were his kinswoman St Leoba, abbess of Tauberbischofsheim, St Walburga, St Lull and others. In 744 his disciple St Sturm founded under his direction the abbey of Fulda, which became a great centre of prayer and learning. About 746 Boniface became bishop of Mainz.

When he was nearly eighty, Boniface handed over Mainz to Lull and returned to Frisia, where he had begun. He preached and baptized there once more. On 5 June 754, near Dokkum, while he waited with his companions for newly baptized Christians who were coming to be confirmed, an armed band attacked the camp. Boniface forbade his men to fight, and he and his companions, about fifty in all, were killed. His body was carried to Fulda, where he is buried. He is honoured as the apostle of Germany, and his letters show a bishop who was both a tireless missionary and a builder of the Church.

Lessons

Leaving home for the Gospel

He left a settled life in England to bring Christ to other peoples.

United with Peter

He built the Church in Germany in close communion with the pope.

Missionary teamwork

He called monks and nuns to work with him and trusted them.

No evil for evil

At the end he forbade his companions to defend themselves with weapons.

Stories

The oak of Geismar (tradition)

Willibald writes that Boniface took an axe to a huge oak that the pagans of Hesse held sacred, and that when it fell and nothing happened to him, many of those who had cursed him believed. From its wood he built a chapel of St Peter. Willibald adds that the tree split into four parts as it fell, a detail that belongs to the tradition of the story.

The book held over his head (tradition)

At Fulda a damaged book is kept which tradition says Boniface raised over his head to shield himself when the sword came down on him. The cuts in the manuscript have long been linked with his death, though this cannot be proved.

Do not return evil for evil (history)

Willibald records that when the attackers came, Boniface told his companions not to fight, because Scripture teaches us not to render evil for evil, and urged them to trust in God and accept death for Christ.

Words of the saint

Let us not be dumb dogs or silent watchmen or hirelings who run away before the wolf, but careful shepherds watching over the flock of Christ.
St Boniface, Letter to Archbishop Cuthbert of Canterbury, in substance
“Do not repay anyone evil for evil, but take thought for what is noble in the sight of all.”
St Paul, Romans 12:17

Homily

1. Go and make disciples

The Church exists to bring Christ to all peoples (Matthew 28:19).

2. Proclaim the message

Be persistent whether the time is favorable or unfavorable (2 Timothy 4:2).

3. No evil for evil

Boniface died without lifting a sword (Romans 12:17).

Dear friends, St Boniface was a respected monk and teacher in England. He could have spent his whole life there in peace, among books and brothers who loved him. But he heard the command of the Lord, Go therefore and make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:19). He crossed the sea, failed at his first attempt, and tried again. He reminds us that missionary work begins with a willingness to leave what is comfortable.

Boniface went first to Rome. He wanted to be sent by the successor of Peter, and all his life he kept close to the popes. For him, mission was not a private adventure. It was the work of the whole Church. In our parishes too, our service bears fruit when it is done in communion, with our bishop and with one another, not as a show of our own strength.

He was a builder. He founded dioceses and monasteries, called monks and nuns from England, and trusted women like St Leoba with great responsibility. Paul writes, proclaim the message; be persistent whether the time is favorable or unfavorable (2 Timothy 4:2). Boniface did this for almost forty years, through dangers, disappointments and quarrels. He wrote that a pastor must not be a silent watchman but must speak and guard the flock.

At the end, an old man of nearly eighty, he went back to the people where he had first failed. When armed men attacked, he told his companions not to fight, and he died a witness to the Gospel. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for missionaries, especially for those working in places where Christ is still unknown. Let us ask St Boniface to make us courageous witnesses and peacemakers, who never repay evil with evil. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who called St Boniface to leave his homeland and to preach your Gospel to many peoples, grant through his prayers that we may keep firmly the faith he taught and sealed with his blood, love the unity of your Church, and proclaim your word with courage, in season and out of season. Amen.
