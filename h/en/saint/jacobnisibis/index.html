Mar Yaqob of Nisibis (St Jacob of Nisibis)

Bishop and Malpan of Nisibis, Father of the Council of Nicaea · Feast: 12 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Unless the LORD builds the house, those who build it labor in vain. Unless the LORD guards the city, the guard keeps watch in vain.” Psalm 127:1

Life

Mar Yaqob, Jacob or James in English, is the first bishop of Nisibis whose name is known to history. Nisibis, today Nusaybin in south-eastern Turkey near the Syrian border, was a strong frontier city of the Roman Empire facing the Persians, and a centre of Syriac-speaking Christianity. According to the account of Theodoret of Cyrrhus, written about a century later, Jacob first lived as a hermit in the mountains, sleeping in the open, eating wild plants and giving himself to prayer. His holiness became known, and he was called to be bishop of the city, probably around the year 308.

In 325 he was one of the bishops at the Council of Nicaea, where the Church confessed that the Son is of one being with the Father. His name appears among the bishops who signed. In this way the Syriac Church was present, through him, at the council whose faith we still profess every time we pray the Creed.

He cared for his people as a father and a teacher, which is why the Church calls him Malpan. Syriac tradition says that he founded a school in Nisibis and that the young St Ephrem was his disciple and helper. Ephrem certainly loved him, and in his hymns on Nisibis he praises Jacob and the bishops who followed him as shepherds who guarded the city by their prayers. Some writings were once thought to be his, but scholars now know they are the Demonstrations of Aphrahat, the Persian Sage.

Jacob died in 338, the year Shapur II of Persia laid siege to Nisibis. The city did not fall. Later accounts tell that the bishop's prayers saved it, and his memory as the protector of Nisibis grew strong. The church of Mar Yaqob in Nusaybin, built in the fourth century, still stands, and a crypt beneath it is shown as his resting place. The Malankara Panchangam keeps his memory on 12 May.

Stories

The siege and the gnats (tradition)

Theodoret tells that when the Persian army besieged Nisibis, the people begged their holy bishop to pray. He climbed the wall and asked God to send a plague of gnats and mosquitoes. A great cloud of them fell on the elephants and horses of the enemy, and the army was driven back in confusion. Historians note that Theodoret may have joined events of different sieges, but the story shows how Nisibis remembered its bishop.

Wood from the ark (legend)

Armenian tradition tells that Jacob longed to see Noah's ark and climbed the mountains of Ararat to find it. Each time he grew weary he fell asleep and woke lower down. At last an angel brought him a piece of the wood of the ark as a reward for his perseverance. A relic said to be this piece is kept at Etchmiadzin in Armenia.

The hymns of his disciple (history)

In the hymns of Nisibis, Ephrem speaks to the city of its three shepherds. He describes Jacob as the first, who stood like a wall around his flock. This loving praise from a great poet is our earliest witness to Jacob's holiness.

Words of the saint

Three shepherds have guarded you, O Nisibis; the first of them was your wall.

Homily

1. The Lord guards the city

Without him the watchman watches in vain (Psalm 127:1).

2. Prayer before action

The hermit became the bishop.

3. Passing on the faith

Every teacher plants for those who come after.

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today, together with Mar Epiphanios, our Church remembers Mar Yaqob, bishop and Malpan of Nisibis. He was one of the bishops at the great Council of Nicaea, which gave us the words we still say in the Creed: that the Son is of one being with the Father. When we say the Creed today, we stand with him.

Nisibis was a frontier city, often attacked by armies. The people built strong walls. But they remembered that their real wall was their bishop's prayer. The psalm says, Unless the LORD guards the city, the guard keeps watch in vain (Psalm 127:1). Mar Yaqob believed that. Before he was a bishop he had spent years alone in prayer in the mountains, and from that prayer came his strength.

He also taught. Syriac tradition remembers that the young Ephrem learned from him. Ephrem became the Harp of the Spirit, and his hymns are still sung in our Church. A good teacher does not only do good himself; he forms others who will carry the faith further than he could.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for our families and our country, asking the Lord to guard them. Let us thank God for our teachers and pray for our Malpans and seminaries. And let us ask Mar Yaqob to teach us to begin every work with prayer. Amen.

Prayer

Lord God, you gave Mar Yaqob of Nisibis to your Church as a shepherd of prayer and a teacher of the true faith. Through his prayers, guard our cities and our homes, keep us steadfast in the faith of Nicaea, and raise up in our Church teachers and saints who will lead your people to you. Amen.
