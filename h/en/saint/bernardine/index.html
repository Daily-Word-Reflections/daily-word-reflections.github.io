St Bernardine of Siena

Priest, Franciscan friar, preacher of the Holy Name of Jesus · Feast: 20 May

Life

Bernardine was born on 8 September 1380 at Massa Marittima in Tuscany, into the noble Albizzeschi family of Siena. He lost his mother when he was three and his father when he was six, and he was brought up in Siena by devout aunts. As a young man he was cheerful and much loved, and he joined a confraternity that served the sick in the great hospital of Santa Maria della Scala.

In 1400, when plague struck Siena, he and a group of young companions took charge of the hospital for about four months, nursing the sick and burying the dead, until he himself fell gravely ill. After his recovery he cared for an aunt who was dying, and then, in 1402, he entered the Franciscan Order among the friars of the Observance, who wished to live the rule of St Francis strictly. He was ordained a priest in 1404 and spent years in prayer and study.

From about 1417 he became the most famous preacher in Italy. He walked from city to city, preaching in the open squares to great crowds for hours at a time, in the simple language of the people, with stories, humour and hard words against gambling, usury, vanity and the feuds that divided the cities. He made peace between families and factions. His sermons at Siena in 1427 were taken down word for word by a listener, so that we can still hear his living voice. Above all he preached love for the Holy Name of Jesus, and he showed the people a tablet with the letters IHS surrounded by golden rays like the sun.

Some accused him of introducing a dangerous novelty, and in 1427 he was called to Rome to answer before Pope Martin V. He was found faithful, and the Pope encouraged his preaching. He refused to become bishop of Siena, Ferrara and Urbino. As vicar general of the Observant friars from 1438 he worked to renew the Franciscan life, and their numbers grew greatly. Worn out by his labours, he died at L'Aquila on 20 May 1444, on the eve of the Ascension, while travelling to preach in Naples. Pope Nicholas V canonised him in 1450, only six years after his death.

Stories

The hospital of Siena (history)

During the plague of 1400 young Bernardine and his companions ran the hospital of Santa Maria della Scala, nursing the dying day and night when many had fled. At the end he fell ill himself and was sick for months.

The card maker (tradition)

Tradition tells that a maker of playing cards complained that Bernardine's preaching against gambling had ruined his trade. The saint told him to paint instead the Name of Jesus in a sun, and the man is said to have earned more from the new tablets than he ever had from cards.

The tablet of the Name (history)

At the end of his sermons Bernardine would hold up a tablet with the monogram IHS in a blazing sun, and the people would kneel to honour the Name of Jesus. Many cities put the sign over their gates and on their public buildings, and it can still be seen on the town hall of Siena.

Homily

1. The name above every name

Every knee bends at the name of Jesus (Philippians 2:9-10).

2. Words people understand

He preached plainly and with joy.

3. Peace in divided cities

He healed old hatreds.

Dear friends, St Bernardine of Siena was a young nobleman who could have lived in comfort. When plague came to his city, he did the opposite of what most people did. While others fled, he and his friends went into the hospital and nursed the dying for months. Before he ever preached a sermon, he preached with his hands.

Later he became the greatest preacher of his day. He walked from town to town and spoke in the squares for hours. He did not use difficult words. He told stories, made people laugh, and then spoke to their hearts about gambling, greed, vanity and revenge. His goal was simple: to bring people back to Jesus, and to bring them back to each other.

His great love was the holy Name of Jesus. Paul writes that God gave him the name that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend (Philippians 2:9-10). Bernardine held up a tablet with that Name shining like the sun, and the people knelt. For him the Name was not a magic word, but a prayer that carries the whole person of the Lord. Many of us learned as children to say the Name of Jesus when we are afraid, when we wake and when we sleep.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for those who taught us to pray the Name of Jesus. Let us pray for preachers, that they may speak simply and from the heart, and for all who nurse the sick in times of epidemic. And let us ask St Bernardine to help us heal the quarrels in our families and our neighbourhoods, in the name of Jesus. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who gave St Bernardine of Siena a great love for the holy Name of Jesus, grant through his prayers that we may call upon that Name with faith, serve the sick and the poor with courage, and become makers of peace in our families and our towns. Amen.
