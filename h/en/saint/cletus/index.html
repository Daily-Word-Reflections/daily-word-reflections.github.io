Pope St Cletus (Anacletus)

Pope, third Bishop of Rome · Feast: 26 April

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“And I tell you, you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it.” Matthew 16:18

Life

Very little is known for certain about St Cletus, and it is better to say so plainly. What we do know comes from the earliest lists of the bishops of Rome. Around the year 180 St Irenaeus of Lyons wrote that the apostles Peter and Paul, after founding the Church in Rome, entrusted its care to Linus; after him came Anencletus, and in the third place from the apostles, Clement. This is the Cletus of our calendar.

His name appears in Greek as Anencletus, which means blameless, and in Latin as Cletus or Anacletus. Some later lists, including the Book of the Popes, treat Cletus and Anacletus as two different popes, but historians today agree that they are one and the same man. He probably led the Church of Rome in the last decades of the first century, perhaps from about 76 to about 88, though other reckonings place him a few years later. None of these dates is certain.

Later traditions add details we cannot check: that he was a Roman, that Peter himself ordained him a priest, that he arranged the clergy of Rome, and that he died a martyr and was buried near St Peter on the Vatican hill. The old Roman calendar kept his feast on 26 April, and the Roman Martyrology still names him on that day. The Syro-Malankara Church remembers him on 27 April.

One witness to him is heard every day around the world. In the First Eucharistic Prayer of the Roman Rite, the Roman Canon, after the apostles the Church names Linus, Cletus, Clement, Sixtus and Cornelius. So the name of this quiet early pope has been spoken at the altar for well over a thousand years. Cletus reminds us that the Church rests not on famous names but on the faith of Peter, handed on faithfully from one shepherd to the next.

Lessons

Faithful, not famous

Cletus is almost unknown, yet he kept the flock of Peter.

Honest about history

The Church does not need invented stories to honour her saints.

Stories

Third from the apostles (history)

St Irenaeus, writing against false teachers, appealed to the list of the bishops of Rome as a living chain back to the apostles. Anencletus is the second link in that chain, between Linus and Clement, who, Irenaeus says, had seen the apostles and heard their preaching.

Ordained by Peter (tradition)

The Book of the Popes and other later sources say that Cletus was ordained by St Peter himself and that he set the number of the priests of Rome. These accounts were written centuries later and cannot be confirmed.

A grave beside Peter (tradition)

Tradition holds that Cletus died a martyr under the Emperor Domitian and was laid to rest near the tomb of St Peter on the Vatican. No sure evidence remains of his martyrdom or his grave.

Words of the saint

The blessed apostles, having founded and built up the Church, handed over the ministry of the episcopate to Linus. Anencletus succeeded him, and after him, in the third place from the apostles, Clement received the episcopate.
St Irenaeus, Against the Heresies 3.3.3, in substance

Homily

1. On this rock

Christ builds his Church on Peter (Matthew 16:18).

2. A link in the chain

Cletus handed on what he received.

3. Holiness unseen

God knows those the world forgets.

Dear friends, today we remember Pope St Cletus. We know almost nothing about him: not his birthplace, not his words, not the exact years he served. We know only that he was the second successor of St Peter as Bishop of Rome, between Linus and Clement. And yet the Church has never forgotten him.

Jesus said to Peter, “you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it” (Matthew 16:18). That promise did not end with Peter's death in Rome. The Church went on, carried by men like Linus and Cletus, whose task was simply to guard and hand on what the apostles had taught. It was quiet work in a dangerous city, and it was enough.

Most of us will never be famous. Our names will not be written in history books. But a parent who teaches a child to pray, a catechist who prepares children for First Communion, a grandmother who keeps the family rosary: these are links in the same chain. God sees what is hidden, and he does not forget.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, we pray for the Pope and all the bishops, successors of the apostles, that they may guard the faith as faithfully as Cletus did. And we thank God for the countless unknown people through whom the faith reached us. Let us ask St Cletus to help us be faithful, not famous. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who built your Church on the rock of Peter and gave St Cletus to tend your flock in its earliest days, grant through his prayers that we may hold faithfully to the faith of the apostles, serve you gladly where no one sees, and remain always one in your holy Church. Amen.
