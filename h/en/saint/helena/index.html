St Helena the Queen

Empress, mother of Constantine the Great · Feast: 21 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.” Galatians 6:14

Life

Helena was born around the year 248, probably at Drepanum in Bithynia, in what is now north-western Turkey. Her family was humble. St Ambrose says that she had worked as a stable-maid at an inn. She became the companion of a Roman officer, Constantius, and around 272 gave birth to their son Constantine at Naissus, the modern Niš in Serbia. Some years later, when Constantius rose to power, he set her aside in order to marry Theodora, a young woman of the family of the emperor Maximian. Helena lived for many years away from the court.

When Constantine became emperor in 306, he brought his mother back to honour. Eusebius of Caesarea, who knew the family, writes that it was Constantine who led her to faith in Christ. She became a Christian of deep and practical devotion. In 324 or 325 her son gave her the title of Augusta, empress, and the right to use the imperial treasury as she wished.

Already near eighty, she travelled to the Holy Land around 326 to 328. Eusebius describes her journey: she prayed at the places where the Lord had lived, built churches at the cave of the Nativity in Bethlehem and on the Mount of Olives, and gave generously to the poor, to soldiers, to prisoners and to those sent into exile. In church she stood among the women in plain clothing, as one of the people. She died around 330, with her son beside her, and was buried in Rome on the Via Labicana. Her great stone coffin is now kept in the Vatican Museums.

From the end of the fourth century, Christians told that during her pilgrimage Helena found the Cross of the Lord on Golgotha. St Ambrose in 395 and the historian Rufinus soon after give the story. Eusebius, writing in her lifetime, does not mention it, so historians treat the finding by Helena as tradition, though very early. What is certain is that by about 350 St Cyril of Jerusalem could say that pieces of the wood of the Cross had spread throughout the world. The Byzantine Churches honour Constantine and Helena together on 21 May, and the Malankara Panchangam keeps her memory on the same day.

Stories

The three crosses (tradition)

Rufinus tells that when the ground on Golgotha was dug, three crosses were found and no one knew which was the Lord's. The bishop Macarius had a dying woman brought to touch each in turn, and at the touch of the third she was healed. So the true Cross was known.

The nails of the Cross (tradition)

St Ambrose says that Helena found the nails that had pierced the Lord, and that she had one set in her son's crown and another made into a bridle for his horse, so that the emperor would rule under the sign of Christ.

The humble empress (history)

Eusebius records that in the churches she visited, the empress was seen standing among the crowd of worshippers, dressed simply. Rufinus adds that in Jerusalem she invited the women consecrated to God to a meal and served them at table herself.

Words of the saint

Homily

1. Boast in the Cross

The Cross is our glory (Galatians 6:14).

2. Never too late

She came to faith late in life and served into old age.

3. Wealth that serves

Her treasury became a gift for the poor.

Dear friends, today our Church remembers St Helena the Queen. St Paul writes, May I never boast of anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ (Galatians 6:14). Helena could have boasted of many things. She was the mother of an emperor. But what she loved most was the Cross of her Lord.

Her life did not begin in glory. She was a woman of humble birth, perhaps a stable-maid at an inn. Her husband set her aside for a more useful marriage. For years she lived in the shadows. Then God lifted her up, and her son brought her to faith in Christ. She shows us that God's plans are not finished when ours seem broken.

As an old woman she made the long journey to the Holy Land. She built churches at Bethlehem and on the Mount of Olives, and she gave to the poor, to prisoners and to exiles wherever she went. Tradition says that she found the Cross on Golgotha. Whether or not she found it with her own hands, she certainly taught the world to honour it.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for the Cross by which we are saved. Let us pray for mothers who pray for their children's faith, and for women who have been abandoned. And let us ask St Helena to help us use what we have, little or much, for those in need. Amen.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you gave St Helena the Queen a great love for your Cross and for the places where you lived, suffered and rose again. Through her prayers, help us to boast only in your Cross, to use what we have for the poor, and to seek you with fresh devotion in every season of life. Amen.
