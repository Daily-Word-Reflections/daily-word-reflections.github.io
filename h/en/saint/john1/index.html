Pope St John I

Pope and martyr · Feast: 18 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“So we are ambassadors for Christ, since God is making his appeal through us” 2 Corinthians 5:20

Life

John was born in Tuscany, in central Italy, in the second half of the fifth century. He served the Roman Church for many years and was one of its deacons. In August 523, already an old man and in poor health, he was elected Bishop of Rome. Italy was then ruled from Ravenna by Theodoric, king of the Ostrogoths, a capable ruler who for many years had left the Catholic Church in peace, although he and his people were Arian Christians, who did not believe that the Son is equal to the Father.

In Constantinople the emperor Justin I took measures against the Arians in his empire, and some of their churches were taken from them. Theodoric was angry and feared for his fellow believers. In 525 he ordered Pope John to go to Constantinople at the head of an embassy, with several bishops and senators, to persuade the emperor to soften these measures. It was a hard mission for a Catholic bishop. The Pope could ask for peace and mercy, but tradition says he would not ask the emperor to send back into Arianism those who had become Catholics of their own will.

John was the first Bishop of Rome to travel to Constantinople. The emperor came out to meet him and is said to have bowed before him, and at Easter of 526 the Pope celebrated the liturgy in the great church of Hagia Sophia, in the Latin language, in the place of honour. The embassy obtained some of what Theodoric wanted, but not everything. Meanwhile in Italy the king had grown suspicious of the Roman senators who looked to Constantinople, and he had put to death the philosopher Boethius and his father-in-law Symmachus.

When John returned to Ravenna, Theodoric received him with displeasure and kept him under arrest. Weakened by age, by the long journey and by harsh treatment, he died there in May 526. His body was carried to Rome and buried in the basilica of St Peter, and the people at once honoured him as a martyr. Theodoric himself died a few months later. The Church keeps the memory of Pope St John I on 18 May.

Lessons

Ambassador for Christ

He went where he was sent, even on a mission he had not chosen.

Peace without betraying the truth

He asked for mercy for others but would not bargain with the faith of converts.

Unity of East and West

His visit joined the Churches of Rome and Constantinople in one celebration.

Faithful to the end

He accepted prison and death rather than act against conscience.

Stories

The Pope at Hagia Sophia (history)

At Easter in 526 John celebrated the liturgy in Hagia Sophia in Constantinople. Ancient accounts say that he was given the first place and that the emperor received a crown from his hands. For the first time a Bishop of Rome had prayed with the Church of the imperial city.

The healing at the gate (tradition)

Pope St Gregory the Great tells in his Dialogues that as John entered Constantinople by the Golden Gate, a blind man asked him to lay his hand on his eyes and received his sight. The story comes from a writer who lived soon after, but we cannot be sure of the details.

The prisoner of Ravenna (history)

On his return, Theodoric received the Pope coldly and kept him in custody at Ravenna. There, in prison and in great hardship, the old man died soon after. His epitaph in St Peter's spoke of him as a victim for Christ.

Homily

1. Ambassadors for Christ

God makes his appeal through us (2 Corinthians 5:20).

2. A mission not chosen

He went where obedience sent him.

3. Take courage

Christ has conquered the world (John 16:33).

Dear friends, Pope St John I was an old and sick man when he was elected. He might have hoped for a few quiet years. Instead a king sent him on a long and dangerous journey to Constantinople, on a mission he had not chosen and could not fully accept. He went, because he was a shepherd, and a shepherd goes where the flock needs him.

Paul writes, So we are ambassadors for Christ, since God is making his appeal through us (2 Corinthians 5:20). An ambassador does not speak in his own name. John spoke for peace between Rome and Constantinople, for mercy toward the Arians, and for the right of those who had become Catholics to remain so. He would not buy peace by selling the faith of others.

Often we too are caught between people who are angry with each other, in a family, a parish or a workplace. We want peace, and peace is good. But true peace is never built on lies or on betraying the weak. John teaches us to seek reconciliation with patience and respect, while keeping our conscience clean before God.

The journey ended in a prison at Ravenna. The man who had been honoured by an emperor died forgotten and in hardship. Yet the Church remembers him as a martyr, a witness who gave his life rather than act against his conscience. At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us pray for the Pope and for all who work for the unity of Christians, and for peace between nations. Let us ask Pope St John I to give us courage when obedience leads us where we would rather not go. Amen.

Prayer

O God, who rewarded the faithfulness of Pope St John I with the crown of martyrdom, grant through his prayers that we may serve the unity of your Church, seek peace without betraying the truth, and remain steadfast in the faith whatever we must suffer. Amen.
