Mar Epiphanios of Cyprus (St Epiphanius of Salamis)

Bishop of Salamis in Cyprus, Father of the Church · Feast: 12 May

Open in the Daily Word app

Word for the day

“Guard the good treasure entrusted to you, with the help of the Holy Spirit living in us.” 2 Timothy 1:14

Life

Epiphanius was born in Palestine, at a village near Eleutheropolis, the modern Beit Guvrin, around the year 315. As a young man he went to Egypt and lived among the monks of the desert, learning prayer, fasting and the Scriptures. While he was there he met a group of Gnostics, teachers of a secret and immoral religion, who tried to draw him in. He refused and reported them to the bishops, and he never forgot how easily a young person could be led astray by false teaching.

Returning to Palestine, he founded a monastery near Eleutheropolis and led it for about thirty years. He became famous for his learning. He is said to have known Hebrew, Syriac, Greek, Coptic and some Latin, and he read widely in the writings of both believers and heretics. He was a friend of the hermit St Hilarion, who later lived for a time in Cyprus.

In 367 he was chosen bishop of Salamis, then called Constantia, the chief city of Cyprus, and he served as its metropolitan for some thirty-six years. In 374 he wrote the Ancoratus, the Anchor, to hold believers steady in the faith of Nicaea. Soon after he wrote his great work, the Panarion, or Medicine Chest, which describes eighty heresies and offers the remedy of true faith for each. In it he defends the perpetual virginity of the Mother of God against those who denied it, and he also rebukes a group of women who offered cakes to Mary as if she were a goddess. Mary must be honoured, he taught, but worship belongs to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit alone.

His zeal sometimes led him into hard disputes, especially against the followers of Origen. In 403, under the influence of Theophilus of Alexandria, he went to Constantinople to act against them and came into conflict with St John Chrysostom, the bishop of that city. He soon saw that he had been used in a quarrel not his own and set out for home. He died at sea on the voyage back to Cyprus in 403. Both he and Chrysostom are honoured as saints. The Byzantine Churches and the Malankara Panchangam keep his memory on 12 May.

Lessons

Guard the faith

He spent his life protecting the teaching handed down from the apostles.

Honour Mary rightly

He honoured the Mother of God and taught that worship belongs to God alone.

Learning in service

His many languages and studies were all put at the service of the Church.

Humility after error

When he saw he was mistaken, he turned back.

Stories

The young monk who said no (history)

Epiphanius tells in the Panarion that as a young man in Egypt he was approached by members of a secret sect who tried to win him with flattery and temptation. By the grace of God he resisted, and afterwards he helped the bishops to expose them.

The beggar who pretended (tradition)

The historian Sozomen relates that two beggars planned to deceive the generous bishop. One lay down pretending to be dead, while the other asked for money to bury him. Epiphanius gave the alms and went his way, but when the beggar called his friend to rise, he found that he had truly died.

The last voyage (history)

Leaving Constantinople in 403, the old bishop took ship for Cyprus. He died on board before reaching home, and his body was buried in Salamis, where his people honoured his tomb.

Words of the saint

Let Mary be held in honour, but let the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit be worshipped; let no one worship Mary.
St Epiphanius, Panarion 79, in substance

Homily

1. Guard the treasure

Keep the faith with the help of the Spirit (2 Timothy 1:14).

2. Honour and worship

Mary is honoured; God alone is worshipped.

3. Turning back

Even saints can be wrong, and saints admit it.

Dear friends, Christ is risen! Today we remember Mar Epiphanios, bishop of Salamis in Cyprus more than sixteen hundred years ago. St Paul wrote to Timothy, Guard the good treasure entrusted to you, with the help of the Holy Spirit living in us (2 Timothy 1:14). Epiphanios gave his whole life to guarding that treasure, the faith we profess in the Creed.

He wrote a book called the Medicine Chest. He thought of false teaching as a sickness and of true faith as the medicine. One of the things he wrote is very useful for us. Some people of his time denied the honour due to the Mother of God. Others went too far and offered sacrifice to her as to a goddess. Epiphanios said: honour Mary, but worship only the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. That is exactly what the Church still teaches. We love the Mother because she leads us to her Son.

He was a man of great zeal, and his zeal sometimes carried him too far. In his old age he was drawn into a sad quarrel with St John Chrysostom. But when he saw that he had been misled, he turned back. He died at sea on his way home. Holiness does not mean never making a mistake. It means letting God correct us.

At this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, let us thank God for those who taught us the faith. Let us ask for wisdom to honour the holy Mother of God rightly. And let us ask Mar Epiphanios for a love of the truth that is always joined to humility. Amen.

Prayer

Lord God, you made Mar Epiphanios a faithful guardian of the faith of the apostles and a wise teacher of your people. Through his prayers, help us to hold firmly to the true faith, to honour the holy Mother of God as your Church teaches, and to worship you alone, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
