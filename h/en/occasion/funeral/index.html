Funeral

For the funeral of a Christian

Readings

  • Old Testament Wisdom 3:1–9 or 2 Maccabees 12:43–46
  • Epistle 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 or Romans 6:3–9
  • Gospel John 11:17–27 or John 14:1–6

Word for the day

“I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live.” John 11:25

Homily points · Short pastoral homily

1. Grief is not faithless

Jesus wept at the tomb of Lazarus. Tears are allowed; despair is not.

2. Christ has conquered death

“I am the resurrection and the life.” Death is not the end but a passage.

3. We pray for the dead

The Church has always prayed and offered the Eucharist for the departed, that they may be purified and enter the joy of God (CCC 1032; 2 Maccabees 12:46).

4. Speak of the person truthfully

Remember the life with gratitude, without exaggeration; the focus is Christ's mercy.

Full homily

Dear family and friends, we have come together with heavy hearts to say farewell to our brother (sister) N. At the tomb of his friend Lazarus, Jesus wept. So we too may weep today. Christian faith does not forbid tears; it gives them hope.

To Martha, standing in her grief, Jesus said: “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live.” These are the words we hold on to today. For the believer, death is not a wall but a door. Christ has passed through it before us and has broken its power.

We remember N. with gratitude: for his (her) love, his (her) work, his (her) faith. But we do not canonise anyone at a funeral. We entrust him (her) to the mercy of God. From the earliest times the Church has prayed and offered the Eucharist for the dead, so that they may be purified and enter fully into God's joy. That is the greatest gift we can still give: our prayer.

Dear family, you are not alone. The parish stands with you. And one day, by God's mercy, we hope to meet again where every tear will be wiped away. Eternal rest grant unto him (her), O Lord. Amen.

Homily option 2 · Martha at the tomb: faith in tears

1. If you had been here

Martha's honest complaint to Jesus (John 11:21). Grief may speak freely to God.

2. I am the resurrection

Jesus answers grief with a person, not a theory (John 11:25).

3. Do you believe this?

The question is put to each of us at this coffin (John 11:26).

4. Jesus wept

God does not watch our sorrow from a distance (John 11:35).

Dear family of N., dear friends, when Lazarus died, his sister Martha went out to meet Jesus and said: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died (John 11:21). Perhaps some of you have said something similar in these days: Lord, where were you? Why now? Why like this? Martha teaches us that we may say such things to God. Faith is not pretending that we do not hurt.

Jesus did not give Martha a lecture. He gave her Himself: I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live (John 11:25). He did not say: there will be a resurrection one day. He said: I am. Our hope is not an idea; it is a person who has walked through death and come out on the other side.

Then Jesus asked Martha: do you believe this? (John 11:26). Today that question stands beside this coffin and is addressed to each of us. Do we believe that death is not the end? Do we believe that N., baptised into Christ, is called to share His risen life? Martha answered: yes, Lord, I believe. Let us make her answer ours, even if our voice trembles.

And a little later the Gospel says simply: Jesus wept (John 11:35). He knew that He was about to raise Lazarus, and still He wept. God is not indifferent to your tears. He stands with you at this grave. Christian hope does not cancel grief; it gives grief a horizon.

So let us do what the Church does: pray for N., that any stain of sin may be washed away and that he (she) may see the face of God. Let us offer this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana for him (her). Let us thank God for the good he (she) did. And let us live in such a way that one day we may all meet again in the house of the Father, where there will be no more tears.

Homily option 3 · Teaching homily: what the Church believes about death

1. Death is real

Death is the end of earthly life and the separation of soul and body (CCC 997, 1007).

2. Judgement and mercy

At death each person receives eternal recompense in a particular judgement (CCC 1021–1022).

3. Purification

Those who die in God's friendship but still imperfectly purified undergo purification, called purgatory (CCC 1030–1031).

4. Our prayer helps

The Church offers the Eucharistic sacrifice and prayers for the dead (2 Maccabees 12:46; CCC 1032).

Dear family and friends of N., at a funeral we are confronted with the question every human being must face: what happens when we die? Let us listen to what the Church teaches, so that our grief may be held by faith.

Death is real. It is the end of our earthly pilgrimage and the separation of the soul from the body (CCC 997, 1007). The body we lay to rest today will return to the earth, and we treat it with reverence because it was a temple of the Holy Spirit. But the soul does not die. And at the end of time, God will raise our bodies, transformed and glorious, as He raised the body of Jesus (CCC 997–1001).

At the moment of death each person stands before Christ in the particular judgement (CCC 1022). This is not a cold courtroom. The Judge is the One who died for us. Yet the judgement is real: our life, our choices, our love or lack of love, will be seen in the light of truth.

Many of us die in God's friendship but not yet completely purified. The Church teaches that such souls undergo a purification, which she calls purgatory, so as to achieve the holiness necessary to enter the joy of heaven (CCC 1030–1031). It is a work of mercy, the fire of God's love cleansing what is still selfish in us.

And here we can help. From the earliest times the Church has prayed for the dead; already Judas Maccabeus made an offering for the dead, that they might be delivered from their sin (2 Maccabees 12:43–46; CCC 1032). The greatest prayer we can offer is the Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana. So let us not only mourn N.; let us pray for him (her), today and in the months ahead. Have Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana offered on the anniversaries, give alms in his (her) name, and pray the Rosary for his (her) soul. Love does not end at the grave; it becomes prayer.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, resurrection and life, receive our brother (sister) N. into your mercy. Forgive his (her) sins, bring him (her) into the light of your face, and console all who mourn with the hope of the resurrection. Amen.
