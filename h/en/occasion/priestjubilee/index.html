Priestly or Religious Jubilee

For the jubilee of a priest or a religious

Readings

  • Old Testament Jeremiah 1:4–9 or Isaiah 61:1–3
  • Epistle 2 Corinthians 4:1–2, 5–7
  • Gospel John 21:15–19

Word for the day

“What shall I return to the LORD for all his bounty to me?” Psalm 116:12

Homily points · Short pastoral homily

1. Gratitude first

The psalmist answers his own question: “I will lift up the cup of salvation and call on the name of the LORD” (Psalm 116:13).

2. Treasure in clay jars

“We have this treasure in clay jars, so that it may be made clear that this extraordinary power belongs to God” (2 Corinthians 4:7).

3. “Do you love me?”

Peter's threefold question is the question of every jubilee: love renewed after years of service and failure.

4. Thank the people

A priest's or religious's life is woven with the people they served.

Full homily

Dear Father (Sister) N., dear friends, today we join the psalmist in asking: “What shall I return to the Lord for all his bounty to me?” And the psalm answers: “I will lift up the cup of salvation and call on the name of the Lord.” For a priest that is exactly what happens every day at the altar.

St Paul says: “We have this treasure in clay jars.” After many years of service, a jubilarian knows better than anyone how fragile the jar is, and how great the treasure. The fruit of these years belongs to God.

By the lake, the risen Jesus asked Peter three times, “Do you love me?” Peter had failed him, and yet Jesus entrusted his sheep to him again. Every jubilee is that question renewed: after all these years, do you love me? And the answer is: “Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you.”

Let us thank God for this life given, and let us pray that our families may give more sons and daughters to the service of the Lord. Amen.

Homily option 2 · Peter on the shore: a love that was restored

1. A charcoal fire

Peter denied Jesus beside a fire; Jesus restored him beside a fire (John 18:18; 21:9).

2. Three questions

Three denials, three declarations of love (John 21:15–17).

3. Feed my sheep

The priest's love for Christ is shown in care for the people.

4. Follow me

Even after many years, the call is the same (John 21:19).

Dear Father N., dear friends, today's Gospel takes us to the shore of the lake after the Resurrection. Jesus has prepared a charcoal fire and some fish for His tired disciples (John 21:9). The last time Peter stood by a charcoal fire, he denied Jesus three times (John 18:18). Now Jesus gives him a new beginning beside a fire.

Three times Jesus asks: Simon, son of John, do you love me? (John 21:15–17). Three denials, three declarations of love. Jesus does not ask Peter about his abilities, his plans or even his mistakes. He asks only about love. Father, after these years of ministry, this is still the only question that matters: do you love Him?

No priest reaches a jubilee without his own failures and regrets. There were moments of tiredness, impatience, discouragement, perhaps sin. Peter's story tells you that the Lord does not dismiss His servants for their weakness. He restores them. Today you can lay all those years before Him and hear Him ask again: do you love me?

And each time Peter answered, Jesus gave him a task: feed my lambs, tend my sheep. The love of a priest for Christ is shown in his love for the people. Think of the children you baptised, the couples you married, the sinners you absolved, the sick you anointed, the dead you buried, the hundreds of Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana you offered. All of that was love made concrete.

Then Jesus said: follow me (John 21:19). Even after a jubilee, that call is not over. Father, keep following Him, in strength or in weakness, wherever He leads. Dear people, thank God for your priest and keep praying for him. Ad multos annos!

Homily option 3 · Teaching homily: the gift and mystery of the priesthood

1. A gift, not a possession

The priesthood belongs to Christ; the priest is its steward (1 Corinthians 4:1).

2. The Eucharist at the centre

The priesthood exists above all for the Eucharist (CCC 1566).

3. The ministry of mercy

Through the priest Christ forgives sins (John 20:22–23; CCC 1461).

4. Faithful to the end

Perseverance is itself a grace to be asked for.

Dear Father N., dear brothers and sisters, a priestly jubilee is a moment of gratitude. It is also a good time to recall, with the Church, what the priesthood is.

St Paul says that we should be regarded as servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries (1 Corinthians 4:1). A steward does not own the house; he manages it for its master. The priesthood is not a personal achievement or a social position. It is Christ's gift, entrusted to a man for the sake of the people.

At the heart of the priesthood is the Eucharist. The Catechism says that it is in the Eucharistic worship that the priest exercises his sacred office most fully (CCC 1566). Father, over these years you have offered the Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana thousands of times. Each time, Christ made present His one sacrifice through your hands. No other work of your ministry is greater than that.

The second great gift is mercy. On Easter evening the risen Lord breathed on the apostles and gave them the power to forgive sins (John 20:22–23). Only God can count the burdens that have been lifted in your confessional (CCC 1461). Those are hidden fruits that will be revealed only in heaven.

Finally, perseverance is itself a grace. Many begin; to remain faithful for so many years is a gift of God to be thanked for. Father, we thank God for you, and we ask Him to keep you faithful until the end. Dear people, pray for priests, love them, and encourage young men in your families to hear the call.

Prayer

Lord, we thank you for the years of service of your servant N. Renew the grace of the first day, forgive what was lacking, and keep him (her) faithful in love until the end. Amen.
