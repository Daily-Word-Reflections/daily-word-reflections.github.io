Blessing of a House

For the blessing of a new house (house warming)

Readings

  • Old Testament Joshua 24:14–15 or Psalm 127:1
  • Epistle Colossians 3:12–17
  • Gospel Luke 19:1–10 or Luke 10:38–42

Word for the day

“…but as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD.” Joshua 24:15

Homily points · Short pastoral homily

1. Unless the Lord builds the house

“Unless the LORD builds the house, those who build it labor in vain” (Psalm 127:1).

2. Salvation came to Zacchaeus' house

“Today salvation has come to this house” (Luke 19:9): Jesus enters, and the household changes.

3. A domestic church

The home is where faith is first learnt: the prayer corner, the family rosary or evening prayer, the Bible on the table.

4. An open door

Hospitality to the poor and to guests is a blessing on the house.

Full homily

Dear family, today we bless this new home. Many years of work and saving are in these walls. But the psalm reminds us: “Unless the Lord builds the house, those who build it labor in vain.” Today you invite the Lord to be the true builder.

When Jesus entered the house of Zacchaeus, he said: “Today salvation has come to this house.” A house is blessed not only by holy water but by the presence of Christ. Let him enter every room: the kitchen, the bedroom, the study, and especially the place of family prayer.

Joshua stood before the people and said: “As for me and my household, we will serve the Lord.” Make that your family motto. Keep a prayer corner, pray together every evening, keep the Bible open, and let the children see their parents kneel.

And keep your door open to guests and to the poor. A house that welcomes is a house that God fills with blessing. Peace be to this house and to all who live in it. Amen.

Homily option 2 · The house of Martha and Mary

1. Jesus as a guest

Martha welcomed Jesus into her home (Luke 10:38).

2. Busy hands

Martha was distracted by much serving; homes can be too busy for God.

3. The better part

Mary sat at His feet and listened (Luke 10:39, 42).

4. Both together

A Christian home needs Martha's work and Mary's prayer.

Dear family, today we bless your new home. The Gospel tells us of a house in Bethany where Jesus loved to visit. Martha welcomed Him into her home (Luke 10:38). What a beautiful thing to say about a house: Jesus was welcome there.

Martha was busy with much serving. She was cooking and cleaning and worrying about getting everything right. There is nothing wrong with work; homes need it. But she became anxious and distracted, and even complained to Jesus about her sister (Luke 10:40). Many homes today are like that: full of activity, phones, television and tension, and too busy for God.

Mary sat at the feet of Jesus and listened. Jesus said that she had chosen the better part, which would not be taken from her (Luke 10:42). The better part is time with Him: a family prayer in the evening, the Bible on the table, a picture of the Sacred Heart or the Holy Family before which you gather.

A Christian home needs both Martha and Mary. It needs hands that serve: parents who work, children who help, meals shared together. And it needs hearts that listen: prayer together, forgiveness before sleep, Sunday Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana as a family. When work and prayer are together, a house becomes a home, and a home becomes a domestic church.

So as I bless these walls, I bless more than a building. I pray that Jesus may always be welcome here; that this door may open to guests and to the poor; that this table may be a place of peace; and that everyone who lives here may one day be welcomed into the house of the Father.

Homily option 3 · Teaching homily: sacramentals and the domestic church

1. What a blessing is

A sacramental by which the Church asks God's protection for persons and places (CCC 1667–1671).

2. Holy water and the cross

Signs that recall Baptism and the victory of Christ.

3. The domestic church

The family is the first school of prayer and virtue (CCC 1657, 2685).

4. Practical customs

Family prayer corner, enthronement of the Sacred Heart, grace before meals.

Dear family, today's blessing is what the Church calls a sacramental. Let us understand what this means, so that this day bears fruit in your home.

Sacramentals are sacred signs instituted by the Church. They do not give grace in the way the sacraments do, but through the prayer of the Church they prepare us to receive grace and sanctify different circumstances of life (CCC 1667–1670). Among them, blessings come first: the Church praises God and asks His protection for persons, places and things (CCC 1671).

When I sprinkle holy water in your rooms, it recalls your Baptism, when you were washed and made children of God. When we place the cross on your wall, it proclaims that Christ, not fear or bad luck, rules this house. No evil eye, no superstition and no fear has power over a home that belongs to Christ. So you do not need charms or omens; you need faith, prayer and the sacraments.

The Catechism calls the Christian family a domestic church, the first place where children learn to pray and to live the virtues (CCC 1657, 2685). Your home is meant to be a little church: where the faith is believed, prayed and lived every day.

Some practical suggestions: keep a prayer corner with the cross, an image of Our Lady and the Bible. Pray together in the evening, even briefly. Say grace before meals. Consider the enthronement of the Sacred Heart. Bless your children before they go out. And come together to the Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana every Sunday. Then this blessing will not end today; it will continue every day in your home.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, as you entered the house of Zacchaeus, enter this home. Bless all who live here and all who visit, keep sickness, quarrels and every evil far from it, and make it a home of prayer, peace and welcome. Amen.
