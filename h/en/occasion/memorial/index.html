Commemoration of the Departed (41st day, anniversary)

For the 41st-day remembrance, a death anniversary or a memorial Mass

Readings

  • Old Testament Wisdom 3:1–9
  • Epistle 2 Corinthians 5:1, 6–10
  • Gospel John 14:1–6 or John 6:37–40

Word for the day

“In my Father's house there are many dwelling places. If it were not so, would I have told you that I go to prepare a place for you?” John 14:2

Homily points · Short pastoral homily

1. Love does not end

Death separates us for a time; it does not break the communion of saints.

2. Why we offer the Eucharist

The Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana offered for the departed is the greatest help we can give them (CCC 1032, 1371).

3. Remember to imitate

Recall one virtue of the departed and ask the family to carry it on.

4. Our own preparation

Every remembrance is also a reminder: live today ready to meet the Lord.

Full homily

Dear family and friends, we have gathered again to remember N. The days have passed, the first tears have perhaps become quieter, but the love remains. And love, in Christ, does not end.

Jesus says: “In my Father's house there are many dwelling places.” Our dear ones have not disappeared; they have gone ahead. We believe in the communion of saints: those on earth, those being purified, and those in heaven form one family in Christ.

That is why we offer this Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana for N. The Church teaches that the Eucharistic sacrifice is offered also for the faithful departed, that they may enter the light and peace of Christ. Our prayer is the most precious gift we can still give.

Let us also remember to imitate. Think of one good thing N. did, and continue it in your own life. And let each of us live today in such a way that we are ready to meet the Lord. Eternal rest grant unto him (her), O Lord. Amen.

Homily option 2 · The many rooms of the Father's house

1. Do not let your hearts be troubled

Jesus speaks these words at the Last Supper, on the eve of His own death (John 14:1).

2. A place prepared

In the Father's house there are many dwelling places (John 14:2).

3. I will come again

Christ comes for each of us at death and at the end of time (John 14:3).

4. The way

Jesus Himself is the way to that house (John 14:6).

Dear family, it is some time now since N. left us. The first shock has passed, but the absence remains. At the table, at festivals, in small daily things, we notice that someone is missing. Today we gather to pray for N. and to let the word of Jesus comfort us again.

At the Last Supper, on the night before He died, Jesus said to His disciples: do not let your hearts be troubled (John 14:1). He knew that they would soon be shattered by grief. He did not deny that sorrow would come; He gave them a promise to hold on to.

In my Father's house there are many dwelling places, He said; I go to prepare a place for you (John 14:2). Think of what this means. Jesus speaks of heaven not as a cold idea, but as a home, a house with rooms, a family dwelling. We pray today that N. may be welcomed into that home, purified of every sin and filled with the joy of God.

Jesus added: I will come again and will take you to myself, so that where I am, there you may be also (John 14:3). The Christian does not die alone. Christ comes to meet each of His own. And when Thomas asked how they could know the way, Jesus answered: I am the way, and the truth, and the life (John 14:6).

So the best way to honour N. is to walk that way ourselves. Let us remember the good he (she) taught us and live it. Let us forgive whatever needs forgiving. Let us keep praying for him (her), especially in the Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana, and keep our own lamps ready. One day, God willing, the family that is divided today by death will be gathered again in the Father's house.

Homily option 3 · Teaching homily: the communion of saints

1. One family in three states

Pilgrims on earth, souls being purified, saints in glory: one Church (CCC 954).

2. Exchange of spiritual goods

Our prayer can help the dead; their intercession can help us (CCC 958).

3. Why the Eucharist

The Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana is the most powerful prayer for the departed (CCC 1371).

4. Remembering well

Anniversaries, alms, indulgences and a changed life.

Dear family and friends, in the Creed we say every Sunday: I believe in the communion of saints. Today, as we remember N., let us look at what those words mean.

The Church teaches that there is one family of God in three conditions: some of us are still pilgrims on earth; others have died and are being purified; others are in glory, contemplating God (CCC 954). Death does not break this family. It changes the way we are together, but it does not end our bond.

Within this family there is an exchange of gifts. We can help those who are being purified by our prayers, our sacrifices and our good works; and those who are with God can intercede for us (CCC 958, 1032). That is why we do not only remember N. with photographs and stories. We pray for him (her).

The greatest of all prayers is the Holy Mass / Holy Qurbana. In it the one sacrifice of Christ is made present, and the Church has always offered it for the living and the dead (CCC 1371). St Monica, dying far from home, asked her son Augustine only one thing: remember me at the altar of the Lord, wherever you are. That is still the best gift we can give our dead.

How else can we remember well? By giving alms in their name, by seeking indulgences for them, by visiting their grave with prayer, and above all by living the faith they handed on to us. Dear family, may N. rest in the peace of Christ, and may we all one day share with him (her) the joy of the saints.

Prayer

Lord, in your Father's house there are many dwelling places. Grant N. a place of light and peace, and keep us faithful, so that one day we may all be gathered together in your kingdom. Amen.
