Word for the day

“Did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see the glory of God?” John 11:40

Reflection

Today is the fortieth day since the Great Fast began on Monday 8 February. The Lord fasted forty days in the desert; Moses stayed forty days on the mountain; Elijah walked forty days to Horeb; and the Church, counting with them, keeps this Friday as the Fortieth Friday, Nalpatham Velli. Our forefathers in Kerala called the whole season the fifty-day fast, because it runs on through the Week of the Passion to Easter; the forty days are complete today, and what remains is the road with Christ to Jerusalem. In many parishes the Way of the Cross is made with special devotion today, and some climb a hill shrine on foot, carrying a small cross. In 2027 this day is also the feast of St Joseph, the just man who guarded the Lord's childhood. It is a fitting meeting: the man of silence who obeyed in the night stands at the door of the week in which his foster Son will obey the Father unto death.

The first reading is short and quiet. After the destruction of Sodom, “Abraham went early in the morning to the place where he had stood before the Lord; and he looked down toward Sodom and Gomorrah and toward all the land of the Plain and saw the smoke of the land going up like the smoke of a furnace.” The day before, at that very place, he had bargained with God for the city: fifty righteous, forty-five, forty, thirty, twenty, ten. Not even ten were found. Yet his prayer was not lost: “So it was that, when God destroyed the cities of the Plain, God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow.” No intercession is wasted. The second reading tells of a near civil war. The tribes who had settled east of the Jordan built a great altar by the river, and the other tribes, fearing rebellion and a second altar against the Lord's, gathered at Shiloh to make war. They sent Phinehas and ten chiefs to ask: “What is this treachery that you have committed against the God of Israel?” The rest of the chapter tells that the altar was meant only as a witness that both sides of the river belonged to the one Lord, and war was avoided because they spoke before they fought. Paul gives the Christian rule for every such tension: “Welcome one another, therefore, just as Christ has welcomed you, for the glory of God.” He ends with a blessing worth praying every day of Holy Week: “May the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, so that you may abound in hope by the power of the Holy Spirit.”

The Gospel brings us to Bethany, to the tomb of Lazarus, the last and greatest sign in John's Gospel before the Passion. “Then Jesus, again greatly disturbed, came to the tomb. It was a cave, and a stone was lying against it. Jesus said, ‘Take away the stone.’” Martha, practical as always, protests: “Lord, already there is a stench because he has been dead four days.” Jesus answers with the word that is today's key: “Did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see the glory of God?” The stone is rolled away. Jesus lifts his eyes and thanks the Father aloud, “so that they may believe that you sent me.” Then “he cried with a loud voice, ‘Lazarus, come out!’ The dead man came out, his hands and feet bound with strips of cloth, and his face wrapped in a cloth. Jesus said to them, ‘Unbind him, and let him go.’” Lazarus returns to ordinary life and will die again; it is a sign, not yet the Resurrection. But it points to the Easter that is coming, when the stone will be rolled away from another tomb, and the grave cloths will be left lying, because the One who comes out will never die again. Notice too that Jesus gives others a part: they must take away the stone, and they must unbind him. The Church does this still. Christ alone gives life; through the ministry of his Church the bonds of sin are loosed in the sacrament of reconciliation. On this Fortieth Friday, what stone needs to be rolled away, and what bands still hold us?

The readings of St Joseph speak of a man who did not fear. “Do not fear, for I am with you, do not be afraid, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, I will help you, I will uphold you with my victorious right hand,” says the Lord through Isaiah. Joseph needed that word. He found the woman he was to marry with child, and “being a righteous man and unwilling to expose her to public disgrace, planned to dismiss her quietly.” Then the angel came in a dream: “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary as your wife, for the child conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit. She will bear a son, and you are to name him Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins.” And the Gospel says simply: “When Joseph awoke from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him.” Not one word of Joseph is recorded in the Gospels. He speaks through obedience, work and care. He names the child Jesus, “the Lord saves”, and so gives the Son of God his place in the house of David. Pope Francis called him a father in the shadows, and Pope Pius IX declared him Patron of the Universal Church. In many Kerala homes and parishes his feast is marked by a meal for the poor, the oottu, where an elderly man, a woman and a child are honoured at table in memory of the Holy Family of Nazareth. As we enter the Week of the Passion, Joseph teaches us to do what the Lord asks, without speeches, and to trust that “God is with us”, even in the night.

Homily outline

Theme: “Did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see the glory of God?”

On the Fortieth Friday and the feast of St Joseph: Abraham's intercession remembered, a quarrel healed by speaking, Lazarus called from the tomb, and the just man who obeyed in the night.

1. God remembered Abraham

  • Smoke over the plain.
  • The prayer that seemed lost.
  • No intercession is wasted.
“So it was that, when God destroyed the cities of the Plain, God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow, when he overthrew the cities in which Lot had settled.” Genesis 19:29
“Therefore confess your sins to one another, and pray for one another, so that you may be healed. The prayer of the righteous is powerful and effective.” James 5:16

Apply: Pray every day of the Week of the Passion for three people far from God, by name.

2. Welcome one another

  • An altar by the Jordan.
  • Speaking before fighting.
  • Christ who welcomed us.
“Have we not all one father? Has not one God created us? Why then are we faithless to one another, profaning the covenant of our ancestors?” Malachi 2:10
“Welcome one another, therefore, just as Christ has welcomed you, for the glory of God.” Romans 15:7

Apply: Before you judge someone's actions this week, go and ask them what they meant.

3. Lazarus, come out!

  • Take away the stone.
  • The loud voice of the Son.
  • Unbind him and let him go.
“Therefore prophesy, and say to them, Thus says the Lord God: I am going to open your graves, and bring you up from your graves, O my people; and I will bring you back to the land of Israel.” Ezekiel 37:12
“The dead man came out, his hands and feet bound with strips of cloth, and his face wrapped in a cloth. Jesus said to them, “Unbind him, and let him go.”” John 11:44

Apply: Make your Easter confession before Pesaha, and let the Lord unbind you.

4. Joseph did as the angel commanded

  • A righteous man in the night.
  • Do not be afraid.
  • Obedience without words.
“do not fear, for I am with you, do not be afraid, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, I will help you, I will uphold you with my victorious right hand.” Isaiah 41:10
“When Joseph awoke from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him; he took her as his wife,” Matthew 1:24

Apply: Do today, without delay or complaint, the one thing you know God is asking of you.

The forty days are complete: God remembers our prayers, heals our quarrels and calls us out of our graves.

With St Joseph, let us enter the Week of the Passion obeying in silence and trusting that God is with us.

Take-home resolution: Make the Way of the Cross today, go to confession before Pesaha, and share a meal with the poor in honour of the Holy Family.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today the forty days of the Great Fast are complete. On this Fortieth Friday, and on the feast of St Joseph, the word of God speaks of prayer, peace, life and obedience.

Abraham goes back to the place where he had pleaded with God for Sodom and sees only smoke. But Scripture says: God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out of the midst of the overthrow (Genesis 19:29). No prayer for another person is ever wasted.

When the tribes east of the Jordan built an altar, the rest of Israel almost went to war. But they spoke first, and the misunderstanding was healed. Paul tells us: welcome one another, therefore, just as Christ has welcomed you (Romans 15:7).

At the tomb of Lazarus, Jesus says: take away the stone. Martha fears the stench of death. Jesus answers: did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see the glory of God? (John 11:40). Then he cries: Lazarus, come out! And he tells the people: unbind him, and let him go.

Christ alone gives life; through his Church he unbinds us. In these days, let us come to the sacrament of reconciliation and let the bands of sin be loosed. Lazarus points to Easter, when Christ will rise never to die again.

St Joseph never speaks in the Gospels. When the angel told him in a dream not to be afraid, he woke up and did as the angel of the Lord commanded (Matthew 1:24). As we enter the Week of the Passion, let us learn from him to obey without delay and to trust that God is with us. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus called out loud to his friend Lazarus who had died: Lazarus, come out! And he came out alive! Jesus is stronger than death.

Today we also remember St Joseph, who took care of Jesus and Mary. Let us help our fathers and mothers today, just as St Joseph helped his family.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, resurrection and life, you wept at the tomb of your friend and called him out with a loud voice. On this fortieth day of our fast, roll away the stones that shut us in, call us out of every grave of sin, and let your Church unbind us and set us free. Through the prayers of St Joseph, the just man who did all that the angel commanded, give us his silent obedience and his fearless trust, and bring us with him to the glory of God. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory for ever. Amen.
