Word for the day

“Bear fruits worthy of repentance.” Luke 3:8

Reflection

Peter's language about the false teachers is some of the strongest in the New Testament. They are “like irrational animals, mere creatures of instinct”. “They have eyes full of adultery, insatiable for sin. They entice unsteady souls. They have hearts trained in greed.” He compares them to Balaam, the prophet who “loved the wages of doing wrong” and had to be corrected by his own donkey. And then comes an image every farmer in Kerala understands at the end of a dry summer: “These are waterless springs and mists driven by a storm.” A spring that promises water and gives none is worse than no spring at all; the traveller walks to it and finds dust. A mist promises rain and blows away. Peter is speaking of teachers, but the warning reaches every Christian. We can look like a spring: baptised, confirmed, present at the feasts, with a holy picture in the house. And yet, when a thirsty person comes to us, a lonely neighbour, a child with questions, a poor man at the gate, do they find water? “Hearts trained in greed” is a striking phrase: greed is learned by practice, and so is generosity. The fast is a training of the heart in the other direction.

In the Gospel John the Baptist speaks to the crowds who came out to be baptised. He refuses to let them rest on their ancestry: “Bear fruits worthy of repentance. Do not begin to say to yourselves, ‘We have Abraham as our ancestor’; for I tell you, God is able from these stones to raise up children to Abraham.” We could say, “We are St Thomas Christians; our families have been Christian for centuries.” It is a great gift, but it cannot replace fruit. “Even now the ax is lying at the root of the trees.” Then the crowd asks the most honest question in the Gospel: “What then should we do?” John's answer is not mystical; it is practical and almost embarrassing in its simplicity: “Whoever has two coats must share with anyone who has none; and whoever has food must do likewise.” Most of us have more than two shirts, and food in the kitchen tonight. The fruit of repentance can be counted in clothes given away and meals shared. The Latin Church today remembers St Cyril of Jerusalem, whose instructions to those about to be baptised at Easter still survive; like John, he taught that conversion must show itself in a changed life.

Homily outline

Theme: “Bear fruits worthy of repentance.”

Waterless springs that promise and do not give, and the Baptist's plain answer: share your coat and your bread.

1. Waterless springs

  • Promise without gift.
  • Hearts trained in greed.
  • Training the heart the other way.
“for my people have committed two evils: they have forsaken me, the fountain of living water, and dug out cisterns for themselves, cracked cisterns that can hold no water.” Jeremiah 2:13
“These are waterless springs and mists driven by a storm; for them the deepest darkness has been reserved.” 2 Peter 2:17

Apply: Ask yourself honestly what a thirsty person would find in you today, and give it.

2. Fruits worthy of repentance

  • Not resting on Abraham.
  • The ax at the root.
  • Heritage that bears fruit.
“Wash yourselves; make yourselves clean; remove the evil of your doings from before my eyes; cease to do evil, learn to do good; seek justice, rescue the oppressed, defend the orphan, plead for the widow.” Isaiah 1:16–17
“Bear fruits worthy of repentance. Do not begin to say to yourselves, ‘We have Abraham as our ancestor’; for I tell you, God is able from these stones to raise up children to Abraham.” Luke 3:8

Apply: Thank God for the faith of your family, and add one fruit of your own to it this week.

3. Two coats

  • What then should we do?
  • A practical answer.
  • Counted in clothes and meals.
“Is it not to share your bread with the hungry, and bring the homeless poor into your house; when you see the naked, to cover them, and not to hide yourself from your own kin?” Isaiah 58:7
“In reply he said to them, “Whoever has two coats must share with anyone who has none; and whoever has food must do likewise.”” Luke 3:11

Apply: Give away clothes you do not wear, and share one meal with someone alone, before Pesaha.

A Christian who promises and does not give is a waterless spring; the fast trains our hearts in generosity.

Our heritage is a gift; the fruit of repentance is a shared coat and a shared meal.

Take-home resolution: Before Pesaha, empty one shelf for the poor and invite one lonely person to your table.

Full homily

Dear friends, Peter describes false teachers with a striking image: they are waterless springs and mists driven by a storm (2 Peter 2:17). They promise water and give dust.

That warning is for us too. We can look like a spring, baptised and present at every feast, and still give nothing to the thirsty who come to us. Greed is learned by practice, and so is generosity. The fast trains our hearts.

John the Baptist tells the crowds: bear fruits worthy of repentance, and do not say, we have Abraham as our ancestor (Luke 3:8). We cannot rest on our Christian heritage. When they ask, what then should we do, he says: whoever has two coats must share with anyone who has none; and whoever has food must do likewise (Luke 3:11).

Let us count our shirts and our meals this week, and share them. That is the fruit of repentance. Amen.

For children

Children, John the Baptist said: if you have two shirts, give one to someone who has none. Look in your cupboard today. Is there something you can give to a child who needs it?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, fountain of living water, do not let us be waterless springs. Train our hearts in generosity instead of greed. Free us from resting on our heritage, and make us bear fruits worthy of repentance, sharing our coats and our bread with those who have none. Amen.
