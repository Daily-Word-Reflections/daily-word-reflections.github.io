Readings

  • Epistle 2 Corinthians 11:1–6 · I promised you in marriage to one husband, Christ
  • Gospel John 7:14–24 · Do not judge by appearances

Word for the day

“Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment.” John 7:24

Reflection

Paul writes to the Corinthians like a father who has arranged his daughter's marriage and now fears for her. “I feel a divine jealousy for you, for I promised you in marriage to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ.” In the customs of the time, the friend of the bridegroom arranged the betrothal and watched over the bride until the wedding day. Paul sees himself in that role: by his preaching he betrothed the Church of Corinth to Christ, and he longs to present her pure on the day of the Lord's coming. His jealousy is “divine”, the jealousy of God himself, who in the prophets calls Israel his bride: “I will take you for my wife forever; I will take you for my wife in righteousness and in justice, in steadfast love, and in mercy” (Hosea 2:19). The Church is not a club of the like-minded; she is a bride, loved and promised to one Husband. Our Syro-Malabar liturgy speaks often of the Church as the bride of Christ, and the crowning of bride and groom at a wedding is a living picture of that mystery.

Then comes the fear: “But I am afraid that as the serpent deceived Eve by its cunning, your thoughts will be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to Christ.” The danger is not that the Corinthians will abandon religion; it is that they will accept “another Jesus”, “a different spirit”, “a different gospel”, and hardly notice. Certain preachers, whom Paul calls with irony “these super-apostles”, had arrived with fine speech and impressive letters. They offered a Jesus without the Cross, a spirit without humility, a gospel of success. Paul admits, “I may be untrained in speech, but not in knowledge.” The question is never how eloquent the preacher is, but whether the Jesus he preaches is the Jesus of the apostles. In this last week of the Season of the Apostles the warning is timely. Our phones bring us a new preacher every hour; some promise wealth and healing as a reward for money, some mock the Church and the sacraments, some offer a Jesus who never asks for repentance. The bride keeps her devotion sincere and pure by staying with the faith of the apostles, handed down in the Church.

The Gospel takes us to Jerusalem at the Festival of Booths. Jesus goes up to the Temple and teaches, and those who hear him are astonished: “How does this man have such learning, when he has never been taught?” He had not studied under a famous rabbi. In John's Gospel the phrase “the Jews” often refers to the religious authorities of Jerusalem who opposed Jesus; it never condemns the Jewish people as a whole, to whom Jesus, his Mother and all the apostles belonged. Jesus answers: “My teaching is not mine but his who sent me.” Here is the test of every true teacher. “Those who speak on their own seek their own glory; but the one who seeks the glory of him who sent him is true.” Jesus himself, the eternal Word, does not present his teaching as his own invention; it is the Father's. How much more must every preacher, priest and catechist in the Church hand on what was received, and not his own opinions.

Then Jesus speaks of the healing he had done on a sabbath (John 5), which had caused such anger. He reasons with them from their own practice: if a boy may be circumcised on the sabbath so that the law of Moses is kept, why be angry that he made a man's whole body well on the sabbath? And he ends with the line that is today's word: “Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment.” The crowd judged by appearances: the man had no degree, so he could not be a teacher; the healing happened on the wrong day, so it could not be from God. We do the same when we judge people by colour, caste, accent, dress, bank balance or the size of their house, or when we decide about a marriage proposal by a photograph. The Lord said to Samuel, “they look on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart” (1 Samuel 16:7). On this Saturday we look to Mary, the Virgin who was wholly devoted to Christ, whose humble appearance in Nazareth hid the purest heart of any creature. She teaches the Church to be the chaste bride Paul longed for, and to see others as God sees them.

Homily outline

Theme: “Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment.”

The Church as the bride promised to Christ, the danger of another Jesus and another gospel, and the right judgment that sees the heart.

1. Promised in marriage to one husband

  • The friend of the bridegroom.
  • A divine jealousy.
  • The Church, a bride and not a club.
“And I will take you for my wife forever; I will take you for my wife in righteousness and in justice, in steadfast love, and in mercy.” Hosea 2:19
“I feel a divine jealousy for you, for I promised you in marriage to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ.” 2 Corinthians 11:2

Apply: Renew your baptismal promises quietly in prayer one evening this week.

2. Another Jesus, another gospel

  • Fine speech and super-apostles.
  • A Jesus without the Cross.
  • Staying with the faith of the apostles.
“Thus says the Lord of hosts: Do not listen to the words of the prophets who prophesy to you; they are deluding you. They speak visions of their own minds, not from the mouth of the Lord.” Jeremiah 23:16
“But I am afraid that as the serpent deceived Eve by its cunning, your thoughts will be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to Christ.” 2 Corinthians 11:3

Apply: Test one religious message you receive this week by the Creed and the teaching of the Church.

3. Not by appearances

  • My teaching is not mine.
  • The healing on the sabbath.
  • God looks on the heart.
“But the Lord said to Samuel, “Do not look on his appearance or on the height of his stature, because I have rejected him; for the Lord does not see as mortals see; they look on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart.”” 1 Samuel 16:7
“Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment.” John 7:24

Apply: Do one kind act for someone you had judged by appearance.

We are a bride promised to Christ: let our devotion be sincere and pure, faithful to the Jesus of the apostles.

Like Mary, let us see as God sees, looking on the heart.

Take-home resolution: This week, renew your love for Christ, test every new voice by the faith of the Church, and correct one rash judgment by an act of kindness.

Full homily

Dear friends, Saint Paul writes to the Corinthians with the love of a father who has arranged his daughter's marriage: “I promised you in marriage to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ” (2 Corinthians 11:2). The Church is the bride of Christ, and each of us belongs to that bride.

Paul is afraid. Clever preachers have come with fine words and a different Jesus: a Jesus without the Cross, a gospel of success. The Corinthians accepted them easily. We too hear many voices today, on our phones and in new groups. Let us test every voice by the faith of the apostles that the Church has kept.

In the Gospel, people are surprised: how can Jesus teach so well when he has not studied? Jesus says: “My teaching is not mine but his who sent me” (John 7:16). A true teacher does not seek his own glory but God's.

Then he says: “Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment” (John 7:24). We often judge by appearance: by colour, caste, dress, money, or a photograph. But God looks at the heart.

On this Saturday let us look to Mary, the humble Virgin of Nazareth, wholly devoted to Christ. May she keep our love for her Son sincere and pure, and give us eyes that see others as God sees them. Amen.

For children

Children, some people did not believe Jesus because he had not gone to a famous school. They looked only at the outside. Jesus said: do not judge by appearances! God looks at the heart. This week, be kind to someone others laugh at because of how they look.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Bridegroom of the Church, you loved her and gave yourself up for her. Keep us, your bride, in a sincere and pure devotion to you. Guard us from every other Jesus, every other spirit and every other gospel, and keep us faithful to the teaching of your apostles. You did not seek your own glory but the glory of the Father who sent you: make us seek his glory, not ours. Give us eyes that do not judge by appearances but see the heart as you see it. Through the prayers of the Blessed Virgin Mary, wholly devoted to you, make us holy and blameless for the day of your coming. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory for ever. Amen.
