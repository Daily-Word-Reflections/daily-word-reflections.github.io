Word for the day

“And we know that the Son of God has come and has given us understanding so that we may know him who is true; and we are in him who is true, in his Son Jesus Christ. He is the true God and eternal life.” 1 John 5:20

Reflection

John ends his first letter by telling us why he wrote it: “so that you may know that you have eternal life.” Christians are not meant to live in anxious guessing. We have “boldness” before God: “if we ask anything according to his will, he hears us.” Then John gives a surprising example of such prayer: praying for a brother or sister who sins. He distinguishes sin that is mortal from sin that is not, the distinction the Church still teaches between mortal and venial sin, and urges us to intercede so that God “will give life” to the one who has fallen. “We know that those who are born of God do not sin”: not that they never stumble, but that they do not make sin their home, because the Son of God protects them. The letter closes with a revelation fit for Denha, “He is the true God and eternal life”, and a final warning: “Little children, keep yourselves from idols.”

In the Gospel Jesus' hearers claim a famous ancestry: “Abraham is our father.” Jesus answers that children resemble their father: “If you were Abraham's children, you would be doing what Abraham did.” Abraham believed and welcomed God's messengers; they are planning to kill the one who tells them the truth. Belonging to God is not a matter of birth certificate but of listening: “Whoever is from God hears the words of God.” The devil, Jesus says, “is a liar and the father of lies.” Every lie we tell, every half-truth we live by, is a small idol. Saturday, the eve of the Lord's Day, is a good day to ask whose children we resemble, to pray for one another's sins, and to prepare for Sunday through the sacrament of Reconciliation.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whoever is from God hears the words of God.”

Confident prayer, intercession for sinners, and the true children of Abraham.

Full homily

Dear friends, John writes so that you may know that you have eternal life (1 John 5:13). We can pray with boldness, because if we ask anything according to his will, he hears us.

John even tells us what to pray for: a brother or sister who sins. He distinguishes mortal sin from sin that is not mortal, as the Church still does, and promises that God will give life to the one for whom we pray. Instead of judging, we intercede.

In the Gospel Jesus tells those who boast of Abraham: if you were Abraham's children, you would be doing what Abraham did (John 8:39). And: whoever is from God hears the words of God (John 8:47).

Tomorrow is the Lord's Day. Today let us keep ourselves from idols and from lies, pray for one another, and prepare our hearts through confession. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says God's children listen to God and tell the truth. Today, let us pray for someone who has done wrong, and say sorry for our own mistakes.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, true God and eternal life, you have come to give us understanding so that we may know the Father. Keep us from every idol and every lie, hear our prayer for our brothers and sisters who have sinned, and make us true children of Abraham who hear the words of God. Amen.
