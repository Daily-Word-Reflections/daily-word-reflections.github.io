Third Saturday of Denha

Word for the day

“Then Jesus answered her, “Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish.” And her daughter was healed instantly.” Matthew 15:28

Reflection

Paul writes to the Philippians from prison with one wish: “live your life in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ”, so that he may hear that they are “standing firm in one spirit, striving side by side with one mind for the faith of the gospel, and are in no way intimidated by your opponents.” He even calls suffering a gift: “he has graciously granted you the privilege not only of believing in Christ, but of suffering for him as well”. In this Unity Octave his words are a plan for all Christians: not rivals, but companions side by side for the one Gospel.

The Gospel shows a faith that will not give up. A Canaanite woman, a foreigner, cries: “Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David; my daughter is tormented by a demon.” Jesus does not answer; the disciples want her sent away; Jesus says he was sent to the lost sheep of Israel. She kneels: “Lord, help me.” Even the hard saying about the children's food does not stop her: “Yes, Lord, yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters’ table.” Jesus replies: “Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish.” Denha celebrates Christ revealed to the nations; here a pagan mother teaches the disciples what faith looks like: humble, persistent, and sure that a crumb from Christ is enough.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul writes from prison to the Philippians: live your life in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ, standing firm in one spirit, striving side by side with one mind for the faith of the gospel (Philippians 1:27). In this Unity Octave, that is God's word to all Christians.

He even says that suffering for Christ is a privilege, a gift of grace (Philippians 1:29). Christians who suffer together for the Gospel discover how much unites them.

In the Gospel a Canaanite woman cries: have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David. Jesus does not answer. The disciples want her sent away. She kneels and says only: Lord, help me (Matthew 15:25). And when Jesus speaks of the children's food, she answers: yes, Lord, yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table.

Jesus says: woman, great is your faith! (Matthew 15:28). She is a foreigner, yet she shows the disciples what faith is: humble, persistent, sure that a crumb from Christ is enough. When God seems silent, let us not give up. Let us say with her: Lord, help me. Amen.

For children

Children, a mother kept asking Jesus to help her sick daughter, even when it seemed he said no. Jesus said: great is your faith! Let us never stop praying. Say with her today: Lord, help me!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on us. Give us the faith of the Canaanite woman, humble and persistent, and help us to live in a manner worthy of your Gospel, standing firm in one spirit with all who believe in you. Amen.
