First Tuesday of Denha · Eve of Denha

5 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Readings

  • Epistle Titus 3:8–11 · Devote yourselves to good works
  • Gospel Luke 13:23–30 · Strive to enter through the narrow door

Word for the day

“Strive to enter through the narrow door; for many, I tell you, will try to enter and will not be able.” Luke 13:24

Reflection

On the eve of Denha St Paul gives Titus a short rule for the baptised. Just before today's passage he has spoken of “the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit”; now he adds: “The saying is sure. I desire that you insist on these things, so that those who have come to believe in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works”. Baptism is not a certificate to be filed away; it is a new life to be lived in deeds. Paul also warns against the opposite: “stupid controversies, genealogies, dissensions, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and worthless.” A Christian community can waste its strength in endless arguments while the poor wait at the door.

In the Gospel someone asks Jesus: “Lord, will only a few be saved?” Jesus does not feed curiosity; he speaks to the heart: “Strive to enter through the narrow door”. To have eaten and drunk in his presence is not enough; what counts is a life he can recognise as his own. Yet the door is not narrow because God is mean. The same Gospel promises that “people will come from east and west, from north and south, and will eat in the kingdom of God.” Tomorrow, at the Jordan, Jesus himself goes down into the water and opens the door. Our ancestors kept this night with lights on plantain stems, crying El Paiya, God is light, and with a bath in river or pond recalling the Jordan. Tonight let us ask for the grace to enter by the door he opens.

Homily outline

Theme: “Strive to enter through the narrow door.”

Good works, not quarrels; the narrow door opened at the Jordan.

1. Devoted to good works

  • The saying is sure.
  • Faith shows itself in deeds.
  • Excellent and profitable to everyone.
“learn to do good; seek justice, rescue the oppressed, defend the orphan, plead for the widow.” Isaiah 1:17
“The saying is sure. I desire that you insist on these things, so that those who have come to believe in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works; these things are excellent and profitable to everyone.” Titus 3:8

Apply: Do one concrete good work for a neighbour this week.

2. Leave the quarrels

  • Controversies and genealogies.
  • Unprofitable and worthless.
  • Unity is a work of faith.
“The beginning of strife is like letting out water; so stop before the quarrel breaks out.” Proverbs 17:14
“But avoid stupid controversies, genealogies, dissensions, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and worthless.” Titus 3:9

Apply: Let go of one argument you keep returning to.

3. The narrow door

  • Will only a few be saved?
  • Strive to enter.
  • From east and west to the feast.
“This is the gate of the LORD; the righteous shall enter through it.” Psalm 118:20
“Strive to enter through the narrow door; for many, I tell you, will try to enter and will not be able.” Luke 13:24

Apply: Light a lamp tonight and pray for all who seek the door of faith.

Tomorrow Christ goes down into the Jordan and the door of heaven opens.

Let us enter with lamps lit and hands full of good works.

Take-home resolution: Keep the Denha eve with a lamp, family prayer and one good work.

Full homily

Dear friends, tonight is the eve of Denha. St Paul gives Titus a rule for the baptised: those who have come to believe in God must be careful to devote themselves to good works; these things are excellent and profitable to everyone (Titus 3:8). The faith we received in Baptism is meant to be seen in our hands.

Paul also warns against what drains a community's strength: stupid controversies, genealogies, dissensions and quarrels, which are unprofitable and worthless (Titus 3:9). How many families and parishes lose years in such arguments, while the poor wait at the door.

In the Gospel someone asks Jesus: Lord, will only a few be saved? Jesus gives no number. He says: strive to enter through the narrow door (Luke 13:23–24). It is not enough to say, we ate and drank with you. The Lord looks for a life he can recognise. And yet he promises that people will come from east and west, from north and south, and will eat in the kingdom of God (Luke 13:29).

Tomorrow Jesus goes down into the Jordan, and the door of heaven opens. Our ancestors kept this night with lamps on plantain stems, crying El Paiya, God is light, and with a bath that recalled the Jordan. Let us light a lamp in our hearts tonight, put aside one quarrel and do one good work, so that we may enter by the door he opens. Amen.

For children

Children, tonight is the eve of Denha! In many homes people light lamps and say: God is light! Light a lamp with your family, and do one good thing for someone before you go to sleep.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, tomorrow you go down into the Jordan to open for us the door of life. Keep me from empty quarrels, make me eager for good works, and give me strength to enter through the narrow door into the feast of your kingdom. Amen.
