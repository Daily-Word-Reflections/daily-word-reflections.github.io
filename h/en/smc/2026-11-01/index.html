Reflection

The Syro-Malabar year ends as it began: looking at the Church. For four weeks, Qudash Edtha, the Dedication of the Church, invites us to contemplate the Church as the Bride whom Christ will present to the Father, and as God's dwelling among his people. The readings follow that dwelling from the desert to the end of time.

In the desert Moses pitches the tent of meeting outside the camp. Whoever seeks the Lord goes out to it; the pillar of cloud comes down to its door, and “the LORD used to speak to Moses face to face, as one speaks to a friend.” God wants friendship with his people, and he chooses a place to meet them.

The Letter to the Hebrews tells us what that tent was pointing to. Christ, the high priest, has entered “the greater and perfect tent, not made with hands”, not with the blood of animals but with his own blood. The Church on earth is the doorway to that sanctuary: every Qurbana is our entry, with Christ, into the Holy of Holies.

The Gospel adds the note of the season: readiness. Ten virgins wait for the bridegroom; five have oil, five do not. The door opens for those who are ready, and then it is shut. The Church is the Bride waiting for the Bridegroom. Oil cannot be borrowed at the last minute: it is faith, prayer and love stored up day by day. Isaiah promises that those who wait for the Lord will not grow weary. The waiting itself becomes strength.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, today our liturgical year enters its last season, Qudash Edtha, the Dedication of the Church. For four weeks we look at the Church herself: the house where God lives with us and the Bride who waits for the Bridegroom.

In the first reading Moses pitches a tent outside the camp and calls it the tent of meeting. The cloud comes down, and the Lord speaks to Moses face to face, as one speaks to a friend. God does not want only servants; he wants friends. The Church is our tent of meeting, and every prayer is a conversation between friends.

The Letter to the Hebrews tells us that the tent in the desert was only a shadow. Christ has entered the true sanctuary, not made with hands, with his own blood. When we come to the Holy Qurbana, we are not spectators in a building. With Christ we enter the presence of the Father.

And the Gospel asks the question of this season: are you ready? Ten young women wait for the bridegroom. All have lamps; only five have oil. When the cry comes at midnight, the oil cannot be borrowed. Faith, prayer and love are not things we can collect from others at the last minute. They are stored day by day.

So this week, store oil. Come to the tent of meeting. Speak to the Lord as a friend. Those who wait for the Lord, says Isaiah, shall renew their strength. May our lamps be burning when he comes. Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, with Qudash Edtha our liturgical year reaches its final season. For four weeks the Church contemplates herself: not with pride, but with wonder at what Christ has made her. The Fathers of the East loved to speak of the Church as the Bride of Christ, and this season is a time to look at her beauty.

St Paul writes that Christ loved the Church and gave Himself up for her, in order to make her holy, so as to present the Church to Himself in splendour, without a spot or wrinkle (Ephesians 5:25–27). The beauty of the Church is not in her buildings or her wealth, but in her holiness, the holiness of Christ given to her in the sacraments, and visible in the lives of the saints. Each of us is part of that Bride.

Today's Gospel tells of ten young women waiting for the bridegroom with lamps (Matthew 25:1–13). Five were wise and brought extra oil; five were foolish and did not. When the bridegroom came at midnight, the foolish asked to borrow oil, but the wise could not give it. Some things cannot be borrowed: personal faith, a life of prayer, love built up day by day. No one can pray for me in my place. The oil must be my own.

The Letter to the Hebrews shows us the true High Priest. Christ entered once for all into the heavenly sanctuary, not with the blood of goats and calves, but with His own blood (Hebrews 9:11–12). In the tent of meeting Moses spoke with God face to face as a friend (Exodus 33:11); in Christ every believer is invited into that friendship. Every Holy Qurbana is a share in the worship of heaven, where the Bridegroom offers Himself to the Father.

So in this season let us renew our love for the Church. Pray for her, defend her, serve her, and do not be ashamed of her, even when her members, ourselves included, are sinful. And let us keep our lamps burning, so that when the Bridegroom comes, we may go in with Him to the wedding feast.

Dear brothers and sisters, during the season of Qudash Edtha our Church invites us to reflect on the mystery of the Church herself. What is the Church? Many people think of buildings, or of priests and bishops, or of an organisation. All of these belong to the Church, but she is much more.

The Second Vatican Council begins its teaching on the Church by saying that the Church, in Christ, is like a sacrament: a sign and instrument of communion with God and of the unity of the whole human race (Lumen Gentium 1). She is at once visible and spiritual, a human community and a divine mystery (CCC 771). The Fathers said that as Eve was formed from the side of the sleeping Adam, the Church was born from the pierced side of Christ asleep on the Cross (CCC 766; John 19:34).

The Bible gives many images of the Church. She is the People of God, a priestly people called out of every nation (CCC 781–786). She is the Body of Christ, in which each member has a gift for the good of all (1 Corinthians 12:12–27; CCC 787–796). She is the Temple of the Holy Spirit, who gives her life and unity (CCC 797–801). And she is the Bride, loved by Christ and waiting for Him.

The Catholic Church is a communion of Churches: the Latin Church and the Eastern Catholic Churches, each with its own liturgy, spirituality, theology and discipline, all united with the Bishop of Rome (Orientalium Ecclesiarum 2–3). Our Syro-Malabar Church, which traces its faith to the preaching of the apostle Thomas, is one of these Churches sui iuris. Its East Syriac liturgy and its heritage are a treasure not only for us but for the whole Church.

So let us love the Church: the universal Church, and our own Church with its heritage. Let us pray for the Pope, our Major Archbishop and our bishops. Let us use our gifts in our parish. And let us remember that to love the Church is to love Christ, who is her Head and her Bridegroom.
