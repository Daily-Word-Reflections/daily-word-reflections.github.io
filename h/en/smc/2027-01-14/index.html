Second Thursday of Denha · St Devasahayam

14 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle 1 Peter 5:5–9 · Cast all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you
  • Gospel Mark 4:35–41 · Even the wind and the sea obey him

Word for the day

“Resist him, steadfast in your faith, for you know that your brothers and sisters in all the world are undergoing the same kinds of suffering.” 1 Peter 5:9

Reflection

At evening, after a long day of teaching, Jesus says to his disciples: “Let us go across to the other side.” On the lake “a great windstorm arose, and the waves beat into the boat”, while Jesus is “in the stern, asleep on the cushion”. The frightened disciples wake him: “Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing?” He rises, rebukes the wind and says to the sea: “Peace! Be still!” Then comes the question for every age of the Church: “Why are you afraid? Have you still no faith?” The disciples are left wondering: “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?” This is a true Denha: the one asleep in their boat is the Lord of creation, the God who “made the storm be still” (Psalm 107:29).

Peter, who was in that boat, later writes to Christians facing persecution: “Cast all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you.” The disciples' cry, do you not care, receives its answer: he cares. But Peter is realistic: “Like a roaring lion your adversary the devil prowls around, looking for someone to devour. Resist him, steadfast in your faith.” Today the Church in India honours St Devasahayam, who lived this word. Born Neelakanta Pillai, an official in the service of the kingdom of Travancore, he was baptised in 1745 and took the name Lazarus, Devasahayam. After his Baptism he refused to treat anyone as untouchable; for his faith he was arrested, paraded through towns and kept in chains for nearly three years, and at Aralvaimozhy in 1752 he was shot dead. The storm did not cease for him on earth, but Christ was in his boat, and his faith did not fail. The first reading of his memorial says it: “whoever believes in him will not be put to shame.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Why are you afraid? Have you still no faith?”

Christ in the storm, the care of God, and the steadfast faith of St Devasahayam.

1. Peace! Be still!

  • A great windstorm.
  • Jesus asleep on the cushion.
  • The Lord of wind and sea.
“he made the storm be still, and the waves of the sea were hushed.” Psalm 107:29
“He woke up and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Peace! Be still!” Then the wind ceased, and there was a dead calm.” Mark 4:39

Apply: Name one storm in your life and bring it to Jesus.

2. Cast all your anxiety on him

  • Do you not care?
  • Yes, he cares.
  • Humble under God's mighty hand.
“Cast your burden on the LORD, and he will sustain you; he will never permit the righteous to be moved.” Psalm 55:22
“Cast all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you.” 1 Peter 5:7

Apply: Each night this week hand one worry to the Lord.

  • The roaring lion.
  • St Devasahayam in chains.
  • Not put to shame.

“as it is written, “See, I am laying in Zion a stone that will make people stumble, a rock that will make them fall, and whoever believes in him will not be put to shame.”” Romans 9:33

St Devasahayam shows that faith can stand firm in the storm.

Take-home resolution: Hand your anxieties to Jesus each night this week, and pray for those who suffer for their faith.

Full homily

Dear friends, the disciples are crossing the lake at evening when a great windstorm arises and the waves begin to fill the boat. Jesus is asleep on a cushion. They wake him: Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing? (Mark 4:38).

Jesus rises and says to the sea: Peace! Be still! And there is a dead calm. Then he asks them: why are you afraid? Have you still no faith? (Mark 4:39–40). The disciples ask the question of Denha: who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?

Years later Peter, who was in that boat, writes: cast all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you (1 Peter 5:7). And he warns: the devil prowls around like a roaring lion; resist him, steadfast in your faith (1 Peter 5:8–9).

Today we honour St Devasahayam. After his Baptism he would not deny Christ. For nearly three years he was kept in chains, and at Aralvaimozhy in 1752 he gave his life. The storm did not stop for him, but Christ was in his boat.

Let us hand our worries to the Lord who cares for us, and ask St Devasahayam to make us steadfast in faith. Amen.

For children

Children, once there was a big storm and the disciples were scared, but Jesus said: Peace! Be still! And the sea became calm. When you are scared, say: Jesus, you are in my boat. I trust you!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you slept in the boat and rose to command the wind and the sea. When storms rise in my life, let me cast all my anxiety on you, because you care for me. Through the prayers of St Devasahayam, who stayed steadfast in faith through chains and death, keep me firm and unashamed in confessing you. Amen.
