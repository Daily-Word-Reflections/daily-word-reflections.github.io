Word for the day

“They said to the woman, “It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is truly the Savior of the world.”” John 4:42

Reflection

The Gospel today is the last scene of one of the great encounters of John's Gospel. At Jacob's well, at noon, Jesus has asked a Samaritan woman for a drink, and he has given her living water instead. She has left her water jar and run back to her town with a strange message: come and see a man who told me everything I have ever done. Now we hear what happened next: “Many Samaritans from that city believed in him because of the woman’s testimony.” This is remarkable on every side. Jews and Samaritans did not share vessels or prayers; the woman herself, it seems, was not well regarded in her own town; and yet her word is enough to bring a whole crowd out to the well. She did not preach a sermon. She told what had happened to her. That is the first form of witness that the Season of the Apostles asks of each of us: not a lecture about religion, but the honest story of what the Lord has done in my life. Only a few years later the deacon Philip would go down to a city of Samaria and proclaim the Messiah there, “so there was great joy in that city” (Acts 8:8); in a sense the woman at the well had already prepared the ground.

Then the Samaritans do something beautiful: “So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them; and he stayed there two days. And many more believed because of his word.” They do not only want to see the wonder-worker; they want him as a guest. And Jesus, who was on a journey to Galilee, stays. Faith grows in the time spent with him. Finally they say to the woman: “It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is truly the Savior of the world.” This is the goal of all witness. The witness brings people to Jesus and then steps back, so that they may meet him themselves. A mother's prayers, a catechism teacher's patience, a friend's invitation to a retreat are all the woman's word; but sooner or later each person must hear him for themselves, in the Scriptures, in the Holy Qurbana, in silent prayer. And notice who first gives Jesus this great title: not the disciples, not the leaders in Jerusalem, but Samaritans, outsiders. The Saviour of the world is recognised first by those whom the world kept at a distance.

Paul's letter to the Philippians speaks to a community that already believes and must now grow. “Therefore, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed me, not only in my presence, but much more now in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure.” Two truths are held together here. We must work; salvation is not something that happens to us while we sleep. Yet the very wanting and the very working are God's gift in us. The Catholic faith has always kept this balance: grace comes first and carries us, and grace calls for our answer. Then Paul gives a practical rule: “Do all things without murmuring and arguing, so that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, in which you shine like stars in the world.” The murmuring of Israel in the wilderness is in the background. A grumbling Christian is a dim star. A family that does not complain at every inconvenience, an office worker who does not join the gossip, a young person who refuses the cynical joke, shine in a dark sky. The secret, Paul says, is “holding fast to the word of life”: the same word of Jesus that the Samaritans heard for themselves.

Theme: “It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is truly the Savior of the world.”

Full homily

Dear friends, today a whole town comes to Jesus because of one woman. She was not a priest or a scholar. She simply ran home and said: come and see a man who told me everything I have ever done. Many believed because of her word (John 4:39).

That is how faith spreads, in the time of the apostles and today: through ordinary people who tell what the Lord has done for them. Each of us has such a story. Let us not keep it hidden.

But the Samaritans went further. They asked Jesus to stay, and he stayed two days. Then they said to the woman: now we believe not because of what you said, but because we have heard for ourselves; this is truly the Saviour of the world (John 4:42). Our parents gave us the faith; but each of us must also meet Jesus personally, in prayer, in the Word and in the Holy Qurbana.

Saint Paul tells the Philippians: work out your salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who is at work in you (Philippians 2:12–13). God gives the will and the strength; we must give our answer. Let us not be lazy with grace.

And he adds: do all things without murmuring, and you will shine like stars in the world. A Christian who complains about everything gives little light. Let us thank God instead, hold fast to his word, and shine where we live and work. Amen.

For children

Children, a woman met Jesus and ran to tell everyone in her town. Then the people met Jesus themselves and loved him too!

Your parents and teachers tell you about Jesus. Now talk to Jesus yourself every day, like a friend. And try not to grumble today: be a little star!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Saviour of the world, you sat by the well and gave living water to a Samaritan woman, and through her word you drew a whole town to yourself. Make us witnesses who tell simply what you have done for us. Stay with us, Lord, so that we may hear you for ourselves. Father, you are at work in us, giving us both the will and the strength to do your pleasure: keep us from murmuring, and let us shine like stars, holding fast to the word of life. Glory to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
