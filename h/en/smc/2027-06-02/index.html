Word for the day

“They said to the woman, “It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is truly the Savior of the world.”” John 4:42

Reflection

The Gospel today is the last scene of one of the great encounters of John's Gospel. At Jacob's well, at noon, Jesus has asked a Samaritan woman for a drink, and he has given her living water instead. She has left her water jar and run back to her town with a strange message: come and see a man who told me everything I have ever done. Now we hear what happened next: “Many Samaritans from that city believed in him because of the woman’s testimony.” This is remarkable on every side. Jews and Samaritans did not share vessels or prayers; the woman herself, it seems, was not well regarded in her own town; and yet her word is enough to bring a whole crowd out to the well. She did not preach a sermon. She told what had happened to her. That is the first form of witness that the Season of the Apostles asks of each of us: not a lecture about religion, but the honest story of what the Lord has done in my life. Only a few years later the deacon Philip would go down to a city of Samaria and proclaim the Messiah there, “so there was great joy in that city” (Acts 8:8); in a sense the woman at the well had already prepared the ground.

Then the Samaritans do something beautiful: “So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them; and he stayed there two days. And many more believed because of his word.” They do not only want to see the wonder-worker; they want him as a guest. And Jesus, who was on a journey to Galilee, stays. Faith grows in the time spent with him. Finally they say to the woman: “It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is truly the Savior of the world.” This is the goal of all witness. The witness brings people to Jesus and then steps back, so that they may meet him themselves. A mother's prayers, a catechism teacher's patience, a friend's invitation to a retreat are all the woman's word; but sooner or later each person must hear him for themselves, in the Scriptures, in the Holy Qurbana, in silent prayer. And notice who first gives Jesus this great title: not the disciples, not the leaders in Jerusalem, but Samaritans, outsiders. The Saviour of the world is recognised first by those whom the world kept at a distance.

Paul's letter to the Philippians speaks to a community that already believes and must now grow. “Therefore, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed me, not only in my presence, but much more now in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; for it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure.” Two truths are held together here. We must work; salvation is not something that happens to us while we sleep. Yet the very wanting and the very working are God's gift in us. The Catholic faith has always kept this balance: grace comes first and carries us, and grace calls for our answer. Then Paul gives a practical rule: “Do all things without murmuring and arguing, so that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and perverse generation, in which you shine like stars in the world.” The murmuring of Israel in the wilderness is in the background. A grumbling Christian is a dim star. A family that does not complain at every inconvenience, an office worker who does not join the gossip, a young person who refuses the cynical joke, shine in a dark sky. The secret, Paul says, is “holding fast to the word of life”: the same word of Jesus that the Samaritans heard for themselves.

Full homily

Dear friends, today a whole town comes to Jesus because of one woman. She was not a priest or a scholar. She simply ran home and said: come and see a man who told me everything I have ever done. Many believed because of her word (John 4:39).

That is how faith spreads, in the time of the apostles and today: through ordinary people who tell what the Lord has done for them. Each of us has such a story. Let us not keep it hidden.

But the Samaritans went further. They asked Jesus to stay, and he stayed two days. Then they said to the woman: now we believe not because of what you said, but because we have heard for ourselves; this is truly the Saviour of the world (John 4:42). Our parents gave us the faith; but each of us must also meet Jesus personally, in prayer, in the Word and in the Holy Qurbana.

Saint Paul tells the Philippians: work out your salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who is at work in you (Philippians 2:12–13). God gives the will and the strength; we must give our answer. Let us not be lazy with grace.

And he adds: do all things without murmuring, and you will shine like stars in the world. A Christian who complains about everything gives little light. Let us thank God instead, hold fast to his word, and shine where we live and work. Amen.

For children

Children, a woman met Jesus and ran to tell everyone in her town. Then the people met Jesus themselves and loved him too!

Your parents and teachers tell you about Jesus. Now talk to Jesus yourself every day, like a friend. And try not to grumble today: be a little star!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Saviour of the world, you sat by the well and gave living water to a Samaritan woman, and through her word you drew a whole town to yourself. Make us witnesses who tell simply what you have done for us. Stay with us, Lord, so that we may hear you for ourselves. Father, you are at work in us, giving us both the will and the strength to do your pleasure: keep us from murmuring, and let us shine like stars, holding fast to the word of life. Glory to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Today’s readings are about faith that shines and spreads. Paul asks the Philippians to work out their salvation with fear and trembling, because God himself is at work in them, so that they may shine like stars in a crooked generation. In John, the Samaritan woman’s testimony brings her townspeople to Jesus, and once they have heard him for themselves they confess him as the Saviour of the world. In the Season of the Apostles the Church learns that every witness, however weak, is meant to lead others to meet Christ in person.

Philippians 2:12–16 · Shine like stars in the world

Setting.

Paul writes to the Philippians from prison (1:13), the community in Macedonia he loved perhaps more than any other. He has just set before them the great hymn of Christ who emptied himself, became obedient unto death on a cross, and was exalted by the Father (2:6–11). “Therefore” in verse 12 draws the consequence: what Christ did must now take shape in their lives.

Structure.

  • vv. 12–13: work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for God is at work in you.
  • vv. 14–15: do all things without murmuring, so as to be blameless children of God, shining like stars.
  • v. 16: hold fast to the word of life, so that Paul’s labour will not have been in vain.

Key words.

  • katergazesthe, work out: A verb for bringing something to completion. Salvation is God’s gift, but the believer must let it grow to its full fruit in daily life.
  • energōn, at work: The word behind our “energy”. God is not an onlooker; he works within the will and the action of the believer. Our working is the fruit of his.
  • phōstēres, stars, lights: The word used in the Greek Old Testament for the lights in the sky (Genesis 1:14). Paul echoes Daniel 12:3, where the wise shine like the brightness of the sky.

Verse by verse.

“work out your own salvation with fear and trembling” (2:12)

“Fear and trembling” is not anxiety about whether God is good, but the reverence of someone who knows that salvation is precious and can be lost through carelessness.

“for it is God who is at work in you, enabling you both to will and to work for his good pleasure.” (2:13)

This verse is the key. We do not work in order to make God act; we work because he is already acting in us. Both the wanting and the doing are his gift.

“Do all things without murmuring and arguing” (2:14)

“Murmuring” recalls Israel grumbling in the desert (Exodus 16:7–8). In the next verse Paul borrows the words “crooked and perverse generation” from Deuteronomy 32:5.

“in which you shine like stars in the world.” (2:15)

The Christians of Philippi were a small group in a Roman colony. Paul does not tell them to withdraw from the world but to shine in it.

How the Church reads it.

The Church reads these verses as a clear statement of how grace and human freedom work together. The Catechism teaches that God’s gracious initiative calls for a human response made in freedom, and that our merits are themselves the gifts of God (CCC 2002, 2009). It quotes St Augustine: God created us without us, but he did not will to save us without us (CCC 1847). The Council of Trent, against the idea that we are merely passive, taught that grace moves the will without destroying its freedom.

John 4:39–42 · This is truly the Saviour of the world

Setting.

At Jacob’s well near the Samaritan town of Sychar, Jesus has spoken with a woman who came to draw water at noon. He told her of living water, revealed that he knew her life, and said to her, “I am he,” the Messiah (4:26). She left her water jar and went back to the town, saying, “Come and see a man who told me everything I have ever done!” (4:29). Meanwhile Jesus told his disciples that the fields were already white for harvest (4:35). These verses show that harvest.

Structure.

  • v. 39: many Samaritans believe because of the woman’s testimony.
  • v. 40: they ask Jesus to stay; he stays two days.
  • v. 41: many more believe because of his own word.
  • v. 42: their confession: we have heard for ourselves; this is truly the Saviour of the world.

Key words.

  • martyria, testimony: The woman bears witness, the same word used of John the Baptist (1:7) and of the beloved disciple (21:24). A woman with a troubled past becomes the first to bring a whole town to Christ in this Gospel.
  • menō, stay, remain: A favourite word in John. The disciples first asked Jesus where he was staying and stayed with him (1:38–39). Now the Samaritans ask him to stay, and faith grows in his company.
  • sōtēr tou kosmou, Saviour of the world: This title appears only here and in 1 John 4:14. In the Roman world it was sometimes given to emperors. People whom many in Judea kept at a distance are the ones who give Jesus this universal title.

Verse by verse.

“Many Samaritans from that city believed in him because of the woman’s testimony, “He told me everything I have ever done.”” (4:39)

She does not preach a sermon; she tells what happened to her. Being known by Jesus, and not rejected, has made her a witness.

“So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them; and he stayed there two days.” (4:40)

Jesus, a Jew, accepts the hospitality of Samaritans, crossing a long-standing division (4:9). His staying is itself a message.

“It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is truly the Savior of the world.” (4:42)

This is the path of all faith: it often begins with someone else’s word and becomes our own in a personal meeting with Christ.

How the Church reads it.

St Augustine saw in this passage the pattern of the Church’s mission: people first hear of Christ through the Church, as the Samaritans heard through the woman, and then they come to believe because of Christ himself (in substance, Tractates on John 15). The Catechism teaches that faith is a personal act, the response a person makes in freedom to God who reveals himself, and yet never an isolated act, because we receive it from others and pass it on (CCC 166). Pope Francis, speaking of the Samaritan woman, noted that she became a missionary straight after her encounter with Jesus (Evangelii Gaudium 120).

For study and teaching

  • Paul says that God is at work in me “both to will and to work”. Where have I noticed God moving my desires as well as my actions?
  • The Samaritans’ faith began with a woman’s word and became their own when they met Jesus. Who first spoke to me of Christ, and to whom can I speak of him?
