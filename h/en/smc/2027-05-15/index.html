Word for the day

“The threshing floors shall be full of grain, the vats shall overflow with wine and oil.” Joel 2:24

Reflection

On the last day of the Season of the Resurrection, on the eve of Pentecost, our Church turns to the Mother of God under a title that belongs to farmers and to the soil: the Protectress of Harvest. In the lands of Mesopotamia where the East Syriac tradition grew up, the middle of May is the time when the ears of grain ripen and the harvest is gathered; it is fitting to place our fields and our bread under the care of the Mother of the One who called himself the bread of life. The Syro-Malankara calendar keeps a similar commemoration today, of the Holy Mother of God for the ears of grain. It is a feast of the earth, and it is also a feast of heaven, because the readings chosen for it lead us straight to the festival that Christians will celebrate tomorrow. The first reading is short: “Three times in the year you shall hold a festival for me.” The second of those festivals is “the festival of harvest, of the first fruits of your labor, of what you sow in the field.” This is the Feast of Weeks, counted seven weeks from the first sheaf, “fifty days” (Leviticus 23:16); in Greek it was called Pentecost, the fiftieth day. On that very festival, in Jerusalem, the Holy Spirit came down on the apostles gathered with Mary, and the first harvest of the Church was gathered in: “that day about three thousand persons were added” (Acts 2:41).

The prophet Joel speaks to a land that had been stripped by locusts and drought. Now God promises a reversal: “Do not fear, O soil; be glad and rejoice, for the LORD has done great things!” The pastures grow green, the fig tree and the vine bear again, the early and the later rain come in their season. “The threshing floors shall be full of grain, the vats shall overflow with wine and oil. I will repay you for the years that the swarming locust has eaten”. Every farmer in Kerala understands those words, in a month when the fields wait for the monsoon that usually arrives around the beginning of June, and many families still pray for the rain before sowing. But Joel goes further than the harvest: “Then afterward I will pour out my spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, and your young men shall see visions.” Tomorrow Peter will stand up in Jerusalem and say: “this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel” (Acts 2:16). The rain on the fields becomes the sign of a greater rain, the Spirit poured out on men and women, young and old, servants and masters alike. Mary, the humble servant of the Lord, is the first to receive that rain in fullness: at the Annunciation the Holy Spirit came upon her, and in the upper room she prays with the apostles for the same Spirit to be poured upon the Church.

Paul's words to the Romans may seem far from the fields, but they speak of God's faithfulness to his first harvest, Israel. “A hardening has come upon part of Israel, until the full number of the Gentiles has come in. And so all Israel will be saved”. Paul, himself a son of Israel, insists: “as regards election they are beloved, for the sake of their ancestors; for the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable.” The Church has learned to read these words with great respect for the Jewish people, the people of the covenant never revoked, to whom belong the patriarchs, the prophets and, according to the flesh, Christ himself and his Mother. Mary is the daughter of Zion in whom the hope of Israel came to its fruit; Elizabeth greeted her: “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb” (Luke 1:42). Paul ends with a word about all of us: “For God has imprisoned all in disobedience so that he may be merciful to all.” The harvest God desires is a harvest of mercy, in which no one is left in the field.

That is exactly what the Gospel says. “Take care that you do not despise one of these little ones; for, I tell you, in heaven their angels continually see the face of my Father in heaven.” Then Jesus tells of the shepherd who leaves the ninety-nine and goes after the one that went astray, and rejoices over it more than over the ninety-nine. “So it is not the will of your Father in heaven that one of these little ones should be lost.” The Protectress of Harvest is not only the guardian of rice and wheat; she is the Mother who watches over the little ones and the lost, as a farmer's wife watches over every sheaf. Mothers in Kerala homes know this: when one child is far away or has stopped praying, the rosary is said with that child's name. The weekday Gospel of the unforgiving servant adds a warning that fits a harvest feast: we who have received everything as a gift must forgive “from your heart” (Matthew 18:35), or the harvest is spoiled. And the weekday Epistle gives Paul's cry, “woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel!” (1 Corinthians 9:16): the Lord of the harvest needs labourers. So on this last evening of Easter we bring to Mary our fields and our work, our children near and far, and our hearts, asking her to prepare them, like good soil, for the rain of the Holy Spirit tomorrow.

Homily outline

Theme: “The threshing floors shall be full of grain, the vats shall overflow with wine and oil.”

On the feast of Mary, Protectress of Harvest, the eve of Pentecost: the festival of harvest, the promised Spirit, God's faithful gifts, and the Mother who watches over the little ones.

1. The festival of harvest

  • Three festivals in the year.
  • Fifty days and the first fruits.
  • The first harvest of the Church.
“You shall count until the day after the seventh sabbath, fifty days; then you shall present an offering of new grain to the LORD.” Leviticus 23:16
“So those who welcomed his message were baptized, and that day about three thousand persons were added.” Acts 2:41

Apply: Offer God the first fruits of your week: your first hour of each day, given to prayer.

2. I will pour out my spirit on all flesh

  • Rain on a ruined land.
  • Sons and daughters, old and young.
  • Mary, first to receive the Spirit's fullness.
“Then afterward I will pour out my spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, and your young men shall see visions.” Joel 2:28
“All these were constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus, as well as his brothers.” Acts 1:14

Apply: Pray tonight with your family for the coming of the Holy Spirit, asking Mary to pray with you.

3. The gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable

  • Israel, beloved for the sake of the ancestors.
  • Mary, daughter of Zion.
  • Mercy to all.
“The LORD will give what is good, and our land will yield its increase.” Psalm 85:12
“for the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable.” Romans 11:29

Apply: Thank God for one gift he has never taken back from you, even when you were unfaithful.

4. Not one of these little ones lost

  • Their angels see the Father's face.
  • The shepherd who searches.
  • The Mother who prays by name.
“May those who sow in tears reap with shouts of joy.” Psalm 126:5
“So it is not the will of your Father in heaven that one of these little ones should be lost.” Matthew 18:14

Apply: Pray the rosary this week for one person who has drifted away, and reach out to them.

The Protectress of Harvest leads us from the fields to the upper room, from the harvest of grain to the harvest of the Spirit.

With her let us bring our first fruits, wait for the rain of the Spirit, and search for every little one.

Take-home resolution: This week, give God the first hour of each day, forgive from the heart, and pray by name for one who has gone astray.

Full homily

Dear friends, on this last day of the Season of the Resurrection, the eve of Pentecost, our Church honours the Blessed Virgin Mary as Protectress of Harvest. In the lands where our East Syriac tradition was born, mid-May is the time of the grain harvest, and it is fitting to place our fields under the care of the Mother of Christ, the Bread of life.

The first reading tells Israel to keep “the festival of harvest, of the first fruits of your labor” (Exodus 23:16). That harvest festival was counted fifty days from Passover. Its Greek name is Pentecost. Tomorrow we keep that very festival, the day on which the Holy Spirit came and the first harvest of the Church was gathered: three thousand people baptised.

Joel promises a ruined land: “The threshing floors shall be full of grain” (Joel 2:24). Then he promises more: “I will pour out my spirit on all flesh” (Joel 2:28). Our farmers know what it is to wait for the rain. On this eve of Pentecost, Mary teaches us to wait for the greater rain, the Holy Spirit, as she waited with the apostles.

Paul reminds us that “the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable” (Romans 11:29). God is faithful to Israel, his first harvest, the people of Mary. And God wishes to show mercy to all.

Jesus says: “it is not the will of your Father in heaven that one of these little ones should be lost” (Matthew 18:14). Our Mother does not forget one sheaf in the field. She prays for every child, especially the one who has gone astray.

So let us bring Mary our fields and our work, our children near and far, and our hearts. Let us forgive whoever has hurt us, so that our heart may be good soil tomorrow for the Holy Spirit. Holy Mother of God, pray for us. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, who gives us rice and bread? God gives the rain and the sun, and farmers work hard. Today we ask Mother Mary to take care of the farmers and the fields.

Tomorrow is Pentecost, the birthday of the Church, when God sent the Holy Spirit. Let us ask Mary to help us get our hearts ready!

Prayer

Holy Mother of God, Protectress of Harvest, you are the good soil from which came forth Christ, the Bread of life. We place under your care our fields and gardens, the rain and the seasons, the work of our hands, and the bread on our tables. Watch over the little ones and over all who have gone astray, that not one may be lost. On this eve of Pentecost, pray with us as you prayed with the apostles, that the Father may pour out his Spirit on our sons and daughters, on the old and the young, and that the harvest of mercy may be gathered in. Through Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen. Alleluia!
