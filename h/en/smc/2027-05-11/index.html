Word for the day

“So it is with those who store up treasures for themselves but are not rich toward God.” Luke 12:21

Reflection

A man in the crowd interrupts Jesus with a family quarrel: “Teacher, tell my brother to divide the family inheritance with me.” Anyone who has lived in Kerala knows how familiar that sentence sounds. A few cents of land, a share in the ancestral house, a dispute over who looked after the parents: brothers stop speaking, cousins go to court, and sometimes the quarrel outlives everyone who began it. Jesus refuses to become the judge of the case: “Friend, who set me to be a judge or arbitrator over you?” He is not indifferent to justice; he goes to the root of the problem: “Take care! Be on your guard against all kinds of greed; for one's life does not consist in the abundance of possessions.” The word for life here is not the life of the body but life itself, the real life of a person. A house can be divided fairly and the heart still be poisoned by greed. Jesus wants to heal the heart.

Then he tells the parable of the rich man whose land produced abundantly. Notice how he speaks: “What should I do, for I have no place to store my crops?” “I will pull down my barns and build larger ones, and there I will store all my grain and my goods. And I will say to my soul, Soul, you have ample goods laid up for many years; relax, eat, drink, be merry.” He talks only to himself. There is no one else in his plans: no God to thank, no neighbour to share with, no poor man at his gate. Then God speaks for the first time, and calls him by his true name: “You fool! This very night your life is being demanded of you. And the things you have prepared, whose will they be?” The answer is painfully simple: someone else's, perhaps the very brothers who will quarrel over them. Jesus ends: “So it is with those who store up treasures for themselves but are not rich toward God.” To be rich toward God is to let our goods pass through our hands into his: in thanksgiving, in almsgiving, in support for the Church and the poor. In these days before Pentecost, the Holy Spirit is the true treasure we ask for; and he is given not to those who fill their barns but to those who empty their hands.

Paul deals with a different question in Corinth, but the root is the same. Some Christians knew that idols are nothing, and so they ate meat sacrificed to idols without a second thought. Paul agrees that “food will not bring us close to God”. But he adds: “take care that this liberty of yours does not somehow become a stumbling block to the weak.” A believer whose conscience is still fragile might see a strong Christian eating in an idol's temple and be led back into idolatry. “So by your knowledge those weak believers for whom Christ died are destroyed.” The phrase cuts deep: the weak brother is one for whom Christ died. Knowledge that ignores him is not wisdom but pride; earlier in the same chapter Paul says, “Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up” (1 Corinthians 8:1). His conclusion is generous: “Therefore, if food is a cause of their falling, I will never eat meat, so that I may not cause one of them to fall.” Our rights, like our riches, are given us for others. A Christian who drinks heavily in front of a young person, who mocks the simple faith of an old woman, or who boasts of his knowledge while his neighbour stumbles, has forgotten that his brother is someone for whom Christ died.

Homily outline

Theme: “So it is with those who store up treasures for themselves but are not rich toward God.”

The fifth day of the novena: greed that poisons families, the fool who spoke only to himself, and the love that will not make a weaker brother stumble.

  • A family quarrel over an inheritance.
  • Jesus goes to the root.
  • Greed that a fair division cannot cure.

“Take care! Be on your guard against all kinds of greed; for one's life does not consist in the abundance of possessions.” Luke 12:15

Apply: Take one step toward peace in any family quarrel over land or money this week.

2. Rich toward God

  • The man who talked only to himself.
  • You fool! This very night.
  • Goods that pass through our hands to God.
“Whoever is kind to the poor lends to the LORD, and will be repaid in full.” Proverbs 19:17
“So it is with those who store up treasures for themselves but are not rich toward God.” Luke 12:21

Apply: Give away one thing you have stored and do not use, and set aside a share of your income for the poor.

  • Knowledge puffs up.
  • The weak brother for whom Christ died.
  • Love builds up.

Apply: Give up one habit or word that could make a younger or weaker person stumble.

Our life is not in our barns, and our rights are given us for others.

Take-home resolution: This week, make peace over property, give away what you hoard, and protect the faith of the weak.

Full homily

Dear friends, a man in the crowd asks Jesus, “Teacher, tell my brother to divide the family inheritance with me.” How often we hear such quarrels in our own families: over land, over the house, over money.

Jesus does not take sides. He goes to the root: “Take care! Be on your guard against all kinds of greed; for one's life does not consist in the abundance of possessions.” A property can be divided fairly, and still the heart can be full of greed. Jesus wants to heal the heart.

Then he tells the story of a rich man. His land gave a great harvest, and he said: I will build bigger barns, and then I will rest, eat, drink and be merry. He spoke only to himself. No God, no neighbour, no poor man in his plans. And God said: “You fool! This very night your life is being demanded of you.” All his grain was left behind.

Paul tells the Corinthians: be careful that your liberty does not become a stumbling block to the weak. The weak brother is someone “for whom Christ died”. “Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up.” Our rights and our riches are given to us for others.

In these days before Pentecost we ask for the greatest treasure, the Holy Spirit. He fills not full barns but open hands. Let us empty one barn this week, make peace in our families, and be rich toward God. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told about a man who kept all his grain for himself in big barns and never shared. God called him foolish!

Jesus wants us to be ‘rich toward God’. How? By sharing! This week, share one of your toys, snacks or pencils with someone, and you will be rich toward God.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you became poor for our sake, so that by your poverty we might become rich. Guard us against every kind of greed. Heal the quarrels in our families over land and money, and teach us that our life does not consist in our possessions. Make us rich toward God by sharing what we have with the poor. Give us a love that builds up, so that we never cause a weaker brother or sister to stumble, remembering that you died for them. Send us your Holy Spirit, the true treasure, and fill our empty hands. Amen. Alleluia!
