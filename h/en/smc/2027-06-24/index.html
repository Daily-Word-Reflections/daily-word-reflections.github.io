Word for the day

“All who heard them pondered them and said, “What then will this child become?” For, indeed, the hand of the Lord was with him.” Luke 1:66

Reflection

The Church keeps the birthdays of very few people. Of most saints she celebrates the day of their death, their birth into heaven. But she celebrates the birth of the Lord at Christmas, the birth of his Mother, and, from the earliest centuries, the birth of John the Baptist. St Augustine already remarked on it: the Church celebrates the birth of no prophet, patriarch or apostle, except this one. The reason is in the Gospel itself. The angel had told Zechariah, “many will rejoice at his birth, for he will be great in the sight of the Lord… even before his birth he will be filled with the Holy Spirit” (Luke 1:14–15). John's birth is already part of the Gospel. Six months before Christmas, in the season when the days in the northern lands begin to grow shorter, we keep the birth of the one who said of Christ, “He must increase, but I must decrease” (John 3:30). The Syriac tradition calls him Yohannan Mamdana, John the Baptiser, and our Church honours him among the greatest of the saints: Jesus himself said, “among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist” (Matthew 11:11).

Luke tells the birth simply and joyfully: “Now the time came for Elizabeth to give birth, and she bore a son. Her neighbors and relatives heard that the Lord had shown his great mercy to her, and they rejoiced with her.” An old woman who had borne the shame of childlessness for a lifetime now holds her son, and the whole village of the hill country shares her joy. Mary had stayed with her about three months (Luke 1:56); the two mothers had already rejoiced together when the unborn John leaped at the sound of Mary's greeting. Then comes the naming. On the eighth day the relatives want to call the child Zechariah after his father, as custom expected. Elizabeth says, “No; he is to be called John.” They object: “None of your relatives has this name.” Zechariah, who had been mute since he doubted the angel's word, “asked for a writing tablet and wrote, ‘His name is John.’ And all of them were amazed.” The name means ‘the Lord has been gracious’. The child does not belong to the family's plans or traditions; he belongs to God's plan. And the moment Zechariah obeys, “Immediately his mouth was opened… and he began to speak, praising God.” Doubt had silenced him; obedience gives him back his voice. Fear and wonder spread through the hill country, and everyone asks the question that every parent asks over a cradle: “What then will this child become?” Luke answers: “For, indeed, the hand of the Lord was with him.”

The reading from Jeremiah shows that John's vocation began before his birth: “Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations.” These words, spoken to Jeremiah, fit John perfectly, and they speak to every human life. Each child in the womb is already known and loved by God; this is why the Church defends human life from conception. Jeremiah protests, “Truly I do not know how to speak, for I am only a boy.” God answers by touching his mouth: “Now I have put my words in your mouth.” John will be “the voice of one crying out in the wilderness” (John 1:23). And God gives the young prophet strength for the struggle ahead: “I for my part have made you today a fortified city, an iron pillar, and a bronze wall… They will fight against you; but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, says the Lord, to deliver you.” John needed that strength when he stood before Herod.

That is why the First Set places beside the feast the story of Nathan and David. King David has taken Uriah's wife and arranged Uriah's death. God sends the prophet Nathan, who tells a story of a rich man with many flocks who takes the one little lamb of a poor man, the lamb that “used to eat of his meager fare, and drink from his cup, and lie in his bosom, and it was like a daughter to him.” David burns with anger: the man deserves to die! “Nathan said to David, ‘You are the man!’” A prophet does not flatter kings. A thousand years later John would tell another king, Herod, “It is not lawful for you to have your brother’s wife” (Mark 6:18), and it would cost him his head. There is another link: Nathan's parable turns on a stolen lamb; John's great word will be to point to the Lamb who is not stolen but given of his own will: “Here is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” (John 1:29). David repented when he heard the truth; Herod imprisoned the one who told it. The feast asks us which we will be when the voice of conscience says, ‘You are the man.’

Paul's letter to the Galatians places John at the hinge of history. Heirs who are minors are “no better than slaves… they remain under guardians and trustees until the date set by the father.” Then: “But when the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the law, in order to redeem those who were under the law, so that we might receive adoption as children.” John is the last of the guardians, the greatest of the prophets standing at the door of the fullness of time: “The law and the prophets were in effect until John came” (Luke 16:16). He does not keep the people for himself; he hands them over to the Bridegroom. Through the Son born of the Virgin Mary, and through the Spirit who cries “Abba! Father!” in our hearts, we are no longer slaves but children and heirs. The weekday readings, which the parish may also hear, fit the feast: Paul speaks of “a remnant, chosen by grace” (Romans 11:5), and in Mark blind Bartimaeus hears the call, “Take heart; get up, he is calling you” (Mark 10:49), throws off his cloak and follows Jesus on the way. That is what John lived for: to prepare a people, open their eyes, and send them after Jesus. In this Season of the Apostles, John reminds every apostle, bishop, priest, catechist and parent: point to Christ, then step aside.

Homily outline

Theme: “All who heard them pondered them and said, ‘What then will this child become?’ For, indeed, the hand of the Lord was with him.”

On the Birth of John the Baptist: a life known before birth, a name given by grace, a prophet who speaks truth to kings, and the last of the prophets who hands us over to the Son.

1. Before I formed you in the womb

  • Known, consecrated, appointed.
  • Filled with the Spirit before birth.
  • Every child in the womb is known by God.
“Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations.” Jeremiah 1:5
“for he will be great in the sight of the Lord. He must never drink wine or strong drink; even before his birth he will be filled with the Holy Spirit.” Luke 1:15

Apply: Pray daily this week for unborn children and their mothers, and support one family expecting a child.

2. His name is John

  • The family's plan and God's plan.
  • The Lord has been gracious.
  • Obedience opens Zechariah's mouth.
“The Lord called me before I was born, while I was in my mother’s womb he named me.” Isaiah 49:1
“He asked for a writing tablet and wrote, “His name is John.” And all of them were amazed.” Luke 1:63

Apply: Find out the meaning of your baptismal name and read the life of your patron saint.

3. You are the man

  • Nathan's parable of the lamb.
  • John before Herod.
  • Repentance or resistance.
“Nathan said to David, “You are the man!” 2 Samuel 12:7
“For John had been telling Herod, “It is not lawful for you to have your brother’s wife.”” Mark 6:18

Apply: Listen to your conscience this week and make a good confession.

4. He must increase

  • The fullness of time.
  • The last of the prophets.
  • Point to the Lamb and step aside.
“Lo, I will send you the prophet Elijah before the great and terrible day of the Lord comes.” Malachi 4:5
“He must increase, but I must decrease.” John 3:30

Apply: In one conversation this week, speak of Jesus rather than of yourself.

John was known and named by God before his birth, and so is every child: let us welcome life and live by the name God gave us.

Like John, let us speak the truth with courage, point to the Lamb of God, and decrease so that Christ may increase.

Take-home resolution: This week, pray for the unborn, learn the meaning of your name, answer your conscience with a good confession, and point one person to Jesus.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Church celebrates a birthday. Of most saints we celebrate the day of their death, but of John the Baptist we celebrate his birth, because his birth was already part of the Good News. The angel had said, “many will rejoice at his birth.”

Elizabeth, old and once barren, gives birth to a son, and her neighbours and relatives rejoice with her. On the eighth day the family want to call him Zechariah. But Elizabeth says, “No; he is to be called John,” and Zechariah writes, “His name is John” (Luke 1:63). This child belongs to God's plan. And when Zechariah obeys, his mouth is opened and he praises God.

God said to Jeremiah, “Before I formed you in the womb I knew you” (Jeremiah 1:5). That is true of John, and it is true of every child. Every life in the womb is known and loved by God. Let us protect and welcome every child.

The first reading shows the prophet Nathan telling King David, “You are the man!” (2 Samuel 12:7). A prophet tells the truth even to kings. John did the same with Herod, and it cost him his life. When our conscience says, ‘You are the man’, let us not argue; let us repent as David did.

Saint Paul says that when the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman, so that we might become children of God (Galatians 4:4–5). John stands at that door. He is the last of the prophets and points to Jesus: “Here is the Lamb of God.”

John's great word is, “He must increase, but I must decrease” (John 3:30). Parents, teachers, priests, every one of us: let us point to Jesus and step aside, so that he may grow in the hearts of others. What then will this child become? With the hand of the Lord upon us, we too can become voices that prepare his way. Amen.

For children

Children, today is John the Baptist's birthday! His old father Zechariah could not speak, but when he wrote, “His name is John,” he could talk again and he praised God.

God knew John before he was born, and God knew you before you were born too. Do you know what your name means? Ask your parents today, and thank God for calling you by name!

Prayer

Lord God, before John was formed in the womb you knew him, before he was born you consecrated him, and you filled him with the Holy Spirit to prepare a people for your Son. On this day of his birth we rejoice with Elizabeth and Zechariah and all their neighbours, for you have shown your great mercy. Open our mouths, as you opened the mouth of Zechariah, to bless you; give us the courage of John to speak the truth to the great and the small; and teach us to point to the Lamb of God and to decrease, so that he may increase in us. Protect every child in the womb, bless every name given in baptism, and make us your children and heirs through the Spirit who cries “Abba, Father” in our hearts. To the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit be glory for ever. Amen.
