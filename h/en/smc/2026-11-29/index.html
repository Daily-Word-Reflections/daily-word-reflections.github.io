  • Second Reading Isaiah 43:1–7, 10–11 · Salvation for Israel

Word for the day

“But the angel said to him, “Do not be afraid, Zechariah, for your prayer has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you will name him John.”” Luke 1:13

Reflection

A new liturgical year begins, and the Syro-Malabar Church begins it not with the end of the world but with an announcement: good news is coming. The season of Suvara (Annunciation) leads us through the great announcements of salvation, to Zechariah, to Mary, to Joseph, until the Nativity of the Lord.

The first announcement is made to old people who had given up hope. Abraham is ninety-nine and Sarah ninety; Abraham falls on his face and laughs: “Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old?” Centuries later Zechariah and Elizabeth are “righteous before God”, yet childless and “getting on in years”. In both stories God does the impossible and gives a son through whom the whole plan of salvation moves forward.

Zechariah is a priest serving in the Temple. As he offers incense, the angel Gabriel says: “Do not be afraid… your prayer has been heard.” Perhaps Zechariah had long stopped praying for a child; God had not stopped listening. His son John will go before the Lord “to turn the hearts of parents to their children… to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.” The preparation for Christ begins in a family.

That is why the Epistle speaks of the family: “Be subject to one another out of reverence for Christ.” Husbands, wives, children and parents are the first place where salvation is lived. Isaiah's words are spoken over each family at the start of this year: “Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Do not be afraid; your prayer has been heard.”

The new year begins with the announcement of John's birth, echoing the promise of Isaac, and with the family as the place where Christ's coming is prepared.

1. The God of the impossible

  • Abraham and Sarah are too old; Abraham laughs.
  • God keeps his promise and gives Isaac.
  • What is impossible for us is possible for God.
“God said, “No, but your wife Sarah shall bear you a son, and you shall name him Isaac. I will establish my covenant with him as an everlasting covenant for his offspring after him.”” Genesis 17:19
“But the angel said to him, “Do not be afraid, Zechariah, for your prayer has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you will name him John.”” Luke 1:13

Apply: Take up again one prayer you had given up.

2. Called by name

  • God redeems and calls his people by name.
  • No one is forgotten in God's plan.
  • The new year is a new call.
“But now thus says the LORD, he who created you, O Jacob, he who formed you, O Israel: Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” Isaiah 43:1
“Both of them were righteous before God, living blamelessly according to all the commandments and regulations of the Lord.” Luke 1:6

Apply: Thank God for calling you by name in baptism.

3. Turning hearts in the family

  • John comes in the spirit of Elijah.
  • He turns the hearts of parents to children.
  • The family prepares the way of the Lord.
“He will turn the hearts of parents to their children and the hearts of children to their parents.” Malachi 4:6
“With the spirit and power of Elijah he will go before him, to turn the hearts of parents to their children, and the disobedient to the wisdom of the righteous, to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.” Luke 1:17

Apply: Plan the 25 Days' Fast together as a family.

God keeps his promises; the year begins in hope.

Let our families be ready for the Lord.

Take-home resolution: Pray together as a family every evening of the 25 Days' Fast.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, a new liturgical year begins today. Our Church does not begin with a warning but with good news: an announcement. For four weeks we will listen to the angel's messages to Zechariah, to Mary and to Joseph, and prepare for the birth of the Lord.

The first two announcements are made to old people. Abraham is almost a hundred and Sarah ninety; when God promises a son, Abraham falls on his face and laughs. Centuries later Zechariah and Elizabeth are righteous, faithful, and childless, and they are old. Humanly, the story was over. But God is the God of the impossible.

Zechariah is offering incense in the Temple when the angel says: “Do not be afraid, Zechariah, your prayer has been heard.” Perhaps he had stopped praying for a child long ago. God had not stopped listening. Is there a prayer you have stopped praying? This season is the time to take it up again.

And notice where salvation begins: in a family. John will turn the hearts of parents to their children. That is why St Paul speaks today of husbands and wives, parents and children: “Be subject to one another out of reverence for Christ.” Christ comes into the world through families.

So let us begin this year with hope. Let us keep the 25 Days' Fast as families, pray together, forgive one another, and make our homes ready for Jesus. God says to each of us what he said through Isaiah: “Do not fear; I have called you by name, you are mine.” Amen.

For children

Children, a new Church year starts today! It's like New Year's Day for the Church!

An angel told old Zechariah that he would have a baby boy called John. Zechariah couldn't believe it, so he couldn't speak until John was born!

God always keeps his promises. This season, let's get our hearts ready for baby Jesus. Say together: “Jesus, I'm getting ready for you!”

Prayer

Lord God, faithful to your promises, at the beginning of this new year you speak to us as to Zechariah: “Do not be afraid; your prayer has been heard.” Renew our hope, bless our families, and prepare our hearts to welcome your Son. Amen.
