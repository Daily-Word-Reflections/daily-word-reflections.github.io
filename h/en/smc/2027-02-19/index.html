Word for the day

“Today I have rolled away from you the disgrace of Egypt.” Joshua 5:9

Reflection

The first reading shows us sin from God's side. “The LORD saw that the wickedness of humankind was great in the earth, and that every inclination of the thoughts of their hearts was only evil continually. And the LORD was sorry that he had made humankind on the earth, and it grieved him to his heart.” We usually think of sin as breaking a rule. Genesis shows it as wounding a heart. The earth “was filled with violence”, and God grieves like a father who watches his children destroy one another. Yet one sentence opens a door: “But Noah found favor in the sight of the LORD.” “Noah was a righteous man, blameless in his generation; Noah walked with God.” On a Friday of the Great Fast, when we stand before the Cross, we see how far that grief of God went. The heart that was grieved in Genesis is the heart pierced on Calvary.

The second reading is a story of new beginnings at Gilgal. The generation that left Egypt had died in the wilderness, “not having listened to the voice of the LORD.” Their children, born on the journey, are circumcised at Gilgal, and God says: “Today I have rolled away from you the disgrace of Egypt.” Then they keep the Passover, and the next day they eat the produce of the land, and “the manna ceased.” The food of the journey gives way to the food of the promised land. During these weeks, and above all around Holy Week, thousands of pilgrims climb the hill of Malayattoor, where tradition remembers St Thomas at prayer. Many go barefoot, some carry wooden crosses, and the slope rings with the old cry, “Ponnin Kurishu Muthappo, ponmala kayattam.” A pilgrimage is a small Gilgal: we carry the disgrace of our old slavery up the hill, and the Lord rolls it away. Paul adds that the law itself was never the problem: “So the law is holy, and the commandment is holy and just and good.” The commandment “You shall not covet” showed Paul his sin, and sin used even the good commandment to deceive him. The law names the illness; only Christ heals it.

In the Gospel the leaders in the temple ask Jesus, “By what authority are you doing these things? Who gave you this authority to do them?” Jesus answers with a question about John's baptism. They calculate: if we say from heaven, he will ask why we did not believe; if we say of human origin, the crowd will turn on us. So they answer: “We do not know.” It is not ignorance; it is refusal. They did know, but they would not say it, because the answer would have asked them to change. Many of our evasions in confession and in prayer sound just like this: I do not know. The Great Fast is the time to stop calculating, to admit what we already know, and to let the Lord roll away our disgrace.

Full homily

Dear friends, Genesis tells us that when the wickedness of humankind became great, the LORD was sorry that he had made humankind, and it grieved him to his heart (Genesis 6:6). Sin is not only breaking a rule. It wounds the heart of God. And on this Friday, before the Cross, we see how far that wound went.

But Noah found favour in the sight of the LORD; he walked with God (Genesis 6:8–9). There is always a door of grace.

At Gilgal God said to his people: today I have rolled away from you the disgrace of Egypt (Joshua 5:9). They kept the Passover, and the manna ceased, because they had reached the land. When we go on pilgrimage this Lent, when we go to confession, the Lord rolls away our disgrace too.

In the Gospel the leaders answer Jesus: we do not know (Mark 11:33). They knew, but they did not want to change. Let us not hide behind those words. Let us say honestly what we know, and let the Lord change us. Amen.

For children

Children, the Bible says that sin makes God sad in his heart. Jesus loves us so much that he died on the Cross to take our sins away. Today, let us say sorry to Jesus and make his heart glad.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, our sins grieved the heart of your Father, and on the Cross your own heart was pierced for us. Roll away from us the disgrace of our old slavery, as you did for Israel at Gilgal. Give us the honesty to say what we know, and the courage to change. Feed us on our journey until we reach the land you have promised. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
