Third Saturday of the Apostles: the Feast of the Immaculate Heart of Mary

5 June 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Readings

  • First Reading Genesis 17:15–22 · Sarah shall give rise to nations
  • Second Reading Isaiah 7:10–16 · The Lord himself will give you a sign: Immanuel
  • Epistle Ephesians 1:2–8 · Chosen in Christ to be holy and blameless
  • Gospel Luke 2:41–51 · His mother treasured all these things in her heart

Word for the day

“Then he went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was obedient to them. His mother treasured all these things in her heart.” Luke 2:51

Reflection

Yesterday the Church looked on the pierced Heart of Jesus; today she turns to the Heart of his Mother. The two belong together. When the infant Jesus was presented in the Temple, old Simeon said to Mary, “and a sword will pierce your own soul too” (Luke 2:35), and at the foot of the Cross that sword went through her heart as the spear went through his. The devotion to the Heart of Mary is old in the Church. St John Eudes in the seventeenth century gave it a liturgical form; at Fatima in 1917 the Mother of God spoke to three shepherd children of her Immaculate Heart; Pope Pius XII consecrated the world to the Immaculate Heart in 1942 and extended the feast to the whole Church in 1944, and Pope Francis renewed the consecration of humanity, and in particular of Russia and Ukraine, on 25 March 2022. The word Immaculate reminds us that this heart was never stained by sin. By a singular grace and in view of the merits of Christ, Mary was preserved from original sin from the first moment of her conception. The word Heart reminds us of her inner life: the place where she received the word of God, kept it, pondered it and answered it with her whole self. In the Syro-Malabar calendar this is an important Marian feast, and it falls fittingly in the Season of the Apostles, for after the Ascension the apostles “were constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus” (Acts 1:14). The first Church was gathered around the heart of the Mother.

The first reading is God's promise to Abraham about his wife: “As for Sarai your wife, you shall not call her Sarai, but Sarah shall be her name. I will bless her, and moreover I will give you a son by her. I will bless her, and she shall give rise to nations; kings of peoples shall come from her.” Abraham falls on his face and laughs: “Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old? Can Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?” God holds to his word: Sarah will bear Isaac, and the everlasting covenant will pass through him. God does not forget Ishmael either: “As for Ishmael, I have heard you; I will bless him.” The Church reads this passage today because Sarah, the barren woman who becomes a mother of nations by God's power, is a figure of the Mother of the Messiah. What God began with Sarah's laughter he completed in Mary's faith. To her the angel said what was said to Abraham long before: “For nothing will be impossible with God” (Luke 1:37). Mary became the mother not of one nation but of all, for from the Cross Jesus gave her to the beloved disciple and, through him, to the whole Church: “Here is your mother” (John 19:27).

In the second reading King Ahaz of Judah is terrified because two neighbouring kings have joined against him. The prophet Isaiah invites him to trust God and even to ask for a sign, as deep as Sheol or as high as heaven. Ahaz refuses with pious words, “I will not ask, and I will not put the Lord to the test”, because he has already decided to trust in a foreign empire rather than in God. Isaiah answers: “Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Look, the young woman is with child and shall bear a son, and shall name him Immanuel.” The Hebrew word speaks of a young woman of marriageable age; the ancient Greek translation made by Jewish scholars before Christ rendered it “virgin”; and St Matthew, quoting that translation, sees the sign fulfilled in Mary: “Look, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall name him Emmanuel,” which means, “God is with us” (Matthew 1:23). The Church professes that Mary conceived by the Holy Spirit without the seed of man and remained a virgin for ever. And notice the contrast between two hearts. Ahaz's heart was closed by fear and calculation; he would not even ask. Mary's heart was open; she asked, “How can this be?”, and then said, “Here am I, the servant of the Lord; let it be with me according to your word” (Luke 1:38).

The Epistle is the great blessing that opens the Letter to the Ephesians: “Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places, just as he chose us in Christ before the foundation of the world to be holy and blameless before him in love.” This is God's plan for the whole Church: to be holy and without blemish. What is promised to all of us, and will be completed only at the end, has already been accomplished in one person. Mary was chosen before the foundation of the world, and from the first moment of her existence she was holy and blameless before God in love. She too was redeemed; Paul's next words apply to her first of all: “In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace that he lavished on us.” She was redeemed, as the Church says, in a more exalted way: not by being washed from sin but by being preserved from it, through the blood her Son would shed. Her Immaculate Heart is the masterpiece of grace, “to the praise of his glorious grace.” That is why honouring Mary never takes anything from Christ; it praises what his grace can do.

The Gospel is the only story from the hidden years of Jesus. At twelve he goes up with Mary and Joseph to Jerusalem for the Passover and stays behind. For three days they search for him. When they find him in the Temple among the teachers, his mother speaks: “Child, why have you treated us like this? Look, your father and I have been searching for you in great anxiety.” Mary does not hide her pain, and she names Joseph first: your father and I. Jesus answers, “Why were you searching for me? Did you not know that I must be in my Father’s house?” And Luke tells us honestly: “But they did not understand what he said to them.” The Immaculate Heart is not a heart that understood everything at once, nor a heart without anxiety. It is a heart that did not close itself in the dark. “Then he went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was obedient to them. His mother treasured all these things in her heart.” She kept what she did not yet understand, and turned it over in prayer until God gave the light. Parents who worry about children growing up and going their own way, mothers who have searched through a long night for a son or daughter, can bring their anxiety to her. And all of us can learn from her the way to hold God's word: not to throw it away when it is hard, but to treasure it, ponder it and wait.

Homily outline

Theme: “His mother treasured all these things in her heart.”

On the Feast of the Immaculate Heart of Mary: Sarah, mother of nations; the sign of Immanuel; the Virgin chosen to be holy and blameless; and the heart that kept God's word in the dark.

1. A mother of nations

  • Sarah's new name.
  • Abraham's laughter.
  • Nothing is impossible with God.
“I will bless her, and moreover I will give you a son by her. I will bless her, and she shall give rise to nations; kings of peoples shall come from her.” Genesis 17:16
“For nothing will be impossible with God.” Luke 1:37

Apply: Entrust to God, through Mary, one situation that seems impossible.

2. The Lord himself will give you a sign

  • Ahaz's closed heart.
  • The virgin with child.
  • Mary's open heart.
“Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Look, the young woman is with child and shall bear a son, and shall name him Immanuel.” Isaiah 7:14
““Look, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall name him Emmanuel,” which means, “God is with us.”” Matthew 1:23

Apply: When fear tempts you to close your heart this week, pray Mary's words: let it be with me according to your word.

3. Holy and blameless before him in love

  • Chosen before the foundation of the world.
  • Preserved from sin by the blood of her Son.
  • The masterpiece of grace.
“You are altogether beautiful, my love; there is no flaw in you.” Song of Songs 4:7
“just as he chose us in Christ before the foundation of the world to be holy and blameless before him in love.” Ephesians 1:4

Apply: Make a good confession soon, asking Mary to help you become what God chose you to be.

4. She treasured all these things

  • Three days of anxiety.
  • They did not understand.
  • Keeping the word and waiting.
“I treasure your word in my heart, so that I may not sin against you.” Psalm 119:11
“Then he went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was obedient to them. His mother treasured all these things in her heart.” Luke 2:51

Apply: Each night this week, recall and keep one thing God did that day.

The Immaculate Heart is God's masterpiece and our model: open to the word, faithful in anxiety, keeping what it does not yet understand.

As the apostles prayed with Mary, let us pray with her for the Spirit and for peace.

Take-home resolution: This week, consecrate yourself to the Immaculate Heart, pray a decade of the rosary daily for peace, and treasure God's word each night.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we looked at the Heart of Jesus; today we look at the Heart of his Mother. Simeon said to Mary: a sword will pierce your own soul too (Luke 2:35). The Heart of the Son and the heart of the Mother are joined in love and in suffering.

Her heart is called Immaculate because it was never stained by sin. God chose her before the foundation of the world to be holy and blameless in love (Ephesians 1:4), and preserved her from all sin from her conception, through the merits of Jesus, her Son and Saviour.

In the first reading God promises old Sarah a son, and Abraham laughs. But nothing is impossible with God. Sarah becomes the mother of a people; Mary becomes the Mother of all peoples, for Jesus gave her to us from the Cross: here is your mother.

Isaiah tells frightened King Ahaz: the Lord himself will give you a sign; the young woman shall bear a son, Immanuel. Saint Matthew sees it fulfilled in the Virgin Mary. Ahaz's heart was closed by fear; Mary's heart was open: here am I, the servant of the Lord (Luke 1:38).

In the Gospel Mary and Joseph search for the boy Jesus for three days in great anxiety. When they find him, they do not understand his words. But his mother treasured all these things in her heart (Luke 2:51). Mary did not understand everything, but she kept God's word and waited.

Parents, when you are worried about your children, bring them to Mary's heart. All of us, when God's ways are hard, let us keep his word as she did. And let us pray the rosary each day for peace, as she asked. Immaculate Heart of Mary, pray for us. Amen.

For children

Children, Mary and Joseph lost Jesus for three days, and they were very worried. When they found him, Mary kept everything in her heart.

Mary is our Mother too. When you are worried or sad, tell her: “Mother Mary, keep me in your heart.” And pray a Hail Mary for peace in the world!

Prayer

God our Father, you prepared a worthy dwelling for your Son in the Immaculate Heart of the Virgin Mary, chosen in Christ before the foundation of the world to be holy and blameless in love. As you gave Sarah a son and gave Ahaz a sign, you gave us Immanuel through the faith of Mary. Give us hearts like hers, open to your word, faithful in anxiety, and treasuring what we do not yet understand. Mother of God and Mother of the Church, as you prayed with the apostles in the Upper Room, pray with us now: keep our families in your heart, bring peace to the nations, and lead us to your Son. Through Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
