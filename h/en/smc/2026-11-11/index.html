Word for the day

“Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb.” Revelation 19:9

Reflection

Heaven bursts into song: “Hallelujah! For the Lord our God the Almighty reigns… the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his bride has made herself ready.” The whole history of salvation is a love story that ends in a wedding: Christ and his Church.

In Jesus' parable the invited guests make excuses, and the king fills the hall with people from the streets, “both good and bad”. But one guest has no wedding garment. The invitation is free, but it must be received: the garment is the new life of grace put on in baptism and kept clean by conversion.

Homily outline

Theme: “The wedding of the Lamb.”

The Bible ends with a wedding; the parable warns us to accept the invitation and wear the garment of grace.

Full homily

Dear friends, heaven bursts into song: Hallelujah! For the Lord our God the Almighty reigns; the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his bride has made herself ready (Revelation 19:6–7). The whole history of salvation is a love story that ends in a wedding: Christ and his Church.

In Jesus' parable a king prepares a wedding feast for his son. The invited guests refuse to come, so the servants gather all they find on the roads, both good and bad. But the man who came without a wedding garment is put out (Matthew 22:9–13). The invitation is for all, but those who accept must prepare their hearts.

Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb (Revelation 19:9). Every Holy Qurbana is a foretaste of that feast. Our wedding garment is grace: received in Baptism and washed again in Confession. This Sunday let us come to the feast with a clean heart. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus invites us to a great wedding feast. The Holy Qurbana is a little taste of that feast. Come to church with joy!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Lamb and Bridegroom, you invite us all to your wedding feast. Clothe us with your grace and make us ready for the marriage supper of the Lamb. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Two wedding feasts. In Revelation, heaven sings Hallelujah because the marriage of the Lamb has come and his bride has made herself ready. In Matthew, a king's wedding banquet is refused by the invited guests and filled with people from the streets, but one guest has no wedding robe. The invitation is for all; the garment is what we must accept with it.

Revelation 19:1–10 · The marriage supper of the Lamb

Setting.

Chapters 17–18 described the fall of “Babylon”, the great city that stands for every power drunk with luxury and with the blood of the saints; for John's first readers it was Rome. Chapter 19 opens in heaven with the answer of praise. After the fall of the false city, the bride appears.

Structure.

  • vv. 1–4: three Hallelujahs over the judgment of Babylon; the elders and the living creatures worship.
  • v. 5: a voice from the throne calls all God's servants to praise.
  • vv. 6–8: the fourth Hallelujah: the Lord reigns; the marriage of the Lamb has come.
  • vv. 9–10: the beatitude of those invited; the angel refuses worship.

Key words.

  • hallēlouia, Hallelujah: Hebrew for “Praise the Lord” (hallĕlû yāh), familiar from the Psalms. In the New Testament it appears only in this chapter, four times.
  • nymphē, bride: The Church, ready for her husband. The prophets had called Israel the Lord's bride (Hosea 2; Isaiah 62:5).
  • byssinos, fine linen: Bright, pure linen; the text itself explains it as the righteous deeds of the saints. Notice that it “has been granted” to her: the garment is a gift, and also a life lived.

Verse by verse.

“for the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his bride has made herself ready;” (19:7)

The bridegroom is the Lamb that was slain (5:6). The marriage is the full communion of Christ and his people at the end of time.

“Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb.” (19:9)

One of the seven beatitudes of Revelation. Every Holy Qurbana is a foretaste of this supper.

“You must not do that! I am a fellow servant with you … Worship God!” (19:10)

Even the angel who reveals the mysteries refuses worship. Worship belongs to God alone.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism calls the Eucharist the Lord's Supper, which anticipates the wedding feast of the Lamb in the heavenly Jerusalem (CCC 1329), and speaks of the Church as the bride of Christ (CCC 796). The whole story of salvation is a love story that ends in a wedding.

Matthew 22:1–14 · Come to the wedding banquet

Setting.

This is the third of three parables Jesus tells in the Temple after his entry into Jerusalem (21:28–22:14), addressed to the chief priests and elders who had questioned his authority (21:23). Luke has a similar parable of a great dinner (Luke 14:15–24); Matthew's version adds the king, his son, the violent response, and the man without a wedding robe.

Structure.

  • vv. 1–7: the first invitations refused; the servants mistreated; the king's anger.
  • vv. 8–10: the invitation goes out to the streets; the hall is filled with good and bad.
  • vv. 11–13: the guest without a wedding robe.
  • v. 14: “Many are called, but few are chosen.”

Key words.

  • gamos, wedding banquet: The prophets picture the age of salvation as a feast (Isaiah 25:6). This feast is for the king's son: the Kingdom is the marriage of God's Son with humanity.
  • endyma gamou, wedding robe: Some think the host provided festive garments; this is uncertain. What is clear is that the guest came in as he was and did not let the invitation change him.
  • klētoi / eklektoi, called / chosen: In Semitic speech “many” can mean “all”. All are called; those who respond are chosen. The saying is a warning, not a head count.

Verse by verse.

“But they made light of it and went away, one to his farm, another to his business,” (22:5)

The refusals are not wicked in themselves: a farm, a business. The danger is ordinary things that seem more urgent than God's invitation.

“Go therefore into the main streets, and invite everyone you find to the wedding banquet.” (22:9)
“Friend, how did you get in here without a wedding robe?” (22:12)

He is called “friend” and given a chance to answer; but he is speechless.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism cites this parable when it says that Jesus invites people to the feast of the Kingdom but also asks for a radical choice (CCC 546). St Gregory the Great, preaching on this Gospel, taught that the wedding garment is charity: one may enter the Church by faith, but without love one is not dressed for the feast (in substance). Verse 7 is often seen as reflecting the destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70; its lesson is the seriousness of refusing God's invitation, not a judgment on the Jewish people (CCC 597).

Questions people ask

Is it unfair to throw out a man brought in from the street?

The parable describes the Kingdom, not a social event. The invitation is undeserved, but it is not without consequence. To accept it means to “put on” Christ (Galatians 3:27; Romans 13:14) and to let grace change one's life.

For study and teaching

  • What “farm” or “business” most often makes me too busy for God's invitation?
  • Revelation says the bride's linen is “the righteous deeds of the saints”. What one deed of love can I weave into that garment today?
