Word for the day

“But Peter said, “I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.”” Acts 3:6

Reflection

On the first Friday after Pentecost the Churches of the Syriac tradition keep a feast with a shining name: the Friday of Gold. It looks back to the first sign worked by the apostles after the Holy Spirit came upon them. Peter and John go up to the Temple “at the hour of prayer, at three o’clock in the afternoon”, the hour at which, only weeks before, Jesus had died on the Cross. At the gate called Beautiful lies a man “lame from birth”, carried there daily to beg. He asks for alms. “Peter looked intently at him, as did John, and said, ‘Look at us.’” Then comes the sentence that gives the day its name, as it is commonly explained: “I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.” The Church of the apostles is poor in gold and rich in the Name. Peter takes the man by the right hand, raises him, and “immediately his feet and ankles were made strong.” The man enters the Temple “walking and leaping and praising God.” The man lame from birth, more than forty years old (Acts 4:22), who had lain outside the gate, now goes in. Pentecost is not only a wind and tongues of fire; it is a hand stretched out to a beggar at the door.

Isaiah had seen this day from afar. To exiles worn out by grief he cried: “Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble knees. Say to those who are of a fearful heart, ‘Be strong, do not fear! Here is your God.’” And then: “the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped; then the lame shall leap like a deer, and the tongue of the speechless sing for joy.” The prophet sees a desert blooming, waters breaking out in the wilderness, and a road through it called “the Holy Way”, on which “the ransomed of the Lord shall return, and come to Zion with singing.” In Acts 3 that prophecy walks into the Temple on healed feet. The lame man leaping at the Beautiful Gate is a living sign that the age of the Messiah has begun, and that the Holy Way now runs through the Church of the apostles. Every parish that welcomes the weak, the disabled, the forgotten, and helps them go in to the house of God is walking that road.

Peter then speaks to the crowd in Solomon’s Portico. He refuses all credit: “why do you stare at us, as though by our own power or piety we had made him walk?” The healing is the work of “the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, the God of our ancestors”, who “has glorified his servant Jesus.” Peter speaks as a son of Israel to his own people, and his words about the Passion are hard. But notice how he continues: “And now, friends, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did also your rulers.” He does not condemn; he calls to repentance and blessing: “Repent therefore, and turn to God so that your sins may be wiped out, so that times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord.” And he ends with the promise to Abraham: “in your descendants all the families of the earth shall be blessed”, adding that God sent his servant “first to you, to bless you.” The Church has always read these words without blaming the Jewish people as a whole for the death of Christ (CCC 597–598; Nostra Aetate 4). It is our sins, the sins of all, that brought the Lord to the Cross, and it is to all of us that the times of refreshing are offered.

Why does the calendar join to this feast Paul's hymn to love? Because the healing at the gate was an act of love before it was an act of power. Paul has just listed the gifts of the Spirit, and now he says, “strive for the greater gifts. And I will show you a still more excellent way.” Without love, tongues are “a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal”; prophecy, knowledge and faith that moves mountains are nothing; even giving away all possessions gains nothing. “Love is patient; love is kind; love is not envious or boastful or arrogant or rude… It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Love never ends.” Peter had no gold, but he had this. He looked at the beggar, which the passers-by never did; he said, “Look at us”; he took him by the hand. The gold of this Friday is not the gold Peter lacked. It is love, which outlasts prophecies and tongues and knowledge: “And now faith, hope, and love abide, these three; and the greatest of these is love.”

The Gospel shows us where the apostles learned this. At the gate of the town of Nain, Jesus meets a funeral: “He was his mother’s only son, and she was a widow.” Before any request is made, “When the Lord saw her, he had compassion for her and said to her, ‘Do not weep.’” He touches the bier, the bearers stand still, and he says, “Young man, I say to you, rise!” and “Jesus gave him to his mother.” When John the Baptist's disciples come to ask, “Are you the one who is to come, or are we to wait for another?”, Jesus answers with Isaiah's own words: “the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, the poor have good news brought to them.” The gate of Nain and the Beautiful Gate belong together: what Jesus did at one gate, his apostles do at the other in his Name. In the Season of the Apostles the Church learns that she has no treasure of her own to give the world, only Christ, and that he is enough. The gold of our Church is not in her buildings or her accounts, but in the Name of Jesus spoken with love to someone lying at the door.

Homily outline

Theme: “I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.”

The Friday of Gold: Isaiah's Holy Way, the lame man raised at the Beautiful Gate, the more excellent way of love, and the compassion of Jesus at the gate of Nain.

1. Strengthen the weak hands

  • Exiles worn out by grief.
  • A desert in bloom and the Holy Way.
  • The lame who leap like a deer.
“Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble knees.” Isaiah 35:3
“Therefore lift your drooping hands and strengthen your weak knees,” Hebrews 12:12

Apply: Visit or call one person who is weak, sick or discouraged, and strengthen them with your presence.

2. What I have I give you

  • The hour of prayer and the Beautiful Gate.
  • Look at us: a gaze that lifts up.
  • The Name of Jesus, richer than gold.
“He raises the poor from the dust, and lifts the needy from the ash heap,” Psalm 113:7
“But Peter said, “I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.”” Acts 3:6

Apply: Give one gift this week that costs no money: time, attention or prayer in Jesus' Name.

3. The more excellent way

  • Gifts without love are noise.
  • Love bears all things.
  • The gold that never ends.
“Many waters cannot quench love, neither can floods drown it. If one offered for love all the wealth of one’s house, it would be utterly scorned.” Song of Songs 8:7
“And now faith, hope, and love abide, these three; and the greatest of these is love.” 1 Corinthians 13:13

Apply: Read 1 Corinthians 13:4–7 each evening this week and examine your day by it.

4. Young man, I say to you, rise

  • A widow at the gate of Nain.
  • Compassion before any request.
  • The signs of the One who is to come.
“then the lame shall leap like a deer, and the tongue of the speechless sing for joy. For waters shall break forth in the wilderness, and streams in the desert;” Isaiah 35:6
“And he answered them, “Go and tell John what you have seen and heard: the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, the poor have good news brought to them.”” Luke 7:22

Apply: Help your parish make one concrete step for the disabled or the grieving: a ramp, a visit, a meal.

The Church has no gold of her own; she has the Name of Jesus and the love of the Spirit, and these are enough to raise the lame.

Let us look at those at the gate, take their hand and give them what we have.

Take-home resolution: This week, strengthen one weak person, give one gift that costs no money, and pray 1 Corinthians 13 each evening.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear friends, today our Church keeps the Friday of Gold, the first Friday after Pentecost. It remembers the first miracle the apostles worked after the Holy Spirit came.

Peter and John go to the Temple at three in the afternoon to pray. At the Beautiful Gate a man lame from birth asks them for money. Peter looks at him and says, “Look at us.” Then: “I have no silver or gold, but what I have I give you; in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, stand up and walk.” He takes his hand, and the man walks, leaps and praises God.

Isaiah had promised this: “then the lame shall leap like a deer.” The age of the Messiah has come, and the Church continues his work in his Name.

Peter tells the crowd that it was not his power but the God of Abraham who healed the man. He calls them “friends” and says, “I know that you acted in ignorance.” He invites them to repent and receive blessing. The Church blames no people for the Cross; we all need that call to repentance.

Paul tells us the secret behind the miracle: love. Without love, all gifts are nothing. “Love is patient; love is kind… Love never ends.” Peter had no gold, but he had love. And Jesus, at the gate of Nain, had compassion on a widow and gave her son back to her.

So today, do not say, I have nothing to give. You have the name of Jesus, a hand to hold out and a heart to love. That is the gold of the Church. Amen.

Homily option 2 · At three o'clock, at the Beautiful Gate

Peter and John went up to the Temple at the hour of prayer. At the gate called Beautiful sat a man who had never once walked in. On the Friday of Gold he walks, leaps and praises God inside.

  • Evening, morning and noon I will pray.
  • The Spirit does not cancel prayer.

📖 Acts 3:1

📖 Leviticus 21:18
📖 Acts 3:2–3

3. Walking, leaping, praising

  • He enters the Temple with them.
  • David danced before the ark.
  • The joy of being brought in.
📖 2 Samuel 6:14–15
📖 Acts 3:8–10

Apply: Praise God aloud for one gift you have received.

The apostles prayed, saw the one at the gate and brought him in.

The gold of the Church is to bring people from the gate into God's house with joy.

Take-home resolution: This week I will keep a fixed hour of prayer and reach out to one person on the edges of our parish.

Dear brothers and sisters, today is the Friday of Gold, when our Church remembers the first miracle of the apostles after Pentecost. Luke begins simply: one day Peter and John were going up to the temple at the hour of prayer, at three o'clock in the afternoon (Acts 3:1). The Holy Spirit had come upon them, yet they kept their hours of prayer.

The Psalm says: evening and morning and at noon I utter my complaint and moan, and he will hear my voice (Psalm 55:17). The Spirit does not make prayer unnecessary. He makes it alive. If we want the power of Pentecost, let us also keep a fixed hour of prayer each day.

At the gate called Beautiful sat a man lame from birth. People carried him there every day to beg (Acts 3:2). He lived at the gate of beauty but had never entered. In the old law, those who were lame could not serve at the altar (Leviticus 21:18). He had been left outside all his life. How many people sit at the edges of our parishes: the poor, the disabled, the lonely, those who feel unworthy?

Peter took him by the right hand and raised him up, and immediately his feet and ankles were made strong. Jumping up, he stood and began to walk, and he entered the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God (Acts 3:7–8). David once danced before the ark with all his might. This man, who had never walked, now danced into God's house.

That is the gold of the Church: not money, but the name of Jesus that brings people from the gate into the house of God. Let us keep our hour of prayer, notice those who sit at our doors, and bring them in with us to the Holy Qurbana, praising God. Amen.

Homily option 3 · Are you the one who is to come?

Even John the Baptist, in prison, sent to ask Jesus: are you the one? Jesus did not scold him. He pointed to what God was doing. Today's Gospel speaks to all who struggle with doubt.

1. The question from prison

  • John hears in prison of Jesus' deeds.
  • He sends two disciples.
  • How long, O LORD?
📖 Psalm 13:1–2
📖 Luke 7:18–20

Apply: Bring your honest questions to God in prayer.

2. Tell what you have seen and heard

  • The blind see, the lame walk.
  • The poor hear good news.
  • The signs of the Messiah.
📖 Isaiah 61:1
📖 Luke 7:21–22

Apply: Write down three things God has done in your life.

3. Blessed is anyone who takes no offense

  • A Messiah different from expectations.
  • A stone of stumbling.
  • Love sees in a mirror, dimly, and trusts.
📖 Isaiah 8:14
📖 Luke 7:23; 1 Corinthians 13:12

Apply: When God does not act as you expected, make an act of trust.

Doubt brought to Jesus becomes deeper faith.

His works answer our questions, and love trusts where it cannot yet see.

Take-home resolution: This week I will bring my questions to God and remember three things he has done for me.

Dear friends, in today's Gospel even John the Baptist has a question. He is in Herod's prison. He hears of what Jesus is doing, and he sends two of his disciples to ask: are you the one who is to come, or are we to wait for another? (Luke 7:19). The greatest of the prophets wonders.

There is no shame in an honest question brought to God. The Psalms are full of them: how long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? (Psalm 13:1). God is not offended when we bring our questions to him. He is grieved only when we walk away with them.

Jesus does not argue. He says: go and tell John what you have seen and heard: the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, the poor have good news brought to them (Luke 7:22). These are the signs Isaiah promised for the anointed one who would bring good news to the oppressed (Isaiah 61:1). The answer to doubt is to look again at what God is doing.

And he adds: blessed is anyone who takes no offense at me (Luke 7:23). John expected a Messiah of fire and judgment. Jesus came with mercy. Isaiah had said that the Lord would become a stone one strikes against. Paul, in today's Epistle, says that now we see in a mirror, dimly, but love bears all things and hopes all things (1 Corinthians 13:7, 12). Love trusts where it cannot yet see.

On this Friday of Gold, when the apostles began to work the same signs in Jesus' name, let us bring our questions to him. Let us write down three things he has done for us. And when he does not act as we expected, let us trust him still, and receive him at the Holy Qurbana. Amen.

For children

Children, Peter and John had no money at all! But they met a man who could not walk, and Peter said, ‘What I have I give you: in the name of Jesus, walk!’ And the man jumped and danced!

You can give something better than money too: a smile, a helping hand, a prayer. That is your gold!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, at the gate of Nain you had compassion on a weeping mother, and at the Beautiful Gate your apostles raised the lame in your holy Name. Pour out on us the Holy Spirit who made them bold and gentle, so that we may look at those who lie at the door and give them what we have: your Name, our hands and our love. Strengthen the weak hands and make firm the feeble knees; open the eyes of the blind and the hearts of the hard. Make your Church poor in gold and rich in charity, until all the ransomed of the Lord come to Zion with singing, and praise the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit for ever. Amen.
