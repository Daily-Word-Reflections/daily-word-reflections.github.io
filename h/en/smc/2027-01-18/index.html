Word for the day

“When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil ways, God changed his mind about the calamity that he had said he would bring upon them; and he did not do it.” Jonah 3:10

Reflection

Today begins the Three Days' Fast, the fast of the Ninevites. Jonah walks into the great city with a short, terrible message: “Forty days more, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!” And something astonishing happens: “the people of Nineveh believed God; they proclaimed a fast, and everyone, great and small, put on sackcloth.” Even the king leaves his throne and sits in ashes. “When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil ways, God changed his mind.” The East Syriac Church has kept this fast since the sixth century, when a plague was stopped after the people fasted and prayed; in Kerala, Kuravilangad and many parishes still draw great crowds.

Moses promises the same mercy: if you “return to the LORD your God… the LORD your God will restore your fortunes and have compassion on you.” In the Gospel Jesus calls Matthew, a tax collector, and eats with sinners. To the critics he says: “Go and learn what this means, ‘I desire mercy, not sacrifice.’ For I have come to call not the righteous but sinners.” Our fast is not a show of strength but an opening to mercy. Jude blesses God “who is able to keep you from falling”. Today the Unity Octave also begins: may our conversion help heal the divisions among Christians.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we begin the Three Days' Fast, the fast of the Ninevites. Jonah walks into the great city of Nineveh and cries: forty days more, and Nineveh shall be overthrown! And the people of Nineveh believed God; they proclaimed a fast, and everyone, great and small, put on sackcloth (Jonah 3:4–5).

Even the king rises from his throne and sits in ashes. And when God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil ways, God changed his mind about the calamity, and he did not do it (Jonah 3:10). God takes no pleasure in punishment; he delights in mercy.

Our Church has kept this fast for fifteen centuries. Many of us go to Kuravilangad and other shrines. But the heart of the fast is not the pilgrimage; it is turning from our evil ways.

In the Gospel Jesus calls Matthew from the tax booth and eats with sinners. He says: go and learn what this means, I desire mercy, not sacrifice. For I have come to call not the righteous but sinners (Matthew 9:13). Our fasting is not to make us proud, but to open us to mercy and make us merciful.

Today the Church Unity Octave also begins. Let our conversion help heal the wounds of division among Christians. Amen.

For children

Children, the people of Nineveh said sorry to God and fasted, and God forgave them! These three days, let us say sorry for something and give up a small treat.

Prayer

Merciful God, when Nineveh fasted and turned from evil, you relented. Look on us in these three days of fasting; turn our hearts, forgive our sins, and keep us from falling. Make all Christians one in your mercy. Amen.
