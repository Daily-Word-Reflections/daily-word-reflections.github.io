Word for the day

“Yet, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter; we are all the work of your hand.” Isaiah 64:8

Reflection

Today begins the Three Days' Fast, the Rogation of the Ninevites, born in a time of plague when the Church of the East fasted and begged God for mercy. The first reading gives us the words for such a prayer. A people back from exile looks at its ruins and cries: “O that you would tear open the heavens and come down”. They do not pretend: “all our righteous deeds are like a filthy cloth. We all fade like a leaf”. Yet they hold on to one truth: “Yet, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter”, and they plead: “do not remember iniquity forever. Now consider, we are all your people.” Habakkuk adds the faith that keeps praying even when the fields are bare: “Though the fig tree does not blossom… yet I will rejoice in the LORD”.

Paul urges that “supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be made for everyone”, even for kings and rulers, because God “desires everyone to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.” A rogation is exactly this: the Church praying for the whole world. But Jesus warns that a prayer for mercy is empty if we refuse mercy. The servant released from a debt of ten thousand talents seizes a fellow servant by the throat over a hundred denarii. The master asks: “Should you not have had mercy on your fellow slave, as I had mercy on you?” And Jesus says the Father will deal so with us “if you do not forgive your brother or sister from your heart.” Our fast begins with forgiveness. Today the Unity Octave also begins: Christians who ask God not to remember their sins must learn to forgive one another.

Homily outline

Theme: “Yet, O LORD, you are our Father.”

The fast of the Ninevites begins: prayer for mercy, prayer for all, mercy to one another.

1. We are the clay

  • A people among ruins.
  • Honest confession.
  • The potter's hands.
“Yet, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter; we are all the work of your hand.” Isaiah 64:8
“But we have this treasure in clay jars, so that it may be made clear that this extraordinary power belongs to God and does not come from us.” 2 Corinthians 4:7

Apply: Keep the three days' fast as fully as you can, as clay in God's hands.

2. Pray for everyone

  • Supplications for all.
  • Even for kings.
  • God desires all to be saved.
“But seek the welfare of the city where I have sent you into exile, and pray to the LORD on its behalf, for in its welfare you will find your welfare.” Jeremiah 29:7
“This is right and is acceptable in the sight of God our Savior, who desires everyone to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.” 1 Timothy 2:3–4

Apply: Pray each day of the fast for our country and its leaders.

3. Forgive from the heart

  • Ten thousand talents.
  • A hundred denarii.
  • Mercy received, mercy given.
“Forgive your neighbor the wrong he has done, and then your sins will be pardoned when you pray.” Sirach 28:2
“Should you not have had mercy on your fellow slave, as I had mercy on you?” Matthew 18:33

Apply: Forgive one person from your heart before the fast ends.

The rogation of the Ninevites is a prayer for mercy for all.

We who ask mercy must give mercy.

Take-home resolution: Make a good confession during the three days, and forgive before you confess.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we begin the Three Days' Fast, the fast of the Ninevites. Our Church of the East first kept it in a time of plague, when the people fasted and begged God for mercy. The first reading gives us the words for that prayer.

A people back from exile looks at its ruins and cries: O that you would tear open the heavens and come down! They confess: all our righteous deeds are like a filthy cloth; we all fade like a leaf. Then comes the turn: yet, O LORD, you are our Father; we are the clay, and you are our potter (Isaiah 64:8). A broken pot can be remade if it stays in the potter's hands.

Habakkuk teaches us to rejoice in God even when the fig tree does not blossom and the fields yield no food. And Paul asks that prayers be made for everyone, for kings and all in authority, because God desires everyone to be saved (1 Timothy 2:4). A rogation is the whole Church praying for the whole world.

But Jesus tells a hard parable. A servant is forgiven a debt he could never pay, then throttles a fellow servant over a small sum. The master asks: should you not have had mercy on your fellow slave, as I had mercy on you? (Matthew 18:33). If we ask God not to remember our sins, we cannot keep a list of the sins of others.

Today the Church Unity Octave also begins. Let our fast begin with forgiveness: in our families, in our parishes, and among divided Christians. Amen.

For children

Children, a king forgave a man a huge debt, but the man would not forgive his friend a tiny one. Jesus wants us to forgive like God forgives us. Is there someone you need to say sorry to, or forgive, today?

Prayer

Merciful Father, we are the clay and you are our potter. As we begin the fast of the Ninevites, do not remember our iniquity forever. Teach us to pray for everyone, and give us hearts that forgive as we have been forgiven. Make all Christians one in your mercy. Amen.
