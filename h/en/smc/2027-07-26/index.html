Word for the day

“He raises up the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap, to make them sit with princes and inherit a seat of honor. For the pillars of the earth are the Lord’s, and on them he has set the world.” 1 Samuel 2:8

Reflection

Yesterday we honoured grandparents, and today the Church gives us the grandparents of Jesus. The Bible does not name the parents of the Virgin Mary. Their names, Joachim and Anne, come to us from an early Christian writing of the second century, the Protoevangelium of James, which is not part of Scripture but which the Church has treasured for its picture of the family into which Mary was born. By tradition, then, Joachim and Anne were an older couple who had long prayed for a child. The Eastern Churches kept their feast from early times, and the West followed. The name Anne is the Greek form of Hannah, which means grace, and in Kerala it lives on in the name Annamma, borne by countless grandmothers, while Joachim, in its Malayalam form Yoakim, is the name of many a grandfather. We honour them as the saints who handed on faith to the one who gave the Saviour to the world.

The Church puts the song of Hannah on their lips. Hannah, long barren, prayed in the temple at Shiloh, and when her son Samuel was born she sang: “My heart exults in the Lord; my strength is exalted in my God.” It is the song that Mary will sing again in the Magnificat. “The bows of the mighty are broken, but the feeble gird on strength. Those who were full have hired themselves out for bread, but those who were hungry are fat with spoil. The barren has borne seven.” And above all: “He raises up the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap, to make them sit with princes and inherit a seat of honor.” God is the one who reverses the world's order. The couple who, according to tradition, bore the shame of childlessness became the grandparents of the Messiah.

Isaiah continues the theme in his word to Jerusalem: “Sing, O barren one who did not bear; burst into song and shout, you who have not been in labor! For the children of the desolate woman will be more than the children of her that is married, says the Lord.” Then comes the command that every grandmother and grandfather needs: “Enlarge the site of your tent, and let the curtains of your habitations be stretched out; do not hold back.” The family of faith grows wider than anyone could have imagined, because the Lord says, “with everlasting love I will have compassion on you.” The letter to the Hebrews shows the same pattern in Abraham and Sarah. Of Abraham the letter says that “he considered him faithful who had promised,” and that “from one person, and this one as good as dead, descendants were born.” Faith is trust in the one who keeps his promise when our own strength is gone.

Matthew's Gospel opens with a list of names: “An account of the genealogy of Jesus the Messiah, the son of David, the son of Abraham.” It is a long chain of ordinary people, some famous, some not, some sinners, who handed on a promise from one generation to the next. Jesus did not fall from the sky; he was born into a family and a history. Joachim and Anne belong to that chain. In every Kerala home, the names of grandparents, the lamp lit at dusk, the Our Father taught at a grandmother's knee are the links of the same chain. And the chain ends in the verse that matters most: “Jacob the father of Joseph the husband of Mary, of whom Jesus was born, who is called the Messiah.”

The weekday readings add a sober word. James says to the rich: “Your riches have rotted, and your clothes are moth-eaten. Your gold and silver have rusted, and their rust will be evidence against you.” The wages kept back by fraud “cry out.” In Luke, Jesus says it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for someone who is rich to enter the kingdom of God. The disciples ask in despair, “Then who can be saved?” and he answers, “What is impossible for mortals is possible for God.” The same tradition that names Joachim also remembers him as a rich man, but one who gave his gifts to the poor and to the Temple, and who, when he was humiliated for having no child, did not turn bitter but fasted and prayed. Riches do not save, and neither does poverty by itself; the grace of God saves, and it asks for a heart that gives and trusts. Peter says: “Look, we have left our homes and followed you.” Jesus answers that no one who has left house or family for the kingdom “will not get back very much more in this age, and in the age to come eternal life.” Grandparents are the best witnesses of this: a long life given away in love comes back as a great family and a rich old age.

Theme: “He raises up the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap.”

On the memorial of Saint Joachim and Saint Anne: the song of the barren, the faith of Abraham and Sarah, the chain of a family, and riches that must be given.

1. The song of the barren

  • Hannah's prayer and Mary's Magnificat.
  • Enlarge the tent: God turns shame into joy.
  • Joachim and Anne, according to tradition, prayed long for a child.
“My heart exults in the Lord; my strength is exalted in my God.” 1 Samuel 2:1
“Sing, O barren one who did not bear; burst into song and shout, you who have not been in labor!” Isaiah 54:1

Apply: Pray the Magnificat this week for someone who is waiting for a child, a healing or a job.

2. By faith: a chain of generations

  • Abraham and Sarah trusted the promise.
  • The genealogy: ordinary people who handed on the promise.
  • We are links in that chain.
“By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to set out for a place that he was to receive as an inheritance; and he set out, not knowing where he was going.” Hebrews 11:8
“An account of the genealogy of Jesus the Messiah, the son of David, the son of Abraham.” Matthew 1:1

Apply: Write down the names of your grandparents and thank God for what each handed on.

3. What is impossible for mortals

  • Your riches have rotted: do not hoard.
  • The camel and the needle's eye.
  • Grace saves, and it asks for a giving heart.
“Your riches have rotted, and your clothes are moth-eaten.” James 5:2
“He replied, “What is impossible for mortals is possible for God.”” Luke 18:27

Apply: Settle one debt or wage you owe, and give one gift to someone in need.

Joachim and Anne, grandparents of Jesus, show how faith is handed on from one generation to the next, through prayer and patient trust.

What we cannot do, God can: let us trust, give and pass on the faith.

Take-home resolution: This week, thank God for your grandparents by name, settle what you owe, and tell a young person how God has carried you.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we honoured grandparents and the elderly. Today the Church remembers the most famous grandparents in the world: Saint Joachim and Saint Anne, the parents of the Virgin Mary and the grandparents of Jesus. The Bible does not give their names. They come from an early Christian tradition. But the Church has loved them for centuries.

The first reading gives us the song of Hannah, a woman who could not have a child and who prayed with all her heart. When God gave her a son she sang: “He raises up the poor from the dust; he lifts the needy from the ash heap” (1 Samuel 2:8). Mary would sing a song just like this in the Magnificat. According to the old tradition, Joachim and Anne also prayed for a child for a long time. God heard them.

The letter to the Hebrews tells us of Abraham and Sarah, who were too old to have children, and yet God made them parents of a great people because they trusted his promise (Hebrews 11:11–12). Faith means trusting the one who keeps promises, even when our own strength is gone.

The Gospel is a list of names: “An account of the genealogy of Jesus the Messiah” (Matthew 1:1). It tells us that Jesus was born into a human family, with ancestors and grandparents. In our families, too, faith comes down through the generations: the grandmother who lights the lamp, the grandfather who takes us to church, the parents who teach us to pray. We are links in that chain.

The weekday readings add a warning. Saint James tells the rich: “Your riches have rotted” (James 5:2). And Jesus says what is impossible for us is possible for God (Luke 18:27). Let us not trust money. Let us thank God for our grandparents, pray for them, and hand on the faith to our children. Saints Joachim and Anne, pray for us. Amen.

For children

Children, do you know who Mary's mummy and daddy were? Their names were Joachim and Anne, and they were Jesus' grandparents! They loved God and prayed a lot. Today, hug your grandparents, or if they are in heaven, say a little prayer: ‘Thank you, God, for my grandparents.’

Prayer

Lord God, you gave your Son a mother and a family, and you chose Joachim and Anne to hand on the faith to the Virgin Mary. Bless our grandfathers and grandmothers, living and departed. Reward their patience, their prayers and the hope they kept for us. Make us grateful for the chain of faith that has reached us, and let us pass it on to our children. Teach us, like Hannah, to pour our hearts before you, and, like Abraham and Sarah, to trust your promise when our own strength is spent. Keep us from the love of money that rots, and give us hearts that give. Through the prayers of Saint Joachim and Saint Anne, grant to the old and the lonely a peaceful home. Amen.
