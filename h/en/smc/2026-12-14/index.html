Word for the day

“The good person out of the good treasure of the heart produces good… for it is out of the abundance of the heart that the mouth speaks.” Luke 6:45

Reflection

Paul tells the Corinthians to “clean out the old yeast so that you may be a new batch… For our paschal lamb, Christ, has been sacrificed.” Sin tolerated in a community spreads like yeast; holiness requires decision.

Jesus teaches that each tree is known by its fruit, and words come from the heart's treasure. St John of the Cross, the Carmelite mystic, taught that the heart must be purified, even through dark nights, so that God alone may fill it. Before Christmas we ask: what is stored in my heart?

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul writes very strongly to the Corinthians about a scandal in their community: a man living in an immoral relationship, and the community proud instead of grieving (1 Corinthians 5:1–2). Paul is not being harsh for its own sake. He knows that sin tolerated spreads.

He uses an image from the Passover: a little yeast leavens the whole batch of dough. Clean out the old yeast so that you may be a new batch, for our paschal lamb, Christ, has been sacrificed; let us celebrate the festival with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth (1 Corinthians 5:6–8).

Before Passover, Jewish families searched the whole house for every crumb of yeast and threw it out. The Christmas fast is our search through the house of the heart: the old grudge, the hidden habit, the dishonest income, the bitter word. Confession is the cleaning.

In the Gospel Jesus says: no good tree bears bad fruit, nor does a bad tree bear good fruit; each tree is known by its own fruit; out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks (Luke 6:43–45). Our words reveal our hearts.

Today we remember St John of the Cross, the Carmelite mystic who taught that the soul must be emptied of attachments to be filled with God. He was imprisoned by his own brothers, yet wrote some of the most beautiful love poetry in the Church. A good tree, even in a dark cell, bore good fruit.

This week, let us listen to our own speech. What comes out of our mouth at home, at work, on social media? If it is criticism and complaint, the root needs care. Let us bring that root to confession before Christmas, and ask God for a heart from which good words flow. Amen.

For children

Children, a good tree gives good fruit. When your heart is good, your words and actions will be good too!

Prayer

Lord, purify my heart as you purified St John of the Cross, so that from its good treasure only good may come, and you alone may fill it. Amen.
