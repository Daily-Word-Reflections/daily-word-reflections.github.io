Word for the day

“For it is the God who said, “Let light shine out of darkness,” who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” 2 Corinthians 4:6

Reflection

On the last Thursday of the Season of the Apostles the Church sets two kinds of preaching side by side: a preacher who has nothing to hide, and teachers who have forgotten why they speak. Paul begins with a sentence of quiet courage: “Therefore, since it is by God’s mercy that we are engaged in this ministry, we do not lose heart.” He writes to the Corinthians, who had heard from other teachers that Paul was unimpressive and unreliable. He does not answer with an attack. He answers with his way of working: “We have renounced the shameful things that one hides; we refuse to practice cunning or to falsify God’s word; but by the open statement of the truth we commend ourselves to the conscience of everyone in the sight of God.” A messenger of the Gospel has no secret plan. He does not bend the word to please, to frighten or to gain. He puts the truth in the open and lets every conscience judge it, because God is watching.

Paul is honest also about the sorrow of preaching: “And even if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled to those who are perishing. In their case the god of this world has blinded the minds of the unbelievers, to keep them from seeing the light of the gospel of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God.” We read this with sorrow, not with scorn. Paul is not speaking of our neighbours of other faiths, whom the Church respects; he speaks of the blindness that can fall on any heart, ours included, when money, comfort or reputation take the place of God. Then comes the sentence that every priest, catechist, parish council member and parent can test their work against: “For we do not proclaim ourselves; we proclaim Jesus Christ as Lord and ourselves as your slaves for Jesus’ sake.” Am I proclaiming Christ, or myself? The reading ends in light: “For it is the God who said, ‘Let light shine out of darkness,’ who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.” Paul has the first day of creation in mind (“Then God said, ‘Let there be light’; and there was light”) and his own day outside Damascus. The light of God is not only an idea; it has a face, and we see it in Jesus.

The Gospel takes us into the Temple in the last days of Jesus' ministry, where he speaks sharply to the scribes and Pharisees. We must hear these words as the Church hears them. Jesus is a Jew speaking to his own people, in the manner of the prophets, who also scolded priests and teachers out of love for the covenant. The “woes” are not a verdict on a whole people, and the Second Vatican Council teaches that what happened in his Passion cannot be charged against all Jews of that time or of ours (Nostra Aetate 4). They are a warning to every religious leader and every believer, and so to us. The first woe: “you lock people out of the kingdom of heaven. For you do not go in yourselves, and when others are going in, you stop them.” Religion can become a gatekeeper instead of a door. The second: a zeal that crosses “sea and land to make a single convert”, but without the truth and love that make a person new. A Church that is busy and loud but has not itself been converted can do harm.

Then Jesus turns to oaths. The teachers had made a system of fine distinctions: an oath by the sanctuary binds nothing, but an oath by the gold of the sanctuary binds; an oath by the altar binds nothing, but an oath by the gift on the altar binds. Jesus answers with plain questions: “which is greater, the gold or the sanctuary that has made the gold sacred?” and “which is greater, the gift or the altar that makes the gift sacred?” Then he closes every loophole: “So whoever swears by the altar, swears by it and by everything on it; and whoever swears by the sanctuary, swears by it and by the one who dwells in it; and whoever swears by heaven, swears by the throne of God and by the one who is seated upon it.” God is present in every word spoken before him, and there is no small oath that he does not hear. In a country where people swear by temples, by their children and by God in courts and markets, this is very practical. The Catechism teaches that the holiness of God's name forbids false and careless oaths (CCC 2153–2155). Let our word be so true that no oath is needed: “Let your word be ‘Yes, Yes’ or ‘No, No’.” The apostle who does not falsify God's word and the believer whose Yes is Yes are the people through whom the light of Christ's face reaches others.

Homily outline

Theme: “For it is the God who said, ‘Let light shine out of darkness,’ who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.”

The preacher with nothing to hide, the light that has a face, and the Yes that needs no oath.

1. We do not lose heart

  • God's mercy in the ministry.
  • No cunning, no falsifying of the word.
  • The open statement of the truth.
“The Lord said: Because these people draw near with their mouths and honor me with their lips, while their hearts are far from me” Isaiah 29:13
“But we have this treasure in clay jars, so that it may be made clear that this extraordinary power belongs to God and does not come from us.” 2 Corinthians 4:7

Apply: Tell one person this week, simply and honestly, what you believe and why.

2. The light has a face

  • Light out of darkness.
  • We proclaim Christ, not ourselves.
  • The image of God.
“Then God said, “Let there be light”; and there was light.” Genesis 1:3
“He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation;” Colossians 1:15

Apply: Spend five minutes this week looking at a crucifix or an image of Christ and thanking God for his face.

3. Woe to the gatekeepers

  • Locking people out of the kingdom.
  • Zeal without conversion.
  • A Church that is a door.
“Ah, you who call evil good and good evil, who put darkness for light and light for darkness” Isaiah 5:20
““But woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you lock people out of the kingdom of heaven. For you do not go in yourselves, and when others are going in, you stop them.” Matthew 23:13

Apply: Welcome someone who is new, shy or distant in your parish this week.

4. The altar and the oath

  • The altar makes the gift sacred.
  • God hears every word.
  • Yes, Yes and No, No.
“You shall not make wrongful use of the name of the Lord your God, for the Lord will not acquit anyone who misuses his name.” Exodus 20:7
“Let your word be ‘Yes, Yes’ or ‘No, No’; anything more than this comes from the evil one.” Matthew 5:37

Apply: Keep one delayed promise before the week ends.

Paul states the truth openly and proclaims Christ and not himself; Jesus warns the gatekeepers who play with words.

Let our word be true, and let the light of Christ's face shine through it.

Take-home resolution: This week, keep one delayed promise, welcome one person at the door of the parish, and pray before you speak.

Full homily

Dear friends, in today's reading Saint Paul says something very brave: “We do not lose heart.” He was tired, criticised and misunderstood. Yet he says: God has been merciful to me, and so I will not give up.

Then he tells us how he works. “We refuse to practice cunning or to falsify God’s word; but by the open statement of the truth we commend ourselves to the conscience of everyone in the sight of God” (2 Corinthians 4:2). A good preacher, and a good parent and a good friend, has nothing to hide. And he adds: “We do not proclaim ourselves; we proclaim Jesus Christ as Lord” (2 Corinthians 4:5).

Why? Because the light is Jesus. “It is the God who said, ‘Let light shine out of darkness,’ who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ” (2 Corinthians 4:6). God does not shine from a distance; he shines from a face, the face of his Son.

In the Gospel Jesus is sad and strong at the same time. Some teachers who should have opened the door of God's kingdom closed it, and played clever games with words and oaths. Jesus tells them: God hears every word. Whoever swears by the altar swears by God, and whoever swears by heaven swears by God's throne (Matthew 23:20–22). We do not need clever games. We need honest hearts.

So let us do one small thing today: keep one promise we have delayed. Let our Yes be Yes and our No be No (Matthew 5:37). Then the light of Christ's face will shine through our words. Amen.

For children

Children, Saint Paul says: Jesus is the light! Light helps us to see, and light helps others to find the way. You can carry a little of that light when you tell the truth, even when it is hard.

Jesus does not like clever tricks with promises. When you say Yes, mean Yes! When you say No, mean No! And remember: God hears every word you say.

Prayer

Lord God, you said, “Let light shine out of darkness”, and you have shone in our hearts in the face of Jesus Christ. Keep us from every cunning and every double heart. Make us preachers who proclaim your Son and not ourselves, believers whose Yes is Yes, and a Church that is a door and not a gatekeeper. Teach us to speak every word as in your sight, and let the light of Christ's face shine in our homes and parishes. Through Christ our Lord, who with you and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns, for ever. Amen.
