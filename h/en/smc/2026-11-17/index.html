Third Tuesday of the Dedication of the Church

Readings

  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 4:1–5 · Stewards must be found trustworthy
  • Gospel Luke 16:1–13 · Faithful in little, faithful in much

Word for the day

“Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much; and whoever is dishonest in a very little is dishonest also in much.” Luke 16:10

Reflection

Paul calls the apostles “servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries,” and says: “it is required of stewards that they be found trustworthy.” Everything we have, money, time, faith, is entrusted to us; we are managers, not owners.

Jesus' parable of the dishonest manager is surprising: the master praises his shrewdness, not his dishonesty. If worldly people are clever with money for their own future, Christians should be wise in using wealth for eternity, “make friends for yourselves by means of dishonest wealth”, through generosity to the poor. And the conclusion is clear: “You cannot serve God and wealth.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Trustworthy stewards.”

Paul's stewards of the mysteries and Jesus' shrewd manager teach the right use of what is entrusted to us.

1. Honour the Lord with your wealth

  • All we have comes from God.
  • Giving the first fruits shows whom we trust.
“Honor the LORD with your substance and with the first fruits of all your produce.” Proverbs 3:9
“And I tell you, make friends for yourselves by means of dishonest wealth so that when it is gone, they may welcome you into the eternal homes.” Luke 16:9

Apply: Set aside a fixed part of your income for the poor.

2. Faithful in little

  • Small honesty prepares great faithfulness.
  • God sees the small things.
“You shall have only a full and honest weight; you shall have only a full and honest measure.” Deuteronomy 25:15
“Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much; and whoever is dishonest in a very little is dishonest also in much.” Luke 16:10

Apply: Correct one small dishonesty today.

3. One master

  • Money is a good servant and a bad master.
  • We are free when God alone is Lord.
“Hear, O Israel: The LORD is our God, the LORD alone.” Deuteronomy 6:4
“No slave can serve two masters; for a slave will either hate the one and love the other, or be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and wealth.” Luke 16:13

Apply: Fast from one unnecessary purchase this week.

We are stewards of God's gifts and mysteries.

Let us be found trustworthy.

Take-home resolution: Review your spending this week in the light of the Gospel.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul calls the apostles servants of Christ and stewards of God's mysteries, and says that it is required of stewards that they be found trustworthy (1 Corinthians 4:1–2). Everything we have, money, time and faith, is entrusted to us; we are managers, not owners.

In the parable of the dishonest manager Jesus says: whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much. You cannot serve God and wealth (Luke 16:10–13). Money is a good servant but a bad master.

Today the Church also remembers St Elizabeth of Hungary, a princess who saw her wealth as belonging to the poor. Let us set aside a share of our income for God and the poor, and honour the Lord with what we have. Amen.

For children

Children, everything we have is a gift from God. Today set aside a little of your pocket money for the poor!

Prayer

Lord, all I have is yours. Make me a trustworthy steward, faithful in little things, generous with the poor, and free to serve you alone. Amen.
