Word for the day

“Simon Peter answered him, “Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life.”” John 6:68

Reflection

Paul's letter to Corinth takes an unusual turn. Some in the community are questioning his authority, and he answers with a burst of questions: “Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen Jesus our Lord? Are you not my work in the Lord?” His credentials are not a diploma but an encounter and a community: he has seen the Risen Lord, and the Corinthians themselves are “the seal of my apostleship in the Lord.” In the Season of the Apostles this matters to us. An apostle is one who has been sent by the Risen Christ, and the proof of the sending is the life of the Church that grows from it. Our own Church in Kerala is the seal of the apostleship of Mar Thoma, and every parish, every family that prays, every child who is baptized, is still that seal. Paul then defends a right: those who preach the Gospel may live by it. A soldier does not pay his own expenses, the one who plants a vineyard eats its fruit, the shepherd drinks the milk of the flock; and the Law says, “You shall not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain.” The Church has always taught that the faithful must help provide for the needs of the Church and of her ministers (CCC 2043).

Then comes the surprise. Having proved his right, Paul refuses to use it: “Nevertheless, we have not made use of this right, but we endure anything rather than put an obstacle in the way of the gospel of Christ.” He worked with his own hands as a tentmaker (Acts 18:3) so that no one could say he preached for money. Here is a principle for every Christian and every minister: what we are entitled to is not always what serves the Gospel. A priest may have a right to comfort, a parish committee to recognition, a parent to an apology, a worker to a holiday, and all may be just. But love sometimes lays a right aside so that Christ may be seen more clearly. The priests and religious who serve in our mission dioceses in North India, often in poverty and far from home, live this word of Paul every day. So do the parents who give up their own plans for a child with special needs. The question Paul puts to us is not ‘What am I owed?’ but ‘What might stand in the way of the Gospel, and can I remove it?’

The Gospel brings the long discourse on the Bread of Life to its crisis. Jesus has said, “Those who eat my flesh and drink my blood have eternal life” (John 6:54), and many disciples find it too hard. “Because of this many of his disciples turned back and no longer went about with him.” Jesus does not run after them to soften his words; he does not explain the Eucharist away as a mere symbol. Instead he turns to the Twelve: “Do you also wish to go away?” And Simon Peter answers for them all: “Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life. We have come to believe and know that you are the Holy One of God.” Peter does not say that he understands everything. He says that there is no one else to go to. That is often the faith of the Church in hard times: not a clever answer to every question, but a loyal heart that knows where life is found. The Catechism places this very scene at the heart of its teaching on the Eucharist: the Eucharist and the Cross are stumbling blocks, and the Lord's question echoes through the ages, inviting us to discover that only he has the words of eternal life (CCC 1336). The Gospel ends with a shadow: “Did I not choose you, the twelve? Yet one of you is a devil.” Even among the chosen, betrayal is possible. Two days before the feast of the Body and Blood of Christ, let us renew our faith in the Real Presence, and pray for those who have walked away, that they may hear again the question of Jesus and come back.

Homily outline

Theme: “Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life.”

Paul's apostleship sealed by a living Church, his rights laid aside so that nothing may block the Gospel, and Peter's loyal faith when many walk away from the Bread of Life.

1. Am I not an apostle?

  • An encounter with the Risen Lord.
  • A community as the seal.
  • Woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel.
“If I say, “I will not mention him, or speak any more in his name,” then within me there is something like a burning fire shut up in my bones; I am weary with holding it in, and I cannot.” Jeremiah 20:9
“If I proclaim the gospel, this gives me no ground for boasting, for an obligation is laid on me, and woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel!” 1 Corinthians 9:16

Apply: Speak of your faith this week to one person who does not know Christ well.

2. No obstacle to the gospel

  • The right to be supported.
  • The ox that treads the grain.
  • Rights laid aside for love.
“You shall not muzzle an ox while it is treading out the grain.” Deuteronomy 25:4
“If others share this rightful claim on you, do not we still more? Nevertheless, we have not made use of this right, but we endure anything rather than put an obstacle in the way of the gospel of Christ.” 1 Corinthians 9:12

Apply: Give generously to the missions of our Church, and give up one right this week for the sake of peace.

3. Lord, to whom can we go?

  • The hard saying about his Body and Blood.
  • Many turn back.
  • Peter's loyal faith.
“Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire other than you.” Psalm 73:25
“Simon Peter answered him, “Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life.” John 6:68

Apply: Visit the Blessed Sacrament before Thursday's feast, and pray for one person who has left the Church.

The apostles were sent by the Risen Lord and gave up their rights so that the Gospel might run unhindered; Peter stayed when many left, because only Jesus has the words of eternal life.

Let us stay with him, support his mission and prepare our hearts for the feast of his Body and Blood.

Take-home resolution: This week, speak of Christ to one person, give generously to the missions, and make a visit to the Blessed Sacrament before Thursday.

Full homily

Dear friends, in today's reading Paul asks, “Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen Jesus our Lord?” He has met the Risen Lord, and the Church at Corinth is the proof of his mission.

Paul shows that those who preach the Gospel have a right to be supported, as a soldier, a farmer or a shepherd lives by his work. The Church still asks us to support her ministers and her mission.

But then Paul says that he gave up this right: “we endure anything rather than put an obstacle in the way of the gospel of Christ.” He worked with his own hands so that no one could say he preached for money.

In the Gospel Jesus has spoken about eating his Body and drinking his Blood. Many disciples find this too hard and leave. Jesus does not change his words. He asks the Twelve, “Do you also wish to go away?”

Peter answers, “Lord, to whom can we go? You have the words of eternal life.” Peter does not understand everything, but he knows that life is found only in Jesus.

On Thursday we celebrate the feast of the Body and Blood of Christ. Let us renew our faith that Jesus is truly present in the Holy Qurbana, and let us say with Peter: Lord, to whom can we go? Amen.

For children

Children, once Jesus said something very hard to understand, and many people walked away. Jesus asked his friends, ‘Will you go away too?’ Peter said, ‘Lord, where would we go? Only you give us eternal life!’

When something is hard, stay close to Jesus like Peter. On Thursday we celebrate Jesus in the Holy Qurbana. Say thank you to him!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Holy One of God, you have the words of eternal life. When your teaching is hard and many turn away, keep us with you, and give us the loyal faith of Peter, who knew there was no one else to go to. Teach us, like your apostle Paul, to lay aside our rights rather than put an obstacle in the way of your Gospel, and to support generously those who proclaim it. Bless the missionaries of our Church who labour far from home. As we prepare for the feast of your Body and Blood, deepen our faith in your true presence, and bring back to your table all who have walked away. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever and ever. Amen.
