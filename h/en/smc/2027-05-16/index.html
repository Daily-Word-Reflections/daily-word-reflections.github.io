Word for the day

“All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other languages, as the Spirit gave them ability.” Acts 2:4

Reflection

Fifty days ago we sang the Resurrection; today the fifty days reach their goal. “When the day of Pentecost had come, they were all together in one place.” Pentecost was already a great feast for the Jewish people, the Feast of Weeks, the harvest festival kept fifty days after Passover, and in later Jewish tradition it was also linked with the giving of the Law on Sinai. That is why our Church opens the feast with Exodus 19. “On the third new moon after the Israelites had gone out of the land of Egypt, on that very day, they came into the wilderness of Sinai.” God speaks to Moses from the mountain: “You have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles’ wings and brought you to myself.” Then the promise: “you shall be for me a priestly kingdom and a holy nation.” God comes in a dense cloud, and a few verses later the mountain is wrapped in smoke “because the LORD had descended upon it in fire” (Exodus 19:18). At Sinai, fire on the mountain gave Israel the Law on tablets of stone; in the upper room, fire on each head writes the new law of love on human hearts, as the prophet had promised: “I will put my spirit within you” (Ezekiel 36:27). The people at Sinai answered, “Everything that the LORD has spoken we will do”; at Pentecost the Spirit gives the power to do it. Peter would later write to the baptised the very words of Sinai: “you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God’s own people” (1 Peter 2:9).

Luke describes the event with signs that every reader of the Scriptures would recognise. “Suddenly from heaven there came a sound like the rush of a violent wind, and it filled the entire house where they were sitting.” Wind and breath are one word in Hebrew and in our Syriac tradition: the breath God breathed into Adam (Genesis 2:7), the wind Ezekiel summoned over the dry bones, the breath of the Risen Lord who said, “Receive the Holy Spirit” (John 20:22). “Divided tongues, as of fire, appeared among them, and a tongue rested on each of them. All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other languages, as the Spirit gave them ability.” The Spirit does not come as a general mood over a crowd; a tongue rests on each one. And the first thing the Spirit does is make them speak. The Church is born speaking, born on mission. The disciples who had hidden behind locked doors now go out into the street. Mary is among them; the Spirit who overshadowed her at Nazareth to form the body of Christ now comes down to form his Body, the Church.

The crowd that gathers is made of “devout Jews from every nation under heaven living in Jerusalem”, and each one hears the apostles “speaking in the native language of each.” Then Luke lists the nations, and we Syro-Malabar Christians should listen closely: “Parthians, Medes, Elamites, and residents of Mesopotamia”. These are the lands of the East where the Gospel soon took root, the lands of the Church of the East, whose faith, liturgy and Syriac language came to the Thomas Christians of Malabar. At Pentecost our own spiritual ancestors were, in a sense, already standing in the crowd. At Babel human pride had confused the languages and scattered the peoples (Genesis 11:9); at Pentecost the Spirit does not abolish the languages but makes the one Gospel heard in each of them. That is why the Church can pray in Syriac and Malayalam, in Latin and Tamil, in English and Hindi, and still be one. The reaction is mixed: “All were amazed and perplexed, saying to one another, ‘What does this mean?’ But others sneered and said, ‘They are filled with new wine.’” The Spirit's work always provokes both wonder and mockery. Joel had promised, and Peter will soon proclaim, that God would pour out his Spirit on all flesh (Acts 2:16–17). It has happened.

Paul tells the Corinthians how to recognise the Spirit. Not by spectacle, but by a confession: “no one can say ‘Jesus is Lord’ except by the Holy Spirit.” Every time a child in our homes lisps the name of Jesus, every time a dying person whispers, Eeshoye, the Spirit is at work. Then Paul describes the Church as a body with many gifts: “Now there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there are varieties of services, but the same Lord; and there are varieties of activities, but it is the same God who activates all of them in everyone.” Notice the Holy Trinity in those three lines: Spirit, Lord, God. The gifts differ: wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, prophecy, discernment, tongues. But the purpose is one: “To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good.” No gift is for showing off, and no one is without a gift. The catechist who teaches on Sunday morning, the choir member, the parent who prays with the children, the youth who visits the sick, the elderly woman who prays the rosary for the parish: all of these “are activated by one and the same Spirit, who allots to each one individually just as the Spirit chooses.” We received the seal of this Spirit in our Thailabhishekam, the anointing with holy Myron given in our Church together with Baptism.

In the Gospel Jesus prepares his disciples for this day. They are sad that he is leaving, and he says something astonishing: “it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Advocate will not come to you; but if I go, I will send him to you.” The Ascension was not a loss; it opened the way for the gift. The Spirit will “prove the world wrong about sin and righteousness and judgment”: he shows the world its sin of unbelief, reveals the righteousness of Christ who returns to the Father, and announces that “the ruler of this world has been condemned.” And for the disciples: “When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth”. He does not invent a new Gospel: “he will take what is mine and declare it to you. All that the Father has is mine.” Here, on the threshold of next Sunday's feast of the Holy Trinity, we glimpse the life of God: the Father who gives all to the Son, the Son who sends the Spirit, the Spirit who glorifies the Son. Today the Season of the Apostles begins, the seven weeks in which our Church contemplates how the Spirit sent the apostles to the ends of the earth, and how one of them, Mar Thoma, came to our shores. The Spirit who came down in the upper room is the same Spirit who keeps the faith alive in Kerala homes and parishes. Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear friends, today is Pentecost, the fiftieth day, the Feast of Our Lord that crowns the Season of the Resurrection and opens the Season of the Apostles. Pentecost was already a Jewish feast, the harvest festival, linked by tradition with the giving of the Law on Sinai. So our first reading takes us to Sinai, where God told Israel: “you shall be for me a priestly kingdom and a holy nation” (Exodus 19:6).

At Sinai God came in cloud and fire and gave his Law on stone. In the upper room he comes in wind and fire and writes his law of love on hearts. “All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit” (Acts 2:4). A tongue of fire rested on each one. Mary was there with them. The Spirit who formed Christ in her womb now forms his Body, the Church.

The first thing the Spirit does is make the apostles speak. Frightened men go out into the street. And people from many nations hear them each “in the native language of each” (Acts 2:6): Parthians, Medes, Elamites and residents of Mesopotamia. Those are the lands of the Church of the East, from which our own liturgy and our Syriac heritage came. Babel scattered the peoples; Pentecost gathers them into one Church with many tongues.

Paul says: “no one can say ‘Jesus is Lord’ except by the Holy Spirit” (1 Corinthians 12:3). There are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit, and each gift is given “for the common good” (12:7). No one in this church today is without a gift. We received the Spirit's seal in our Baptism and Thailabhishekam. The question is whether we use our gift for others.

Jesus told the sad disciples: “it is to your advantage that I go away” (John 16:7). He went to the Father so that the Advocate could come. “When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth” (16:13). The Spirit takes what belongs to Christ and gives it to us; and next Sunday we shall celebrate the Holy Trinity whom he reveals.

Today the Season of the Apostles begins. The Spirit who sent the apostles to the ends of the earth brought Mar Thoma to our shores. Let us not keep the fire to ourselves. Let us say every day, Come, Holy Spirit, and let us go out, each with our gift, to tell the world that Jesus is Lord. Alleluia! Amen.

Take-home resolution: In this Season of the Apostles I will make a good confession and pray daily for the Spirit's help.

Dear brothers and sisters, on the night before he died Jesus saw that his disciples were full of sorrow. He told them: it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Advocate will not come to you (John 16:7). Today, at Pentecost, the Advocate has come.

The word Advocate means one who is called to stand beside you, a defender, a counsellor. Isaiah foretold that the spirit of the LORD would rest on the Messiah: the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might (Isaiah 11:2). That same Spirit is now given to the Church and to each of us. We are never alone.

But Jesus also said: when he comes, he will prove the world wrong about sin and righteousness and judgment (John 16:8). The Spirit shows us our sin, not to crush us but to heal us. On Easter evening the risen Lord breathed on the apostles and said: receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them (John 20:22–23). Every confession is a little Pentecost. God promised through Ezekiel: a new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you (Ezekiel 36:26).

And Jesus said of the Spirit: he will glorify me, because he will take what is mine and declare it to you (John 16:14). The Spirit does not draw attention to himself. He makes Jesus known. Joel had promised: I will pour out my spirit on all flesh (Joel 2:28). Today that promise is kept, and every baptised person is called to make Christ known.

So let us pray each morning: Come, Holy Spirit, stand beside me today. Let us make a good confession in this Season of the Apostles. And let us ask the Spirit to give us the courage to speak of Christ, and to receive him with burning hearts at the Holy Qurbana. Alleluia! Amen.

Apply: At the next Qurbana, pay attention to the prayer calling down the Holy Spirit.

The Spirit came upon a Church united in prayer with Mary.

He still descends at every Qurbana to make the gifts holy and to make us one.

Take-home resolution: This week I will pray with Mary for the Spirit and make peace with someone before next Sunday.

Dear friends, before the great day of Pentecost, the apostles did not organise a campaign. They prayed. Luke says: all these were constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus (Acts 1:14). And when the day came, they were all together in one place (Acts 2:1).

The Psalm says: how very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity (Psalm 133:1). The Spirit came upon a Church that was united and praying. If our families and parishes want the fire of the Spirit, we must first be together, forgiving one another and praying with one heart.

Mary was there. Long before the apostles, the Spirit had come upon her. At Nazareth the angel had said to her: the Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you (Luke 1:35). The Spirit who formed Christ in her womb now forms the Body of Christ, the Church. That is why we call her Mother of the Church. Where Mary prays, the Spirit is near.

At Mount Carmel, when Elijah prayed, the fire of the LORD fell and consumed the offering (1 Kings 18:38). Paul speaks of an offering made acceptable, sanctified by the Holy Spirit (Romans 15:16). In every Holy Qurbana our Church calls upon the Holy Spirit to come down upon the bread and wine. The fire of Pentecost falls again on our altar, the gifts become the Body and Blood of Christ, and we who receive them are made one.

So in this Season of the Apostles let us make peace with someone before next Sunday. Let us pray the Rosary, asking Mary to obtain the Spirit for our families. And at the next Holy Qurbana, when the Spirit is invoked, let us bow our hearts and say: Come, Holy Spirit. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, on Pentecost the Holy Spirit came like a strong wind and like little flames of fire on Jesus' friends and on Mother Mary. Then they were not afraid any more!

You received the Holy Spirit when you were baptised. Today, say this little prayer: “Come, Holy Spirit, make me brave and kind.”

Prayer

Father, on this holy day you fulfilled the promise of your Son and sent the Holy Spirit upon the apostles gathered with Mary, the Mother of Jesus. Pour out the same Spirit on your Church spread throughout the world, on our Syro-Malabar Church, heir of Mar Thoma, and on our families. As at Sinai you made a holy nation, make us a priestly people who live by the law of love written on our hearts. Let the Spirit of truth guide us into all the truth, give each of us our gift for the common good, and loosen our tongues to proclaim in every language that Jesus is Lord. Glory to you, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen. Alleluia!
