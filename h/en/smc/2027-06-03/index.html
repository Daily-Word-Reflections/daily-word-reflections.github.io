Word for the day

“But as for that in the good soil, these are the ones who, when they hear the word, hold it fast in an honest and good heart, and bear fruit with patient endurance.” Luke 8:15

Reflection

In the first days of June the southwest monsoon reaches Kerala, and across the paddy fields of Palakkad and Kuttanad, in the hill farms of Idukki and Wayanad, farmers prepare the ground and sow. Today's Gospel could have been told in a Kerala village. “A sower went out to sow his seed; and as he sowed, some fell on the path and was trampled on, and the birds of the air ate it up. Some fell on the rock; and as it grew up, it withered for lack of moisture. Some fell among thorns, and the thorns grew with it and choked it. Some fell into good soil, and when it grew, it produced a hundredfold.” The first surprise is the sower himself. He throws seed generously, even where it seems wasted: on the beaten path, on the thin soil over rock, among the thorns. God does not ration his word to those who seem likely to receive it. Every person, even the hardest, hears the word at least once. Then Jesus cries out, “Let anyone with ears to hear listen!” The parable is not only a description; it is an appeal.

Jesus himself explains it: “Now the parable is this: The seed is the word of God.” The path is the heart where “the devil comes and takes away the word from their hearts, so that they may not believe and be saved”: we hear on Sunday and have forgotten by the time we reach the church gate. The rock is the heart that receives the word with joy but has no root: a retreat, a feast, a moment of fervour, and then in the time of testing the faith falls away. The thorns are the heart that hears, but “as they go on their way, they are choked by the cares and riches and pleasures of life, and their fruit does not mature.” Notice the three thorns: cares, riches and pleasures. Worry and wealth and comfort are not obviously sins, but they can quietly choke the word. And the good soil: “these are the ones who, when they hear the word, hold it fast in an honest and good heart, and bear fruit with patient endurance.” Hold it fast, and wait. A farmer does not dig up his seed every morning to see if it has sprouted. He keeps the field, clears the weeds and trusts the rain. Fruit in the spiritual life takes the same patience.

Paul's letter shows what happens when a community is still poor soil. “I fed you with milk, not solid food, for you were not ready for solid food. Even now you are still not ready, for you are still of the flesh. For as long as there is jealousy and quarreling among you, are you not of the flesh?” The sign of spiritual infancy in Corinth was not a lack of gifts; it was faction. “For when one says, ‘I belong to Paul,’ and another, ‘I belong to Apollos,’ are you not merely human?” Paul refuses to be the head of a party: “What then is Apollos? What is Paul? Servants through whom you came to believe, as the Lord assigned to each. I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.” Parishes and dioceses can still divide around personalities, preferences and old grievances, and every such quarrel keeps a community on milk. The cure Paul offers is to look away from the servants to the Lord of the harvest: “For we are God’s servants, working together; you are God’s field, God’s building.” The Church is not the property of any preacher or group. She is God's field, sown by the apostles, watered by their successors, and made to grow by God alone.

The Latin Church today remembers the Martyrs of Uganda, St Charles Lwanga and his companions. Missionaries had arrived in Buganda only in 1879. Within a few years young men at the court of King Mwanga II, many of them royal pages, had received the word with joy and held it fast. When the king demanded what their faith forbade, they refused. On 3 June 1886, at Namugongo, Charles Lwanga and a group of companions were burned to death; others were killed in the months before and after, the youngest, Kizito, still a boy of about fourteen. Anglican Christians died with them. Pope Paul VI canonised the twenty-two Catholic martyrs in 1964 and in 1969 became the first Pope to visit Africa, going to Namugongo. Today Uganda's Church is one of the most vigorous in the world. The seed that fell on that good soil bore fruit a hundredfold, exactly as the parable says, with patient endurance.

Homily outline

Theme: “But as for that in the good soil, these are the ones who, when they hear the word, hold it fast in an honest and good heart, and bear fruit with patient endurance.”

The generous sower, the soils of the heart, the God who gives the growth, and the patient endurance of the martyrs.

1. God gave the growth

  • Milk, not solid food.
  • I belong to Paul, I belong to Apollos.
  • God's field, God's building.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.” 1 Corinthians 3:6

Apply: Refuse to take part in any talk that divides your parish into parties.

2. The seed is the word of God

  • A sower who throws widely.
  • Let anyone with ears listen.
  • The path where the devil steals the word.
“so shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty, but it shall accomplish that which I purpose, and succeed in the thing for which I sent it.” Isaiah 55:11
““Now the parable is this: The seed is the word of God.” Luke 8:11

Apply: Keep one sentence of Sunday's Gospel all week.

3. The rock and the thorns

  • Joy without roots.
  • The time of testing.
  • Cares, riches and pleasures.
“For thus says the Lord to the people of Judah and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem: Break up your fallow ground, and do not sow among thorns.” Jeremiah 4:3
“As for what fell among the thorns, these are the ones who hear; but as they go on their way, they are choked by the cares and riches and pleasures of life, and their fruit does not mature.” Luke 8:14

Apply: Name the thorn that chokes your prayer and offer it to God.

4. Fruit with patient endurance

  • An honest and good heart.
  • Holding fast.
  • The seed of the martyrs.
“May those who sow in tears reap with shouts of joy.” Psalm 126:5
“But as for that in the good soil, these are the ones who, when they hear the word, hold it fast in an honest and good heart, and bear fruit with patient endurance.” Luke 8:15

Apply: Persevere in one good practice this week even if you see no fruit.

God sows generously; our hearts decide what becomes of the seed.

United as God's field, and patient like the martyrs, we will bear fruit a hundredfold.

Take-home resolution: This week, keep the word, clear one thorn, and refuse every faction.

Full homily

Dear friends, the monsoon has come and the farmers are sowing. Jesus tells us about a sower who throws his seed everywhere, on the path, on the rock, among thorns and on good soil. God is that generous sower: he gives his word to everyone.

The seed is the word of God (Luke 8:11). Some hear it and the devil takes it away at once. Some receive it with joy, but have no root, and fall away when trouble comes. Some hear it, but the cares, riches and pleasures of life choke it. Which soil am I?

The good soil, Jesus says, is the heart that holds the word fast and bears fruit with patient endurance (Luke 8:15). Holding fast means remembering, praying over the word, living it. Patient endurance means not giving up when fruit is slow.

Saint Paul tells the Corinthians that their quarrels show they are still infants: one says, I belong to Paul, another, I belong to Apollos. Paul says: I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. You are God's field. Let us never divide our parish into parties; we all belong to Christ.

Today the Latin Church remembers the Martyrs of Uganda, young men who received the Gospel only a few years before they died for it. The seed fell on good soil, and today their Church is full of life. May God's word find such soil in us. Amen.

For children

Children, a farmer threw seeds. Some fell on the path, some on rocks, some among thorns, and some on good soil, where they grew into a big harvest.

Jesus says his word is the seed, and your heart is the soil. Listen to Jesus and keep his word in your heart, and it will grow into kindness and goodness!

Prayer

Lord God, Lord of the harvest, you sow your word in every heart, on the path, on the rock and among the thorns. Break up the hard ground in us, give our faith deep roots, and clear away the cares, riches and pleasures that choke your word. Keep us from jealousy and factions, for we are your field and your building, and you alone give the growth. By the prayers of the Martyrs of Uganda, give us honest and good hearts that hold your word fast and bear fruit with patient endurance. Through Christ our Lord, to whom with you and the Holy Spirit be glory for ever. Amen.
