Readings

  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 12:12–20 · We are one body in the Spirit
  • Gospel John 6:25–29 · Believe in the one whom he has sent

Word for the day

“Jesus answered them, “This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.”” John 6:29

Reflection

The first week after Pentecost ends with Paul's great image of the Church: “For just as the body is one and has many members, and all the members of the body, though many, are one body, so it is with Christ.” Notice the last words. Paul does not say ‘so it is with the Church’, but “so it is with Christ.” The Church is not a club of people who admire Jesus; she is his Body, and he is her Head. At Pentecost the Spirit did not make the apostles a committee; he made them one living organism with the Risen Lord. Paul tells how this happened to each of us: in the one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, and we were all made to drink of one Spirit (1 Corinthians 12:13). Baptism places us in the Body, Chrismation (Confirmation) gives us the Spirit to drink, and the Holy Qurbana, the one Bread, makes us one Body (1 Corinthians 10:17). The Catechism says that the unity of the body does not do away with the diversity of its members (CCC 791).

Then Paul turns playful, and his humour hides a sharp lesson. “If the foot would say, ‘Because I am not a hand, I do not belong to the body,’ that would not make it any less a part of the body.” He imagines an ear sulking because it is not an eye. “If the whole body were an eye, where would the hearing be?” There are two temptations in a parish. One is envy: I am only a foot, so I am not needed; let the priest and the committee members do everything. The other is pride: I am the eye, and I see everything; the others are useless. Paul answers both with one sentence: “But as it is, God arranged the members in the body, each one of them, as he chose.” No one is in the Church by accident. The grandmother who prays the rosary at the back of the church, the altar server who rings the bell, the catechism teacher, the choir singer, the young man who carries chairs after the feast, the sick person offering pain at home: each is placed by God. In the Season of the Apostles, when we recall how the Church was born and sent, we are invited to ask not what the Church does for me, but where God has placed me in the Body.

The Gospel brings us to the morning after the multiplication of the loaves. The crowds have crossed the lake to find Jesus, and he sees through them: “you are looking for me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate your fill of the loaves.” Then he gives one of the great commands of John's Gospel: “Do not work for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures for eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you.” They ask the practical question: “What must we do to perform the works of God?” And Jesus answers: “This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.” Faith is not idleness; it is the first and greatest work, the work that God himself does in us and with us. Everything else in the Christian life, our prayer, our service, our place in the Body, flows from believing in the One whom the Father sent. On Saturdays many of our families turn with love to the Blessed Virgin Mary, the first to believe the word of the angel and the Mother whom the apostles gathered around in the upper room (Acts 1:14). She is the model member of the Body: hidden, faithful, and wholly turned towards her Son. With her we learn to seek Jesus not for the bread he gives, but for himself, the Bread that endures for eternal life.

Homily outline

Theme: “This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.”

The Church, the one Body of Christ with many members, each placed by God, and faith as the first work, learnt with the Blessed Virgin Mary.

1. So it is with Christ

  • One body, many members.
  • Baptized into one Body, made to drink of one Spirit.
  • The Church, the Body of the Risen Lord.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“For just as the body is one and has many members, and all the members of the body, though many, are one body, so it is with Christ.” 1 Corinthians 12:12

Apply: Pray this week for someone in your parish with whom you find unity difficult.

2. As God arranged, as he chose

  • The foot that envies the hand.
  • The eye that despises the ear.
  • No one in the Body by accident.
“Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations.” Jeremiah 1:5
“But as it is, God arranged the members in the body, each one of them, as he chose.” 1 Corinthians 12:18

Apply: Offer one hour of service to your parish this month, in the place where help is needed.

3. The food that endures

  • Seeking Jesus for bread.
  • Working for the food of eternal life.
  • Faith, the work of God, with Mary.
“Why do you spend your money for that which is not bread, and your labor for that which does not satisfy? Listen carefully to me, and eat what is good, and delight yourselves in rich food.” Isaiah 55:2
“Do not work for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures for eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you. For it is on him that God the Father has set his seal.” John 6:27

Apply: Before each meal this week, ask for the bread that endures for eternal life, and receive the Qurbana with renewed faith.

We are one Body in Christ, each member placed by God and needed by all.

Let us seek Jesus for himself, and believe in the One whom the Father has sent.

Take-home resolution: This week, find your place of service in the parish, thank one hidden member, and pray a decade of the rosary for the unity of the Church.

Full homily

Dear friends, on this Saturday after Pentecost, Paul describes the Church as a body. “For just as the body is one and has many members… so it is with Christ.”

The Church is the Body of Christ, and he is the Head. In Baptism we were placed in that Body, and we drank the one Spirit.

Paul jokes about a foot that says, I am not a hand, so I do not belong. And an ear that says, I am not an eye. How foolish! Every part is needed. “God arranged the members in the body, each one of them, as he chose.”

In our parish there is no unimportant member: the one who sings, the one who teaches, the one who cleans, the one who prays in a sickbed at home. Each is placed by God.

In the Gospel the people follow Jesus because he gave them bread. Jesus says: work for the food that endures for eternal life. And the work of God is this: to believe in the One whom he has sent.

Like the Blessed Virgin Mary, who prayed with the apostles, let us seek Jesus for himself, and serve him gladly in the place he has given us. Amen.

For children

Children, can your eye say to your hand, ‘I don't need you’? No! Your body needs every part. The Church is like that: everyone is important, even the smallest child.

What is your part? Maybe singing, maybe helping, maybe praying for others. Do it with joy, because you belong to Jesus' Body!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Head of the Church, by the one Spirit you baptized us into one Body and gave us one Spirit to drink. Forgive us when we envy the gifts of others or despise our own place; help each of us to serve where the Father has placed us. Give us hunger for the food that endures for eternal life, and the faith that is the work of God. With the Blessed Virgin Mary, who prayed with the apostles in the upper room, may we seek you for yourself and remain one in you, to the glory of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
