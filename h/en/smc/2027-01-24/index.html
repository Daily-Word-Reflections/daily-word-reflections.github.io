Word for the day

“but those who drink of the water that I will give them will never be thirsty. The water that I will give will become in them a spring of water gushing up to eternal life.”” John 4:14

Reflection

Denha is the season of Christ's manifestation, and today he is revealed beside a well. In Genesis Jacob, a fugitive far from home, comes to a well, rolls away the stone, waters Rachel's flock, “kissed Rachel, and wept aloud.” A well in the Bible is a place of meeting and of new beginnings. The second reading tells how the Samaritans came to be: foreigners settled in Samaria by the Assyrian king, who learned something of the LORD but mixed his worship with their own gods. By Jesus' time Jews and Samaritans did not share even a drinking vessel.

So when Jesus, tired from the journey, sits by Jacob's well at noon and says to a Samaritan woman, “Give me a drink”, he crosses every barrier: of race, of religion, of gender, of reputation. He offers her “living water”, and she misunderstands, thinking of the well. “Everyone who drinks of this water will be thirsty again, but those who drink of the water that I will give them will never be thirsty.” He reads her life with truth and without contempt. She speaks of the place of worship; he answers that “God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.” When she says the Messiah is coming, Jesus makes one of the clearest revelations in the Gospel: “I am he, the one who is speaking to you.”

Hebrews urges believers to move beyond the beginnings of faith toward maturity, and not to “become sluggish, but imitators of those who through faith and patience inherit the promises.” The woman left her water jar and ran to tell the town. Denha asks us the same: have we drunk the living water, and are we telling others? The Church is meant to be a well where every thirsty person can meet Christ.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, in Denha Christ is revealed, and today he is revealed beside a well. In Genesis Jacob, far from home, meets Rachel at a well and weeps for joy. In the Bible wells are places of meeting and of new beginnings.

The second reading explains the Samaritans: foreigners settled in Samaria who mixed the worship of the LORD with other gods. By Jesus' time Jews and Samaritans would not even share a cup.

Yet Jesus, tired, sits by Jacob's well at noon and says to a Samaritan woman: give me a drink (John 4:7). He crosses the barriers of race, religion, gender and reputation. Then he offers her living water: those who drink of the water that I will give them will never be thirsty (John 4:14).

He reads her life truthfully but without contempt. He teaches that God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth (John 4:24). And when she speaks of the Messiah, he says: I am he, the one who is speaking to you (John 4:26).

Hebrews tells us not to become sluggish, but imitators of those who through faith and patience inherit the promises (Hebrews 6:12). The woman did not stay still. She left her water jar and ran to the town.

We have received living water in Baptism and receive Christ in the Qurbana. Let us leave our jars, cross barriers, and bring others to the well. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus asked a woman for water and then gave her living water: God's love that never runs out!

Who can you be kind to this week, especially someone who is left out?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, at Jacob's well you asked for a drink and offered living water. Satisfy our thirst with your Holy Spirit, teach us to worship in spirit and truth, and make us, like the Samaritan woman, eager to leave our jars and bring others to you. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
