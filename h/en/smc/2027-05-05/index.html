Readings

  • Epistle Ephesians 5:1–5 · Be imitators of God, as beloved children
  • Gospel John 15:11–17 · I have called you friends

Word for the day

““This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you.”” John 15:12

Reflection

On the eve of the Ascension the Church gives us words from the Last Supper, words of a friend who is about to go away. “I have said these things to you so that my joy may be in you, and that your joy may be complete.” Jesus speaks of joy at the very hour when he is about to suffer. His joy is not the absence of trouble; it is the joy of the Son who knows himself loved by the Father and who loves to the end. He wants that joy in us, and he tells us where it is found: “This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you.” The measure is not our feelings or our convenience but his own love: “No one has greater love than this, to lay down one’s life for one’s friends.” Tomorrow we will see him go up into heaven. Before he goes, he reminds us that the proof of his love was the Cross, and that the only way to remain in his joy is to love in the same way.

Then comes a word that every Christian should hear as addressed to himself: “I do not call you servants any longer, because the servant does not know what the master is doing; but I have called you friends, because I have made known to you everything that I have heard from my Father.” In the Old Testament only a few were called friends of God: Abraham (Isaiah 41:8; James 2:23) and Moses, with whom the Lord spoke “face to face, as one speaks to a friend” (Exodus 33:11). Now Jesus gives that name to fishermen and tax collectors, and through them to us. And he adds: “You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last”. Friendship with Christ is not a private consolation; it sends us out. The disciples would understand this after the Ascension and Pentecost, when the friends of Jesus became his witnesses to the ends of the earth. It is worth asking, on this eve: do I relate to Christ as a slave who obeys from fear, or as a friend who knows the Master's heart and shares his work?

Paul's letter to the Ephesians draws the practical conclusion: “Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children, and live in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God.” Children imitate the parents they love; beloved children of God learn to love as he loves. Then Paul becomes very concrete. Sexual impurity and greed “must not even be mentioned among you, as is proper among saints”, and “Entirely out of place is obscene, silly, and vulgar talk; but instead, let there be thanksgiving.” In our time much obscene and vulgar talk passes through the screens in our hands, in jokes forwarded in groups and in what is watched in secret. Paul's remedy is not only a list of things to avoid but a positive habit: thanksgiving. A heart that thanks God is slow to soil itself. Greed, Paul adds, is a form of idolatry, the worship of things in place of God. On the eve of the Ascension we lift our eyes to the One who goes up to the Father, and we let him lift our hearts from what is low to what lasts: love, purity and gratitude.

Homily outline

Theme: “This is my commandment, that you love one another as I have loved you.”

On the eve of the Ascension: the joy of Jesus, the friendship he offers, and the call to imitate God in love, purity and thanksgiving.

1. Be imitators of God

  • Beloved children imitate the Father.
  • Live in love as Christ loved us.
  • A fragrant offering.
“You shall be holy, for I the Lord your God am holy.” Leviticus 19:2
“Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children, and live in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us” Ephesians 5:1–2

Apply: Choose one way this week to love as Christ loved: patiently, humbly, at a cost.

2. I have called you friends

  • Not servants any longer.
  • Friends who know the Master's heart.
  • Greater love than this.
“But you, Israel, my servant, Jacob, whom I have chosen, the offspring of Abraham, my friend” Isaiah 41:8
“but I have called you friends, because I have made known to you everything that I have heard from my Father.” John 15:15

Apply: Spend some minutes each evening this week speaking to Jesus as to a friend.

3. Fruit that will last

  • You did not choose me.
  • Appointed to go.
  • Thanksgiving in place of vulgar talk.
“They are like trees planted by streams of water, which yield their fruit in its season, and their leaves do not wither. In all that they do, they prosper.” Psalm 1:3
“You did not choose me but I chose you. And I appointed you to go and bear fruit, fruit that will last” John 15:16

Apply: Replace one habit of careless or unclean talk with a habit of thanksgiving.

Before he ascends, Jesus calls us friends and sends us to love as he loved.

Let us imitate God as beloved children and bear fruit that will last.

Take-home resolution: This week, do a hidden act of love each day, speak to Jesus as a friend each evening, and thank God for three gifts before sleeping.

Full homily

Dear friends, tomorrow is the Ascension. Today Jesus speaks to us like a friend who is going away: “I have said these things to you so that my joy may be in you, and that your joy may be complete” (John 15:11).

He gives us one commandment: “love one another as I have loved you” (John 15:12). How did he love us? “No one has greater love than this, to lay down one’s life for one’s friends.”

Then he says something wonderful: “I have called you friends” (John 15:15). Not servants, but friends. And he chose us to go and bear fruit that will last.

Paul tells us to be “imitators of God, as beloved children” (Ephesians 5:1). That means living in love, keeping our hearts and words pure, and instead of vulgar talk, giving thanks.

Tonight, let us speak to Jesus as a friend, and tomorrow come to the Holy Qurbana to celebrate his Ascension with joy. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus calls you his friend! A friend shares, helps and loves.

Tomorrow Jesus goes up to heaven, but he is still our friend. Talk to him tonight before you sleep.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, on the eve of your Ascension you call us your friends and give us your commandment of love. Let your joy be in us and make our joy complete. Teach us to love as you have loved us, to the laying down of our lives. Keep our hearts and our words clean, and fill our mouths with thanksgiving. You chose us: send us to bear fruit that will last, and tomorrow lift our hearts with you to the Father. Amen. Alleluia!
