Word for the day

“The water that I will give will become in them a spring of water gushing up to eternal life.” John 4:14

Reflection

It is noon in Samaria. “Jesus, tired out by his journey, was sitting by the well.” The Lord who has now ascended to the Father once sat tired on the edge of Jacob's well, in the heat of the day, and asked a woman for water: “Give me a drink.” She is astonished, for “Jews do not share things in common with Samaritans”, and a Jewish teacher did not usually speak alone with a woman of Samaria. Jesus crosses both walls at once. He is not ashamed to need something from her. That is how God often begins with us: not with a command but with a request. He asks for a little of our time, a little of our attention, a cup of water, so that he can give far more. “If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that is saying to you, ‘Give me a drink,’ you would have asked him, and he would have given you living water.” The woman thinks of running water from a spring rather than the still water of the cistern. Jesus means something deeper: the gift of God himself.

She objects with the practical sense of a woman who has carried water every day of her life: “Sir, you have no bucket, and the well is deep.” And she asks the great question: “Are you greater than our ancestor Jacob?” Jesus answers by comparing two kinds of water. “Everyone who drinks of this water will be thirsty again, but those who drink of the water that I will give them will never be thirsty. The water that I will give will become in them a spring of water gushing up to eternal life.” John explains later what this water is: “Now he said this about the Spirit, which believers in him were to receive; for as yet there was no Spirit, because Jesus was not yet glorified” (John 7:39). Now Jesus has been glorified. He has gone up to the Father, and the Spirit is about to be poured out. That is why we read this Gospel in the days of the novena. Every thirst we carry, for love, for meaning, for forgiveness, for peace, is in the end a thirst for God, and the Holy Spirit is the spring that God places inside us. The woman's first answer is still on the surface: “Sir, give me this water, so that I may never be thirsty or have to keep coming here to draw water.” But she has begun to ask, and the conversation that follows will make her the first missionary of her town.

Paul writes to the Thessalonians with gratitude, because the same Spirit has already been at work in them. “God chose you as the first fruits for salvation through sanctification by the Spirit and through belief in the truth.” The first fruits were the first part of the harvest, offered to God as a promise of the rest. The young Church of Thessalonica was the first harvest in its city; the Syro-Malabar Church, born from the preaching of Thomas, was among the first fruits of the Gospel in India. Paul's advice to such a Church is simple: “So then, brothers and sisters, stand firm and hold fast to the traditions that you were taught by us, either by word of mouth or by our letter.” The faith comes to us by the living voice of the Church and by the Scriptures together. Our Mar Thoma tradition, the prayers our grandparents taught us, the way our Church celebrates the Qurbana, are not dead customs; they are channels through which the living water has reached us. Paul ends with a blessing for these days of waiting: “may our Lord Jesus Christ himself and God our Father, who loved us and through grace gave us eternal comfort and good hope, comfort your hearts and strengthen them in every good work and word.”

  • God begins with a request.

“A Samaritan woman came to draw water, and Jesus said to her, “Give me a drink.”” John 4:7

Apply: Cross one wall this week by speaking kindly to someone your circle keeps at a distance.

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Gospel takes us to a well in Samaria at noon. Jesus is tired, and he sits down. A woman comes to draw water, and Jesus asks her: “Give me a drink.”

She is surprised. Jews and Samaritans did not mix, and a Jewish teacher did not talk alone with a Samaritan woman. But Jesus crosses those walls. He is not ashamed to ask. God often begins with us like this: not with an order, but with a request. He asks for a little of our time, so that he can give us much more.

Jesus says: “If you knew the gift of God,” you would have asked me, and I would have given you living water. Whoever drinks ordinary water will be thirsty again. But “the water that I will give will become in them a spring of water gushing up to eternal life.” This living water is the Holy Spirit. Jesus has now gone up to the Father, and the Spirit is about to be poured out. In this novena, let us bring him our thirst.

Paul tells the Thessalonians: God chose you as first fruits. Stand firm and hold fast to the traditions you were taught. Our Church received the faith from St Thomas, and our grandparents handed it to us. These traditions are channels through which the living water has reached us. Let us keep them and hand them on.

The woman said, “Sir, give me this water.” Let that be our prayer today: Lord, give us this water. Fill us with your Spirit at Pentecost. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus was thirsty, and he asked a woman for water. Then he told her about a special water that he gives, the Holy Spirit.

When we pray, the Holy Spirit fills our heart like a little spring of water that never dries. Say today: ‘Jesus, give me your living water!’

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, tired from your journey you sat by the well and asked for a drink, so that you might give us the living water. Now you are glorified at the Father's right hand: pour out on us the Spirit you promised, and let him become in us a spring gushing up to eternal life. Satisfy our deepest thirst, help us to cross the walls that divide people, and keep us faithful to the traditions handed down to us from the apostles. Comfort our hearts and strengthen them in every good work and word. Amen. Alleluia!
