Word for the day

“And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” John 17:3

Reflection

On the night before he died, at the end of the Last Supper, Jesus lifted his eyes to heaven and prayed aloud before his disciples. The Church calls this prayer, which fills the seventeenth chapter of John, the prayer of the Hour or the high-priestly prayer. “Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you.” The hour is the hour of the Cross, and Jesus calls it glory. On the Cross the love of the Father and the Son is shown to the world, and through the Cross the Son returns to the glory he had “before the world existed”. We read this prayer in the days after the Ascension because the prayer has been answered: the Son has been glorified and has gone back to the Father. But the prayer has not ended. The Letter to the Hebrews says that the Risen Lord “always lives to make intercession” for us (Hebrews 7:25). What Jesus prayed in the upper room he goes on praying at the Father's right hand, and in these nine days the Church in the upper room joins her prayer to his.

At the centre of the prayer is a definition: “And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” Eternal life is not first of all a long life after death; it is a relationship that begins now. In the Bible to know someone is more than to know facts about him; it is to love him, to share his life, as husband and wife know each other. So eternal life is to know the Father and the Son in the love of the Holy Spirit. A child preparing for First Holy Communion who learns to talk to Jesus has begun eternal life. An old grandmother saying her rosary in the evening is living it. Jesus adds, “I glorified you on earth by finishing the work that you gave me to do.” His whole life was obedience, and that obedience is his glory. We too glorify God not by great deeds but by doing faithfully the work he gives us, in the kitchen, the classroom, the hospital ward, the field.

The First Letter of John shows what that knowledge looks like in practice. “No one has ever seen God; if we love one another, God lives in us, and his love is perfected in us.” We cannot see God, but we can see our brother, and in loving him the invisible God becomes present. Then comes the link with Pentecost: “By this we know that we abide in him and he in us, because he has given us of his Spirit.” The Spirit is the gift that makes this love possible. John puts it in words that sum up the whole Gospel: “God is love, and those who abide in love abide in God, and God abides in them.” And he draws a bold consequence: “There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear.” Many Christians live with a fearful religion, afraid of God as of a harsh judge. John says that the closer we come to God's love, the less room fear has. But he will not let us escape into sweet feelings: “Those who say, ‘I love God,’ and hate their brothers or sisters, are liars.” On this day of the novena we ask the Spirit for a love that is real: love that casts out fear before God and love that reaches the brother we can see.

Homily outline

Theme: “And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.”

The sixth day of the novena: the prayer of the Hour that the Ascended Lord still prays, eternal life as knowing God, and the love poured out by the Spirit that casts out fear.

1. Father, the hour has come

  • The Cross as glory.
  • The prayer answered at the Ascension.
  • The High Priest who still intercedes.
“I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore I have continued my faithfulness to you.” Jeremiah 31:3
“Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you.” John 17:1

Apply: Read John 17 a few verses each day this week, and join your prayer to the prayer of Jesus.

2. This is eternal life

  • To know, not only to know about.
  • A relationship that begins now.
  • Glorifying God by finishing our work.
“For I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings.” Hosea 6:6
“And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” John 17:3

Apply: Spend ten minutes today speaking to Jesus as to a friend.

3. Perfect love casts out fear

  • God is love.
  • The Spirit, the gift that makes love possible.
  • The brother we can see.
“Do not fear, for I am with you, do not be afraid, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, I will help you, I will uphold you with my victorious right hand.” Isaiah 41:10
“There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear; for fear has to do with punishment, and whoever fears has not reached perfection in love.” 1 John 4:18

Apply: Entrust one fear to God's love in the novena, and do one act of love for someone difficult.

The Lord who prayed for us on the night before he died prays for us still, and gives us his Spirit of love.

To know him is eternal life; to love the brother is the proof that we know him.

Take-home resolution: This week, pray John 17, speak to Jesus as a friend each day, and let love overcome one fear and one dislike.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the night before he died, Jesus lifted his eyes to heaven and prayed: “Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son so that the Son may glorify you.” The hour was the Cross, and Jesus called it glory, because on the Cross God's love was shown to the world.

We read this prayer after the Ascension because it has been answered: Jesus has returned to the glory of the Father. But he has not stopped praying. He “always lives to make intercession” for us. In this novena, we join our prayer to his.

Jesus says: “This is eternal life, that they may know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.” Eternal life begins now. To know God is not only to know facts about him; it is to love him and live with him, as a friend. A child who talks to Jesus has begun eternal life.

John tells us: “God is love, and those who abide in love abide in God.” God has given us his Spirit so that we can love. And “perfect love casts out fear.” We do not need to live afraid of God. But John adds: if you say you love God and hate your brother, you are a liar. Love for God must reach the brother we can see.

Let us ask the Holy Spirit today for this love: love that takes away fear, and love that reaches the people around us. Come, Holy Spirit! Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, John says: God is love! God loves you more than anyone can.

When we love one another, God lives in us. And when we know how much God loves us, we are not afraid. Today, say: ‘Jesus, I love you, and I am not afraid!’

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, on the night of your Passion you lifted your eyes to heaven and prayed for us, and now at the Father's right hand you live for ever to intercede for us. Grant us eternal life: to know the Father, the only true God, and you whom he has sent. Pour into our hearts the Spirit of love, so that perfect love may cast out all fear, and we may love the brothers and sisters we can see. Let us glorify the Father by finishing the work he gives us each day. With Mary, who abode in your love, we pray: come, Holy Spirit. Amen. Alleluia!
