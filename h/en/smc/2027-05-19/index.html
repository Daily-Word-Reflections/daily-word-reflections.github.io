Word for the day

“The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed that someone took and sowed in his field;” Matthew 13:31

Reflection

Three days after Pentecost, in the first week of the Season of the Apostles, the Gospel gives us two small parables about the growth of the Church. “The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed that someone took and sowed in his field; it is the smallest of all the seeds, but when it has grown it is the greatest of shrubs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and make nests in its branches.” Think of the beginning: about one hundred and twenty people in an upper room, a handful of fishermen, a few women, the Mother of Jesus. Think of what came of it: on the day of Pentecost three thousand, and within a few generations communities from Spain to Persia and, as our tradition holds, to the coast of Malabar. The image of the tree in whose branches the birds nest comes from the prophets. Ezekiel saw God plant a tender sprig on the mountain of Israel, so that it would become a noble cedar: “Under it every kind of bird will live” (Ezekiel 17:23). The birds are the nations. Our Church, which began, as tradition holds, with the preaching of Mar Thoma among a few families of Malabar, is one of those branches. That is not a reason for pride but for gratitude, and for patience: God's work begins small and grows slowly, and it is God who gives the growth.

The second parable is even more hidden. “The kingdom of heaven is like yeast that a woman took and mixed in with three measures of flour until all of it was leavened.” Three measures of flour is a great quantity, enough bread for a large household; it is what Sarah prepared when the three visitors came to Abraham (Genesis 18:6). The yeast disappears into the dough; no one sees it working, yet nothing stays as it was. So the Gospel works in the world: not usually through spectacle, but through Christians mixed into ordinary life, in offices and fields, in hospitals and classrooms, in families, quietly changing everything from within. Matthew adds that Jesus spoke in parables to fulfil the prophecy: “I will open my mouth to speak in parables; I will proclaim what has been hidden from the foundation of the world.” What was hidden from the beginning is God's plan to gather all peoples into one, and Pentecost has revealed it. The apostles were the first yeast; today it is our turn to be mixed into the dough of our society without losing our flavour.

Paul's words to the Corinthians concern the Lord's Supper, and they come right after his account of its institution. Some in Corinth were eating their own meal before the poor arrived, so that “one goes hungry and another becomes drunk.” Paul warns: “But if we judged ourselves, we would not be judged.” And he gives a simple rule: “So then, my brothers and sisters, when you come together to eat, wait for one another.” Before we receive the Body of the Lord, we examine ourselves; our Church has always taught that one who is conscious of grave sin should first approach the sacrament of Reconciliation. And the Body we receive makes us one body with the poor, the late, the one who has nothing to bring. The mustard seed and the yeast grow in a community that waits for one another. It is fitting that the Syro-Malankara calendar recalls today the tradition of the first offering of the Holy Qurbono by Mar Yaqob Shleeha in Jerusalem: from that small first breaking of bread, the Eucharist of the apostles has spread like yeast through the whole world, and it reaches our altar every day.

Homily outline

Theme: “The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed that someone took and sowed in his field;”

The Church growing from small beginnings, the hidden yeast that changes the world, and a Eucharistic community that waits for one another.

1. The smallest of all the seeds

  • One hundred and twenty in an upper room.
  • The tree where the birds nest.
  • God gives the growth.
“On the mountain height of Israel I will plant it, in order that it may produce boughs and bear fruit, and become a noble cedar. Under it every kind of bird will live;” Ezekiel 17:23
“it is the smallest of all the seeds, but when it has grown it is the greatest of shrubs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the air come and make nests in its branches.” Matthew 13:32

Apply: Begin one small, faithful practice this week and keep it every day.

2. Yeast mixed into the dough

  • Three measures of flour.
  • Hidden, yet changing everything.
  • Christians in daily life.
“For whoever has despised the day of small things shall rejoice,” Zechariah 4:10
“The kingdom of heaven is like yeast that a woman took and mixed in with three measures of flour until all of it was leavened.” Matthew 13:33

Apply: Do one hidden act of goodness each day this week at your work or school, without telling anyone.

3. Wait for one another

  • Judging ourselves before the Supper.
  • The poor and the late.
  • One Body at one table.
“Search me, O God, and know my heart; test me and know my thoughts.” Psalm 139:23
“So then, my brothers and sisters, when you come together to eat, wait for one another.” 1 Corinthians 11:33

Apply: Make a careful examination of conscience before your next Holy Communion, and go to confession if you need to.

The Church of the apostles grows like a seed and works like yeast, and her heart is a table where all wait for one another.

Let us be faithful in small things, hidden and active in the world, and prepared at the altar.

Take-home resolution: This week, keep one small daily practice, do one hidden good deed each day, and prepare well for Holy Communion.

Full homily

Dear friends, three days after Pentecost Jesus tells us how his Church grows. “The kingdom of heaven is like a mustard seed” (Matthew 13:31), the smallest of seeds, that becomes a tree where the birds of the air make their nests.

The Church began with about one hundred and twenty people in an upper room. Today she is in every land, and the birds of every nation find shelter in her branches. Our own Church in Kerala began, as tradition holds, with Mar Thoma and a few families. God's work starts small and grows slowly, and it is God who gives the growth.

Then Jesus says the kingdom is like yeast mixed into three measures of flour until all is leavened (13:33). Yeast is hidden, but it changes everything. Christians are meant to be such yeast in offices, fields, schools and homes: not apart from the world, but mixed into it, without losing their strength.

Paul warns the Corinthians about their Lord's Supper: “if we judged ourselves, we would not be judged” (1 Corinthians 11:31). Before we receive the Body of the Lord we examine our conscience. And he adds: “when you come together to eat, wait for one another” (11:33). The Eucharist makes us one body with the poor and the last.

So let us not despise small beginnings, let us be yeast in the world, and let us come to the Holy Qurbana prepared and attentive to one another. Amen.

For children

Children, a mustard seed is tiny, like a speck of dust, but it grows into a big plant where birds can sit! Jesus says small good things done with love grow big with God. What small good thing will you do today?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you compared the kingdom to a mustard seed and to a little yeast. Make your Church grow from small beginnings into a tree that shelters every nation, and make us yeast hidden in the dough of our society, changing it from within. Teach us to examine ourselves before we come to your table, and to wait for one another, especially for the poor and the last. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
