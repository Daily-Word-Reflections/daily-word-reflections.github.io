Fifth Wednesday of Denha

Readings

Word for the day

“My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to complete his work.” John 4:34

Reflection

Paul writes to Timothy as an old man near the end of his race, and his words have the weight of a last will: “I solemnly urge you: proclaim the message; be persistent whether the time is favorable or unfavorable; convince, rebuke, and encourage, with the utmost patience in teaching.” He knows what is coming: “the time is coming when people will not put up with sound doctrine, but having itching ears, they will accumulate for themselves teachers to suit their own desires.” Our age of endless voices, each telling us what we like to hear, is not new. Timothy must stay steady: “always be sober, endure suffering, do the work of an evangelist, carry out your ministry fully.” And Paul himself is ready to give the last drop: “As for me, I am already being poured out as a libation, and the time of my departure has come.”

In the Gospel the Samaritan woman has just met Jesus at the well. The disciples come back and are astonished that he is speaking with a woman. She, meanwhile, “left her water jar and went back to the city.” The jar she came for is forgotten; she has found living water. She says to the people, “Come and see a man who told me everything I have ever done! He cannot be the Messiah, can he?” The disciples urge Jesus, “Rabbi, eat something,” and he answers, “I have food to eat that you do not know about.” They think someone has brought him lunch. He explains: “My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to complete his work.” Then he points to the Samaritans already walking toward him across the fields: “look around you, and see how the fields are ripe for harvesting.” One sows and another reaps. In Denha Christ is revealed even to the Samaritans, the outsiders, through a woman who simply said: come and see. We in Kerala reap what the apostle Thomas and many generations of priests, religious and catechists sowed. “Others have labored, and you have entered into their labor.”

Homily outline

Theme: “My food is to do the will of him who sent me.”

The apostle's last charge, the woman who left her water jar, and the harvest that others sowed.

1. My food is to do his will

  • Rabbi, eat something.
  • Food you do not know about.
  • To complete his work.
“I delight to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart.” Psalm 40:8
“Jesus said to them, “My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to complete his work.” John 4:34

Apply: Begin today by asking: Father, what is your will for me today?

2. The fields are ripe

  • She left her water jar.
  • Come and see.
  • One sows and another reaps.
“May those who sow in tears reap with shouts of joy.” Psalm 126:5
“Do you not say, ‘Four months more, then comes the harvest’? But I tell you, look around you, and see how the fields are ripe for harvesting.” John 4:35

Apply: Invite one person who has drifted away to come with you to the Holy Qurbana.

3. Proclaim the message

  • In season and out of season.
  • Itching ears.
  • Poured out as a libation.
“How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of the messenger who announces peace, who brings good news, who announces salvation, who says to Zion, “Your God reigns.”” Isaiah 52:7
“proclaim the message; be persistent whether the time is favorable or unfavorable; convince, rebuke, and encourage, with the utmost patience in teaching.” 2 Timothy 4:2

Apply: Speak a word of faith this week where you would usually stay silent.

Christ is fed by the Father's will, and he feeds us so that we may do it too.

Leave your water jar, look at the fields, and proclaim him.

Take-home resolution: Thank and pray for those who first taught you the faith, and share it with one person this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul charges Timothy: proclaim the message; be persistent whether the time is favorable or unfavorable (2 Timothy 4:2). People will look for teachers who say what their itching ears want to hear. But Timothy must do the work of an evangelist.

In the Gospel the Samaritan woman leaves her water jar and runs back to the city: come and see a man who told me everything I have ever done! She came for water and found the Messiah.

The disciples urge Jesus to eat. He says: my food is to do the will of him who sent me and to complete his work (John 4:34). Then he shows them the fields ripe for harvesting.

We reap what others have sown: the apostle Thomas, our missionaries, our parents and catechists. Let us thank them, leave our water jars, and say to someone: come and see. Amen.

For children

Children, a lady met Jesus at a well and was so happy that she forgot her water jar! She ran to tell everyone: come and see Jesus! Who can you tell about Jesus today?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, at the well you gave living water to a woman who came only for water, and she ran to bring others to you. Make your Father's will our food, give us courage to proclaim your word in every season, and let us gather with joy the harvest that others have sown. Amen.
