Word for the day

“Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” And I said, “Here am I; send me!”” Isaiah 6:8

Reflection

This is the Sunday after the Ascension. Three days ago we watched the Lord taken up; next Sunday we will celebrate the coming of the Spirit. Today the Church stands in between, and the four readings tell one story: the Lord who reigns in glory sends people out. The Gospel of Mark ends with that double movement. The Risen Lord appears to the eleven “as they were sitting at the table”; he reproaches them for their unbelief, and then, astonishingly, he entrusts the whole world to these same slow-hearted men: “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” Then “the Lord Jesus, after he had spoken to them, was taken up into heaven and sat down at the right hand of God.” And the last verse of the Gospel says: “they went out and proclaimed the good news everywhere, while the Lord worked with them and confirmed the message by the signs that accompanied it.” The Lord is seated in heaven and working on earth at the same time. That is the meaning of the Ascension for the Church: he has not gone away from his work; he does it now through us. One of those eleven was Thomas, and the good news he carried reached our shores. Every Syro-Malabar Christian who hears this Gospel is the fruit of that sending.

The first reading shows us the throne from which the sending comes. “In the year that King Uzziah died, I saw the Lord sitting on a throne, high and lofty; and the hem of his robe filled the temple.” The seraphs cry to one another: “Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory.” We sing that hymn in every Holy Qurbana, joining the angels before the throne of the One who has ascended. Isaiah's first response is fear: “Woe is me! I am lost, for I am a man of unclean lips.” Then a seraph touches his lips with a live coal from the altar, and says, “your guilt has departed and your sin is blotted out.” Syriac Fathers such as St Ephrem saw in that coal, taken from the altar with tongs and placed on the lips, a figure of the Holy Qurbana, the fire of the Lord's Body given to us. Only after he is purified does Isaiah hear the question: “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” And he answers, “Here am I; send me!” The mission that follows is hard: the people will not listen, the land will be laid waste. Yet the chapter ends with hope: “The holy seed is its stump.” God never abandons his people; from the stump of Jesse the Messiah will come.

The Acts of the Apostles shows the Church obeying that sending in the days between the Ascension and Pentecost. Just before today's reading we hear that the apostles “were constantly devoting themselves to prayer, together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus” (Acts 1:14). Then Peter stands up among about a hundred and twenty believers. The circle of the Twelve has been broken by the betrayal of Judas, and it must be restored before the Spirit comes. The one chosen must be a witness: “one of the men who have accompanied us during all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us, beginning from the baptism of John until the day when he was taken up from us, one of these must become a witness with us to his resurrection.” They propose two, Joseph called Barsabbas and Matthias. They do not campaign; they pray: “Lord, you know everyone's heart. Show us which one of these two you have chosen.” The lot falls on Matthias, “and he was added to the eleven apostles.” Here, in the presence of Mary, we see the apostolic Church taking care for its own continuity: the ministry of the apostles is handed on. Our bishops stand in that same line of witnesses, and the Synod of our Church still begins its elections with prayer to the Lord who knows every heart.

Paul's letter to the Philippians sings the whole mystery we are celebrating. “Let the same mind be in you that was in Christ Jesus, who, though he was in the form of God, did not regard equality with God as something to be exploited, but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave, being born in human likeness.” He went down, all the way to the Cross. “Therefore God also highly exalted him and gave him the name that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.” The Ascension is that exaltation made visible: the One who emptied himself now sits at the right hand of God. But Paul did not write this hymn only to tell us about Christ; he wrote it to change the way the Philippians lived together. “Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility regard others as better than yourselves.” The way up for the Church is the way Christ went: the way down, in humility and service. A parish quarrelling about positions, a family where everyone wants the first place, will not proclaim the Gospel well. A community that has the mind of Christ will be sent, like Isaiah and like the eleven, and the Lord will work with it.

Homily outline

Theme: “Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, ‘Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?’ And I said, ‘Here am I; send me!’”

On the Sunday after the Ascension: the thrice-holy God who purifies and sends, the witness chosen in prayer with Mary, the self-emptying and exaltation of Christ, and the mission to all the world.

1. Holy, holy, holy

  • The throne, high and lofty.
  • Woe is me: the sinner before the Holy One.
  • The live coal from the altar.
“Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory.” Isaiah 6:3
“Holy, holy, holy, the Lord God the Almighty, who was and is and is to come.” Revelation 4:8

Apply: Sing the angels' hymn at the Qurbana with attention, and receive Holy Communion as the live coal that purifies you.

2. Here am I; send me

  • The question comes after the purification.
  • A hard mission and a holy seed.
  • Isaiah's answer, and ours.
“Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” And I said, “Here am I; send me!”” Isaiah 6:8
“And he said to them, “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.”” Mark 16:15

Apply: Offer one concrete service to your parish or neighbourhood this week.

3. One must become a witness

  • The circle of the Twelve restored.
  • Prayer, not campaigning.
  • The apostles' successors today.
“O LORD, you have searched me and known me.” Psalm 139:1
“Then they prayed and said, “Lord, you know everyone's heart. Show us which one of these two you have chosen.”” Acts 1:24

Apply: Pray each day this week for the bishops of our Synod and for every decision your family must take.

4. Every tongue shall confess that Jesus Christ is Lord

  • He emptied himself.
  • Therefore God exalted him.
  • The same mind in us.
“By myself I have sworn, from my mouth has gone forth in righteousness a word that shall not return: “To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear.”” Isaiah 45:23
“So that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.” Philippians 2:10–11

Apply: In one quarrel or competition this week, give the first place to the other.

The Lord who emptied himself is exalted at the right hand of the Father, and from there he sends us and works with us.

Purified at the altar and praying with Mary, let us answer: Here am I; send me.

Take-home resolution: This week, offer one service, pray daily for our bishops, choose the last place once, and pray the novena with Mary for the Holy Spirit.

Full homily

Dear friends, on Thursday we celebrated the Ascension, and next Sunday we will celebrate Pentecost. Today we stand in between, and the readings tell us one thing: the Lord who reigns in glory sends his people out.

Isaiah sees the Lord on a high throne, and the angels singing, “Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts.” We sing that same hymn at every Holy Qurbana. Isaiah is afraid: “Woe is me! I am lost, for I am a man of unclean lips.” An angel touches his lips with a burning coal from the altar, and his sin is taken away. Our Syriac Fathers saw in that coal a picture of the Holy Qurbana, placed on our lips. Then God asks, “Whom shall I send?” And Isaiah answers, “Here am I; send me!”

In Acts, the apostles are praying with Mary, the mother of Jesus. Judas is gone, and the circle of the Twelve must be complete before the Spirit comes. They choose a witness to the Resurrection. They do not make speeches; they pray: “Lord, you know everyone's heart.” Matthias is chosen. The work of the apostles is handed on. Our bishops stand in that line today. Let us pray for them.

Paul sings of Jesus, who “emptied himself, taking the form of a slave”, and went down to the Cross. “Therefore God also highly exalted him.” The Ascension is that exaltation. But Paul says: let the same mind be in you. Do nothing from selfish ambition. Regard others as better than yourselves. The Church goes up the way Christ went: by going down in humility and service.

In the Gospel the Risen Lord scolds his disciples for their unbelief, and then he sends these same weak men: “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” He is taken up into heaven, and “they went out and proclaimed the good news everywhere, while the Lord worked with them.” He is in heaven and at work on earth at the same time.

Thomas was one of those eleven. Because he went, we are here. Now the Lord asks each of us: Whom shall I send? Maybe across the sea, maybe across the road to a neighbour. Let us answer with Isaiah: Here am I; send me. And let us pray with Mary this week for the Holy Spirit, who will give us the strength to go. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Isaiah heard God ask, “Whom shall I send?” and he said, “Here am I; send me!”

Before Jesus went up to heaven, he sent his friends to tell everyone about him. Jesus wants you to be his helper too. This week, can you tell one friend something good about Jesus? Say to him: ‘Here I am, Jesus!’

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you emptied yourself and became obedient unto death, and the Father has exalted you and seated you at his right hand. With the seraphs we cry, Holy, holy, holy. Touch our lips with the live coal of your altar, take away our guilt, and make us answer, Here am I; send me. Guide the successors of your apostles as you guided the choice of Matthias. Give us your mind, humble and loving, so that our communities may proclaim you. Work with us, Lord, wherever you send us, until every tongue confesses that you are Lord, to the glory of the Father. With Mary your Mother we pray: send us your Holy Spirit. Amen. Alleluia!
