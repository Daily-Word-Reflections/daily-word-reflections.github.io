Word for the day

“I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live.” John 11:25

Reflection

Today the whole Church prays for the faithful departed. We do it not out of sentiment alone but out of faith: we believe in the resurrection of the body, and we believe in the communion of saints, in which the living and the dead remain one family in Christ.

Ezekiel stands in a valley of dry bones. “Can these bones live?” God answers by his word and his Spirit: “I will open your graves… I will put my spirit within you, and you shall live.” What seemed final is not final for God. Judas Maccabeus, finding that fallen soldiers had worn idols, prays and offers a sacrifice for them, “so that they might be delivered from their sin”. Scripture calls it “a holy and pious thought”. Here the Church finds an ancient witness to prayer for the dead.

The Catholic faith teaches that those who die in God's grace but are not yet fully purified undergo a purification after death, which the Church calls purgatory, so as to enter the joy of heaven (Catechism 1030–1032). Our prayers, and above all the Holy Qurbana offered for them, help them. This is love that death does not end.

At Bethany, Martha meets Jesus with grief and faith: “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.” Jesus answers not with an explanation but with himself: “I am the resurrection and the life.” Christian hope is not an idea about the afterlife; it is a Person who has conquered death. With Paul we can say: “Death has been swallowed up in victory.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Love that death does not end.”

On the Commemoration of the Dead we pray for the faithful departed, confessing the resurrection of the body and the communion of saints.

1. Can these bones live?

  • Ezekiel's valley of dry bones: what seems final is not final for God.
  • God's word and Spirit bring life where there is none.
“And you shall know that I am the LORD, when I open your graves, and bring you up from your graves, O my people. I will put my spirit within you, and you shall live.” Ezekiel 37:13–14
“Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live.”” John 11:25

Apply: Bring one hopeless situation to God in prayer this week.

2. A holy and pious thought

  • Judas Maccabeus offers sacrifice for the dead.
  • The Church prays for souls being purified (CCC 1030–1032); the Qurbana is the greatest help.
“But if he was looking to the splendid reward that is laid up for those who fall asleep in godliness, it was a holy and pious thought. Therefore he made atonement for the dead, so that they might be delivered from their sin.” 2 Maccabees 12:45
“So you have pain now; but I will see you again, and your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you.” John 16:22

Apply: Have a Qurbana offered for the souls in purgatory this month.

3. In the hand of God

  • The souls of the righteous are in God's hand.
  • Even in the darkest valley the Shepherd is with us.
“But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and no torment will ever touch them.” Wisdom 3:1
“And everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?” John 11:26

Apply: Visit the cemetery with your family and pray there.

Our grief is real; our hope is greater, because it is Christ himself.

Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord, and let perpetual light shine upon them.

Take-home resolution: Pray for your departed by name every evening in November.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today we come to the altar carrying names: parents, grandparents, a spouse, a child, a friend. We remember them with tears, but not without hope.

Ezekiel stood in a valley full of dry bones. God asked him, “Can these bones live?” Humanly the answer is no. But God spoke his word and sent his Spirit, and the bones stood up alive. For God, death is not the last word.

In the second reading Judas Maccabeus prays and offers sacrifice for soldiers who had died in sin. Scripture calls it a holy and pious thought. The Church has always prayed for the dead. Those who die in God's friendship but are not yet fully purified are helped by our prayers and above all by the Holy Qurbana.

At Bethany Martha says to Jesus, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.” How many of us have said the same. Jesus does not give a lecture; he gives himself: “I am the resurrection and the life.” Then he asks her, and he asks us: “Do you believe this?”

Today let us answer with Martha: “Yes, Lord, I believe.” And let our love for our dead become prayer. Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord, and let perpetual light shine upon them. Amen.

For children

Children, do you remember someone in your family who has died?

Jesus told Martha: I am the resurrection and the life. He loves our grandparents and everyone who has died, and he will raise them up.

Some of them may still need our prayers to be fully ready for heaven. We can help them by praying.

Every night this month, let us say together: Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord, and let perpetual light shine upon them.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, resurrection and life, grant to our departed brothers and sisters eternal rest. Purify them in your mercy, bring them into the light of your face, and keep us in the hope of meeting them again in your Kingdom. Amen.
