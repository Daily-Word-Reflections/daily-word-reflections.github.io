Readings

  • Epistle Hebrews 8:7–13 · The new covenant inscribed in the heart
  • Gospel John 2:13–25 · Jesus is the true temple

Word for the day

“Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” John 2:19

Reflection

Four days after Pentecost the Letter to the Hebrews sets before us the promise that the Holy Spirit came to fulfil. The author quotes the prophet Jeremiah at length: “The days are surely coming, says the Lord, when I will establish a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah.” It will not be like the covenant made on the day God took his people “by the hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt.” What will be new? “I will put my laws in their minds, and write them on their hearts, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.” At Sinai the commandments were written on tablets of stone and carried in the ark. They were holy and good, but they stood outside the human heart, and the heart could break them. The new covenant does not throw the law away; it carries it inside. That is exactly what happened in the upper room. The Spirit who came down as fire did not hand the apostles a new set of tablets; he became the law of love within them. The Catechism says it simply: the New Law is the grace of the Holy Spirit given to the faithful through faith in Christ (CCC 1965–1966). And the last line of the promise is the sweetest: “I will remember their sins no more.” The covenant written on the heart begins with forgiveness.

We must read the author's words about the first covenant with care. When he says that God has “made the first one obsolete”, he is speaking of the sacrifices and the priesthood of the Tabernacle, now fulfilled in the one sacrifice of Christ. He is not saying that God has abandoned the people he first loved. The Church teaches that the Old Covenant has never been revoked (CCC 121), and Paul says that “the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable” (Romans 11:29). Jeremiah's promise was first spoken to Israel, “the house of Israel and the house of Judah”, and we Gentiles have been welcomed into it as guests made children. This is why the Church reads the Old Testament every day with reverence, and why our Syro-Malabar liturgy, heir of the Church of the East, keeps so many prayers and psalms of the Jewish people. The Spirit who writes the law on our hearts writes in the same handwriting that once traced the commandments on stone.

The Gospel takes us into the Temple of Jerusalem at Passover. Jesus finds cattle, sheep and doves for sale and money changers at their tables. He makes a whip of cords, drives them out, overturns the tables and says, “Take these things out of here! Stop making my Father’s house a marketplace!” The disciples remember the psalm: “Zeal for your house will consume me.” His anger is not contempt for the Temple; it is love for it. Jesus went up to the Temple as a child, prayed there, taught there, and called it his Father's house (CCC 583–584). When the leaders ask for a sign, he answers, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” They think of the stones that had been under construction for forty-six years, “But he was speaking of the temple of his body.” The evangelist adds a line that belongs to this season: “After he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this; and they believed the scripture and the word that Jesus had spoken.” Remembering is the work of the Spirit, who teaches us everything and reminds us of all that Jesus said (John 14:26). The Risen Body of Christ is now the true Temple, the place where God and humanity meet (CCC 586).

Two more truths follow from this Gospel. First, those who belong to Christ become part of that Temple: “Do you not know that you are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit dwells in you?” (1 Corinthians 3:16). Our parish churches, with the madbaha set apart and the altar at its heart, are signs of the living Church made of people; the cleansing of the Temple must therefore happen first in us, where greed, noise and bargaining with God can so easily set up their tables. Second, the Gospel ends quietly: many believed because of the signs, “But Jesus on his part would not entrust himself to them, because he knew all people… for he himself knew what was in everyone.” He knows what is in us, every hidden motive. That could frighten us, but Hebrews has already given the answer: the One who knows our hearts is the One who writes his law on them and remembers our sins no more. Today the calendar also recalls the inauguration in 1993 of the first Synod of Bishops of our Church, after the Syro-Malabar Church was raised to a Major Archiepiscopal Church. A synod is a ‘walking together’. Let us pray that our bishops, gathered in the Spirit, may keep the house of God a house of prayer, and that the whole Church may walk together under the law of love.

Homily outline

Theme: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.”

The new covenant written by the Spirit on our hearts, the zeal of Jesus for his Father's house, and the Risen Body of Christ, the true Temple in which we are living stones.

1. I will write it on their hearts

  • Tablets of stone at Sinai.
  • The Spirit poured out at Pentecost.
  • A covenant that begins with forgiveness.
“But this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, says the LORD: I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.” Jeremiah 31:33
“I will put my laws in their minds, and write them on their hearts, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.” Hebrews 8:10

Apply: Ask the Holy Spirit each morning this week to write one commandment on your heart as a law of love.

2. Zeal for your Father's house

  • The market in the Temple courts.
  • The whip of cords and the overturned tables.
  • Love that cleanses what it loves.
“It is zeal for your house that has consumed me; the insults of those who insult you have fallen on me.” Psalm 69:9
“He told those who were selling the doves, “Take these things out of here! Stop making my Father’s house a marketplace!”” John 2:16

Apply: Overturn one table this week: a habit of greed, bargaining with God or noise that keeps you from prayer.

3. The temple of his body

  • Destroyed on the Cross, raised on the third day.
  • The disciples remembered and believed.
  • We are God's temple, and the Spirit dwells in us.
“Then he brought me back to the entrance of the temple; there, water was flowing from below the threshold of the temple toward the east (for the temple faced east); and the water was flowing down from below the south end of the threshold of the temple, south of the altar.” Ezekiel 47:1
“Do you not know that you are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit dwells in you?” 1 Corinthians 3:16

Apply: Come early to the Sunday Qurbana and spend five minutes in silence, offering yourself as his temple.

The Spirit writes on our hearts the law that Christ fulfilled, and makes us living stones of the Temple that rose on the third day.

Let us keep the house of God, and our own hearts, as houses of prayer.

Take-home resolution: This week, ask the Spirit to write one commandment on your heart, overturn one table of greed, and pray daily for our Synod of Bishops.

Full homily

Dear friends, four days after Pentecost the readings speak of a promise and a Temple.

Hebrews quotes Jeremiah: God will make a new covenant. “I will put my laws in their minds, and write them on their hearts.” At Sinai the law was written on stone. At Pentecost the Holy Spirit wrote it on human hearts. That is the new covenant: not a law outside us, but love within us.

Hebrews ends the promise with these words: “I will remember their sins no more.” The new covenant begins with mercy. God first gave this promise to Israel; he has not taken back his love for them, and he has welcomed us too.

In the Gospel Jesus finds the Temple full of buying and selling. He drives the merchants out: “Stop making my Father’s house a marketplace!” He does this out of love for his Father's house. Then he says, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” He was speaking of his own body, which rose on the third day.

Now we too are God's temple, and the Spirit lives in us. Let Jesus clean that temple: let him drive out greed, anger and every bargain we try to make with God.

Today our Church also remembers the first gathering of our Synod of Bishops in 1993. Let us pray for our bishops, and let us keep our churches and our hearts as houses of prayer. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus got very upset when people turned God's house into a noisy market. He loved his Father's house!

Did you know that you are God's house too? The Holy Spirit lives in your heart. Keep it clean with kind words, prayers and saying sorry when you do wrong.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, true Temple of God, you were destroyed on the Cross and raised up on the third day. Pour out your Holy Spirit upon us and write your law on our hearts, so that we may know you from the least to the greatest. Cleanse the temple of our lives of every greed and every bargain, and make our parishes houses of prayer. Bless the bishops of our Synod, that they may walk together in your Spirit and lead your people in love. You know what is in each of us: remember our sins no more, and make us your living temple, to the glory of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
