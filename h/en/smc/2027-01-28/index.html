Fourth Thursday of Denha · St Thomas Aquinas

28 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle Galatians 3:15–20 · Jesus is the fulfilment of the promise
  • Gospel John 11:17–27 · I am the resurrection and the life

Word for the day

“Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live, and everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?”” John 11:25–26

Reflection

Paul reasons with the Galatians like a lawyer. A will, once ratified, cannot be changed by a later addition. God made a promise to Abraham four hundred and thirty years before the law of Moses, and the law cannot cancel it. And the promise was made “to your offspring”, singular: “that is, to one person, who is Christ.” The whole history of Israel was moving toward one person. In Denha, the season of manifestation, Christ is revealed as the one in whom every promise of God finds its yes.

In the Gospel Jesus arrives at Bethany four days after Lazarus has died. Martha goes out to meet him with grief and faith mixed together: “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. But even now I know that God will give you whatever you ask of him.” Jesus reveals himself: “I am the resurrection and the life.” And Martha makes one of the great confessions of the Gospel: “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.” Today the Church honours St Thomas Aquinas, whose great mind served this same faith. He taught that reason can prepare the way, but only faith receives the truth that Christ is the Son of God; and he ended his life in adoration of Christ in the Eucharist, the pledge of our resurrection.

  • One offspring.
  • Christ, the fulfilment.

“Now the promises were made to Abraham and to his offspring; it does not say, “And to offsprings,” as of many; but it says, “And to your offspring,” that is, to one person, who is Christ.” Galatians 3:16

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul reminds the Galatians that God's promise to Abraham came long before the law, and the law cannot cancel it. And the promise was made to Abraham's offspring, that is, to one person, who is Christ (Galatians 3:16). The whole story of Israel moves toward Jesus.

In the Gospel Martha meets Jesus four days after Lazarus died: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died (John 11:21). She brings both grief and faith.

Jesus answers: I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live (John 11:25). And Martha confesses: Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God (John 11:27).

St Thomas Aquinas, whom we honour today, gave his great mind to this faith and ended in adoration of Christ in the Eucharist, the pledge of our resurrection. Let us bring our sorrows to Jesus and say with Martha: Yes, Lord, I believe. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told Martha: I am the resurrection and the life! Martha said: Yes, Lord, I believe! Let us say it too.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, offspring of Abraham and fulfilment of every promise, you are the resurrection and the life. Strengthen our faith in sorrow, and through the prayers of St Thomas Aquinas give us minds that seek you and hearts that adore you, until we rise with you. Amen.
