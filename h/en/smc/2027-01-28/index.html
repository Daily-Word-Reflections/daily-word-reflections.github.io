Fourth Thursday of Denha · St Thomas Aquinas

Word for the day

“For the love of Christ urges us on, because we are convinced that one has died for all; therefore all have died.” 2 Corinthians 5:14

Reflection

Paul explains to the Corinthians what drives him. Some boast “in outward appearance and not in the heart”; he has a different motive: “For the love of Christ urges us on, because we are convinced that one has died for all; therefore all have died.” Christ's love is not only something Paul feels for Christ; it is Christ's own love for him, pressing on him like a current, carrying him toward others. In the Gospel that love begins with a glance and a question. John the Baptist sees Jesus walking by and exclaims, “Look, here is the Lamb of God!” Two disciples follow. Jesus turns and asks them, “What are you looking for?” They answer with another question, calling him Rabbi, Teacher: “where are you staying?” He says, “Come and see.” They stay with him that day; John even remembers the hour, about four o'clock in the afternoon. Andrew then finds his brother: “We have found the Messiah”, and Jesus looks at Simon and gives him a new name, Cephas. Denha shows us how Christ is made known: one person tells another, and Christ himself does the rest.

Today the Church honours St Thomas Aquinas, the Dominican friar whose great mind served the faith. He taught that reason can prepare the way, but only faith receives the truth that Christ is the Son of God, and he wrote that grace does not destroy nature but perfects it. His whole work answered Jesus' first question, “What are you looking for?” According to the early accounts of his life, he heard the Lord ask him in prayer what reward he wanted for all he had written, and he answered: nothing but you, Lord. Near the end of his life, after an experience at the Holy Qurbana, he stopped writing and said that all he had written seemed like straw beside what he had seen. Like the first disciples, he came, saw, and remained. The love of Christ urged him on.

Homily outline

Theme: “For the love of Christ urges us on.”

The love that drives the apostle, the first disciples who came and saw, and St Thomas Aquinas.

1. What are you looking for?

  • The Lamb of God.
  • Jesus turns.
  • The first question.
“One thing I asked of the LORD, that will I seek after: to live in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to behold the beauty of the LORD, and to inquire in his temple.” Psalm 27:4
“When Jesus turned and saw them following, he said to them, “What are you looking for?” They said to him, “Rabbi” (which translated means Teacher), “where are you staying?”” John 1:38

Apply: Answer Jesus' question honestly in prayer today.

2. Come and see

  • They remained with him.
  • We have found the Messiah.
  • A new name.
“O taste and see that the LORD is good; happy are those who take refuge in him.” Psalm 34:8
“He said to them, “Come and see.” They came and saw where he was staying, and they remained with him that day. It was about four o'clock in the afternoon.” John 1:39

Apply: Invite one person to come and see Jesus with you.

3. The love of Christ urges us on

  • One died for all.
  • Wisdom asked in prayer.
  • Nothing but you, Lord.
“Therefore I prayed, and understanding was given me; I called on God, and the spirit of wisdom came to me.” Wisdom 7:7
“For the love of Christ urges us on, because we are convinced that one has died for all; therefore all have died.” 2 Corinthians 5:14

Apply: Pray before study or work today for wisdom, as St Thomas did.

Christ asks what we are looking for and invites us to come and see.

Let his love urge us on, until we want nothing but him.

Take-home resolution: Bring one person closer to Christ this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul says: the love of Christ urges us on, because we are convinced that one has died for all (2 Corinthians 5:14). Christ's love is like a current that carries us toward others.

In the Gospel John points to Jesus: look, here is the Lamb of God! Two disciples follow. Jesus turns and asks: what are you looking for? (John 1:38). They ask where he is staying, and he says: come and see. They remain with him that day.

Then Andrew finds his brother Simon and says: we have found the Messiah (John 1:41). He brings him to Jesus, who gives him a new name. This is how Christ is made known: one person tells another.

St Thomas Aquinas, whom we honour today, gave his great mind to Christ. The early accounts tell that when the Lord asked him what reward he wanted, he answered: nothing but you, Lord. May we too seek nothing but Christ, and bring others to him. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus asked two friends: what are you looking for? Then he said: come and see! Who can you invite to come and see Jesus?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Lamb of God, you asked your first disciples what they were looking for and invited them to come and see. Let your love urge us on, make us bring others to you as Andrew brought Simon, and through the prayers of St Thomas Aquinas give us minds that seek you and hearts that want nothing but you. Amen.
