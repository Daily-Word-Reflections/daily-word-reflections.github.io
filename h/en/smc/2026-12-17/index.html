Third Thursday of Annunciation

17 December 2026 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle Galatians 3:26–29 · Those who have put on Christ
  • Gospel Luke 1:67–79 · The Canticle of Zechariah

Word for the day

“By the tender mercy of our God, the dawn from on high will break upon us, to give light to those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace.” Luke 1:78–79

Reflection

Filled with the Holy Spirit, Zechariah sings: “Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has looked favourably on his people and redeemed them.” He sees God's ancient promises, to David, to Abraham, being fulfilled. Then he turns to his child: “And you, child, will be called the prophet of the Most High.” The song ends with an image of dawn: “the dawn from on high will break upon us.”

Paul tells the Galatians: “As many of you as were baptised into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ… you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to the promise.” The promises Zechariah sang about are ours through baptism. The Church sings his Benedictus every morning at dawn.

Homily outline

Theme: “The dawn from on high.”

Zechariah's Benedictus and our baptismal inheritance.

1. He has raised up a mighty saviour

  • God remembers his covenant with David.
  • The promise is fulfilled in Jesus.
“Your house and your kingdom shall be made sure forever before me; your throne shall be established forever.” 2 Samuel 7:16
“Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has looked favorably on his people and redeemed them. He has raised up a mighty savior for us in the house of his servant David.” Luke 1:68–69

Apply: Pray the Benedictus each morning.

2. Prophet of the Most High

  • John prepares the Lord's way.
  • He gives knowledge of salvation by forgiveness.
“See, I am sending my messenger to prepare the way before me.” Malachi 3:1
“And you, child, will be called the prophet of the Most High; for you will go before the Lord to prepare his ways, to give knowledge of salvation to his people by the forgiveness of their sins.” Luke 1:76–77

Apply: Go to confession before Christmas.

3. Light for those in darkness

  • Isaiah saw a great light.
  • Christ is the dawn from on high.
“The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light; those who lived in a land of deep darkness—on them light has shined.” Isaiah 9:2
“By the tender mercy of our God, the dawn from on high will break upon us, to give light to those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace.” Luke 1:78–79

Apply: Make peace with one person before Christmas.

God has visited his people.

Let the dawn of Christ shine on us.

Take-home resolution: Pray the Benedictus at sunrise until Christmas.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul writes: in Christ Jesus you are all children of God through faith; as many of you as were baptised into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ (Galatians 3:26–27). There is no longer Jew or Greek, slave or free, male and female; all are one in Christ.

In the Gospel Zechariah, filled with the Holy Spirit, sings: blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for He has looked favourably on His people and redeemed them (Luke 1:68). His long silence ends in praise.

He sings to his little son: you, child, will be called the prophet of the Most High, for you will go before the Lord to prepare His ways, to give knowledge of salvation by the forgiveness of sins (Luke 1:76–77).

And he ends: by the tender mercy of our God, the dawn from on high will break upon us (Luke 1:78). Christmas is near. Let us pray the Benedictus this week and get ready for the dawn. Amen.

For children

Children, Zechariah sang a beautiful song when John was born. Sing a song of thanks to God today!

Prayer

Blessed are you, Lord God of Israel, for you have visited and redeemed your people. Let the dawn from on high shine on all in darkness and guide our feet into the way of peace. Amen.
