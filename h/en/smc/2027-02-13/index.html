First Saturday of the Great Fast

13 February 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle Romans 3:21–26 · Those who believe are justified
  • Gospel Matthew 6:9–15 · The prayer taught by Christ

Word for the day

“And forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors.” Matthew 6:12

Reflection

On Thursday Paul left us with every mouth silenced: no one is righteous, not even one. Today he opens a door: “But now, apart from law, the righteousness of God has been disclosed, and is attested by the law and the prophets, the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe.” The sentence that follows is one of the most important in the New Testament: “since all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; they are now justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, whom God put forward as a sacrifice of atonement by his blood, effective through faith.” This matters at the end of the first week of the Great Fast. We do not buy God's favour with our fasting. Grace comes first, freely, through the blood of Christ. Our fasting, prayer and almsgiving are our response to that gift, and grace itself makes them fruitful. Whoever forgets this turns the fast into a ledger of achievements; whoever remembers it fasts with gratitude.

The Gospel gives us the prayer that holds the whole Great Fast together: “Pray then in this way: Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come. Your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors. And do not bring us to the time of trial, but rescue us from the evil one.” The Holy Qurbana of our Church opens with this prayer and returns to it before Communion. Each line is a Lenten programme: God's name first, his kingdom before our plans, bread asked for one day at a time by people who are fasting, forgiveness given and received, and rescue in the time of trial, as Jesus himself was tested in the wilderness. Of all the petitions, Jesus comments on only one: “For if you forgive others their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you; but if you do not forgive others, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses.” The week that began with the gift left before the altar ends with the same lesson: the forgiven must forgive.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul says that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; they are now justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus (Romans 3:23–24). Our fasting does not buy God's love. His love comes first.

So we fast with gratitude, not with pride. Our prayer, fasting and almsgiving are our answer to a gift already given, and God's grace makes even that answer possible.

Jesus gives us the prayer of the whole Great Fast: Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name (Matthew 6:9). We pray it at the beginning of the Holy Qurbana and again before Communion. In it we ask for daily bread, for forgiveness, and for rescue in the time of trial.

Jesus explains one line only: if you forgive others, your heavenly Father will also forgive you (Matthew 6:14). As this first week of the fast ends, let us forgive from the heart, so that we can pray this prayer honestly. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus taught us to call God our Father! Tonight, pray the Our Father slowly with your family, and think of someone you need to forgive.

Prayer

Our Father in heaven, all of us have sinned and fallen short of your glory, and you justify us freely by your grace in Christ Jesus. Let us fast with gratitude and not with pride. Hallow your name in us, bring your kingdom, give us today our daily bread, forgive us as we forgive, and rescue us from the evil one. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
