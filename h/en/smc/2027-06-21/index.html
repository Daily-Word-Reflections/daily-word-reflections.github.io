Word for the day

“For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” Matthew 12:34

Reflection

For three chapters of his letter to the Romans Paul has been wrestling with a question that pained him deeply: why had so many of his own people, the Jewish people, not accepted Jesus as the Messiah, while Gentiles were coming to faith in great numbers? He does not answer with contempt. He has just written that his people remain “beloved, for the sake of their ancestors; for the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable” (Romans 11:28–29). The Second Vatican Council quoted these very words in Nostra Aetate when it taught that the Jews remain most dear to God and must never be presented as rejected or accursed. Now Paul sees the whole of history as a single story of mercy: “Just as you were once disobedient to God but have now received mercy because of their disobedience, so they have now been disobedient in order that, by the mercy shown to you, they too may now receive mercy. For God has imprisoned all in disobedience so that he may be merciful to all.” No one is saved by merit, Gentile or Jew; all are saved by mercy. And having reached that point, Paul stops arguing and begins to sing: “O the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways!”

This hymn is a lesson in humility for every believer. Paul, the greatest theologian of the apostolic Church, ends his most difficult argument not with a neat solution but with wonder: “For who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has been his counselor?” “Or who has given a gift to him, to receive a gift in return?” We cannot put God in our debt, and we cannot fit his ways into our small calculations. The next verse, which closes the chapter, gives the reason: “For from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be the glory forever. Amen.” When we do not understand why a prayer seems unanswered, why a child has wandered from the faith, why a good person suffers, we are in Paul's company. The apostle does not pretend to know everything. He knows enough to trust. In the Season of the Apostles we remember that the mission to the nations was not a human plan; it was the unsearchable wisdom of God working through disobedience, persecution and scattering, until the Gospel reached even our own shores.

The Gospel brings us from the depths of God to the depths of the human heart. Some Pharisees had just said that Jesus cast out demons by the power of Beelzebul (Matthew 12:24). Jesus answers with an image every Kerala farmer understands: “Either make the tree good, and its fruit good; or make the tree bad, and its fruit bad; for the tree is known by its fruit.” A jackfruit tree does not produce bitter gourds. What comes out of us shows what is inside us. Then he speaks with the same severity as John the Baptist (Matthew 3:7): “You brood of vipers! How can you speak good things, when you are evil? For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” Our words are fruit; the heart is the root. “The good person brings good things out of a good treasure, and the evil person brings evil things out of an evil treasure.” Then comes a sentence that should make every one of us pause: “I tell you, on the day of judgment you will have to give an account for every careless word you utter; for by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned.” Every careless word: the gossip after the Qurbana in the church courtyard, the cruel joke about a classmate, the forwarded message we did not check, the comment typed in anger at midnight. Words are never only words. They build up or destroy reputations, families and parishes. But the remedy Jesus offers is not only to guard the tongue; it is to change the treasure of the heart. Fill the heart with the word of God, with prayer, with gratitude, and the mouth will speak from that abundance.

Today the First Set calendar lists St Aloysius Gonzaga (1568–1591), whom the Latin Church keeps as an obligatory memorial. Born the eldest son of an Italian marquis, he gave up his inheritance to his brother and entered the Society of Jesus in Rome at seventeen. During an epidemic in 1591 he carried the sick to hospital and nursed them himself; he caught the disease and died on 21 June, at twenty-three. His spiritual director was St Robert Bellarmine. A saying attributed to him sums up his life: what is this in the light of eternity? The Church has made him patron of Christian youth. In June, as schools and colleges reopen across Kerala, his short life is a good fruit from a good tree: a young man whose pure heart overflowed in service of the sick.

Homily outline

Theme: “For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.”

The unsearchable ways of the God who shows mercy to all, the tree known by its fruit, and the account we will give for every word.

1. O the depth

  • Mercy for Gentile and Jew alike.
  • The gifts and calling of God are irrevocable.
  • Trust where we cannot understand.
“For my thoughts are not your thoughts, nor are your ways my ways, says the Lord.” Isaiah 55:8
“O the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways!” Romans 11:33

Apply: When something in your life makes no sense this week, pray Paul's hymn instead of complaining.

2. The tree is known by its fruit

  • A good tree, a good fruit.
  • The heart is the root.
  • The good treasure.
“They are like trees planted by streams of water, which yield their fruit in its season, and their leaves do not wither.” Psalm 1:3
“Either make the tree good, and its fruit good; or make the tree bad, and its fruit bad; for the tree is known by its fruit.” Matthew 12:33

Apply: Plant God's word in your heart: read one verse each morning and repeat it during the day.

3. Every careless word

  • Gossip in the courtyard.
  • Forwarded without checking.
  • Justified or condemned by our words.
“Death and life are in the power of the tongue, and those who love it will eat its fruits.” Proverbs 18:21
“for by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned.” Matthew 12:37

Apply: For one day, say nothing about an absent person that you would not say to their face.

God's ways are beyond us, but his mercy is for all: we trust and give him glory.

The heart is the root and our words are the fruit: fill the heart with God, and the tongue will bless.

Take-home resolution: This week, trust God where you cannot understand, plant one verse of Scripture in your heart each day, and keep one day without a careless word.

Full homily

Dear friends, at the end of a long and difficult argument, Saint Paul stops and sings: “O the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways!” (Romans 11:33).

Paul has been thinking about his own people and about the Gentiles, and he sees that all are saved by the mercy of God. He does not understand everything, but he trusts. When we do not understand what God is doing in our lives, let us pray his words and trust.

In the Gospel Jesus says: “the tree is known by its fruit.” A good tree gives good fruit. Our words are fruit; our heart is the root. “For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks” (Matthew 12:34).

Jesus warns us that we will give an account for every careless word. Think of gossip, cruel jokes, angry messages and rumours forwarded without checking. Words can destroy a person. But the answer is not only to keep quiet; it is to fill our hearts with God's word and love, so that good words overflow.

Today we remember St Aloysius Gonzaga, a young man who gave up riches to serve God and died at twenty-three caring for the sick. His heart was good, and his life bore good fruit. Let us ask him to pray for our young people, and for all of us, that our hearts may be good trees. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says a good tree gives good fruit. Your words are like fruit: if your heart is full of love, kind words will come out. Today, say three kind words to someone in your family, and ask St Aloysius to help you be good at school!

Prayer

O God, how deep are your riches and wisdom and knowledge, how unsearchable your judgments and how inscrutable your ways! You have shown mercy to all, and your gifts and calling are irrevocable. Teach us to trust you where we cannot understand. Lord Jesus, you know the heart from which our words come: make the tree good, so that its fruit may be good; fill the treasure of our hearts with your word, and keep our tongues from careless and wounding speech. Through the prayers of St Aloysius Gonzaga, give our young people pure hearts and generous hands. For from you and through you and to you are all things: to you be glory for ever. Amen.
