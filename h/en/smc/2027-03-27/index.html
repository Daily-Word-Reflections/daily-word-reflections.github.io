Great Saturday

27 March 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading Genesis 22:1–19 · The sacrifice of Abraham
  • Second Reading Jonah 2:1–10 · Jonah in the belly of the fish three days and three nights
  • Epistle Romans 6:3–11 · United with Christ in his death and resurrection
  • Gospel Matthew 28:1–20 · The good news of the Resurrection

Word for the day

“Therefore we have been buried with him by baptism into death, so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.” Romans 6:4

Reflection

Great Saturday is the quietest day of the year. The body of the Lord lies in the new tomb in the garden, the stone is rolled across the door, and the disciples hide behind locked doors. Mary keeps watch in faith. Nothing seems to happen, and yet this is the day on which the Church confesses in the Creed: he descended into hell, among the dead. The First Letter of Peter says that Christ was “put to death in the flesh, but made alive in the spirit, in which also he went and made a proclamation to the spirits in prison” (1 Peter 3:18–19). An ancient homily for this day imagines the Lord going down to find Adam, taking him by the hand and saying: Awake, O sleeper, I did not make you to be held a prisoner in the underworld. The Saviour goes to the very bottom of human loss, so that no one, not even the dead of the ages before him, is beyond the reach of his rescue. The Church reads again the story of Abraham and Isaac, because Isaac was received back from the mountain as if from the dead; the Letter to the Hebrews says Abraham believed that God was able even to raise someone from the dead (Hebrews 11:17–19). The father who walked down Moriah with his living son is a first glimpse of the morning that is coming.

The second reading is the prayer of Jonah from the belly of the fish. Jonah had run from God and been thrown into the sea; now, from the depths, he prays: “I called to the LORD out of my distress, and he answered me; out of the belly of Sheol I cried, and you heard my voice.” He describes himself as already among the dead: “I went down to the land whose bars closed upon me forever; yet you brought up my life from the Pit, O LORD my God.” And then the strange and joyful ending: “Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it spewed Jonah out upon the dry land.” Jesus himself chose this sign: “For just as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the sea monster, so for three days and three nights the Son of Man will be in the heart of the earth” (Matthew 12:40). Jonah went down because of his disobedience; Jesus went down because of ours. Jonah came out to preach to Nineveh; the Risen One comes out to send his apostles to all the nations. The East Syriac tradition loves this sign: the Lord who was swallowed by death was, like Jonah, too much for death to keep.

Paul explains what this means for every baptised Christian. “Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? Therefore we have been buried with him by baptism into death, so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.” Baptism is our own Great Saturday and our own Easter: the old self goes down into the water as into a tomb, and a new person rises. “We know that Christ, being raised from the dead, will never die again; death no longer has dominion over him.” That is why the ancient Church baptised on this night, and why the first week of Easter was the week of the newly baptised. Then the Gospel runs ahead of the clock: “After the sabbath, as the first day of the week was dawning”, the women come to the tomb, the earth shakes, an angel rolls back the stone and sits on it, and says: “Do not be afraid; I know that you are looking for Jesus who was crucified. He is not here; for he has been raised, as he said.” The Risen Lord meets the women on the road, and at the end he sends the eleven from a mountain in Galilee: “Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit… And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” The great silence of Saturday is the silence before the shout.

How a family can keep Great Saturday, step by step (the order and hours differ between dioceses and parishes; follow your own parish). First, the morning: in many parishes the faithful gather for the blessing of new fire and new water. The new fire speaks of Christ the light who cannot be put out; the blessed water speaks of Baptism. The people then renew their baptismal promises: they renounce Satan and all his works, and profess faith in the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. The Holy Qurbana follows in many places, and families carry the blessed water home to bless the house and the sick. Second, the day: the church stays quiet, and the faithful visit the tomb where the Lord's image lies. At home the house is cleaned and the Easter meal is prepared, but without noise: it is still a day of waiting, and many continue the fast until the Easter celebration. Third, the night: late on Saturday night or in the early hours of Sunday, the parish gathers in the dark for the Resurrection service (Uyirppu). The priest proclaims that Christ is risen, the church is filled with light, the bells ring out, the image of the Risen Lord is lifted up and carried in procession with candles, and the Holy Qurbana of the Resurrection is celebrated. Fourth, the return home: in many Kerala homes the fifty days of the Great Fast are broken only after this Qurbana, with appam and stew shared in joy. A good habit for today: let each person in the family find the date of his or her baptism, and thank God for it before going to the church tonight.

Homily outline

Theme: “Therefore we have been buried with him by baptism into death, so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.”

The great silence of the tomb, the sign of Jonah, our burial and rising in Baptism, and the stone rolled away.

1. The great silence

  • The King is asleep.
  • He goes down among the dead.
  • Mary waits in faith.
“Abraham called that place “The LORD will provide”; as it is said to this day, “On the mount of the LORD it shall be provided.”” Genesis 22:14
“For Christ also suffered for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, in order to bring you to God. He was put to death in the flesh, but made alive in the spirit,” 1 Peter 3:18

Apply: Keep one hour of today in silence, waiting with Mary.

2. The sign of Jonah

  • From the belly of Sheol.
  • Three days and three nights.
  • Death cannot keep him.
“I called to the LORD out of my distress, and he answered me; out of the belly of Sheol I cried, and you heard my voice.” Jonah 2:2
“For just as Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the sea monster, so for three days and three nights the Son of Man will be in the heart of the earth.” Matthew 12:40

Apply: Bring to God the place in your life that feels like the bottom of the sea.

3. Buried with him in Baptism

  • Baptised into his death.
  • The old self crucified.
  • Walking in newness of life.
“I will sprinkle clean water upon you, and you shall be clean from all your uncleannesses, and from all your idols I will cleanse you.” Ezekiel 36:25
“Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death?” Romans 6:3

Apply: Renew your baptismal promises today with full attention, and find your baptism date.

4. He is not here

  • The earthquake and the angel.
  • Do not be afraid.
  • Go to all nations.
“Then the LORD spoke to the fish, and it spewed Jonah out upon the dry land.” Jonah 2:10
“He is not here; for he has been raised, as he said. Come, see the place where he lay.” Matthew 28:6

Apply: Bring the whole family to the Resurrection service tonight.

The silence of Saturday is the silence before the shout: the Lord who went down among the dead is about to rise.

Bury the old self with him, renew your promises, and come to the Uyirppu with your lamp lit.

Take-home resolution: Today, keep part of the day in silence, renew your baptismal promises, find your baptism date, and bring your family to the Uyirppu.

Full homily

Dear friends, today is the day of the great silence. The Lord lies in the tomb. The disciples hide in fear. Only Mary waits in faith. And yet the Church says that today something tremendous is happening: Christ goes down among the dead to seek Adam and all his children.

We read again of Abraham and Isaac. Isaac came down the mountain alive, as if received back from the dead. Abraham believed that God could even raise the dead. Today we wait with that same faith.

Jonah prayed from the belly of the fish: “I called to the LORD out of my distress, and he answered me.” Jesus said: as Jonah was three days in the fish, so the Son of Man will be three days in the heart of the earth. Death has swallowed him, but it cannot keep him.

Paul tells us that we are part of this story. When we were baptised, we were buried with Christ, so that we might rise with him and walk in newness of life. Today we renew our baptismal promises. We say no again to Satan and his works, and yes to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

And the Gospel already lets us hear the morning: the earth shakes, the stone is rolled away, and the angel says, “He is not here; for he has been raised, as he said.” The Risen Lord sends us to all nations, and promises: “I am with you always.”

So let us keep this day quietly, prepare our homes and our hearts, and come tonight to the Uyirppu, all of us, even the sleepy children. The bridegroom is coming. Amen.

For children

Children, today Jesus is resting in the tomb. The church is quiet, like a house waiting for someone special to come home.

When you were baptised, you got new life from Jesus. Ask your parents today: on what day was I baptised?

Tonight we will hear the best news ever: Jesus is risen! Get ready to sing Alleluia!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, today you rest in the tomb, and the whole earth keeps silence. You went down among the dead to seek Adam and all his children, and death could not hold you. We were buried with you in Baptism; raise us with you to newness of life. Keep us faithful in the waiting, like Mary your Mother, and let the light of your Resurrection fill our homes and our hearts. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
