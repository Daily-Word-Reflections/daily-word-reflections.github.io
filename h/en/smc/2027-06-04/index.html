Word for the day

“Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.” John 19:34

Reflection

Eight days after the Feast of the Body and Blood of Christ, on the First Friday of June, the Church turns to the source of that gift: the Heart of Jesus. In very many Syro-Malabar homes a picture of the Sacred Heart hangs above the family altar, with a small lamp burning before it, and the family has been consecrated to the Sacred Heart, often on the day the house was blessed. On First Fridays parishes hold adoration, confessions are heard, and many of the faithful come to the Holy Qurbana. June is the month of the Sacred Heart. The devotion is not a sentimental extra. In the Bible the heart is the centre of the person, the place where one thinks, decides, loves and meets God. To speak of the Heart of Jesus is to speak of the whole person of the Word made flesh, who loved the Father and loved us with a human heart. The Church found this devotion in the Gospel itself, in the pierced side of the Crucified; it was deepened by medieval mystics, spread through the revelations given to St Margaret Mary Alacoque at Paray-le-Monial between 1673 and 1675, extended to the whole Church by Pope Pius IX in 1856, explained by Pius XII in the encyclical Haurietis Aquas (1956), and recommended anew by Pope Francis in Dilexit Nos (2024).

The first reading may seem at first to have little to do with the feast. Abram and his nephew Lot have both grown rich, and the land cannot support their flocks; their herders quarrel. Abram is the elder and the head of the clan; by right, the first choice is his. Instead he says: “Let there be no strife between you and me, and between your herders and my herders; for we are kindred. Is not the whole land before you? Separate yourself from me. If you take the left hand, then I will go to the right; or if you take the right hand, then I will go to the left.” Lot looks, and chooses with his eyes: “the plain of the Jordan was well watered everywhere like the garden of the Lord.” The text adds quietly that he moved his tent as far as Sodom, and “the people of Sodom were wicked, great sinners against the Lord.” Abram, who has given up the best land for the sake of peace, is not the loser. God speaks to him: “Raise your eyes now, and look from the place where you are, northward and southward and eastward and westward; for all the land that you see I will give to you and to your offspring forever.” Abram builds an altar at Mamre. Here is a heart that prefers peace to rights, kinship to profit, and God's promise to what the eye can grasp. The Church reads it on this feast because it is a first sketch of the Heart of Christ, who did not hold on to what was his, and who says in the fuller Gospel of the day: “learn from me; for I am gentle and humble in heart” (Matthew 11:29). In Kerala, where families are so often divided by quarrels over land and inheritance, Abram's words to Lot are a word for brothers and sisters today.

The second reading is the song of the suffering Servant, which the Church reads on Good Friday: “He was despised and rejected by others; a man of suffering and acquainted with infirmity… Surely he has borne our infirmities and carried our diseases… But he was wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the punishment that made us whole, and by his bruises we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have all turned to our own way, and the Lord has laid on him the iniquity of us all.” The Heart of Jesus is not a heart that loves from a safe distance. It is a wounded heart, a heart that carries. In the Season of the Apostles we remember how the deacon Philip met an Ethiopian official on the road to Gaza who was reading this very passage, “like a sheep he was led to the slaughter”, and asked of whom the prophet spoke. “Then Philip began to speak, and starting with this scripture, he proclaimed to him the good news about Jesus” (Acts 8:35). The apostles preached the Servant song as the key to the Cross: the One who was pierced bore our sins out of love.

Paul's Epistle opens with Paul's own heart: “Brothers and sisters, my heart’s desire and prayer to God for them is that they may be saved.” He is speaking of his own people, Israel, whom he loves and for whom, in the chapter before, he says he has great sorrow and unceasing anguish. This is not the language of contempt but of love, and Christians must always read it so, honouring the Jewish people to whom belong the covenants and the promises. Then Paul places faith exactly where today's feast places it, in the heart: “The word is near you, on your lips and in your heart (that is, the word of faith that we proclaim); because if you confess with your lips that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. For one believes with the heart and so is justified, and one confesses with the mouth and so is saved.” Devotion to the Sacred Heart is not a feeling to be enjoyed in private. It is faith in the risen Lord believed with the heart and confessed with the lips: at the family prayer, before meals in a restaurant, in the office, in the hospital ward.

The Gospel takes us to Calvary at the moment after death. “When Jesus had received the wine, he said, ‘It is finished.’ Then he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.” Because the next day was the great Sabbath of Passover, the bodies were not to remain on the crosses, and the soldiers came to break the legs of the crucified. “But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs. Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.” The evangelist, who stood there with the Mother of Jesus, solemnly testifies to what he saw, “so that you also may believe.” He sees two Scriptures fulfilled: “None of his bones shall be broken”, as with the Passover lamb (Exodus 12:46), and “They will look on the one whom they have pierced” (Zechariah 12:10). The Fathers saw in the blood and water the sacraments of the Church, Baptism and the Eucharist, and they saw the Church herself born from the side of the new Adam asleep on the Cross, as Eve was formed from the side of the first Adam. And our own father in the faith comes into this mystery. Eight days after Easter the risen Lord said to Thomas, “Reach out your hand and put it in my side. Do not doubt but believe” (John 20:27). The apostle of India, invited to touch the wounded side of Christ, confessed, “My Lord and my God!” (John 20:28). The Mar Thoma Christians received their faith from the one to whom the Lord showed his opened side. Today we look on the One whom we have pierced, not to stay in grief, but to drink from the spring of mercy, and to say with Thomas and with every First Friday pilgrim: Jesus, meek and humble of heart, make our hearts like yours.

Homily outline

Theme: “Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.”

On the Feast of the Sacred Heart and First Friday: Abram's peacemaking heart, the wounded Servant, faith believed in the heart and confessed with the lips, and the fountain opened on Calvary.

1. Let there be no strife

  • Abram yields the first choice.
  • Lot chooses with his eyes.
  • God is not outdone in generosity.
“Then Abram said to Lot, “Let there be no strife between you and me, and between your herders and my herders; for we are kindred.” Genesis 13:8
“Let each of you look not to your own interests, but to the interests of others.” Philippians 2:4

Apply: Take one step toward peace in a family quarrel, even at a cost to yourself.

2. Wounded for our transgressions

  • Despised and rejected.
  • He bore our infirmities.
  • Philip preaches the Servant as Jesus.
“But he was wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the punishment that made us whole, and by his bruises we are healed.” Isaiah 53:5
“But God proves his love for us in that while we still were sinners Christ died for us.” Romans 5:8

Apply: Bring one hidden wound to the Lord in confession this First Friday.

3. One believes with the heart

  • Paul's heart for his people.
  • The word is near you.
  • Believe in the heart, confess with the lips.
“A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you; and I will remove from your body the heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh.” Ezekiel 36:26
“For one believes with the heart and so is justified, and one confesses with the mouth and so is saved.” Romans 10:10

Apply: Make the sign of the cross and pray before every meal this week, also in public.

4. Blood and water

  • None of his bones broken.
  • The door of life opened.
  • Thomas invited to the side.
“On that day a fountain shall be opened for the house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, to cleanse them from sin and impurity.” Zechariah 13:1
“Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.” John 19:34

Apply: Renew the consecration of your family to the Sacred Heart this evening.

The Heart of Jesus is gentle and humble, wounded for us, believed and confessed, and opened as the fountain of the sacraments.

Like Thomas, invited to that wounded side, let us say, My Lord and my God.

Take-home resolution: This First Friday, go to confession and Holy Qurbana, renew the family consecration, and make peace where there is strife.

Full homily

Dear friends, on this First Friday the Church celebrates the Feast of the Sacred Heart of Jesus. In many of our homes the picture of the Sacred Heart hangs above the family altar, with a lamp before it. In the Bible the heart is the centre of the person. The Heart of Jesus means the whole love of Jesus for the Father and for us.

In the first reading Abram and Lot quarrel over land. Abram has the right to choose first, but he says: let there be no strife between us; we are kindred; you choose. Lot takes the best-looking land and goes toward Sodom. Abram gives up for peace, and God gives him the whole land. Here is a heart like the Heart of Jesus, who says: learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart (Matthew 11:29).

Isaiah shows us the suffering Servant: he was wounded for our transgressions; by his bruises we are healed (Isaiah 53:5). The Heart of Jesus is a wounded heart. He does not love us from far away. He carries our sins and sorrows.

Saint Paul says: if you confess with your lips that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved (Romans 10:9). Devotion to the Sacred Heart is faith with the heart and courage with the lips. Let us not be ashamed to show that we belong to Jesus.

In the Gospel a soldier pierces the side of Jesus, and blood and water come out (John 19:34). The Fathers saw in them Baptism and the Eucharist, and the Church born from the side of Christ. Eight days after Easter Jesus said to our father Thomas: put your hand in my side. And Thomas said: My Lord and my God!

Today let us look on the One whom we have pierced. Let us go to confession, receive him in the Qurbana, and renew the consecration of our families. And let us pray: Jesus, meek and humble of heart, make our hearts like yours. Amen.

For children

Children, when Jesus died, a soldier opened his side with a spear, and blood and water came out. It shows how much Jesus loves us: he gave us everything!

Look at the picture of the Sacred Heart at home. Say to Jesus: “Jesus, I love you. Make my heart gentle and kind like yours.”

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, gentle and humble of heart, your side was opened by the spear, and from your Heart flowed blood and water, the fountain of our salvation. Like Abram, teach us to choose peace over our rights; like the Servant, you bore our infirmities: heal us by your wounds. Give us hearts that believe and lips that confess that you are Lord. As you showed your side to Mar Thoma, show us your mercy, that we too may say, My Lord and my God. Make our families your dwelling, bless our priests, and make our hearts like your Heart. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory and honour for ever. Amen.
