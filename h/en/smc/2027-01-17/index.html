Word for the day

“even to your old age I am he, even when you turn gray I will carry you. I have made, and I will bear; I will carry and will save.” Isaiah 46:4

Reflection

This Sunday asks a simple question: who carries whom? Moses, worn out by a complaining people, cries to God: “I am not able to carry all this people alone, for they are too heavy for me.” He even asks God whether he must “carry them in your bosom, as a nurse carries a sucking child”. God does not scold him. He takes some of the spirit that is on Moses and gives it to seventy elders, so that “they shall bear the burden of the people along with you so that you will not bear it all by yourself.”

Isaiah answers the question with a picture from Babylon. On feast days the statues of the gods were carried through the streets; now, says the prophet, “Bel bows down, Nebo stoops”, loaded on weary animals, idols that must be carried and cannot save. The God of Israel is the opposite: his people have been “borne by me from your birth, carried from the womb”, and he promises: “even when you turn gray I will carry you. I have made, and I will bear; I will carry and will save.” So he can call the whole world: “Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth!”

In the Gospel John the Baptist sees Jesus coming and cries: “Here is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” The Lamb lifts and carries away the weight that no Moses could carry: the sin of the world. John testifies that the Spirit came down and remained on him, and that “this is the Son of God.” Hebrews adds the goal of the journey: “a sabbath rest still remains for the people of God”, and the door is open “today”. Tomorrow we begin the Three Days' Fast. We fast not to carry ourselves by our own strength, but to hand our burden to the Lamb who carries it, and to enter his rest.

Homily outline

Theme: “I will carry and will save.”

Who carries whom? Moses cannot, idols cannot; God carries, and the Lamb takes away the sin of the world.

1. Too heavy for me

  • Moses' honest prayer.
  • The Spirit shared with the elders.
  • Burdens carried together.
“I am not able to carry all this people alone, for they are too heavy for me.” Numbers 11:14
“Bear one another’s burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ.” Galatians 6:2

Apply: Carry part of someone's burden this week.

2. I will carry and will save

  • Bel and Nebo on weary animals.
  • Idols must be carried.
  • God carries from the womb to gray hair.
“even to your old age I am he, even when you turn gray I will carry you. I have made, and I will bear; I will carry and will save.” Isaiah 46:4
“Come to me, all you that are weary and are carrying heavy burdens, and I will give you rest.” Matthew 11:28

Apply: Name one idol you carry and put it down during the fast.

3. The Lamb who takes away

  • John points to Jesus.
  • The sin of the world.
  • The Spirit remains on him.
“He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he did not open his mouth; like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent, so he did not open his mouth.” Isaiah 53:7
“Here is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” John 1:29

Apply: Make a good confession during the Three Days' Fast.

4. Enter his rest today

  • The desert generation.
  • Today, not tomorrow.
  • A sabbath rest remains.
“O that today you would listen to his voice! Do not harden your hearts, as at Meribah, as on the day at Massah in the wilderness,” Psalm 95:7–8
“So then, a sabbath rest still remains for the people of God; for those who enter God’s rest also cease from their labors as God did from his.” Hebrews 4:9–10

Apply: Keep Sunday as a day of rest with God and family.

Idols are carried; the true God carries us.

The Lamb takes away our sin and leads us into his rest.

Take-home resolution: Keep the Three Days' Fast faithfully this week, handing your burdens to the Lamb of God.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today's readings ask: who carries whom? Moses is exhausted. The people complain about food, and he cries to God: I am not able to carry all this people alone, for they are too heavy for me (Numbers 11:14). Many parents, priests and caregivers know that prayer.

God does not scold Moses. He takes some of the spirit that is on him and puts it on seventy elders, so that they shall bear the burden of the people along with you (Numbers 11:17). God's answer to our exhaustion is his Spirit, and brothers and sisters to share the load.

Isaiah gives a strong picture. In Babylon the statues of Bel and Nebo were carried through the streets on festival days. The prophet sees them loaded on weary animals, bowing and stooping: gods that have to be carried and cannot save. Then God speaks to his people: I have made, and I will bear; I will carry and will save (Isaiah 46:4). Idols are carried. The true God carries.

In the Gospel John the Baptist sees Jesus and cries: here is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29). The heaviest burden of all, the sin of the world, no Moses could carry. The Lamb lifts it and carries it away, to the cross.

Hebrews tells us where the journey ends: a sabbath rest still remains for the people of God (Hebrews 4:9). And it warns: today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts. Tomorrow we begin the Three Days' Fast. Our ancestors kept it with great devotion; many still go to Kuravilangad and other shrines.

Let us keep it in the spirit of today's readings. Put down the idols we carry. Share someone else's burden. Give our sins to the Lamb who takes them away. And let him carry us, as he promised, even when we turn gray. Amen.

For children

Children, John the Baptist pointed to Jesus and said: here is the Lamb of God! Jesus takes away our sins.

When you are tired or sad, remember: God says, I will carry you. Tomorrow the Three Days' Fast begins. What small thing can you give up for Jesus?

Prayer

Lord our God, you carried your people from the womb and promised to carry them to old age. When our burdens are too heavy for us, give us your Spirit and companions to share them. Take away our sins through your Lamb, and as we begin the fast of the Ninevites, let us not harden our hearts today, but enter the rest you have prepared. Glory to you, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
