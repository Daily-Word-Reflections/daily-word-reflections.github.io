Word for the day

“do not fear, for I am with you, do not be afraid, for I am your God; I will strengthen you, I will help you, I will uphold you with my victorious right hand.” Isaiah 41:10

Reflection

The theme of this Sunday is election: God chooses. Moses, old and about to die, calls Joshua before all Israel: “Be strong and bold… it is the LORD who goes before you. He will be with you; he will not fail you or forsake you.” Through Isaiah God speaks to his discouraged people in exile: “You are my servant, I have chosen you and not cast you off; do not fear, for I am with you.” To be chosen by God is not a privilege to boast about; it is a mission, and God's presence is its strength.

In the Gospel crowds come from everywhere, and then Jesus goes up the mountain and “called to him those whom he wanted”. He appoints the Twelve. Paul tells the Philippians what being chosen did to him: everything he once counted as gain, his birth, his zeal, his law-keeping, he now regards as loss “because of the surpassing value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord.” He has not arrived: “forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal for the prize of the heavenly call of God in Christ Jesus.”

Tomorrow we begin the Three Days' Fast, the Rogation of the Ninevites, much loved in our Church. It is a time to answer God's choice with conversion, prayer and fasting. The chosen are called not to sit back, but to press on.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today's readings are about God's choice. Moses, near death, calls Joshua before all Israel: be strong and bold… it is the LORD who goes before you; he will not fail you or forsake you (Deuteronomy 31:7–8). Joshua's name means the LORD saves, the same name as Jesus.

Through Isaiah God speaks to his discouraged people in exile: you are my servant, I have chosen you and not cast you off; do not fear, for I am with you (Isaiah 41:9–10). Many of us feel we are not good enough. God does not choose us because we are perfect, but because he loves us and has a mission for us.

In the Gospel Jesus goes up the mountain and calls to him those whom he wanted. He appoints the Twelve to be with him and to be sent out (Mark 3:13–14). Fishermen, a tax collector, a zealot: ordinary people, chosen.

Paul tells us what being chosen did to him. Everything he once boasted of, his birth, his zeal, his religious achievements, he now counts as loss because of the surpassing value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord (Philippians 3:8). And he has not stopped: forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal (Philippians 3:13–14).

Tomorrow we begin the Three Days' Fast, the fast of the Ninevites. Our ancestors kept it with great devotion; many go on pilgrimage to Kuravilangad and other churches. It is our answer to God's choice: conversion, prayer and fasting.

So let us not sit back. Let us count some things as loss for Christ during these three days, and press on toward the prize of God's call. Do not fear: he goes before you. Amen.

For children

Children, God told Joshua: be strong and brave, I am with you! God says the same to you.

Tomorrow the Three Days' Fast begins. What small thing can you give up for Jesus?

Prayer

Lord our God, you chose Joshua, Israel and the Twelve, and you have called us in your Son. Make us strong and bold, free from fear, ready to count all things loss for the surpassing value of knowing Christ. As we begin the fast of the Ninevites, turn our hearts to you, and let us press on toward the prize of your heavenly call. Glory to you, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
