Word for the day

“Fear seized all of them; and they glorified God, saying, “A great prophet has risen among us!” and “God has looked favorably on his people!”” Luke 7:16

Reflection

The day after Denha the Church goes on contemplating what God has revealed. St Paul speaks of a wisdom that is “not a wisdom of this age or of the rulers of this age”, but “God's wisdom, secret and hidden, which God decreed before the ages for our glory.” The powerful did not grasp it; “if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.” Now it has been opened to us. “What no eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor the human heart conceived, what God has prepared for those who love him”, God has revealed “through the Spirit; for the Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God.”

In the Gospel this hidden wisdom shows its face. At the gate of Nain Jesus meets a funeral: a widow is burying “his mother's only son”. No one asks him for anything. Luke says simply that the Lord saw her and “had compassion for her”. He tells her not to weep, touches the bier and commands: “Young man, I say to you, rise!” Then “Jesus gave him to his mother”, the very gesture of Elijah at Zarephath. The crowd cries out: “A great prophet has risen among us!” and “God has looked favorably on his people!” In the season of Denha this is what manifestation means: the wisdom of God revealed as a compassion that stops at our funerals and gives life.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the day after Denha St Paul speaks of a wisdom that is not of this age. God's wisdom, secret and hidden, was decreed before the ages for our glory (1 Corinthians 2:7). The rulers of this age did not understand it; if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.

This wisdom cannot be reached by cleverness. What no eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor the human heart conceived, God has prepared for those who love him, and he has revealed it to us through the Spirit (1 Corinthians 2:9–10).

In the Gospel we see that wisdom with a human face. At the gate of Nain a widow follows the body of her only son. Nobody asks Jesus for help. He sees her, has compassion for her, and says: do not weep. He touches the bier and says: young man, I say to you, rise! And he gives him back to his mother (Luke 7:13–15).

The people cry out: a great prophet has risen among us; God has looked favourably on his people (Luke 7:16). This is the manifestation of Denha: God's wisdom is a compassion that stops at our funerals and gives life. Let us ask for it, and let us stop for those who weep. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus saw a mother crying because her son had died. He felt sorry for her and brought her son back to life! Jesus cares when we are sad. Let us care for sad people too.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, wisdom of God made visible, you stopped at the gate of Nain and gave a widow back her son. Open my eyes by your Spirit to what God has prepared for those who love him, and give me your compassion for those who weep. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

The day after Denha, the manifestation continues. Paul speaks of a wisdom hidden from the rulers of the age and revealed by the Spirit. Luke shows Jesus stopping a funeral at Nain and giving a dead son back to his mother. What God had hidden now shows itself as compassion.

1 Corinthians 2:6–10 · God’s wisdom, secret and hidden

Setting.

Paul has told the Corinthians that he came preaching only “Jesus Christ, and him crucified” (2:2). Yet he insists that this is real wisdom. It is not the kind that the world’s powerful people recognise, for it is the wisdom of the cross, planned by God before the ages.

Structure.

  • v. 6: a wisdom for the mature, not of this age.
  • v. 7: God’s hidden wisdom, decreed before the ages.
  • v. 8: the rulers did not understand.
  • vv. 9–10: what no eye has seen is revealed through the Spirit.

Key words.

  • sophia, wisdom: In Corinth a word of high prestige. Paul keeps the word and changes its content: the wisdom of God is the crucified Christ (1:24).
  • mystērion, mystery: Translated here as “secret”. It does not mean a puzzle, but God’s plan, once hidden and now revealed.
  • archontes tou aiōnos toutou, rulers of this age: The human rulers responsible for the crucifixion, and perhaps, as some think, the unseen powers behind them. Paul’s stress is on their failure to understand.

Verse by verse.

“None of the rulers of this age understood this; for if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.” (2:8)

“Lord of glory”, the highest title, is joined to the most shameful death. The Catechism uses this verse to teach that we must not place the blame for the Cross on the Jewish people; all sinners were its authors (CCC 597–598).

“What no eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor the human heart conceived, what God has prepared for those who love him” (2:9)

Paul’s source is uncertain. The words seem to echo Isaiah 64:4 and 65:17. They are often used about heaven, as the Catechism uses them (CCC 1027), but Paul applies them first to what the Spirit is revealing now.

“these things God has revealed to us through the Spirit; for the Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God.” (2:10)

The Spirit who came down at the Jordan is the one who opens this to us.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that sinners were the authors of Christ’s Passion, and it quotes the Roman Catechism on those who keep crucifying the Lord by their sins (CCC 598). It describes the blessedness of heaven with the words of v. 9 (CCC 1027). So what the rulers could not see is offered to all “who love him”.

Luke 7:11–17 · Young man, I say to you, rise

Setting.

This story is found only in Luke. Nain is a village in Galilee, on the slope of the hill of Moreh, not far from Shunem, where Elisha raised a woman’s son (2 Kings 4:8–37). Luke has just told of the centurion’s servant (7:1–10), and next come John’s disciples asking, “Are you the one who is to come?” Jesus answers, “the dead are raised” (7:20–22).

A widow who lost her only son lost her protection and her livelihood as well as her child. The crowd of mourners and the crowd of disciples meet at the town gate.

Structure.

  • vv. 11–12: two crowds meet at the gate.
  • v. 13: he sees her, has compassion, and speaks.
  • vv. 14–15: he touches the bier, gives the command, and returns the son.
  • vv. 16–17: fear, praise, and the report that spreads.

Key words.

  • ho kyrios, the Lord: Here Luke, as narrator, first calls Jesus “the Lord”. He uses the title at the very moment of compassion.
  • esplanchnisthē, he had compassion: A verb from the word for the inward parts: to be moved in one’s depths. Luke also uses it for the good Samaritan and for the father of the prodigal son (10:33; 15:20).
  • epeskepsato, looked favorably: Literally “visited”, as God visits to save. Zechariah sang the same word (1:68). The Malayalam POC keeps the sense of visiting.

Verse by verse.

“When the Lord saw her, he had compassion for her and said to her, “Do not weep.”” (7:13)

No one asks him to act. She does not ask, and there is no statement of faith. The whole initiative is his.

“Then he came forward and touched the bier, and the bearers stood still.” (7:14)

The Law treated contact with the dead as a cause of ritual impurity (Numbers 19:11–16). Jesus does not hesitate, and his touch carries life.

“The dead man sat up and began to speak, and Jesus gave him to his mother.” (7:15)

These are almost the same words as in the Greek of 1 Kings 17:23, where Elijah gives a widow’s son back to her. So the crowd says “A great prophet”, and the reader knows that he is more than that.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism lists the son of the widow of Nain among those raised before Easter, and it makes a distinction: they returned to ordinary life and would die again, whereas Christ’s Resurrection is of another order (CCC 646). St Augustine, in a sermon, sees three kinds of sinners in the three dead whom Jesus raised: the girl in the house, the young man carried out through the gate, and Lazarus in the tomb (in substance). St Ambrose sees in the weeping widow an image of the Church, a mother who prays for her children (in substance).

For study and teaching

  • Where has the wisdom of the cross turned my ideas about success upside down?
  • Jesus saw the widow before she said anything. Who around me is grieving without asking for anything? What would it mean to stop for them?
