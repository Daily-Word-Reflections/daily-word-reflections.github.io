Second Sunday of the Great Fast

14 February 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading Genesis 5:19–31 · Those who walked with God
  • Second Reading Joshua 4:15–24 · Through the Jordan into the land of salvation
  • Epistle Romans 6:15–23 · Not under law but under grace
  • Gospel Matthew 7:21–27 · The true disciple

Word for the day

““Everyone then who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on rock.” Matthew 7:24

Reflection

The first reading sounds at first like a list of names and numbers, with one refrain tolling like a bell: “Thus all the days of Jared were nine hundred sixty-two years; and he died.” Methuselah lives longest of all, nine hundred sixty-nine years, “and he died.” One name breaks the pattern. “Enoch walked with God; then he was no more, because God took him.” He did not walk with God in a desert or a monastery: he “walked with God after the birth of Methuselah three hundred years, and had other sons and daughters.” His holiness was lived in an ordinary household. Sirach calls him “an example of repentance to all generations”, and Hebrews says that “he had pleased God.” At the end of the passage Lamech names his son Noah, saying, “Out of the ground that the LORD has cursed this one shall bring us relief from our work and from the toil of our hands.” Even under the long shadow of death God keeps hope alive. In this second week the Great Fast shows its real goal: not a list of things given up, but a life that walks with God.

In the second reading the priests carrying the ark come up out of the Jordan, and as soon as their feet touch dry ground the river returns and overflows its banks as before. Israel camps at Gilgal, and Joshua sets up the twelve stones taken from the riverbed. He gives the reason: “When your children ask their parents in time to come, ‘What do these stones mean?’ then you shall let your children know, ‘Israel crossed over the Jordan here on dry ground.’” The stones exist so that the story will be told, “so that all the peoples of the earth may know that the hand of the LORD is mighty.” Our Lenten customs are stones of this kind: the ash of the Vibhoodi, the abstinence kept by the whole house, the Way of the Cross on Fridays, the Puthen Pana sung in the evenings. When a child asks why we do not eat fish now, the answer is the story of Christ. Paul tells the baptised that they too have crossed a river. Everyone serves someone: “you are slaves of the one whom you obey, either of sin, which leads to death, or of obedience, which leads to righteousness.” “But now that you have been freed from sin and enslaved to God, the advantage you get is sanctification. The end is eternal life.” Then comes the great contrast between what we earn and what we are given: “For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

The Gospel is the ending of the Sermon on the Mount, and it is sobering: “Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father in heaven.” Some will claim to have prophesied, cast out demons and done mighty works in his name, and he will answer, “I never knew you.” Then Jesus tells of two builders. The same rain falls on both houses, the same floods come, the same winds beat against them. The only difference is the foundation. The house of the one who hears and acts “did not fall, because it had been founded on rock”; the house of the one who hears and does not act falls, “and great was its fall!” Kerala knows what floods do to houses. The storms of illness, loss and temptation come to every home; what we build on is decided before they arrive. The Great Fast is digging time: hearing the word and doing it, until our life rests on Christ the rock. Today the Latin Church also honours Sts Cyril and Methodius, the brothers who gave the Slav peoples the Scriptures and the liturgy in their own language, so that the word might be both heard and lived.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, the first reading is a long list of names, and after almost every name the same words: and he died. But one man is different: Enoch walked with God; then he was no more, because God took him (Genesis 5:24). He walked with God while raising sons and daughters, in an ordinary home.

That is the goal of the Great Fast. Not simply to give up fish and meat, but to walk with God, day after day, in our homes and at our work.

Israel crossed the Jordan and set up twelve stones, so that when the children asked, what do these stones mean?, the parents would tell them the story of God's mighty hand (Joshua 4:21–22). Our Lenten customs are such stones. When our children ask why we fast, let us tell them about Jesus.

Paul reminds us that everyone serves someone. For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord (Romans 6:23). Sin pays wages; God gives gifts.

Jesus warns us: not everyone who says to me, Lord, Lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father (Matthew 7:21). The two houses face the same storm. The difference is the foundation: the one who hears and acts has built on rock.

This week, let us choose one word of Jesus and do it. Let us dig down to the rock while the sky is clear. Then, when the storms come, our house will stand. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told a story about a house on the rock and a house on the sand. When the rain came, only the house on the rock stood strong!

When we listen to Jesus and do what he says, we are building on the rock. What will you do this week?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, rock of our salvation, you brought us through the waters of baptism into the land of your grace. Teach us to walk with God like Enoch, to tell our children what our Lenten stones mean, and to choose the free gift of eternal life rather than the wages of sin. Let us not only call you Lord, but do the will of your Father, so that our house may stand when the storms come. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
