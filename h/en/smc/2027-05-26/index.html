Word for the day

“Everything that the Father gives me will come to me, and anyone who comes to me I will never drive away;” John 6:37

Reflection

This week the Gospel takes us back to the synagogue at Capernaum and to the long discourse on the bread of life in the sixth chapter of John. Over the coming days we will hear it piece by piece, and on Thursday, the Feast of the Body and Blood of Christ, we will hear its heart. Today Jesus speaks of those who come to him: “Everything that the Father gives me will come to me, and anyone who comes to me I will never drive away.” The promise is unconditional. There is no person so wounded, so sinful or so unimportant that Jesus would push them back from his door. He explains why: “for I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me. And this is the will of him who sent me, that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but raise it up on the last day.” The Father's will is not a cold law; it is the will of a Father who does not want to lose a single child. Then come the murmurs: “Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? How can he now say, ‘I have come down from heaven’?” The difficulty is familiarity. They know the carpenter's family, and so they cannot see the One who came from the Father. The evangelist uses the same word that the Bible uses for Israel's grumbling in the wilderness over the manna; these listeners, like the disciples a little later in the same chapter (John 6:60), stand where every believer sometimes stands, finding the words of Jesus hard. The danger is the same for us. We have known Jesus since our baptism, since the catechism class and the Sunday Qurbana of our childhood, and familiarity can hide him.

Jesus answers the grumbling with a word about grace: “No one can come to me unless drawn by the Father who sent me; and I will raise that person up on the last day.” Faith begins not with our cleverness but with the Father's drawing. That is good news for parents who pray for children who have drifted away, and for anyone who feels that faith is beyond them. The Father is already drawing; our part is not to resist. Four times in this chapter Jesus repeats the promise, “I will raise them up on the last day” (John 6:39, 40, 44, 54). The bread of life is not only for this life. It is a pledge of the resurrection of the body.

Paul's letter speaks of that same body. In Corinth some believers had a slogan: “All things are lawful for me.” Paul quotes it and corrects it twice: “but not all things are beneficial” and “but I will not be dominated by anything.” Christian liberty is not the right to be enslaved by appetite. The Corinthians thought that what one did with the body did not matter, since the body would perish anyway. Paul answers: “The body is meant not for fornication but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body. And God raised the Lord and will also raise us by his power.” Because the body will rise, the body matters now. Then comes one of the great sentences of the New Testament: “Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, which you have from God, and that you are not your own? For you were bought with a price; therefore glorify God in your body.” Ten days ago, at Pentecost, we celebrated the coming of the Spirit on the apostles. In this Season of the Apostles Paul reminds us where the Spirit lives: in our bodies. That changes how we eat and drink, how we use our eyes on a screen at midnight, how we treat our own bodies and the bodies of others, how we care for the sick and the old, and how we lay our dead to rest. A temple is not something one abuses, rents out or neglects. It is something one keeps clean and fills with praise.

Theme: “Everything that the Father gives me will come to me, and anyone who comes to me I will never drive away.”

The open door of Jesus, the Father who draws us, and the body as a temple of the Holy Spirit destined to rise.

  • Murmurs of familiarity.
  • The door that is never shut.

Apply: Go to confession this week if you have stayed away, trusting the open door.

  • Faith begins with grace.
  • The Father draws those we pray for.
  • The bread of life, pledge of the resurrection.

  • Lawful, but not beneficial.
  • The body is for the Lord.

Jesus never drives away anyone who comes; the Father is drawing us and those we love.

Take-home resolution: This week, come back to the Lord in confession if needed, pray daily for one person by name, and keep one discipline for the sake of the temple.

Full homily

Dear friends, today Jesus makes a promise that we can hold on to all our lives: “anyone who comes to me I will never drive away” (John 6:37). No sin is too big and no person is too small. The door of Jesus is never shut to one who comes.

Why? Because Jesus came down from heaven to do the will of the Father, and the Father's will is that not one of those given to him should be lost. Jesus says it again and again: I will raise them up on the last day.

But some who heard him grumbled: is not this the son of Joseph? We know his family. They knew him too well to listen to him. We too have known Jesus since childhood. Let us ask for new ears, so that familiarity does not hide him from us.

Saint Paul speaks to Christians who thought the body did not matter. He says: your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit; you are not your own; you were bought with a price; therefore glorify God in your body (1 Corinthians 6:19–20). The Spirit who came at Pentecost lives in us.

So let us take care of this temple. Let us keep it clean from what enslaves, use it to serve, care for the bodies of the sick and the old with reverence, and receive into it the Body of Christ, who will raise it up on the last day. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says he will never send away anyone who comes to him. You can always run to Jesus, even when you have done something wrong. And your body is like a little church where the Holy Spirit lives. So take care of it, and never hurt anyone else's body!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you came down from heaven to do the will of the Father, who wills that none of those he gives you should be lost. You never drive away anyone who comes to you. Draw us, and those we love who have wandered, into your company. Holy Spirit, you dwell in our bodies as in a temple: keep them pure and strong, make them instruments of praise and service, and raise them up with Christ on the last day. To the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit be glory for ever. Amen.
