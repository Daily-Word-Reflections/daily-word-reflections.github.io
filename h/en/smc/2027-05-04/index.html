Readings

  • Epistle 2 Peter 3:10–13 · New heavens and a new earth
  • Gospel Mark 13:32–37 · Keep awake

Word for the day

“Beware, keep alert; for you do not know when the time will come.” Mark 13:33

Reflection

Two days before the Ascension the Church gives us a Gospel about the Lord's return. Jesus is speaking on the Mount of Olives, the very hill from which Luke says he will be taken up (Acts 1:12). “But about that day or hour no one knows, neither the angels in heaven, nor the Son, but only the Father.” The saying has puzzled readers from the beginning: how can the Son not know? The Church's answer is careful. Jesus is truly God and truly man; in his human knowledge he knew all that he came to reveal, and he says plainly that the day of judgment is not among the things he was sent to make known (Catechism 474). The point of the saying is practical, not speculative: “Beware, keep alert; for you do not know when the time will come.” Then a little parable. A man goes on a journey, “puts his slaves in charge, each with his work, and commands the doorkeeper to be on the watch.” Each has his work; none is idle; one stands at the door. That is the Church after the Ascension. The Master has gone; the house is in our hands; every member has a task, and all must watch.

Jesus names the four watches of the night: “for you do not know when the master of the house will come, in the evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or at dawn, or else he may find you asleep when he comes suddenly.” Mark's first readers would have noticed something. Only a few days later, on the night of the Passion, those same hours come one after another: in the evening the Supper; at midnight the agony in Gethsemane, where the disciples fall asleep three times; at cockcrow Peter's denial; at dawn Jesus handed over to Pilate. The disciples failed to keep awake on that night. After the Resurrection and the gift of the Spirit they learned to watch, and the Church has kept the night watches ever since in her prayer. In the Syro-Malabar tradition the Liturgy of the Hours, the Yamangal, keeps the rhythm of evening, night and morning prayer; the Night Prayer (Lelya) has a vigil character, and in many families the evening prayer before the crucifix still keeps the household awake to the Lord. The last word is for everyone: “And what I say to you I say to all: Keep awake.”

The Second Letter of Peter tells us what we are waiting for. “But the day of the Lord will come like a thief, and then the heavens will pass away with a loud noise, and the elements will be dissolved with fire, and the earth and everything that is done on it will be disclosed.” The language is the vivid language of the prophets, and its point is moral: “Since all these things are to be dissolved in this way, what sort of persons ought you to be in leading lives of holiness and godliness, waiting for and hastening the coming of the day of God”. We can hasten it, Peter says, by holy lives, by prayer, by spreading the Gospel. And then the promise: “But, in accordance with his promise, we wait for new heavens and a new earth, where righteousness is at home.” The Church teaches that the universe itself will be renewed and transformed, not thrown away (Catechism 1042–1047). Kerala has seen in recent years how fragile our hills and rivers are, in floods and landslides that took homes and lives. The Christian who waits for a new earth does not despise this one; he cares for it as a steward who will give an account, and longs for the day when righteousness will be at home everywhere. Every Holy Qurbana is celebrated in this waiting: we proclaim the Lord's death “until he comes” (1 Corinthians 11:26), and the early Church's prayer, Maranatha, Our Lord, come, is still on our lips.

Homily outline

Theme: “Beware, keep alert; for you do not know when the time will come.”

Two days before the Ascension: the hidden day, the servants left in charge, the night watches, and the new heavens and new earth.

1. Keep alert

  • No one knows the day or hour.
  • The point is not curiosity but readiness.
  • Living each day as the Lord's.
“For there is still a vision for the appointed time; it speaks of the end, and does not lie. If it seems to tarry, wait for it; it will surely come, it will not delay.” Habakkuk 2:3
“Beware, keep alert; for you do not know when the time will come.” Mark 13:33

Apply: Each night this week, examine your day briefly before sleeping.

2. Each with his work

  • The Master gone on a journey.
  • The house left in our hands.
  • The doorkeeper on the watch.
“So you, mortal, I have made a sentinel for the house of Israel” Ezekiel 33:7
“Blessed is that slave whom his master will find at work when he arrives.” Luke 12:43

Apply: Do your daily duty this week as a service to the Lord who will return.

3. New heavens and a new earth

  • The day of the Lord like a thief.
  • What sort of persons ought we to be?
  • Where righteousness is at home.
“For I am about to create new heavens and a new earth; the former things shall not be remembered or come to mind.” Isaiah 65:17
“But, in accordance with his promise, we wait for new heavens and a new earth, where righteousness is at home.” 2 Peter 3:13

Apply: Do one concrete thing this week to care for God's creation.

The Lord who ascends will return; until then he has left us his house and our work.

Let us keep awake in prayer and holiness, waiting for the new creation.

Take-home resolution: This week, examine your day each night, do your work as for the Lord, and care for creation in one practical way.

Full homily

Dear friends, in two days we celebrate the Ascension. Today Jesus speaks about his return: “about that day or hour no one knows... Beware, keep alert” (Mark 13:32–33).

He tells of a master who goes on a journey and leaves each servant his work, and tells the doorkeeper to watch. That is the Church after the Ascension. The Lord has gone to the Father; the house is in our care. Each of us has a task.

He may come “in the evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or at dawn” (Mark 13:35). Those were the hours of the night of the Passion, when the disciples slept. After the Resurrection they learned to watch. The Church still keeps watch in her evening and night prayer.

Peter says the day of the Lord will come like a thief, and asks, “what sort of persons ought you to be”? We wait for “new heavens and a new earth, where righteousness is at home” (2 Peter 3:11, 13). Let us live holy lives and care for God's creation while we wait.

At every Qurbana we proclaim the Lord's death until he comes. Let us say with the first Christians, Maranatha, come, Lord Jesus, and keep awake. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told us to stay awake for him, like a doorkeeper waiting for the master to come home.

How do we stay awake for Jesus? By praying, being kind and doing our work well every day.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you have gone to the Father and left your house in our hands. Keep us awake and faithful, each at the work you have given us. Do not let your coming find us asleep, in the evening, at midnight, at cockcrow or at dawn. Make us holy and godly as we wait for new heavens and a new earth where righteousness is at home, and keep the prayer of your Church alive on our lips: Maranatha, come, Lord Jesus. Amen. Alleluia!
