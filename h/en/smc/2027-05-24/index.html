Word for the day

“Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live,”” John 11:25

Reflection

Paul writes to the Philippians from prison, and he writes as a father: “Only let us hold fast to what we have attained. Brothers and sisters, join in imitating me, and observe those who live according to the example you have in us.” It may sound bold to say ‘imitate me’, but Paul has just told them that he himself is still running, pressing on towards the goal (Philippians 3:12–14). He does not offer himself as a finished saint, but as a runner who has not stopped. Faith is passed on this way, from life to life. Every one of us learned to pray from someone: a mother at the evening prayer, a grandfather with his rosary, a catechism teacher, a priest. The Season of the Apostles reminds us that the Church was built on witnesses whose lives could be copied. The question for us is simple: if someone younger imitated my faith, would they come closer to Christ?

Then Paul's voice breaks: “For many live as enemies of the cross of Christ; I have often told you of them, and now I tell you even with tears.” He describes them without hatred, with grief: “Their end is destruction; their god is the belly; and their glory is in their shame; their minds are set on earthly things.” These seem to be not pagans far away but baptized people who have made comfort and appetite their god. The danger is close to every one of us. Against it Paul sets one of the most beautiful lines of the New Testament: “But our citizenship is in heaven, and it is from there that we are expecting a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ.” Philippi was a Roman colony, proud of its citizenship; Paul tells them they belong to a greater city. And the Saviour we await “will transform the body of our humiliation that it may be conformed to the body of his glory.” Our bodies, weak and ageing, are not prisons to escape but seeds of the resurrection. The Catechism quotes these words when it teaches how we will rise: Christ will change our lowly body to be like his glorious body (CCC 999).

The Gospel shows that hope at the graveside. Lazarus has been in the tomb four days. Martha goes out to meet Jesus and speaks with the honesty of grief: “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. But even now I know that God will give you whatever you ask of him.” Jesus says, “Your brother will rise again.” Martha answers with the faith of a devout Jewish woman of her time: “I know that he will rise again in the resurrection on the last day.” And Jesus brings the last day into the present: “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live, and everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?” Resurrection is not only an event at the end; it is a Person standing before her. Martha's answer is one of the great confessions of the Gospel: “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.” In the Season of the Apostles, when the Church goes out to proclaim the Risen Lord, Martha teaches us the heart of that proclamation. Every Christian funeral in our parishes, with its prayers and its incense and its candles, repeats her words. And every time we visit a grieving family, we bring the same Lord to the door, asking quietly: “Do you believe this?”

Full homily

Dear friends, today Paul writes from prison to the Christians of Philippi: “join in imitating me.” He is not boasting. He is a runner still pressing on, and he asks them to run with him.

Then he weeps for those who live as enemies of the Cross, whose god is the belly and whose minds are set on earthly things. And he reminds us: “our citizenship is in heaven.” We belong to God's kingdom, and we wait for the Saviour who will transform our weak bodies to be like his glorious body.

In the Gospel Lazarus has died. Martha runs to meet Jesus and says honestly, “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died.” Jesus does not scold her. He says, “Your brother will rise again.”

Martha thinks of the last day, but Jesus says, “I am the resurrection and the life.” The resurrection is not only a future day; it is a Person, and he is here.

Jesus asks her, “Do you believe this?” Martha answers, “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God.”

Today he asks the same question of each of us, especially those who are grieving. Let us answer with Martha, and live as citizens of heaven. Amen.

For children

Children, Martha was sad because her brother Lazarus had died. But Jesus told her, ‘I am the resurrection and the life.’ That means Jesus is stronger than death!

When someone we love goes to heaven, we can be sad, but we can also say like Martha: ‘Yes, Lord, I believe!’

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you are the resurrection and the life. At Bethany you met Martha in her grief and drew from her the confession of faith; meet us also in our sorrows, and make us say with her, Yes, Lord, I believe. Keep us from making our belly our god and setting our minds on earthly things. Remind us that our citizenship is in heaven, and teach us to live so that others may imitate us and come to you. When our bodies grow weak and our days end, transform the body of our humiliation into the likeness of your glorious body, and raise us up to live with the Father and the Holy Spirit for ever. Amen.
