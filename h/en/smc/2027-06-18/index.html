Word for the day

“but in your hearts sanctify Christ as Lord. Always be ready to make your defense to anyone who demands from you an accounting for the hope that is in you;” 1 Peter 3:15

Reflection

The First Letter of Peter was written to small Christian communities scattered across Asia Minor, people who were misunderstood, slandered and sometimes mistreated by their neighbours. Peter does not tell them to hit back, nor to hide. He writes: “But even if you do suffer for doing what is right, you are blessed. Do not fear what they fear, and do not be intimidated, but in your hearts sanctify Christ as Lord.” The answer to fear is to enthrone Christ in the heart. Then comes the sentence that has shaped Christian witness ever since: “Always be ready to make your defense to anyone who demands from you an accounting for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and reverence.” The word for defense gives us the word apologetics, but Peter's apologetics is not a debate to be won. It is an account of hope, given with gentleness toward the questioner and reverence toward God. Christians in India know this situation well. We are a small flock among neighbours of many faiths, and most of our neighbours are friends; yet sometimes we are asked hard questions, and in some places Christians are misunderstood or falsely accused. Peter's counsel holds: know why you hope, answer when asked, and answer kindly. “Keep your conscience clear, so that, when you are maligned, those who abuse you for your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame.” A good life is the most persuasive defense.

Peter grounds all this in the Cross, which we remember on every Friday: “For it is better to suffer for doing good, if suffering should be God’s will, than to suffer for doing evil. For Christ also suffered for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, in order to bring you to God. He was put to death in the flesh, but made alive in the spirit.” Once for all: the sacrifice of Calvary needs no repetition, and the Holy Qurbana does not repeat it; it makes that one sacrifice present, so that we may enter into it. The righteous for the unrighteous: the innocent One suffered in place of the guilty. In order to bring you to God: the whole purpose of the Passion is reconciliation. When a Christian suffers unjustly and does not return evil for evil, he is drawn into the same pattern, and his patience becomes a quiet preaching of the Cross.

The Gospel shows us the same patience from God's side. A householder sows good seed in his field, “but while everybody was asleep, an enemy came and sowed weeds among the wheat, and then went away.” The weed Jesus means was probably darnel, which in its early growth looks almost exactly like wheat; farmers in Kuttanad know such weeds in the young paddy. Only at heading time can the difference be seen. The servants are scandalised: “Master, did you not sow good seed in your field? Where, then, did these weeds come from?” The answer is plain: “An enemy has done this.” Evil is not God's sowing. But when they offer to pull up the weeds, the master says: “No; for in gathering the weeds you would uproot the wheat along with them. Let both of them grow together until the harvest.” Later, in the house, Jesus explains: the field is the world, the good seed the children of the kingdom, the weeds the children of the evil one, the harvest the end of the age, the reapers the angels. The judgement is real, and it is serious; but it belongs to God and to his time. Until then the Church is a field where wheat and weeds grow side by side, and the line between them runs through every human heart, including our own. The parable forbids two things: despair when we see evil in the Church and the world, and violence in the name of purity, whether by persecuting those we judge to be weeds or by cutting off brothers and sisters we have given up on. As the Fathers loved to say (in substance), today's weed may by grace become tomorrow's wheat. And it gives one great promise: “Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father. Let anyone with ears listen!”

Homily outline

Theme: “but in your hearts sanctify Christ as Lord. Always be ready to make your defense to anyone who demands from you an accounting for the hope that is in you;”

Witness without fear, an account of hope given with gentleness, Christ who suffered once for all, and the patience of the Lord of the harvest.

1. Blessed when you suffer for doing right

  • Do not fear what they fear.
  • Sanctify Christ as Lord in your hearts.
  • A clear conscience and a good life.
“The Lord is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the stronghold of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?” Psalm 27:1
“But even if you do suffer for doing what is right, you are blessed. Do not fear what they fear, and do not be intimidated,” 1 Peter 3:14

Apply: When you are misunderstood for your faith this week, answer with a prayer before you answer with words.

2. An account of hope, with gentleness and reverence

  • Know why you hope.
  • Answer kindly, never with contempt.
  • Christ suffered once for all to bring us to God.
“A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.” Proverbs 15:1
“but in your hearts sanctify Christ as Lord. Always be ready to make your defense to anyone who demands from you an accounting for the hope that is in you;” 1 Peter 3:15

Apply: Write down in a few sentences why you hope in Christ, so that you are ready when someone asks.

3. Let both grow until the harvest

  • An enemy has done this.
  • The patience of God with the mixed field.
  • The righteous will shine like the sun.
“Through such works you have taught your people that the righteous must be kind, and you have filled your children with good hope, because you give repentance for sins.” Wisdom 12:19
“Let both of them grow together until the harvest; and at harvest time I will tell the reapers, Collect the weeds first and bind them in bundles to be burned, but gather the wheat into my barn.’”” Matthew 13:30

Apply: Pray each day this week for someone you had written off, and pull out one weed in your own heart.

With Christ enthroned in our hearts we need not fear; we give an account of our hope with gentleness, as he suffered for us with love.

The Lord of the harvest is patient: we leave the judgement to him, and by his grace weeds may yet become wheat.

Take-home resolution: This week, prepare a short account of your hope, answer every question about your faith with gentleness, and pray daily for someone you had given up on.

Full homily

Dear friends, Saint Peter wrote to Christians who were misunderstood and mistreated. He told them: “even if you do suffer for doing what is right, you are blessed. Do not fear what they fear… but in your hearts sanctify Christ as Lord” (1 Peter 3:14–15). When Christ is Lord in our hearts, fear loses its power.

Peter adds: “Always be ready to make your defense to anyone who demands from you an accounting for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and reverence.” Many people around us, friends of other faiths, colleagues, even our own children, may ask us why we believe. Let us know our faith, and let us answer with gentleness and respect, never with pride or anger.

Today is Friday. Peter reminds us that “Christ also suffered for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, in order to bring you to God” (1 Peter 3:18). The innocent One suffered for the guilty. When we suffer unjustly and do not return evil, we walk with him.

In the Gospel, an enemy sows weeds among the wheat. The servants want to pull them out at once, but the master says: wait until the harvest, or you will pull up the wheat too. God is patient. He gives everyone time to change. The Church is a field where good and bad grow together, and the weeds grow in our own hearts too.

So let us not despair when we see evil, and let us never use violence or contempt in the name of God. Let us pray for those we find hard to love, let us pull out the weeds in our own hearts, and let us trust the Lord of the harvest, who promises that “the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father” (Matthew 13:43). Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus tells a story about a farmer. An enemy planted weeds among his wheat. The farmer did not pull them out at once, because he did not want to hurt the good wheat. God is patient like that farmer. He gives everyone time to become good. So be patient with others, and be kind, even to people who are not kind to you!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you suffered for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring us to God. When we are misunderstood or mistreated for doing what is right, keep us from fear, and let us sanctify you as Lord in our hearts. Make us ready to give an account of our hope to anyone who asks, always with gentleness and reverence. Lord of the harvest, you are patient with the field of the world and with the field of our hearts: keep us from despair and from violence, let your grace turn weeds into wheat, and on the last day let us shine like the sun in the kingdom of your Father. To the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit be glory for ever. Amen.
