Word for the day

“Very truly, I tell you, before Abraham was, I am.” John 8:58

Reflection

“The LORD appeared to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre, as he sat at the entrance of his tent in the heat of the day.” Three travellers appear in the shimmering noon, and the old man, who could have stayed in the shade, runs to meet them and bows to the ground: “My lord, if I find favor with you, do not pass by your servant.” He offers “a little water” and “a little bread”, and then sets out a feast: cakes of the finest flour, a tender calf, curds and milk, and “he stood by them under the tree while they ate.” Abraham does not know at first whom he is serving; the Letter to the Hebrews remembers that “by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it” (Hebrews 13:2). The guests bring a promise: Sarah, long past the age of childbearing, will have a son. Listening behind the tent flap, Sarah laughs to herself. The LORD answers with the question that runs through the whole Bible: “Is anything too wonderful for the LORD?” Many Fathers saw in the three visitors, addressed as one Lord, a first hint of the mystery of the Holy Trinity, and the Eastern Churches have painted this scene as the icon of the Trinity's hospitality. The second reading shows us another old man who did not settle into the shade. Caleb, one of the two spies who had trusted God forty-five years earlier, comes to Joshua at the age of eighty-five: “I am still as strong today as I was on the day that Moses sent me.” He does not ask for a quiet field but for the hill country where the strongest enemies lived, because “I wholeheartedly followed the LORD my God.” Age is no retirement from faith. Among us there are grandparents who still walk to the first Qurbana before dawn on every day of the Great Fast, and who carry the whole family in their prayer. They are our Calebs.

Paul gives the Romans one debt that can never be paid off: “Owe no one anything, except to love one another; for the one who loves another has fulfilled the law.” Then he sounds a morning bell: “the night is far gone, the day is near. Let us then lay aside the works of darkness and put on the armor of light.” In the summer of 386, in a garden in Milan, a brilliant young teacher of rhetoric named Augustine, weeping over the slavery of his own desires, heard a child's voice chanting “take up and read”. He opened the letters of Paul and his eyes fell on these very words: “let us live honorably as in the day, not in reveling and drunkenness, not in debauchery and licentiousness, not in quarreling and jealousy. Instead, put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to gratify its desires.” He wrote later in his Confessions that at that moment the light of certainty flooded his heart. In the Gospel Jesus speaks in the temple with hearers who are scandalised by his claims: “Very truly, I tell you, whoever keeps my word will never see death.” They answer that Abraham died, and the prophets too. Jesus replies: “Your ancestor Abraham rejoiced that he would see my day; he saw it and was glad.” And then the words that sum up John's Gospel: “Very truly, I tell you, before Abraham was, I am.” I AM is the name God gave to Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:14). Some of his hearers took up stones, and Jesus went out of the temple. When John writes of “the Jews” in such debates he is speaking of particular opponents of Jesus in that hour, not of the Jewish people, to whom Jesus, Mary and the apostles belonged; the Church firmly rejects any blame of the Jewish people for the rejection or the death of Christ. The question is put to every one of us: who do I say that he is? The One who sat at Abraham's table is the One who now calls us to his, and before whom every Friday of the fast we kneel at the foot of the Cross.

Homily outline

Theme: “Very truly, I tell you, before Abraham was, I am.”

Abraham's hospitality at Mamre, Caleb's wholehearted old age, the armour of light, and the eternal Son who is the I AM.

1. By the oaks of Mamre

  • Running to meet the strangers.
  • Is anything too wonderful for the Lord?
  • Angels unawares.
“Is anything too wonderful for the LORD? At the set time I will return to you, in due season, and Sarah shall have a son.” Genesis 18:14
“Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it.” Hebrews 13:2

Apply: Invite someone lonely to your table before Easter.

2. Put on the Lord Jesus Christ

  • The one debt we always owe.
  • The night is far gone.
  • Augustine in the garden.
“I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my whole being shall exult in my God; for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation, he has covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decks himself with a garland, and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels.” Isaiah 61:10
“Instead, put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to gratify its desires.” Romans 13:14

Apply: Lay aside one work of darkness this week, and replace it with a work of love.

3. Before Abraham was, I am

  • Whoever keeps my word.
  • Abraham saw my day and was glad.
  • The divine name.
“God said to Moses, “I AM WHO I AM.” He said further, “Thus you shall say to the Israelites, ‘I AM has sent me to you.’”” Exodus 3:14
“Very truly, I tell you, before Abraham was, I am.” John 8:58

Apply: Pray the Way of the Cross today, and at each station adore Jesus as true God.

The God who visited Abraham is the Son who was before Abraham, and who comes to our table.

Welcome him in every guest, follow him wholeheartedly, and put him on like a garment of light.

Take-home resolution: Pray the Way of the Cross today, and invite someone lonely to your home before Easter.

Full homily

Dear friends, in the heat of the day Abraham sees three strangers and runs to welcome them: my lord, do not pass by your servant (Genesis 18:3). He serves them a feast, and receives God's promise of a son. When Sarah laughs, the Lord asks: is anything too wonderful for the LORD? (Genesis 18:14).

Caleb, at eighty-five, says: I am still as strong today as I was on the day that Moses sent me, and asks for the hardest hill country, because he has wholeheartedly followed the Lord (Joshua 14:8–12). Faith does not retire.

Paul tells us: owe no one anything, except to love one another; the night is far gone, the day is near; put on the Lord Jesus Christ (Romans 13:8, 12, 14). These were the words that converted St Augustine in a garden in Milan.

And Jesus says: very truly, I tell you, before Abraham was, I am (John 8:58). He is God, the same who spoke to Moses from the bush. On this Friday of the fast, as we walk the Way of the Cross, let us kneel before him and say: my Lord and my God. Amen.

For children

Children, Abraham ran to welcome three visitors and gave them food, and they were God's messengers! When we welcome people kindly, we welcome God. And Jesus tells us that he was alive even before Abraham, because he is God!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you are the I AM, who was before Abraham and who sat at his table by the oaks of Mamre. Do not pass by your servants. Teach us to welcome you in every guest, to follow you wholeheartedly like Caleb even in old age, and to pay every day the debt of love. Clothe us with yourself, the armour of light, and on this Friday of the fast keep us close to your Cross, so that we may rejoice to see your day. Amen.
