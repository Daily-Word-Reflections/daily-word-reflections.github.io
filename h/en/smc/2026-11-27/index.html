Fourth Friday of the Dedication of the Church

27 November 2026 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle Revelation 7:9–12 · A great multitude worships before the throne
  • Gospel Mark 12:18–27 · He is God not of the dead, but of the living

Word for the day

“He is God not of the dead, but of the living.” Mark 12:27

Reflection

The Sadducees, who denied the resurrection, try to ridicule it with the story of a woman married to seven brothers. Jesus answers that they know “neither the scriptures nor the power of God.” In the resurrection life is transformed; and God is “the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob”: “He is God not of the dead, but of the living.”

John sees the fulfilment: “a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages,” robed in white with palm branches, crying: “Salvation belongs to our God… and to the Lamb!” On the last Friday of the year we look at the end of the story: the Church gathered in praise.

Homily outline

Theme: “The God of the living.”

Jesus defends the resurrection; John sees the great multitude before the Lamb.

1. The God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob

  • God revealed himself to Moses by the names of the patriarchs.
  • They live to him.
“He said further, “I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.” And Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at God.” Exodus 3:6
“And as for the dead being raised, have you not read in the book of Moses, in the story about the bush, how God said to him, ‘I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob’? He is God not of the dead, but of the living.” Mark 12:26–27

Apply: Pray for your ancestors in faith.

2. A multitude from every nation

  • The Church is catholic: every tribe and language.
  • Salvation belongs to God and the Lamb.
“In days to come… all the nations shall stream to it.” Isaiah 2:2
“After this I looked, and there was a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, robed in white, with palm branches in their hands.” Revelation 7:9

Apply: Pray for Christians of another nation today.

3. Eternal praise

  • Heaven's liturgy is praise.
  • Our Qurbana joins it.
“For from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations.” Malachi 1:11
“Singing, “Amen! Blessing and glory and wisdom and thanksgiving and honor and power and might be to our God forever and ever! Amen.”” Revelation 7:12

Apply: Sing a hymn of praise at family prayer tonight.

God is the God of the living; our hope is the resurrection.

The Church's end is eternal praise.

Take-home resolution: Pray the Creed slowly each day this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Sadducees, who denied the resurrection, try to ridicule it with the story of a woman married to seven brothers. Jesus answers that they are wrong because they know neither the scriptures nor the power of God. In the resurrection life is transformed; and God is the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob; he is God not of the dead but of the living (Mark 12:24–27).

The Book of Revelation shows us those living ones: a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, tribe, people and language, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, robed in white, with palm branches in their hands, crying out: salvation belongs to our God and to the Lamb (Revelation 7:9–10).

In that multitude are our beloved dead, and generations of St Thomas Christians. Let us remember them today before the God of the living, and prepare our lives to join them in eternal praise. Amen.

For children

Children, in heaven people from every country praise God together. Pray today for your grandparents or others who have died.

Prayer

God of the living, you are the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Strengthen our faith in the resurrection and gather us with the great multitude to praise you and the Lamb for ever. Amen.
