Readings

Word for the day

“The police answered, “Never has anyone spoken like this!”” John 7:46

Reflection

Paul writes to Christians living in the capital of the empire, under rulers who were not Christian and not always just: “Let every person be subject to the governing authorities; for there is no authority except from God, and those authorities that exist have been instituted by God.” He is not flattering the emperor. He is saying that ordered government, which protects the weak and restrains the violent, is part of God's providence, and that the ruler is meant to be “God's servant for your good.” So Christians pay their taxes honestly, give respect and honour where they are due, and obey “not only because of wrath but also because of conscience.” Elsewhere the Church learned the limit of this obedience from the apostles themselves: when the authorities forbade them to preach Christ, Peter answered, “We must obey God rather than any human authority” (Acts 5:29). Jesus had already drawn the line: “Give therefore to the emperor the things that are the emperor's, and to God the things that are God's” (Matthew 22:21). The St Thomas Christians lived this balance for centuries under Hindu kings. The Tharisappalli copper plates of 849 record the privileges granted to the Christian community of Quilon by a local ruler, and our forefathers were known as loyal subjects and honest traders. A Christian citizen today pays tax without cheating, does not bribe, votes with conscience, prays for those in authority, and refuses only what is against God.

In the Gospel the chief priests and Pharisees had sent temple police to arrest Jesus. The police come back empty-handed. “Why did you not arrest him?” Their answer is one of the simplest confessions in the Gospel: “Never has anyone spoken like this!” They were not theologians; they had only listened. Their superiors are angry: “Surely you have not been deceived too, have you? Has any one of the authorities or of the Pharisees believed in him?” Then one voice rises in the council. Nicodemus, “who had gone to Jesus before, and who was one of them”, asks a careful question: “Our law does not judge people without first giving them a hearing to find out what they are doing, does it?” It is not yet a bold confession, only a plea for fairness, and he is mocked for it: “Surely you are not also from Galilee, are you?” But this small courage grows. On Good Friday the same Nicodemus will come openly to the Cross, “bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, weighing about a hundred pounds” (John 19:39), to bury the Lord. The Passion is drawing near. In families, parishes and offices, someone is always being condemned without a hearing. The Great Fast asks us for the courage of Nicodemus: to say, quietly and clearly, let us first hear him.

Homily outline

Theme: “Never has anyone spoken like this!”

The Christian as citizen under God, the guards who only listened, and Nicodemus who asked for a fair hearing.

1. Authority from God

  • God's servant for your good.
  • Taxes, respect, conscience.
  • God first, always.
“By me kings reign, and rulers decree what is just;” Proverbs 8:15
“Let every person be subject to the governing authorities; for there is no authority except from God, and those authorities that exist have been instituted by God.” Romans 13:1

Apply: Make sure your taxes, bills and documents are honest, and refuse every bribe.

2. Never has anyone spoken like this

  • Sent to arrest him.
  • They only listened.
  • The word that stops us.
“I will raise up for them a prophet like you from among their own people; I will put my words in the mouth of the prophet, who shall speak to them everything that I command.” Deuteronomy 18:18
“The police answered, “Never has anyone spoken like this!”” John 7:46

Apply: Read the Passion according to John slowly before Palm Sunday, as if hearing it for the first time.

3. Give him a hearing

  • One voice in the council.
  • Mocked for fairness.
  • Small courage that grows.
“You must not be partial in judging: hear out the small and the great alike; you shall not be intimidated by anyone, for the judgment is God's. Any case that is too hard for you, bring to me, and I will hear it.”” Deuteronomy 1:17
““Our law does not judge people without first giving them a hearing to find out what they are doing, does it?”” John 7:51

Apply: When someone is criticised in their absence, ask that their side be heard first.

A Christian is an honest citizen, a careful listener and a fair judge, faithful to God above all.

Listen to the One whose word is like no other, and find the courage of Nicodemus.

Take-home resolution: This week, be honest in every duty, and defend one person who is being judged without a hearing.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul writes: let every person be subject to the governing authorities; for there is no authority except from God (Romans 13:1). Authority is meant to be God's servant for our good. So a Christian pays tax honestly, refuses bribes, gives respect and prays for those who govern.

But there is a limit. When the authorities forbade the apostles to preach, Peter said: we must obey God rather than any human authority (Acts 5:29). We give to the emperor what is his, and to God what is God's.

The guards sent to arrest Jesus came back empty-handed and said: never has anyone spoken like this! (John 7:46). They had only listened. If we listen to Jesus with open hearts during this fast, his word will stop us too.

Nicodemus asked the council: does our law judge people without first giving them a hearing? (John 7:51). It was a small act of courage, and it grew until he came openly to bury the Lord. Let us also defend those who are judged without a hearing. Amen.

For children

Children, the guards went to catch Jesus, but when they heard him speak they came back and said: no one ever talked like this man! Let us listen to Jesus too. And when someone is being blamed, let us say: let's hear their side first!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, the guards who came to arrest you went away saying: never has anyone spoken like this. Give us ears to listen to your word, honest hands in our duties as citizens, and the courage of Nicodemus to defend those who are condemned without a hearing. Bless all who govern, that they may serve the good of all, and keep us always faithful to God above every human power. Amen.
