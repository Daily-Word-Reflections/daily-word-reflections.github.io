Readings

  • Epistle Ephesians 6:1–9 · The Lord rewards every good deed
  • Gospel Mark 12:35–40 · Jesus warns against the scribes

Word for the day

“Render service with enthusiasm, as to the Lord and not to men and women, knowing that whatever good we do, we will receive the same again from the Lord, whether we are slaves or free.” Ephesians 6:7–8

Reflection

Paul's letter to the Ephesians walks into the ordinary household of his time: parents and children, masters and slaves. To children he says, “Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right”, and he recalls the commandment, “Honor your father and mother.” To fathers he gives a warning that every parent needs: “do not provoke your children to anger, but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.” To slaves and masters he speaks in a world where slavery was taken for granted, and he plants a seed that would one day break it open: “you know that both of you have the same Master in heaven, and with him there is no partiality.” The key to the whole passage is one phrase: work done “as to the Lord.” Every service, in the kitchen, the office, the field or the hospital ward, can be offered to Christ, “knowing that whatever good we do, we will receive the same again from the Lord.” In the Gospel Jesus shows the opposite. He has just reminded the crowd that David himself calls the Messiah “Lord”, and the people listen “with delight”. Then he warns them: “Beware of the scribes, who like to walk around in long robes, and to be greeted with respect in the marketplaces, and to have the best seats in the synagogues and places of honor at banquets! They devour widows' houses and for the sake of appearance say long prayers.” Religion can be used for honour and profit. Even a fast can be kept for appearance. Jesus asks for service that seeks no seat of honour and that protects the widow instead of devouring her house.

Today the Syro-Malabar Church keeps the memory of Blessed Rani Maria, who lived exactly that kind of service. Mariam Vattalil was born in 1954 at Pulluvazhy near Perumbavoor, joined the Franciscan Clarist Congregation and received the name Rani Maria. She served in mission stations in North India, and from 1992 at Udainagar in the diocese of Indore. There she worked among poor tribal families: she helped them to learn, to save, to form groups, to know their rights and to free themselves from moneylenders who held them in debt. This angered those who profited from their poverty. On 25 February 1995, as she travelled by bus towards Indore, a man hired by them attacked her with a knife, and she died of her wounds by the roadside. She was forty-one. Her readings today speak of her: “See, I am sending you out like sheep into the midst of wolves”, and “we have this treasure in clay jars, so that it may be made clear that this extraordinary power belongs to God and does not come from us.” What followed is a Gospel in itself. Her killer, Samundar Singh, was sentenced to prison, and there he came to repentance. Her sister, Sr Selmy, also a Clarist sister, visited him and tied a rakhi on his wrist, accepting him as her brother, and her parents received him in their home as a son. On 4 November 2017 Rani Maria was beatified at Indore, the first woman martyr from India to be declared Blessed. Like the mother in the book of Maccabees, her family showed that the love of God is stronger than death and stronger than hatred.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul speaks to parents and children, masters and servants. His key word is this: render service with enthusiasm, as to the Lord and not to men and women (Ephesians 6:7). Every honest work can be offered to Christ.

Jesus warns us about religion for show: those who like long robes and the best seats, who devour widows' houses and for the sake of appearance say long prayers (Mark 12:38–40). Even our fasting can become a show. Jesus wants service that protects the poor.

Today we remember Blessed Rani Maria, a Clarist sister from Pulluvazhy. At Udainagar near Indore she helped poor tribal families to learn, to save and to free themselves from moneylenders. On 25 February 1995 she was killed on a bus by a man hired by those she had angered. She was a sheep sent among wolves (Matthew 10:16).

Her killer repented in prison. Her sister tied a rakhi on his wrist and called him brother, and her parents welcomed him as a son. We carry the treasure of Christ in clay jars (2 Corinthians 4:7), but his power can make even a broken jar shine. Let us serve like Rani Maria, and forgive like her family. Amen.

For children

Children, Sister Rani Maria from Kerala went far away to help poor families learn to read and to be free. Some bad people killed her, but her family forgave the man who did it. Jesus' love is stronger than hate!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you came not to be served but to serve, and you protected the widow and the poor. Teach us to work as to you, without seeking honour, and to stand with the poor against those who devour their homes. Through the prayers of Blessed Rani Maria, who served the poor and gave her life for you, give us courage among wolves and a forgiveness stronger than hatred. Amen.
