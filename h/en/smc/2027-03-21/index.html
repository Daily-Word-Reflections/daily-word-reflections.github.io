Word for the day

“Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest heaven!” Matthew 21:9

Reflection

Today the Church walks with Jesus into Jerusalem, and the Great Fast gives way to the Week of the Passion, which our tradition calls Hasha Week. Hosanna Sunday is one of the days Kerala Christians love most, and its rite is simple enough for every family to follow. First, the people gather before the Holy Qurbana, often outside the church or at a nearby cross, each holding a tender coconut leaf, the kuruthola. Second, the celebrant prays over the palms and blesses them, sprinkling them with holy water. Third, the Gospel of the Lord's entry into Jerusalem is proclaimed, so that we hear what we are about to do. Fourth, the procession moves toward the church and around it, with the cross going before, while everyone waves the palms and sings the Hosanna hymns, as the crowds did on the road from Bethphage. Fifth, the procession enters the church, and the Holy Qurbana continues with the readings of the day: the King we have welcomed with palms now comes to us on the altar. Afterwards the families take the blessed palms home and keep them in the prayer corner. On Pesaha a small cross folded from the blessed palm is pressed into the Pesaha appam; and many parishes keep the old palms to burn them for the ashes at the beginning of next year's Great Fast. So the palm of welcome, the bread of the Supper and the ashes of repentance are woven into one year of faith. Customs vary from eparchy to eparchy and parish to parish; follow the order your parish uses.

The first reading is the blessing of the dying Jacob over his sons. To Judah he says: “Judah, your brothers shall praise you; your hand shall be on the neck of your enemies; your father's sons shall bow down before you.” And then a promise that Israel treasured for centuries: “The scepter shall not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, until tribute comes to him; and the obedience of the peoples is his.” Strangely, the next line speaks of a donkey: “Binding his foal to the vine and his donkey's colt to the choice vine, he washes his garments in wine and his robe in the blood of grapes.” The Fathers saw here the King from Judah who would come riding on a donkey's colt, and whose garments would be washed in the wine of his own blood. The reading also gives the blessing of Joseph, the son who suffered at the hands of his brothers and saved them: “Joseph is a fruitful bough, a fruitful bough by a spring; his branches run over the wall. The archers fiercely attacked him; they shot at him and pressed him hard. Yet his bow remained taut, and his arms were made agile by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob.” In the King from Judah and the rejected brother who saves his family, the Church has always seen one face: Jesus, the Son of David, who goes up to Jerusalem to be rejected and to save.

Zechariah's prophecy is the key to everything that happens on the road from Bethany: “Rejoice greatly, O daughter Zion! Shout aloud, O daughter Jerusalem! Lo, your king comes to you; triumphant and victorious is he, humble and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey.” Kings rode into cities on war horses. This King comes on a farmer's animal, and the prophet explains why: “He will cut off the chariot from Ephraim and the war-horse from Jerusalem; and the battle bow shall be cut off, and he shall command peace to the nations.” He wins his kingdom not by killing but by dying, and he seals it “because of the blood of my covenant with you”. And he calls out to all who are trapped: “Return to your stronghold, O prisoners of hope.” In Kerala, where elephants and caparisons and loud processions mark our feasts, the Lord's chosen mount is a quiet rebuke and a great comfort. He does not come to frighten us; he comes humble, to set prisoners free.

Paul, writing to Gentile Christians in Rome, uses an image every Kerala farmer understands: grafting. The Church of Israel is a cultivated olive tree with a holy root. Some branches were broken off; the Gentiles, “a wild olive shoot”, have been grafted in “to share the rich root of the olive tree”. Paul's warning is sharp: “do not boast over the branches. If you do boast, remember that it is not you that support the root, but the root that supports you.” “So do not become proud, but stand in awe.” And he insists that God has not finished with his people: “God has the power to graft them in again.” This reading belongs on Hosanna Sunday. As we enter the Week of the Passion, the Church forbids us to blame the Jewish people as a whole for the death of Jesus; the Second Vatican Council teaches that the Jews remain most dear to God, whose gifts and call are irrevocable, and that his Passion cannot be charged against all the Jews of that time or of today. Christ died for the sins of all of us, and we are the branches who live from the root.

Then the Gospel. Jesus sends two disciples to bring a donkey and her colt, and Matthew tells us why: “This took place to fulfill what had been spoken through the prophet.” The crowds spread their cloaks on the road, cut branches from the trees, and shout: “Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest heaven!” Hosanna is a Hebrew cry meaning “Save us, we pray”, taken from Psalm 118, the pilgrims' psalm. “When he entered Jerusalem, the whole city was in turmoil, asking, ‘Who is this?’” That question will echo through the whole week. Matthew goes straight on to the temple: Jesus drives out the sellers and buyers and overturns the tables, saying, “It is written, ‘My house shall be called a house of prayer’; but you are making it a den of robbers.” Then “the blind and the lame came to him in the temple, and he cured them”, and the children go on crying “Hosanna to the Son of David”. When the authorities object, Jesus quotes the psalm: “Out of the mouths of infants and nursing babies you have prepared praise for yourself.” The King's first act in his city is to cleanse the house of prayer and to heal; and the purest praise comes from children. Today, as our children wave their palms, let us ask whether our own hearts, our homes and our parishes are houses of prayer, and whether the cry Hosanna on our lips today will still be there on Friday.

Homily outline

Theme: “Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest heaven!”

Hosanna Sunday: the King from Judah, the humble King of peace, the cleansing of the house of prayer, and the branches grafted onto a holy root.

1. The scepter of Judah

  • Jacob's blessing.
  • The colt bound to the vine.
  • The Lion of Judah who conquers by the Cross.
“The scepter shall not depart from Judah, nor the ruler’s staff from between his feet, until tribute comes to him; and the obedience of the peoples is his.” Genesis 49:10
“Then one of the elders said to me, “Do not weep. See, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered, so that he can open the scroll and its seven seals.”” Revelation 5:5

Apply: Give Christ the throne of one area of your life you have kept for yourself.

2. Humble and riding on a donkey

  • Not a war horse.
  • Peace to the nations.
  • Prisoners of hope set free.
“Rejoice greatly, O daughter Zion! Shout aloud, O daughter Jerusalem! Lo, your king comes to you; triumphant and victorious is he, humble and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey.” Zechariah 9:9
“The crowds that went ahead of him and that followed were shouting, “Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest heaven!”” Matthew 21:9

Apply: Choose gentleness over force in one conflict at home or at work this week.

3. A house of prayer

  • The tables overturned.
  • The blind and the lame healed.
  • The praise of children.
“these I will bring to my holy mountain, and make them joyful in my house of prayer; their burnt offerings and their sacrifices will be accepted on my altar; for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples.” Isaiah 56:7
“He said to them, “It is written, ‘My house shall be called a house of prayer’; but you are making it a den of robbers.”” Matthew 21:13

Apply: Gather your family for prayer every evening this week, and let the house be quieter.

4. The root supports you

  • The cultivated olive tree.
  • Do not boast over the branches.
  • God's kindness and his faithfulness.
“But I am like a green olive tree in the house of God. I trust in the steadfast love of God forever and ever.” Psalm 52:8
“do not boast over the branches. If you do boast, remember that it is not you that support the root, but the root that supports you.” Romans 11:18

Apply: Refuse every word of contempt this week, and pray for the Jewish people and for all Christians.

The King promised to Judah comes humble on a donkey, cleanses the house of prayer and heals; the children greet him with Hosanna.

We are branches grafted by grace; let our Hosanna last through the whole week, to the Cross and the empty tomb.

Take-home resolution: Keep the blessed palm, pray with your family every evening, and follow the Lord through every service of the Week of the Passion.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today we carried blessed palms and sang Hosanna, as the crowds did on the road into Jerusalem. The Great Fast now gives way to the Week of the Passion. The palms we take home will be folded into a cross for the Pesaha appam, and next year they will become the ashes of a new Great Fast.

Long ago Jacob blessed his son Judah: the scepter shall not depart from Judah (Genesis 49:10). And he spoke of a donkey's colt bound to the vine. Today the Son of David from the tribe of Judah comes riding on that colt.

Zechariah tells us what kind of King he is: lo, your king comes to you; triumphant and victorious is he, humble and riding on a donkey (Zechariah 9:9). He does not come on a war horse. He comes to command peace to the nations and to set free the prisoners of hope by the blood of the covenant.

Paul warns us Gentile Christians not to boast: it is not you that support the root, but the root that supports you (Romans 11:18). This week we will hear the Passion. Let us never blame the Jewish people for the death of Jesus. He died for the sins of all of us.

The crowds cried: Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! (Matthew 21:9). Hosanna means save us, we pray. Then Jesus cleansed the temple: my house shall be called a house of prayer (Matthew 21:13). He healed the blind and the lame, and the children went on singing.

The same crowd that sings Hosanna today will shout Crucify on Friday. Let our Hosanna last all week. Let our homes be houses of prayer, let us come to Pesaha, to Passion Friday and to the Easter Qurbana, and let us follow our humble King to the Cross and to the empty tomb. Amen.

For children

Children, today we waved palm leaves and sang Hosanna, just like the children in Jerusalem! Jesus loved hearing their praise.

Jesus came riding on a little donkey, not on a big war horse, because he is a gentle King of peace. Take your blessed palm home and keep it near the cross in your prayer corner.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Son of David and King of peace, you came to Jerusalem humble and riding on a donkey, and the children cried Hosanna. Today we take up their cry: save us, we pray. Cleanse the temple of our hearts and make our homes houses of prayer. Keep us humble, grafted by grace into the olive tree of your people, and let our Hosanna not fall silent when the week grows dark, but follow you to the Cross and to the joy of your Resurrection. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
