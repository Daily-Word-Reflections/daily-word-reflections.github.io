Fourth Wednesday of Denha

27 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle 1 John 2:7–11 · The true light is already shining
  • Gospel John 9:1–11 · Jesus gives sight to the man born blind

Word for the day

“As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.”” John 9:5

Reflection

John writes about an old commandment that is also new: love one another. It is old because it was given from the beginning; it is new because in Christ “the darkness is passing away and the true light is already shining.” Then the test: “Whoever says, ‘I am in the light,’ while hating a brother or sister, is still in the darkness.” “Whoever loves a brother or sister lives in the light, and in such a person there is no cause for stumbling.” Hatred blinds; love gives sight.

In the Gospel Jesus sees a man blind from birth. The disciples ask whose sin caused it. Jesus refuses to blame: “he was born blind so that God's works might be revealed in him.” “As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.” He spits on the ground, makes mud, spreads it on the man's eyes, and sends him: “Go, wash in the pool of Siloam” (which means Sent). “Then he went and washed and came back able to see.” The mud recalls the dust from which God formed Adam; Christ is re-creating this man. The washing recalls Baptism, which the early Church called illumination. Denha is the feast of light; today Christ gives light to one who had never seen.

Homily outline

Theme: “I am the light of the world.”

Love that gives sight, and Christ who re-creates the blind.

1. The old new commandment

  • From the beginning.
  • Darkness passing.
  • True light shining.
“You shall not take vengeance or bear a grudge against any of your people, but you shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am the LORD.” Leviticus 19:18
“Whoever loves a brother or sister lives in the light, and in such a person there is no cause for stumbling.” 1 John 2:10

Apply: Let go of one grudge today.

2. The light of the world

  • Not whose sin.
  • God's works revealed.
  • A light to the nations.
“I am the LORD, I have called you in righteousness, I have taken you by the hand and kept you; I have given you as a covenant to the people, a light to the nations, to open the eyes that are blind, to bring out the prisoners from the dungeon, from the prison those who sit in darkness.” Isaiah 42:6–7
“As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.”” John 9:5

Apply: Do not blame; serve someone who suffers.

3. Mud and Siloam

  • Dust re-created.
  • Sent to wash.
  • Seeing for the first time.
“the LORD opens the eyes of the blind. The LORD lifts up those who are bowed down; the LORD loves the righteous.” Psalm 146:8
“saying to him, “Go, wash in the pool of Siloam” (which means Sent). Then he went and washed and came back able to see.” John 9:7

Apply: Thank God for your Baptism today.

Love keeps us in the light; hatred blinds.

Christ, the light, opens eyes and hearts.

Take-home resolution: Visit or help someone with a disability this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, John speaks of an old commandment that is new: love one another. In Christ the darkness is passing away and the true light is already shining (1 John 2:8).

The test is simple: whoever loves a brother or sister lives in the light (1 John 2:10). Hatred blinds; love gives sight.

In the Gospel the disciples ask whose sin made a man blind. Jesus refuses to blame. He says: as long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world (John 9:5). He makes mud, anoints the man's eyes and sends him to wash in Siloam. He came back able to see.

The mud recalls Adam formed from the dust; Christ re-creates him. The washing recalls Baptism. In this season of light, let us ask Jesus to heal our blindness and to let us walk in love. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus helped a blind man to see! Jesus is the light of the world. Let us ask him to help our hearts see clearly and love everyone.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, light of the world, you opened the eyes of the man born blind. Heal the blindness of our hearts, free us from hatred, and let us walk in the true light that is already shining. Amen.
