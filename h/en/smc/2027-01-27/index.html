Fourth Wednesday of Denha

Readings

  • Epistle Galatians 1:6–10 · There is only the gospel of Christ
  • Gospel John 10:1–6 · He calls his own sheep by name

Word for the day

“The gatekeeper opens the gate for him, and the sheep hear his voice. He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.” John 10:3

Reflection

In Galatians Paul skips the thanksgiving with which he usually greets a church and goes straight to his alarm: “I am astonished that you are so quickly deserting the one who called you in the grace of Christ and are turning to a different gospel.” Then he corrects himself: “not that there is another gospel.” There is only one Gospel, because there is only one Christ. Some were telling the Galatians that grace was not enough, that they must add the old observances to be saved. Paul answers with fierce words, because to change the Gospel is to lose Christ. And he examines his own heart: “Am I now seeking human approval, or God's approval? Or am I trying to please people? If I were still pleasing people, I would not be a servant of Christ.” A preacher who tells people only what they want to hear has stopped serving the Lord.

In the Gospel Jesus speaks of the sheepfold. Thieves and bandits climb in another way; the shepherd comes through the gate, and “the sheep hear his voice. He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.” The sheep “will not follow a stranger”, because they do not know the voice of strangers. Here is the answer to the confusion in Galatia: the Church is kept in the truth not by clever arguments but by knowing the Shepherd's voice. That voice is learned by long listening, in Scripture, in the Holy Qurbana, in the teaching handed on by the apostles. Denha reveals a Shepherd who is not distant: he knows each of us by name, goes ahead of us, and leads us out.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul writes to the Galatians in alarm: I am astonished that you are so quickly deserting the one who called you in the grace of Christ (Galatians 1:6). There is no other gospel, because there is no other Christ.

Then he examines his own heart: if I were still pleasing people, I would not be a servant of Christ (Galatians 1:10). The temptation to please people is strong for every disciple.

In the Gospel Jesus says the shepherd calls his own sheep by name and leads them out, and the sheep follow him because they know his voice (John 10:3–4). They will not follow a stranger.

We learn the Shepherd's voice by listening every day, in Scripture, in the Holy Qurbana and in the teaching of the Church. Then no stranger's voice will lead us away. Amen.

For children

Children, a good shepherd knows the name of every sheep! Jesus knows your name. Let us listen to him every day so that we know his voice.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, good Shepherd, you call each of us by name and lead us out. Keep us faithful to the one Gospel we have received, free us from the desire to please people more than God, and teach us to know your voice and follow you. Amen.
