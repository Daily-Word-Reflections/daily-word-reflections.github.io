Word for the day

“Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live, and everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?”” John 11:25–26

Reflection

Of all the friends of Jesus, the house at Bethany seems to have been the one where he felt most at home. The Gospel of John says it in a single sentence: “Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus.” Bethany lay about two miles from Jerusalem, on the road he walked on his way to the feast, and there a woman named Martha welcomed him into her home. We know her from three scenes. In Luke she is the one busy with “many tasks,” who comes to the Lord and says: “Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to do all the work by myself?” In John, at the death of her brother, she is the one who runs out to meet him on the road and makes a confession of faith. And at the supper in Bethany six days before the Passover, “Martha served.” Today the Church honours her as a saint, a woman who loved the Lord in the practical ways of the kitchen and the road, and who also found the words of faith.

The reading from John is one of the greatest passages of the Gospel. Lazarus “had already been in the tomb four days,” and “many of the Jews had come to Martha and Mary to console them about their brother.” When Martha hears that Jesus is coming, she goes out to meet him, while Mary stays at home. Her first words are full of grief and of trust: “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. But even now I know that God will give you whatever you ask of him.” Jesus says, “Your brother will rise again.” Martha answers with the faith of her people, “I know that he will rise again in the resurrection on the last day.” And then Jesus lifts her faith higher: “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live, and everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?” The resurrection is not only an event of the last day; it is a person standing on the road in front of her. Martha's answer, in the verse that follows the reading, belongs among the great confessions: “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.” It is the faith that Peter confesses at Caesarea Philippi, and it is spoken by a woman, before Lazarus has been raised, in the middle of her tears.

The reading from the First Letter of John explains why Martha's service and her faith belong together: “Beloved, let us love one another, because love is from God; everyone who loves is born of God and knows God. Whoever does not love does not know God, for God is love.” Love is not first our achievement. “In this is love, not that we loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the atoning sacrifice for our sins.” Martha's kitchen, her bread and her fire were an answer to a love that had come first. “No one has ever seen God; if we love one another, God lives in us, and his love is perfected in us.” And the letter ends with the words that could stand over every Christian home: “God is love, and those who abide in love abide in God, and God abides in them.” Kerala is famous for its hospitality, for the cup of tea and the rice offered to every guest; the letter to the Hebrews says that “some have entertained angels without knowing it.” Martha was a woman who knew how to welcome Christ in a guest.

The weekday readings meet her at the same door. James says: “Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you.” In John, Martha does precisely this: when Jesus comes, she draws near, runs to meet him, and speaks to him without fear. James also says, “Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you,” and he warns against speaking evil of one another and judging a neighbour. In Luke 10 the two sisters are tempted to judge each other, one for being busy, the other for being idle; the Lord does not tell Martha to stop serving, but to stop being “worried and distracted by many things.” He says, “there is need of only one thing,” and Mary has chosen it. The Gospel of the day then speaks with an unexpected voice: “I came to bring fire to the earth, and how I wish it were already kindled! I have a baptism with which to be baptized, and what stress I am under until it is completed!” The fire is the love of God that Jesus came to set alight, and the baptism is his death. Even at Bethany, the raising of Lazarus will lead directly to the chief priests' decision to put Jesus to death (John 11:53). Love of this kind is costly, and it divides households between those who accept it and those who refuse it.

We are in Kaitha, the season of harvest, and Martha is a saint of the harvest. The work of the harvest is also the work of serving: of feeding the workers, washing the pots, welcoming the guest, and keeping the household going. The Gospel does not tell us that this work is a lesser thing; it says that it must be done with the heart of Mary, sitting at the feet of the Lord. In the Holy Qurbana we do both: we sit and listen to the word, and then we rise and serve at the table of the poor. Let us ask Saint Martha to teach us both how to run and meet the Lord, and how to sit and listen; and let us answer in our own lives the question that Jesus asked her: “Do you believe this?”

Homily outline

Theme: “I am the resurrection and the life… Do you believe this?”

On the memorial of Saint Martha: faith spoken in grief, love that serves, and drawing near to the Lord who kindles the fire.

1. Martha's faith on the road

  • Grief and trust in the same breath.
  • The resurrection is a person standing before her.
  • A woman's confession: the Messiah, the Son of God.
“Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live,” John 11:25
“She said to him, “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world.”” John 11:27

Apply: Say aloud this week, in the middle of a worry, “Yes, Lord, I believe.”

2. Love that serves

  • God loved us first.
  • Martha served at Bethany.
  • Welcoming a guest is welcoming Christ.
“Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it.” Hebrews 13:2
“Beloved, let us love one another, because love is from God; everyone who loves is born of God and knows God.” 1 John 4:7

Apply: Welcome one guest, neighbour or stranger into your home or your day.

3. Draw near, and keep the fire

  • Submit to God and draw near.
  • Humble yourselves before the Lord.
  • Jesus came to kindle fire on the earth.
“The grass withers, the flower fades; but the word of our God will stand forever.” Isaiah 40:8
“Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you.” James 4:8

Apply: Give five silent minutes before the Lord each day this week, before your busiest hour.

Martha shows that faith and service belong together, and that the Lord who loves our homes is the resurrection and the life.

Let us run to meet him, sit at his feet, and serve him in every guest.

Take-home resolution: This week, welcome a guest, sit in silence five minutes a day, and say with Martha, “Yes, Lord, I believe.”

Full homily

Dear friends, today the Church remembers Saint Martha, the friend of Jesus. We know her from three scenes. In Luke she is the busy woman who welcomes Jesus into her home and cooks for him. In John she is the sister who runs to meet Jesus when her brother has died. At the supper in Bethany, “Martha served.” The Gospel of John says: “Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus” (John 11:5).

The Gospel today is one of the most beautiful of all. Lazarus has been four days in the tomb. Martha hears that Jesus is coming and goes out to meet him. Her words are full of grief and trust: “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. But even now I know that God will give you whatever you ask of him” (John 11:21–22). And Jesus answers: “I am the resurrection and the life. Those who believe in me, even though they die, will live, and everyone who lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?” (John 11:25–26).

This is the heart of our faith. Resurrection is not only an idea for the last day. It is a person: Jesus himself. And Martha answers, in the next verse: “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, the one coming into the world” (John 11:27). She says it in the middle of her tears. When we stand at a grave, in our own grief, the same question is put to us: Do you believe this?

Saint John writes in the second reading: “Beloved, let us love one another, because love is from God… God is love” (1 John 4:7–8). Martha's service was an answer to love. Our hospitality in Kerala, the cup of tea, the meal for the guest, is a way of loving God, if we do it with a heart that is also sitting at the Lord's feet. The Lord does not scold Martha for serving; he asks her not to be “worried and distracted by many things” (Luke 10:41).

The weekday readings say: “Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you” (James 4:8). And Jesus says: “I came to bring fire to the earth” (Luke 12:49), the fire of love. Let us pray today: Lord, light that fire in our homes. Let us serve like Martha and listen like Mary, and answer when he asks: Do you believe this? Saint Martha, pray for us. Amen.

For children

Children, Martha was a friend of Jesus who lived in Bethany. She cooked and served for him with love. When her brother Lazarus died, she ran to Jesus and said, ‘I believe that you are the Son of God!’ And Jesus brought Lazarus back to life. At home, you can be like Martha: help set the table, welcome guests with a smile. And you can be like Mary too: sit still and say a prayer to Jesus. Say with me: Jesus, I believe!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you loved Martha and her sister and her brother, and you were at home in their house. You are the resurrection and the life; teach us to answer, as Martha did, ‘Yes, Lord, I believe.’ Give us hearts that run to meet you, hands that serve in love, and ears that listen at your feet. Heal our worry and our distraction; keep us from judging one another. Light in our hearts the fire of your love, and let our homes in Kerala be houses of welcome where you are loved in every guest. Through the prayers of Saint Martha, comfort all who grieve, and raise us with all your friends to the life that does not end. Amen.
