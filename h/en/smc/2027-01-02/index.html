Word for the day

“You also must be ready, for the Son of Man is coming at an unexpected hour.” Luke 12:40

Reflection

The Nativity season closes with a lamp in the hand. Jesus tells his disciples: “Be dressed for action and have your lamps lit; be like those who are waiting for their master to return from the wedding banquet”. The servant's task is simple: stay awake, keep the lamp burning, open the door the moment the master knocks. Then comes a surprise that only the Child of Bethlehem can explain: “he will fasten his belt and have them sit down to eat, and he will come and serve them.” The Lord who came as a servant in the manger will come again as a servant at the table of his kingdom.

How do we stay awake? St Paul answers with the gift of the Spirit. As only a person's own spirit knows what is within, so “no one comprehends what is truly God's except the Spirit of God.” And “we have received not the spirit of the world, but the Spirit that is from God, so that we may understand the gifts bestowed on us by God.” The unspiritual person finds God's gifts foolish; the spiritual person discerns. Vigilance is not anxious clock-watching. It is a heart taught by the Spirit to recognise the Lord when he knocks: in prayer, in the poor at the gate, in the events of a new year. “You also must be ready, for the Son of Man is coming at an unexpected hour.”

Full homily

Dear friends, on this Saturday at the close of the Nativity season Jesus puts a lamp into our hands. Be dressed for action and have your lamps lit, he says, like servants waiting for their master to return from a wedding banquet, ready to open the door as soon as he knocks (Luke 12:35–36).

Then he says something astonishing. Blessed are those servants whom the master finds alert: he will fasten his belt, have them sit down to eat, and come and serve them (Luke 12:37). Masters do not serve servants. This Master does. We have just seen him lying in a manger; we will see him kneeling to wash feet. The one we wait for is the one who serves.

How do we stay awake? St Paul says we have received not the spirit of the world but the Spirit that is from God, so that we may understand the gifts God gives us (1 Corinthians 2:12). The Spirit teaches us to discern: to recognise the Lord's knock in a word of Scripture, in a poor person at the gate, in the quiet voice of conscience.

A new year has just begun. We do not know at what hour the Lord will come, to the world or to each of us. You also must be ready, Jesus says (Luke 12:40). Let us keep our lamps lit with prayer and good works, so that whenever he knocks, we may open at once. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told a story about servants who stayed awake at night with their lamps lit, waiting for their master. When he came, he served them dinner! Let us keep the lamp of our love for Jesus burning every day by praying and being kind.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you came among us as one who serves, and you will come again at an hour we do not know. Pour your Spirit into my heart, that I may discern what is from God, keep my lamp burning, and open the door to you whenever you knock. Amen.
