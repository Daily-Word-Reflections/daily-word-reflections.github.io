Fifth Tuesday of Denha · Mayaltho: The Presentation of the Lord in the Temple

2 February 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading Leviticus 12:1–8 · The purification of mothers
  • Second Reading Isaiah 42:1–7 · The Servant, a light to the nations
  • Epistle Romans 9:30–10:4 · Christ, the end of the law
  • Gospel Luke 2:22–38 · The Presentation of the Lord in the Temple

Word for the day

“for my eyes have seen your salvation, which you have prepared in the presence of all peoples, a light for revelation to the Gentiles and for glory to your people Israel.” Luke 2:30–32

Reflection

Forty days after his birth the Lord enters his Temple. The Church of the East calls this feast Mayaltho, the Entrance: the child carried in Mary's arms is the Lord of the Temple coming into his own house. The first reading shows the law the Holy Family obeyed. After the birth of a son a mother waited forty days for her purification, and then brought “a lamb in its first year for a burnt offering, and a pigeon or a turtledove for a sin offering.” The law made room for the poor: “If she cannot afford a sheep, she shall take two turtledoves or two pigeons.” Luke tells us that Mary and Joseph offered “a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons.” They were poor. They also fulfilled another law, that every firstborn son belongs to God: “Every firstborn male shall be designated as holy to the Lord.” The true Lamb was carried into the Temple, and the poor man's offering was made for him.

Two old people were waiting. Simeon was “righteous and devout, looking forward to the consolation of Israel, and the Holy Spirit rested on him.” Guided by the Spirit he came into the Temple at the very moment the parents brought the child in. He took the child in his arms and praised God: “Master, now you are dismissing your servant in peace, according to your word; for my eyes have seen your salvation, which you have prepared in the presence of all peoples, a light for revelation to the Gentiles and for glory to your people Israel.” Then he turned to the mother with a harder word: “This child is destined for the falling and the rising of many in Israel, and to be a sign that will be opposed”, and a sword would pierce her own soul too. Anna, a widow of eighty-four, “never left the temple but worshiped there with fasting and prayer night and day.” She too began to praise God and to speak about the child “to all who were looking for the redemption of Jerusalem.” The prophet Malachi had promised that “the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple.” He came quietly, as a baby, and only those who were waiting recognized him.

Isaiah describes the one Simeon held: “Here is my servant, whom I uphold, my chosen, in whom my soul delights.” He will not shout or crush the weak: “a bruised reed he will not break, and a dimly burning wick he will not quench.” God gives him “as a covenant to the people, a light to the nations, to open the eyes that are blind.” Simeon's song repeats this promise: a light for the Gentiles, and so for us in India too. Paul, in the Epistle, carries the sorrow of the sword. He grieves that many of his own people stumbled over “the stumbling stone”, and he prays that they may be saved. Yet he states the meaning of this feast in one line: “For Christ is the end of the law so that there may be righteousness for everyone who believes.” The child who was presented according to the law is the goal toward which the whole law was moving.

The weekday readings keep the Denha light turned toward the end of time. Paul proclaims that “as all die in Adam, so all will be made alive in Christ”, and Jesus warns, “Keep awake therefore, for you do not know on what day your Lord is coming.” Simeon and Anna kept awake for a lifetime, and their eyes saw salvation. Today is also the World Day for Consecrated Life. Religious sisters, brothers and priests, so many of them from our own families, are presented to God like the child in the Temple, poor, chaste and obedient, so that the light of Christ may reach every people.

Homily outline

Theme: “My eyes have seen your salvation.”

Mayaltho: the Lord enters his Temple, presented with the offering of the poor and received by those who waited.

  • Forty days.
  • The offering of the poor.
  • The firstborn belongs to God.

“When the time came for their purification according to the law of Moses, they brought him up to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord” Luke 2:22

Apply: Offer yourself and your family to God at the start of each day.

2. A light to the nations

  • The chosen Servant.
  • The bruised reed.
  • Light for the Gentiles, light for India.
“I am the LORD, I have called you in righteousness, I have taken you by the hand and kept you; I have given you as a covenant to the people, a light to the nations, to open the eyes that are blind, to bring out the prisoners from the dungeon, from the prison those who sit in darkness.” Isaiah 42:6–7
“Master, now you are dismissing your servant in peace, according to your word; for my eyes have seen your salvation, which you have prepared in the presence of all peoples, a light for revelation to the Gentiles and for glory to your people Israel.” Luke 2:29–32

Apply: Be gentle this week with someone who is bruised and weak.

3. A sign that will be opposed

  • The stone of stumbling.
  • Christ, the end of the law.
  • The sword in Mary's soul.
“See, I am laying in Zion a foundation stone, a tested stone, a precious cornerstone, a sure foundation: “One who trusts will not panic.”” Isaiah 28:16
“Then Simeon blessed them and said to his mother Mary, “This child is destined for the falling and the rising of many in Israel, and to be a sign that will be opposed so that the inner thoughts of many will be revealed—and a sword will pierce your own soul too.”” Luke 2:34–35

Apply: When following Christ brings opposition, unite your pain with Mary's.

4. Fasting and prayer night and day

  • Anna the prophet.
  • The Lord comes to his temple.
  • Keep awake.
“See, I am sending my messenger to prepare the way before me, and the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple.” Malachi 3:1

Apply: Spend some quiet time before the Blessed Sacrament this week, waiting on the Lord.

The Lord of the Temple came as a poor child, and only those who waited recognized him.

Keep awake with Simeon and Anna, and you too will see his salvation.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today the Lord enters his Temple. Forty days after his birth, Mary and Joseph bring him to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord, as the law commanded, and they offer a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons (Luke 2:24). It was the offering of the poor.

The law of Leviticus said that a mother who could not afford a lamb could bring two birds. But on that day the true Lamb was in his mother's arms. The poor offering was made for the One who would offer himself for the whole world.

Simeon, righteous and devout, has waited all his life. Guided by the Spirit he comes into the Temple, takes the child in his arms and says: master, now you are dismissing your servant in peace; for my eyes have seen your salvation (Luke 2:29–30).

Then he tells Mary that this child will be a sign that will be opposed, and that a sword will pierce her soul. Paul, in the Epistle, grieves that many stumbled over this stone. Yet Christ is the end of the law, so that there may be righteousness for everyone who believes (Romans 10:4).

Anna, eighty-four years old, worships night and day with fasting and prayer, and speaks about the child to all who are waiting. Isaiah had promised a Servant who would be a light to the nations. Simeon and Anna see that light in a baby.

On this World Day for Consecrated Life let us thank God for our religious sisters, brothers and priests, presented to God like the child in the Temple. And let us keep awake, like Simeon and Anna, so that when the Lord comes we may recognize him and say: my eyes have seen your salvation. Amen.

For children

Children, when Jesus was a tiny baby, Mary and Joseph took him to the Temple to give him to God. Old Simeon held him and was so happy!

Each morning you can also say: Jesus, I give you my day!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, firstborn Son of the Father, you were carried into your Temple and offered with the gift of the poor. Let us, like Simeon, take you into our arms with faith, and like Anna, wait for you with fasting and prayer. Shine on all the nations as the light of revelation, strengthen your mother's children when the sword pierces their hearts, and bless all who have consecrated their lives to you. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
