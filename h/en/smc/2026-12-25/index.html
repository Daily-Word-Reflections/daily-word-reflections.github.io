Word for the day

“But the angel said to them, “Do not be afraid; for see—I am bringing you good news of great joy for all the people: to you is born this day in the city of David a Savior, who is the Messiah, the Lord.”” Luke 2:10–11

Reflection

“To you is born this day in the city of David a Savior, who is the Messiah, the Lord.” Christmas is not a sentimental story about a baby; it is the announcement that God has become man. The eternal Son, through whom all things were made, is born of the Virgin Mary in Bethlehem, laid in a manger because there was no room in the inn.

The prophets had promised it. Isaiah: “Look, the young woman is with child and shall bear a son, and shall name him Immanuel”; and “a child has been born for us, a son given to us… Prince of Peace.” Micah: from little Bethlehem will come one “whose origin is from of old, from ancient days”, who will feed his flock and “be the one of peace.” Paul gathers it all: “When the fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, born of a woman… so that we might receive adoption as children.”

The first to hear the news are shepherds, poor and on the margins. The sign given them is humble: “a child wrapped in bands of cloth and lying in a manger.” Heaven sings, “Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace among those whom he favours.” The shepherds go “with haste”, find the child, and return glorifying God. Mary “treasured all these words and pondered them in her heart.”

The Syriac tradition loves to say that the One who feeds all creation lay in a feeding trough. Bethlehem, “house of bread”, gives us the Bread of Life. Christmas leads straight to the altar: the One born in a manger gives himself to us in the Holy Qurbana.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, a happy and holy Christmas to all of you! Tonight the angel speaks to us as he spoke to the shepherds: “Do not be afraid; I am bringing you good news of great joy for all the people: to you is born this day a Saviour, who is the Messiah, the Lord.”

This is the heart of our faith. The eternal Son of God, through whom all things were made, has become a man. He did not come as a king in a palace, but as a baby in a manger, because there was no room for him in the inn. Isaiah had promised: “A child has been born for us, a son given to us… Prince of Peace.” Micah had promised that he would come from little Bethlehem. Tonight the promises are fulfilled.

Who heard the news first? Not the powerful in Rome or Jerusalem, but poor shepherds in the fields at night. God comes first to the little, the forgotten, those working through the night. And the sign is so humble: a child wrapped in cloths, lying in a manger. If we look for God in power and noise, we will miss him. He is found in humility.

Bethlehem means “house of bread”, and the manger is a feeding place. Our Syriac Fathers loved this: the Bread of Life was laid where food is placed. In a few moments that same Lord will be on our altar, and we will receive him. That is our Christmas: Christ born in us.

So let us do what the shepherds did: go with haste, adore him, and then go back glorifying God and telling others. And let us do what Mary did: treasure these things in our hearts. May the Prince of Peace bring peace to your homes, to our country and to the whole world. A blessed Christmas to you all! Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, a joyful and blessed Christmas to all of you! Tonight let us go in spirit to a small town in Judea. Its name was Bethlehem. In Hebrew, Bethlehem means house of bread. It was there, and nowhere else, that God chose to be born.

The prophet Micah had foretold it: from you, Bethlehem, small among the clans of Judah, shall come forth one who is to rule in Israel (Micah 5:2). God plans with care. The One who would say I am the bread of life (John 6:35) was born in the house of bread.

And where did Mary lay Him? In a manger (Luke 2:7). We have made the crib beautiful with lights and decorations, but a manger was a feeding trough, the place where animals came to eat. The prophet Isaiah had said that the ox knows its owner and the donkey its master's crib, but Israel does not understand (Isaiah 1:3). The Son of God was placed where food is placed. From His first night on earth He was saying: I have come to be your food.

That is why Christmas leads us to the altar. In a few moments, on this altar, the same Christ born in Bethlehem will become present under the appearance of bread. The shepherds knelt at the manger; we kneel before the Holy Qurbana. They saw a child; we see bread. In both cases faith sees God hidden in humility. Receive Him tonight with the faith of the shepherds.

And the shepherds did not keep the good news to themselves. They told everyone what they had heard (Luke 2:17). Bread is meant to be shared. This Christmas, share your bread with someone who is hungry, lonely or forgotten: a meal, a visit, a phone call. Then Christmas will not end tonight. It will continue in every house where Christ, the Bread of Life, is received and shared.

Homily option 3 · Teaching homily: the Incarnation, true God and true man

Christmas is not only a sweet story. It is the heart of our faith: God became man.

1. The Word became flesh

  • The eternal Son took a human nature (John 1:14).
  • Not a phantom, not a half-god.
  • For us and for our salvation (Nicene Creed).
📖 Isaiah 7:14; 9:6
📖 John 1:1–14

Apply: Bow deeply at the words of the Creed: and became man.

2. True God and true man

  • One divine Person, two natures (CCC 464–469).
  • Councils of Nicaea, Ephesus, Chalcedon.
  • Mary is truly Mother of God, Theotokos (CCC 495).
📖 Isaiah 9:6–7
📖 Philippians 2:5–11

Apply: Call Mary Mother of God with faith in the Hail Mary.

3. Why God became man

  • To reconcile us with God (CCC 457).
  • To show us God's love (CCC 458).
  • To be our model of holiness and to make us sharers in the divine nature (CCC 459–460).
📖 Exodus 34:6
📖 2 Peter 1:4

Apply: Thank God that He came to make you His child.

The Incarnation is the greatest proof of God's love.

God became man so that we might share His divine life.

Take-home resolution: This Christmas I will bow at the words of the Creed and thank God for becoming man.

Dear brothers and sisters, a blessed Christmas to you all! We have heard the familiar story again: the journey, the manger, the shepherds. But behind the story is the most astonishing truth ever proclaimed: God became man. Tonight let us look at this mystery as the Church teaches it.

St John writes: in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; and the Word became flesh and lived among us (John 1:1, 14). The Child in the manger is not a good man whom God later adopted, nor a god who only seemed human. He is the eternal Son of God who took to Himself a full human nature, body and soul, in the womb of the Virgin Mary. That is why, in the Creed, at the words and became man, we bow; on Christmas day, we kneel.

The early Church had to defend this truth against many errors. The Councils of Nicaea, Ephesus and Chalcedon taught that Jesus Christ is one divine Person in two natures, divine and human, without confusion and without separation (CCC 464–469). Because the Child is God, the Council of Ephesus proclaimed Mary Theotokos, Mother of God (CCC 495). This is not an exaggeration about Mary; it is a protection of the truth about Jesus.

Why did God become man? The Catechism gives four reasons. To save us by reconciling us with God. So that we might know God's love. To be our model of holiness. And to make us partakers of the divine nature (CCC 457–460; 2 Peter 1:4). The Fathers of the Church summed it up: the Son of God became a son of man so that we might become children of God.

So Christmas is not only for children; it is the foundation of our whole faith. At this Holy Qurbana the same Word made flesh comes to us. Let us receive Him with wonder, and let His love, so humble and so close, change the way we live.
