Word for the day

“And hope does not disappoint us, because God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit that has been given to us.” Romans 5:5

Reflection

One week after Pentecost the Church lifts her eyes from all that God has done to who God is. In the Season of the Apostles we have recalled the coming of the Spirit, the first healing in the Name of Jesus, the Church as one Body. Today we adore the source of it all: one God, Father, Son and Holy Spirit. The Catechism calls the mystery of the Most Holy Trinity “the central mystery of Christian faith and life”, the mystery of God in himself and the light that shines on every other mystery (CCC 234). We did not invent it, and no philosopher discovered it. God revealed it by acting: the Father sent the Son, and the Father and the Son sent the Spirit. This is why our faith begins and ends in the Name. We were baptized “in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (Matthew 28:19); we sign ourselves with the Cross in that Name; every Holy Qurbana begins and ends with the glory of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, and the Church of the East has always loved to sing it. The Trinity is not a puzzle for scholars. It is the family into which we have been born again.

The readings for the feast begin under the oaks of Mamre. “The Lord appeared to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre, as he sat at the entrance of his tent in the heat of the day. He looked up and saw three men standing near him.” Abraham runs to meet them, bows to the ground, and calls them “My lord”, in the singular. He washes their feet, Sarah bakes cakes from the finest flour, a tender calf is prepared, “and he stood by them under the tree while they ate.” The text does not say that Abraham saw the Trinity; the Old Testament does not yet reveal the three Persons. But the Fathers of the Church, reading this page in the light of Christ, saw in the three visitors who speak as one Lord a hint of the mystery to come, and the Christian East has painted it again and again as the icon of the Hospitality of Abraham. Two lessons stay with us. God comes as a guest, and he is welcomed by hospitality: “some have entertained angels without knowing it” (Hebrews 13:2). And the God who is three Persons in one communion of love is himself, in a sense, eternal welcome: the Father gives everything to the Son, the Son returns everything to the Father, and, as St Augustine taught, the Spirit is the love between them.

Isaiah gives us the voice of the one God who is “the first” and “the last”. “Listen to me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I called: I am He; I am the first, and I am the last.” His hand laid the foundation of the earth and spread out the heavens. And then a striking line: “And now the Lord GOD has sent me and his spirit.” The Fathers heard in it the Son speaking, sent by the Father with the Spirit. Whether or not the prophet saw so far, the Church reads this oracle today because it shows how God has always revealed himself: as the One who calls, who sends and who teaches. The reading ends with a tender promise: “I am the LORD your God, who teaches you for your own good, who leads you in the way you should go.” The doctrine of the Trinity is not cold. It tells us that the God who made the heavens wants to walk with us on the road.

Paul, writing to Rome, shows the Trinity at work in the life of every believer. “Therefore, since we are justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have obtained access to this grace in which we stand.” Peace with the Father, access through the Son. And then the third Person: “hope does not disappoint us, because God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit that has been given to us.” Notice that this happens in the middle of suffering: “we also boast in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces endurance, and endurance produces character, and character produces hope.” The Trinity does not float above our troubles. The Father reconciles us, the Son opens the door, and the Spirit pours love into hearts that are hurting. The Catechism uses this very verse to say that God is love, and love is his first gift, containing all the others (CCC 733).

In the Gospel Jesus, on the night before he died, tells the apostles: “I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now. When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth.” And he describes the life of God in three short sentences: the Spirit “will glorify me, because he will take what is mine and declare it to you. All that the Father has is mine.” Everything the Father has, he gives to the Son; everything of the Son, the Spirit makes known to us. The Church professes one God in three Persons, really distinct, yet one in nature, each wholly God (CCC 253–255). The Sunday readings, also set for today, show the same Trinity in our salvation: Paul tells the Corinthians, “you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and in the Spirit of our God” (1 Corinthians 6:11); Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, says that “there is no other name under heaven” (Acts 4:12); and the woman at Simon's table weeps at the feet of Jesus and hears, “Your sins are forgiven.” The Trinity is not far away. We are invited into it, and Jesus promises: “Those who love me will keep my word, and my Father will love them, and we will come to them and make our home with them” (John 14:23). The goal of our whole life is to enter the perfect unity of the Blessed Trinity (CCC 260).

Homily outline

Theme: “God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit that has been given to us.”

The feast of the Most Holy Trinity: the three visitors at Mamre, the God who is first and last, the love of the Father poured out through the Son and the Spirit, and the Spirit of truth who takes what is the Son's and gives it to us.

1. Three men at the oaks of Mamre

  • Abraham at the door of his tent.
  • Three visitors addressed as one Lord.
  • God the guest, welcomed by hospitality.
“He looked up and saw three men standing near him. When he saw them, he ran from the tent entrance to meet them, and bowed down to the ground.” Genesis 18:2
“Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing that some have entertained angels without knowing it.” Hebrews 13:2

Apply: Invite one lonely or forgotten person to share a meal in your home this week.

2. I am the first, and I am the last

  • The Creator who laid the foundations of the earth.
  • The One who calls and sends.
  • The God who teaches and leads.
“Listen to me, O Jacob, and Israel, whom I called: I am He; I am the first, and I am the last.” Isaiah 48:12
“I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end.” Revelation 22:13

Apply: Begin each day this week by asking the Father to lead you in the way you should go.

3. God's love poured into our hearts

  • Peace with God through Christ.
  • Suffering that produces hope.
  • The Spirit, the love of God given to us.
“A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you; and I will remove from your body the heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh.” Ezekiel 36:26
“And hope does not disappoint us, because God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit that has been given to us.” Romans 5:5

Apply: Offer one present suffering to the Father, through Jesus, in the Spirit, and ask that it may produce hope.

4. All that the Father has is mine

  • Things the disciples could not yet bear.
  • The Spirit of truth who guides into all truth.
  • The life of God shared with us.
“The spirit of the LORD shall rest on him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and the fear of the LORD.” Isaiah 11:2
“All that the Father has is mine. For this reason I said that he will take what is mine and declare it to you.” John 16:15

Apply: Make the sign of the Cross slowly each morning and evening, and pray the Glory Be with your family.

The Trinity is not a distant puzzle but the life of God into which we were baptized: the Father loves us, the Son opens the way, the Spirit pours love into our hearts.

Let us welcome God in every guest, trust him in every suffering, and let the Spirit guide us into all the truth.

Take-home resolution: This week, make the sign of the Cross with attention, welcome one guest as Abraham did, and pray the Glory Be at the beginning and end of each day.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear friends, today, the Second Sunday of the Apostles, our Church celebrates the feast of the Most Holy Trinity: one God, the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

We meet this mystery every day. We were baptized in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. We make the sign of the Cross in that Name. Every Holy Qurbana begins and ends with the glory of the Three.

In Genesis the Lord appears to Abraham at Mamre as three visitors. Abraham runs to meet them, washes their feet and serves them a meal. The Fathers of the Church saw in this scene a sign of the Trinity. God comes to us as a guest, and we meet him when we welcome others.

Isaiah hears God say, “I am the first, and I am the last,” and, “I am the LORD your God, who teaches you for your own good, who leads you in the way you should go.” God is not far away; he leads us on our road.

Paul shows the Three at work in us: we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ, and “God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit.” Even our sufferings, Paul says, can produce hope.

Jesus says that the Spirit will take what is his and give it to us, and “All that the Father has is mine.” The Father gives all to the Son, the Spirit shares it with us. That is the life of God, and we are invited into it. Let us live today as children of the Father, brothers and sisters of the Son, temples of the Holy Spirit. Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, today we celebrate the Most Holy Trinity. When we were baptised, the priest did not say: in the names. He said: in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit (Matthew 28:19). One Name, three Persons. Our whole life as Christians is lived inside that Name.

God told Moses to bless the people with the words: the LORD bless you and keep you; the LORD make his face to shine upon you; the LORD lift up his countenance upon you, and give you peace. Then he added: so they shall put my name on the Israelites, and I will bless them (Numbers 6:24–27). In Baptism God's Name was placed upon us. When parents sign their children with the cross at night, they put that Name on them again.

Israel's great prayer says: hear, O Israel: the LORD is our God, the LORD alone (Deuteronomy 6:4). We Christians do not believe in three gods. Paul says: for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things (1 Corinthians 8:6). And the Spirit is the Lord, the giver of life. One God in three Persons, equal in glory, one in being.

Isaiah saw the Lord on his throne, and the seraphs called to one another: holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts (Isaiah 6:3). John heard the same song in heaven (Revelation 4:8). In every Holy Qurbana we join that song. We do not fully understand the Trinity; we adore.

So this week let us bless our children each night with the sign of the cross. Let us pray the Creed slowly, phrase by phrase. And at the Holy Qurbana let us sing the Holy, holy, holy with full hearts, as people who bear the Name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

Homily option 3 · A hope that does not disappoint

Paul's words in today's Epistle were written for people who suffer. The Father gives peace through the Son, and the Spirit pours love into our hearts, so that even suffering can become hope.

1. Peace with God

  • Justified by faith.
  • Peace through our Lord Jesus Christ.
  • The effect of righteousness will be peace.
📖 Isaiah 32:17
📖 Romans 5:1–2

Apply: Make your peace with God in confession if you have been away.

2. Suffering produces endurance

  • We boast in our sufferings.
  • Endurance, character, hope.
  • Gold tested in fire.
📖 Job 23:10
📖 Romans 5:3–4

Apply: Offer one present suffering to God with trust.

3. Hope does not disappoint

  • Those who wait for the Lord shall not be put to shame.
  • The Spirit has been given to us.
  • Hope rests on love already received.
📖 Isaiah 49:23
📖 Romans 5:5

Apply: Visit or call someone who is suffering and bring them hope.

The Trinity is not a distant puzzle but the God who holds us in suffering.

Father, Son and Spirit turn our trials into hope.

Take-home resolution: This week I will offer one suffering to God and bring hope to someone who is suffering.

Dear friends, today's feast can seem like a difficult doctrine for theologians. But listen to how Paul speaks of the Trinity in today's Epistle. He is not writing a textbook. He is writing to people who suffer.

He begins: since we are justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ (Romans 5:1). The Father is not our enemy. Through his Son he has made peace with us. Isaiah said: the effect of righteousness will be peace (Isaiah 32:17). If we have been far from God, the door of confession is open.

Then Paul says something surprising: we also boast in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces endurance, and endurance produces character, and character produces hope (Romans 5:3–4). Job, in his pain, said: when he has tested me, I shall come out like gold (Job 23:10). Suffering in itself is not good. But in God's hands it can shape us, like gold in the fire.

And hope does not disappoint us, because God's love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit that has been given to us (Romans 5:5). God promised through Isaiah: those who wait for me shall not be put to shame (Isaiah 49:23). Our hope is not wishful thinking. It rests on the love of the Father, shown in the Son, and poured into us by the Spirit.

So the Trinity is not far from our kitchens and hospital beds. The Father holds us, the Son walks with us, the Spirit comforts us. Let us offer one suffering to God this week with trust, and let us bring hope to someone who is suffering, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

For children

Children, God is one, but God is three Persons: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. That is a big mystery, bigger than the sky! Even grown-ups cannot understand it fully.

But you can pray to the Holy Trinity every day. Make the sign of the Cross slowly: In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen. Each time, you are saying: I belong to God's family!

Prayer

Holy Father, almighty God, you sent your only Son, the Word of truth, and your Holy Spirit, the giver of life, to make known to us the wonderful mystery of your being. We adore you, one God in three Persons, the first and the last, who teaches us and leads us on the way. As Abraham welcomed you under the oaks of Mamre, may we welcome you in every guest and stranger. Pour your love into our hearts through the Holy Spirit, that our sufferings may become hope that does not disappoint. Lord Jesus, all that the Father has is yours; let your Spirit guide us into all the truth and make our hearts your home. To you, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, be glory, honour and thanksgiving, now and always and for ever and ever. Amen.
