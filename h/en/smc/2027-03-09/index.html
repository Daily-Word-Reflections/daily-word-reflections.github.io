Word for the day

“Go and do likewise.” Luke 10:37

Reflection

Paul is collecting money. For years he gathered a collection from the Gentile churches of Greece and Asia for the poor believers in Jerusalem, and in this chapter he encourages the Corinthians to finish what they had promised. He does not scold; he speaks of God as a farmer who supplies the farmer: “He who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed for sowing and increase the harvest of your righteousness.” Giving is sowing, and God is the one who makes it grow. “You will be enriched in every way for your great generosity, which will produce thanksgiving to God through us.” What is remarkable is where Paul's eyes rest. The collection will feed the hungry, but it will do more: it will make the poor of Jerusalem give thanks to God, and pray for the Gentiles who once seemed strangers. The gift builds the one Church: “they long for you and pray for you because of the surpassing grace of God that he has given you.” And then, as if all talk of money had brought him to the greatest Gift of all, Paul breaks into praise: “Thanks be to God for his indescribable gift!” The Great Fast has always had three companions: prayer, fasting and almsgiving. Almsgiving here is not a tax paid to God, but seed sown in his field, which returns as thanksgiving and communion.

The Gospel takes us to the road from Jerusalem down to Jericho, a steep and lonely road where robbers waited. A man lies there half dead. A priest and a Levite pass by on the other side. “But a Samaritan while traveling came near him; and when he saw him, he was moved with pity.” Jesus' hearers did not expect the hero to be a Samaritan, a member of a people with whom they had long quarrelled. Everything the Samaritan does is concrete: “He went to him and bandaged his wounds, having poured oil and wine on them. Then he put him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and took care of him.” He pays two denarii, about two days' wages, and promises more. The lawyer had asked, “who is my neighbor?”, a question about where to draw the line. Jesus turns it round: “Which of these three, do you think, was a neighbor to the man who fell into the hands of the robbers?” “The one who showed him mercy.” “Go and do likewise.” The Fathers saw Christ himself in the Samaritan, who came down to our road, poured the oil and wine of the sacraments on our wounds, and brought us to the inn of the Church. The Latin Church today remembers St Frances of Rome, a noblewoman, wife and mother who, through famine and plague, turned her family's house into a storehouse and hospital for the poor of Rome. She did not leave her home or her marriage to be a neighbour; she opened them.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul tells the Corinthians that God supplies seed to the sower and bread for food, and will multiply the harvest of our righteousness (2 Corinthians 9:10). When we give alms in this Great Fast, we are not paying God a tax. We are sowing in his field.

And the harvest is more than bread for the hungry: it is thanksgiving to God, and prayer and love between people who were strangers. That is why Paul ends with a cry of praise: thanks be to God for his indescribable gift! (2 Corinthians 9:15).

In the Gospel a priest and a Levite pass by a wounded man. But a Samaritan, a stranger, sees him and is moved with pity. He bandages his wounds, pours oil and wine, carries him to an inn and pays for his care (Luke 10:33–35).

Jesus says to us: go and do likewise (Luke 10:37). St Frances of Rome opened her house to the poor during famine and plague. This week, let us come near to one person we have been passing by. That is how the fast becomes mercy. Amen.

For children

Children, a man was hurt and lying on the road. Two people walked past, but a kind stranger stopped and helped him. Jesus says: be like that kind man. Who can you help this week, even in a small way?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you came down to our road, saw us half dead, and were moved with pity. You poured oil and wine on our wounds and brought us to the inn of your Church. Make us neighbours to everyone we meet; open our hands to sow generously, and our lips to give thanks for your indescribable gift. Through the prayers of St Frances of Rome, make our homes places of mercy. Amen.
