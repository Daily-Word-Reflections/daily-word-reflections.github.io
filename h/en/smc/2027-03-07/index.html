Fifth Sunday of the Great Fast

7 March 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading Genesis 16:6–16 · Hagar and the God who sees
  • Second Reading Joshua 9:16–27 · Hewers of wood and drawers of water
  • Epistle Romans 12:1–11 · A living sacrifice
  • Gospel John 7:37–39; 8:12–20 · Rivers of living water and the light of the world

Word for the day

““Let anyone who is thirsty come to me, and let the one who believes in me drink. As the scripture has said, ‘Out of the believer's heart shall flow rivers of living water.’”” John 7:37–38

Reflection

The first reading begins in a household that has gone wrong. Sarai, unable to bear children, had given her Egyptian slave-girl Hagar to Abram; Hagar conceived and looked down on her mistress; Sarai complained; and Abram washed his hands of it: “Your slave-girl is in your power; do to her as you please.” “Then Sarai dealt harshly with her, and she ran away from her.” A pregnant foreign servant, alone, runs into the desert toward Egypt. And there, of all places, “The angel of the LORD found her by a spring of water in the wilderness.” He asks her the question the Great Fast asks each of us: “Hagar, slave-girl of Sarai, where have you come from and where are you going?” He promises her a son and gives him a name, Ishmael, God hears, “for the LORD has given heed to your affliction.” Then Hagar does something no one in Scripture has done before her: she gives God a name. “So she named the LORD who spoke to her, ‘You are El-roi’; for she said, ‘Have I really seen God and remained alive after seeing him?’” El-roi, the God who sees. The first person in the Bible to name God is not a patriarch or a priest, but a runaway slave woman. Thousands of women from Kerala work as housemaids and carers far from home, some of them treated harshly behind closed doors where no one sees. God sees. And he asks us whether we see them too, in our own homes, in the women who work for us, and in the women of our families whose hurts are hidden.

The second reading finishes last Friday's story. Three days after the treaty, Israel discovers that the Gibeonites are neighbours, not travellers from afar. The congregation murmurs, but the leaders hold firm: “We have sworn to them by the LORD, the God of Israel, and now we must not touch them.” An oath made in God's name is kept, even when it was obtained by a trick and even when it costs. The psalm praises the one “who stand by their oath even to their hurt” (Psalm 15:4). Our marriage vows, our baptismal promises and our word given in business are to be kept the same way. And notice how the story ends. The Gibeonites are spared, and Joshua makes them “hewers of wood and drawers of water for the congregation and for the altar of the LORD.” The outsiders who came with mouldy bread end their story beside the altar of the God of Israel. Christian readers have seen in this wood and water carried to the altar a hint of the wood of the Cross and the water of Baptism, by which God brings strangers near to his altar.

Paul now turns from the mercy of God to the life it makes possible: “I appeal to you therefore, brothers and sisters, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship. Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your minds.” This is what the Great Fast is for. We do not fast because the body is bad, but because the body is to be offered: eyes, tongue, hands, appetite and time placed on the altar with Christ. And the sacrifice is not private. “For as in one body we have many members, and not all the members have the same function, so we, who are many, are one body in Christ, and individually we are members one of another.” Paul ends with a list that could hang on the wall of every Christian home: “Let love be genuine; hate what is evil, hold fast to what is good; love one another with mutual affection; outdo one another in showing honor. Do not lag in zeal, be ardent in spirit, serve the Lord.”

The Gospel takes us to the festival of Booths in Jerusalem. During the feast, as Jewish tradition describes it, water was carried in procession from the pool of Siloam and poured out at the altar, and great lamps were lit in the temple courts so that the whole city glowed. Into that water and that light Jesus speaks. “On the last day of the festival, the great day, while Jesus was standing there, he cried out, ‘Let anyone who is thirsty come to me, and let the one who believes in me drink.’” John explains: “Now he said this about the Spirit, which believers in him were to receive.” Then, in the treasury near the great lamps, he says: “I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.” Water and light: these are the two signs of our Baptism, the Mamodisa, when we were washed in water and a burning candle was placed in our hands. At the Easter Vigil the Church will bless the water and carry the new light into a dark church, and those preparing for Baptism will receive both. Hagar found a spring in the desert and met the God who sees; the Gibeonites carried water to the altar; Christ himself is the spring from which rivers of living water flow, and the light that the darkness cannot overcome. His hour “had not yet come”, but it is coming: on the Cross water will flow from his side, and on Easter morning the light will rise.

Homily outline

Theme: “Let anyone who is thirsty come to me, and let the one who believes in me drink.”

Hagar and the God who sees, an oath kept and strangers brought to the altar, the living sacrifice, and Christ the living water and the light of the world.

1. The God who sees

  • A runaway slave in the desert.
  • Where have you come from, where are you going?
  • The first to name God.
“So she named the LORD who spoke to her, “You are El-roi”; for she said, “Have I really seen God and remained alive after seeing him?”” Genesis 16:13
“But whenever you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you.” Matthew 6:6

Apply: Notice one person who serves you unseen; thank them by name and pay them justly.

2. Wood and water for the altar

  • A treaty obtained by a trick.
  • An oath kept even to one's hurt.
  • Strangers brought near.
“But on that day Joshua made them hewers of wood and drawers of water for the congregation and for the altar of the LORD, to continue to this day, in the place that he should choose.” Joshua 9:27
“For the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.”” Mark 10:45

Apply: Keep one promise you have delayed, and offer one humble service in your parish.

3. A living sacrifice

  • By the mercies of God.
  • Bodies offered, minds renewed.
  • Members one of another.
“The sacrifice acceptable to God is a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise.” Psalm 51:17
“I appeal to you therefore, brothers and sisters, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship.” Romans 12:1

Apply: Choose one discipline of eyes, tongue or hands and offer it each morning this week.

4. Living water and the light of the world

  • Water poured at the altar.
  • Lamps that lit the city.
  • The signs of our Baptism.
“With joy you will draw water from the wells of salvation.” Isaiah 12:3
“Again Jesus spoke to them, saying, “I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.”” John 8:12

Apply: Light a candle at home on the anniversary of your Baptism, or this Sunday evening, and renew your baptismal promises as a family.

The God who saw Hagar sees us; the God who brought the Gibeonites to his altar brings us near; the Son pours out the Spirit like rivers and shines as the light of the world.

Let us see the unseen, keep our word, offer our bodies, and come thirsty to Christ.

Take-home resolution: This week, thank one unseen servant by name, keep one delayed promise, and renew your baptismal promises with a lit candle.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, Hagar was a servant, a foreigner and a pregnant woman, treated harshly and alone in the desert. But the angel of the Lord found her by a spring of water and asked: where have you come from and where are you going? (Genesis 16:8). That is the question of the Great Fast for each of us.

Hagar gave God a name: El-roi, the God who sees (Genesis 16:13). The first person in the Bible to name God was a runaway slave woman. God sees those whom no one sees. Let us ask him to give us his eyes for the servants, the migrants and the hidden sufferers around us.

Israel discovered that the Gibeonites had tricked them, yet the leaders said: we have sworn to them by the Lord, and we must not touch them (Joshua 9:19). They kept their word even when it cost. Our vows and promises must be kept the same way. And the outsiders ended up carrying wood and water for the altar of the Lord: God brings strangers near.

Paul says: present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God (Romans 12:1). That is why we fast. Our eyes, tongue, hands and time are placed on the altar with Christ, and we are transformed by the renewing of our minds.

At the feast of Booths, when water was poured at the altar and great lamps lit up the temple, Jesus cried out: let anyone who is thirsty come to me; and he said: I am the light of the world (John 7:37; 8:12). Water and light are the signs of our Baptism.

This week, let us see one person we have overlooked, keep one promise we have delayed, offer one discipline as a living sacrifice, and come thirsty to Jesus. At Easter he will give us the living water and the light. Amen.

For children

Children, Hagar was alone in the desert and thought nobody cared. But God found her and she called him: the God who sees me! God sees you too, when you are happy and when you are sad.

Jesus said: if you are thirsty, come to me. He is like a spring of fresh water and a bright light in the dark. When you see water or a candle this week, remember Jesus!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, spring of living water and light of the world, you are El-roi, the God who saw Hagar in the desert and who sees us now. See the hidden sufferings of the poor, the servants and the strangers, and teach us to see them with your eyes. Make us faithful to our word, bring near to your altar all who are far away, and receive our bodies as a living sacrifice. Pour out your Spirit like rivers in our hearts, and let us walk in your light until we come to the light of Easter. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
