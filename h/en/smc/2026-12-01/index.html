Readings

Word for the day

“Because you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their time, you will become mute, unable to speak, until the day these things occur.” Luke 1:20

Reflection

Paul's call fits the beginning of the fast: “You know what time it is, how it is now the moment for you to wake from sleep. For salvation is nearer to us now than when we became believers; the night is far gone, the day is near. Let us then lay aside the works of darkness and put on the armour of light… put on the Lord Jesus Christ.”

Zechariah asks for a sign: “How will I know that this is so?” Gabriel's answer is both a rebuke and a gift: he will be silent until the promise is fulfilled. His nine months of silence become a retreat. During this fast, a little silence, from screens, gossip and noise, can prepare us to speak praise at Christmas.

Homily outline

Theme: “Wake up, and be silent before the Word.”

Paul calls us to wake; Zechariah's doubt becomes a fruitful silence.

1. The messenger prepares the way

  • Malachi promised a messenger before the Lord.
  • Gabriel announces that messenger: John.
“See, I am sending my messenger to prepare the way before me, and the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple.” Malachi 3:1
“The angel replied, “I am Gabriel. I stand in the presence of God, and I have been sent to speak to you and to bring you this good news.”” Luke 1:19

Apply: Listen today for a message God may be sending.

2. Doubt and silence

  • Zechariah asks for proof.
  • Silence becomes his school of faith.
“Can a child be born to a man who is a hundred years old?” Genesis 17:17
“Zechariah said to the angel, “How will I know that this is so? For I am an old man, and my wife is getting on in years.”” Luke 1:18

Apply: Choose one daily silence during the fast.

3. The night is far gone

  • Salvation is nearer than when we first believed.
  • Put on the Lord Jesus Christ.
“The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light.” Isaiah 9:2
“Because you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their time, you will become mute, unable to speak, until the day these things occur.” Luke 1:20

Apply: Lay aside one “work of darkness” today.

Salvation is near; wake up.

Let silence prepare us for praise.

Take-home resolution: Keep a daily hour of silence during the 25 Days' Fast.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today our Church begins the twenty-five-day fast before Christmas, the Nombu that our ancestors kept with great seriousness. In many Kerala homes this fast still means no meat, simpler meals, more prayer and more almsgiving. But why do we fast before a feast? Because a great feast needs a prepared heart.

St Paul gives us the reason: you know what time it is; now is the moment to wake from sleep; salvation is nearer to us now than when we became believers; the night is far gone, the day is near (Romans 13:11–12). Christian life is a waking up. We can spend years half asleep: going through religious customs without noticing the Lord who comes.

Paul is very practical about what waking up means. Let us lay aside the works of darkness: not in drunkenness, not in quarrelling and jealousy. Instead, put on the Lord Jesus Christ (Romans 13:13–14). The fast from food is meant to strengthen a deeper fast: from sin, from anger at home, from the bottle, from the phone that steals our evenings.

In the Gospel the angel Gabriel tells the old priest Zechariah that his long prayer has been heard: Elizabeth will bear a son. But Zechariah asks: how will I know this? I am old. And because he does not believe, he becomes unable to speak until the promise is fulfilled (Luke 1:18–20).

Zechariah's silence was a correction, but also a gift. For nine months he could not talk; he could only listen, watch and pray. When his son was born, his first words were not complaints but the great song of praise, the Benedictus. Silence prepared his heart to sing.

This Nombu, let us include some silence. Less noise, less screen, less gossip. More time in the church, more family prayer, more reading of the Gospel. Then, like Zechariah, we will have a song ready when Christmas comes.

Let us make three simple resolutions today: a fast from one food, a fast from one sin, and a daily act of charity. And let us ask Mary, who carried the Word in silence, to help us keep this fast with joy. Amen.

For children

Children, the fast before Christmas is like waking up early to get ready for a big feast. Give up one small thing for Jesus this month!

Prayer

Lord, wake me from sleep and clothe me with your light. Forgive my doubts, and let the silence of these days prepare my heart to sing your praise. Amen.
