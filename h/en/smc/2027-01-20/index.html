  • Second Reading Jonah 3:1–10 · The fasting and prayer of Nineveh
  • Epistle Romans 9:19–29 · God shows mercy: objects of mercy from every people

Word for the day

“When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil ways, God changed his mind about the calamity that he had said he would bring upon them; and he did not do it.” Jonah 3:10

Reflection

On the last day of the fast we read the story that gave it its name. Jonah walks into the great city with a short warning: “Forty days more, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!” And “the people of Nineveh believed God; they proclaimed a fast, and everyone, great and small, put on sackcloth.” The king leaves his throne, sits in ashes and says: “Who knows? God may relent and change his mind”. “When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil ways, God changed his mind about the calamity”. Joel shows the same scene in Jerusalem: “Blow the trumpet in Zion; sanctify a fast; call a solemn assembly”; gather the old and the children, “even infants at the breast”, and let the priests weep between the vestibule and the altar: “Spare your people, O LORD”. Then “the LORD became jealous for his land, and had pity on his people.” This is Moonnu Nombu: a whole people, young and old, together before the altar.

Paul uses the image of the potter to say that all depends on God's mercy, and that he gathers “objects of mercy” not only from Israel but from the Gentiles: “Those who were not my people I will call ‘my people’”. Nineveh, a pagan city that repented, was an early sign of it. Jesus adds a warning so that our fast does not end in a show: “Beware of practicing your piety before others in order to be seen by them”. When we give alms, the left hand must not know what the right hand is doing, and “your Father who sees in secret will reward you.” Today we also honour St Sebastian, much loved in Kerala, and pray for our bishops who bear his name. Tradition remembers him as an officer who quietly strengthened Christians in prison before he confessed Christ openly with his blood. Let our fast end with mercy done in secret and faith confessed without fear.

Homily outline

Theme: “God changed his mind… and he did not do it.”

The fast of the Ninevites ends: a whole people turns, God has pity, and mercy is done in secret.

1. Sanctify a fast

  • The trumpet in Zion.
  • Old and young together.
  • Good done in secret.
“Blow the trumpet in Zion; sanctify a fast; call a solemn assembly; gather the people. Sanctify the congregation; assemble the aged; gather the children, even infants at the breast. Let the bridegroom leave his room, and the bride her canopy.” Joel 2:15–16
“But when you give alms, do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing, so that your alms may be done in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you.” Matthew 6:3–4

Apply: Give what you saved by fasting, in secret.

2. Who knows? God may relent

  • Nineveh believes.
  • The king in ashes.
  • God has pity.
“When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil ways, God changed his mind about the calamity that he had said he would bring upon them; and he did not do it.” Jonah 3:10
“The people of Nineveh will rise up at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, because they repented at the proclamation of Jonah, and see, something greater than Jonah is here!” Matthew 12:41

Apply: Turn from one specific sin as the fast ends.

3. Acknowledge me (St Sebastian)

  • The soldier of Christ.
  • Fearless witness.
  • Christ acknowledges his own.
“But I said, “I have labored in vain, I have spent my strength for nothing and vanity; yet surely my cause is with the LORD, and my reward with my God.”” Isaiah 49:4
““Everyone therefore who acknowledges me before others, I also will acknowledge before my Father in heaven;” Matthew 10:32

Apply: Do not hide your faith this week.

God had pity on Nineveh, and he has pity on us.

End the fast with mercy in secret and faith without fear.

Take-home resolution: Break the fast with an act of hidden charity for someone in need.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the last day of our fast we read the story that gave it its name. Jonah walks into Nineveh and cries: forty days more, and Nineveh shall be overthrown! And the people of Nineveh believed God; they proclaimed a fast, and everyone, great and small, put on sackcloth (Jonah 3:5).

The king leaves his throne and sits in ashes. He says: who knows? God may relent. And when God saw how they turned from their evil ways, God changed his mind about the calamity, and he did not do it (Jonah 3:10). Joel shows the same: blow the trumpet, sanctify a fast, gather even the infants, let the priests weep at the altar: spare your people, O LORD. And the LORD had pity on his people (Joel 2:15–18).

Paul says God gathers objects of mercy from every nation: those who were not my people I will call my people (Romans 9:25). Nineveh was a pagan city, and God had pity on it. No one is outside his mercy.

Jesus warns us not to spoil the fast at the end. Beware of practicing your piety before others in order to be seen by them. When you give alms, let your left hand not know what your right hand is doing, and your Father who sees in secret will reward you (Matthew 6:1–4). Let us give quietly what we have saved by fasting.

Today we honour St Sebastian and pray for our bishops who bear his name. He served Christ quietly among the soldiers, and when the time came he confessed him openly, without fear. Let us end this fast the same way: mercy in secret, faith without fear. Amen.

For children

Children, the whole city of Nineveh said sorry to God, even the king and the little children, and God forgave them! Today, do one kind thing for someone without telling anybody. Only God will know.

Prayer

Merciful God, when Nineveh fasted and turned from evil, you had pity and did not do the evil you had threatened. Look on your people at the end of these three days; spare us, Lord, and make us objects of your mercy. Teach us to do good in secret, and through the prayers of St Sebastian make us brave in confessing your name. Amen.
