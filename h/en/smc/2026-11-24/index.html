Word for the day

“I have other sheep that do not belong to this fold. I must bring them also, and they will listen to my voice. So there will be one flock, one shepherd.” John 10:16

Reflection

Peter applies to the baptised the titles God gave Israel at Sinai: “a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God's own people,” called “out of darkness into his marvelous light.” Once we were “not a people”; now we are God's people.

Jesus is the gate and the Good Shepherd who lays down his life for the sheep. He has “other sheep that do not belong to this fold”; his desire is “one flock, one shepherd.” As the year ends the Church prays for her unity and for all who do not yet know the Shepherd's voice.

Full homily

Dear friends, Peter gives to the baptised the titles God gave Israel at Sinai: you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God's own people, in order that you may proclaim the mighty acts of him who called you out of darkness into his marvellous light (1 Peter 2:9). Once you were not a people; now you are God's people.

Jesus is the gate for the sheep: I came that they may have life, and have it abundantly. I have other sheep that do not belong to this fold; I must bring them also, so there will be one flock, one shepherd (John 10:10, 16).

Royal priesthood means that our whole life is an offering to God: work, family, sufferings. Today, at the Holy Qurbana or in prayer at home, offer your whole day as a gift, and invite back into the flock one sheep who has drifted away. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is the good shepherd who wants to gather all his sheep together. Today invite a friend who is alone to join your game!

Prayer

Good Shepherd, you laid down your life for your sheep. Gather all your scattered children into one flock, and make us a holy people who proclaim your marvellous light. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Today’s readings show who belongs to God’s people and how. Peter tells the baptised that they are living stones built into a spiritual house, “a royal priesthood”. In John, Jesus is the gate of the sheepfold and the good shepherd who lays down his life and gathers “one flock”. The Church is both a house of living stones and a flock gathered by one Shepherd.

1 Peter 2:1–10 · Living stones, a royal priesthood

Setting.

The letter is addressed “to the exiles of the Dispersion” in five regions of Asia Minor (1:1): Christians, mostly of Gentile background, who felt like strangers in their own towns. Much of its language is baptismal. Having been “born anew” (1:23), they are now told what they have become.

Structure.

  • vv. 1–3: put away malice; long for the pure spiritual milk like newborn infants.
  • vv. 4–8: come to Christ, the living stone, and be built into a spiritual house; three Old Testament texts about the stone.
  • vv. 9–10: the titles of God’s people, taken from Exodus 19:6, Isaiah 43:20–21 and Hosea.

Key words.

  • lithos zōn, living stone: A paradox: stones are dead things. Christ is the stone that was rejected and is now risen and living; joined to him, believers become living stones too.
  • basileion hierateuma, royal priesthood: From the Greek of Exodus 19:6. The whole people shares in Christ’s priesthood, offering “spiritual sacrifices”, that is, lives given to God in the Spirit.

Verse by verse.

“Like newborn infants, long for the pure, spiritual milk, so that by it you may grow into salvation” (2:2)

An image for the newly baptised. The milk is the word of God and the grace of Christ; the verb “long for” describes a hungry baby’s cry.

“like living stones, let yourselves be built into a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.” (2:5)
“But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God’s own people, in order that you may proclaim the mighty acts of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light.” (2:9)

The titles of Israel at Sinai are now given to the Church, not to replace Israel but to share in her calling. The purpose is mission: to proclaim God’s mighty acts.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism applies this passage to baptism: the baptised “have become ‘living stones’ to be ‘built into a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood’” (CCC 1268). The whole community of believers is priestly, sharing in Christ’s priesthood by baptism, while the ministerial priesthood serves this common priesthood (CCC 1546–1547; Lumen Gentium 10).

John 10:7–16 · One flock, one shepherd

Setting.

Jesus speaks after healing the man born blind, whom some leaders had driven out (John 9). His words recall Ezekiel 34, where God condemns the shepherds of Israel who fed themselves and not the flock, and promises, “I myself will search for my sheep” (34:11).

Structure.

  • vv. 7–10: “I am the gate”: whoever enters by him will find pasture and abundant life.
  • vv. 11–13: “I am the good shepherd”, contrasted with the hired hand.
  • vv. 14–16: mutual knowledge, the shepherd’s life laid down, and the other sheep to be gathered.

Key words.

  • thyra, gate: The door of the fold. Jesus is the only rightful way in for the sheep and for those who would shepherd them.
  • kalos, good: More than morally good: noble, beautiful, true. He is the real shepherd, the one a shepherd is meant to be.
  • misthōtos, hired hand: One who works for pay and has no bond with the sheep. When danger comes, he thinks of himself.

Verse by verse.

“I came that they may have life, and have it abundantly.” (10:10)

The purpose of Jesus’ coming in one line. “Life” in John is the life of God himself, given now and lasting for ever.

“The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” (10:11)

Said several times in this chapter in different forms (10:11, 15, 17–18). Ordinary shepherds risked their lives; this Shepherd gives his.

“I have other sheep that do not belong to this fold. I must bring them also, and they will listen to my voice. So there will be one flock, one shepherd.” (10:16)

The other sheep are the Gentiles who will come to faith. Unity is the Shepherd’s work and his desire.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism says that the Church is a sheepfold whose one and necessary door is Christ, and a flock of which God himself foretold that he would be the shepherd (CCC 754). St Augustine, preaching on this chapter, notes that Christ is both the door and the shepherd: he enters by himself (in substance). The words “one flock, one shepherd” stand behind the Church’s prayer and work for the unity of all Christians (Unitatis Redintegratio 1–2).

Questions people ask

What does “a royal priesthood” mean if we also have ordained priests?

All the baptised share in Christ’s priesthood by offering their lives, prayers and work to God. Ordained priests receive a different sacramental ministry to serve this priestly people in Christ’s name, above all in the Holy Qurbana. The two are related but differ in essence, not only in degree (Lumen Gentium 10).

For study and teaching

  • Peter calls Christians “living stones”. What does it mean for me to let myself be built into the parish community rather than stand apart?
  • Who are the “other sheep” in my surroundings that the Shepherd wants to gather, and how can I help?
