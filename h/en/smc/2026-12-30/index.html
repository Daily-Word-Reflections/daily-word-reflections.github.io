Word for the day

“But he said to them, “It is I; do not be afraid.”” John 6:20

Reflection

Paul, burdened by a “thorn in the flesh”, begs the Lord three times to remove it and hears: “My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness.” He learns to boast of his weakness, “so that the power of Christ may dwell in me.”

The disciples row against the wind at night; Jesus comes to them walking on the sea and says, “It is I; do not be afraid.” As the year ends, many of us look back on storms and weaknesses. The Child of Bethlehem is the Lord of the storm; he comes to us in the dark and says the same words.

Homily outline

Theme: “It is I; do not be afraid.”

Grace in weakness; Jesus walking on the sea.

1. The Lord of the waters

  • God parted the sea for Israel.
  • Jesus walks on the sea.
“Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. The LORD drove the sea back by a strong east wind all night, and turned the sea into dry land; and the waters were divided.” Exodus 14:21
“When they had rowed about three or four miles, they saw Jesus walking on the sea and coming near the boat, and they were terrified.” John 6:19

Apply: Name one storm and give it to Jesus.

2. Storm stilled

  • God hushes the waves.
  • Jesus' word calms our fear.
“He made the storm be still, and the waves of the sea were hushed.” Psalm 107:29
“But he said to them, “It is I; do not be afraid.”” John 6:20

Apply: Pray “Jesus, I trust in you” when anxious.

3. Grace is enough

  • Paul's thorn remains; grace suffices.
  • Weakness becomes the place of Christ's power.
“But those who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength.” Isaiah 40:31
“But he said to me, “My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness.”” 2 Corinthians 12:9

Apply: Offer one weakness to God.

The Lord of the storm is with us.

His grace is enough for the new year.

Take-home resolution: Enter the new year with an act of trust.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul speaks of a thorn in his flesh, a messenger of Satan to torment him. Three times he begged the Lord to take it away. But the Lord said: my grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness (2 Corinthians 12:7–9).

We do not know what Paul's thorn was: an illness, a persecution, a temptation. What we know is that God did not remove it. Instead he gave grace. Paul concludes: so I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may dwell in me; whenever I am weak, then I am strong (2 Corinthians 12:9–10).

At the end of the year, many of us carry thorns: illness that did not heal, a problem that was not solved, a prayer that was not answered as we wished. God's answer to Paul is also for us: my grace is sufficient for you.

In the Gospel the disciples are rowing across the lake at night in a strong wind. Then they see Jesus walking on the sea and coming near the boat, and they are terrified. But he says: it is I; do not be afraid (John 6:19–20).

Jesus comes to us across the storms, not always by stopping them, but by being with us in them. The Child of Bethlehem is the Lord of the waters. His words are the same tonight: it is I; do not be afraid.

As this year ends, let us bring our thorns and our storms to him. Let us thank him for the grace that carried us this year, and enter the new year not with fear, but with trust: his grace is sufficient. Amen.

For children

Children, when you are scared, remember Jesus says: it is I, do not be afraid. He is always near you!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you came to your disciples in the storm and said, “It is I; do not be afraid.” Let your grace be sufficient for me, and your strength work in my weakness. Amen.
