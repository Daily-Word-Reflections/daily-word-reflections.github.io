Word for the day

“He replied, ‘Sir, let it alone for one more year, until I dig around it and put manure on it. If it bears fruit next year, well and good; but if not, you can cut it down.’” Luke 13:8–9

Reflection

On the last day of the civil year the Church gives us a parable of mercy and urgency. A fig tree has borne no fruit for three years; the owner says, “Cut it down!” The gardener pleads: “Sir, let it alone for one more year, until I dig around it and put manure on it.” The new year is that “one more year”: a gift of patience, and a call to bear fruit.

The other readings complete the picture: atonement and reconciliation (Leviticus), the passing of all things compared to the eternal reward of the righteous (Wisdom), and Paul's charge, “There is great gain in godliness combined with contentment… Fight the good fight of the faith; take hold of the eternal life, to which you were called.” Thank God for the year, ask pardon for its sins, and begin again.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the last day of the year Jesus tells a parable. A man had a fig tree that bore no fruit for three years. He said to the gardener: cut it down! Why should it be wasting the soil? The gardener replied: sir, let it alone for one more year, until I dig around it and put manure on it. If it bears fruit next year, well and good; but if not, you can cut it down (Luke 13:6–9).

The new year is that one more year. It is a gift of God's patience. Christ, the gardener, pleads for us and works on us: digging around our roots through trials, feeding us with his word and his sacraments.

But the parable is also urgent: if not, you can cut it down. Patience is not endless. God gives us time so that we may bear fruit, not so that we may waste it.

St Paul tells Timothy: there is great gain in godliness combined with contentment; for we brought nothing into the world, so that we can take nothing out of it. Fight the good fight of the faith; take hold of the eternal life, to which you were called (1 Timothy 6:6–7, 12).

The Book of Wisdom reminds us that wealth and pride pass like a shadow, but the righteous live forever (Wisdom 5:9, 15). As the year ends, let us ask: what did I build that will last? What fruit did I bear?

Tonight, let us do three things: thank God for every grace of this year, ask pardon for its sins, and make one resolution for the new year. Let us take hold of eternal life and fight the good fight. And let us begin the new year in the arms of the Child who was born for us. Amen.

For children

Children, God gives you a whole new year! Thank Him for this year and ask Him to help you grow good next year.

Prayer

Lord of time and eternity, we thank you for every blessing of this year and ask your pardon for our sins. Give us one more year of grace, and help us bear fruit for your glory. Amen.
