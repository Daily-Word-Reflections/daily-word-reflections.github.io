Readings

  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 7:17–24 · Lead the life to which God called you
  • Gospel Luke 16:9–17 · You cannot serve God and wealth

Word for the day

“Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much; and whoever is dishonest in a very little is dishonest also in much.” Luke 16:10

Reflection

On the last day of June, and among the last days of the Season of the Apostles, Paul speaks to Christians who wondered whether becoming a disciple meant changing everything in their outward lives. Should a married convert leave an unbelieving spouse? Should a Jewish Christian hide the marks of circumcision, or a Gentile Christian receive it? Should a slave run away? Paul's rule is calm and deep: “let each of you lead the life that the Lord has assigned, to which God called you. This is my rule in all the churches.” Holiness is not found by escaping our situation but by living it with God. “Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing; but obeying the commandments of God is everything.” Three times he repeats it: remain in the condition in which you were called, and the last time he adds two words that change everything: “there remain with God.” The farmer in Wayanad, the nurse in a hospital in the Gulf, the autorickshaw driver, the mother at home with small children, the student in a hostel in Bengaluru, the old man in the parish who can no longer walk: each has a calling, and each can become a saint exactly there. The Second Vatican Council taught that the lay faithful seek the kingdom of God precisely by engaging in ordinary work and family life and ordering them according to God.

Paul's words to slaves need careful reading. In the Roman world slavery was a legal institution, and the small Christian communities had no power to abolish it. Paul does not bless it. The verse on slaves is read in two ways: Paul tells them not to be troubled by their state, and the Malayalam Bible reads that they may take their liberty when it is offered. He puts master and slave on the same level before Christ: “For whoever was called in the Lord as a slave is a freed person belonging to the Lord, just as whoever was free when called is a slave of Christ.” In his letter to Philemon he sends back the runaway Onesimus “no longer as a slave but more than a slave, a beloved brother” (Philemon 16). That seed grew, slowly, until the Church taught clearly that the enslavement of human beings is a sin against their dignity (Catechism 2414). And Paul gives a word for every age: “You were bought with a price; do not become slaves of human masters.” A Christian, bought by the Blood of Christ, must not sell himself again: not to the opinion of others, not to a political party, not to a caste prejudice, not to a bank loan taken for show, not to an addiction.

The Gospel continues the teaching that follows the parable of the dishonest manager, who used his master's money to make friends before he lost his post. Jesus draws the lesson: “make friends for yourselves by means of dishonest wealth so that when it is gone, they may welcome you into the eternal homes.” Money, which so easily becomes unjust, can be turned into friendship with the poor, and the poor will be the ones who welcome us into heaven. Then: “Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much; and whoever is dishonest in a very little is dishonest also in much.” Character is built in small things: the correct change returned, the exam without copying, the honest weighing in the shop, the hour of work actually worked. “If then you have not been faithful with the dishonest wealth, who will entrust to you the true riches?” And the decisive sentence: “No slave can serve two masters; for a slave will either hate the one and love the other, or be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and wealth.” Some of the Pharisees who heard him, Luke says, were lovers of money and ridiculed him. Jesus answers them, and us: “You are those who justify yourselves in the sight of others; but God knows your hearts; for what is prized by human beings is an abomination in the sight of God.” The warning is not for one group of long ago; it is for every heart that has learned to admire what God despises.

Our society knows this temptation well. Gold for weddings counted in sovereigns, dowry demands that crush families, houses built bigger than needed with loans that cannot be repaid, the quiet competition of parish feasts and family functions: wealth has many ways of becoming a master. Jesus does not ask us to despise money; he asks us to make it a servant, a means of friendship with the poor. Today the Latin calendar remembers the First Martyrs of the Church of Rome, the many unnamed Christians put to death under Nero after the great fire of the year 64; the Roman historian Tacitus records that a great multitude died in cruel ways. They had learned to be faithful in very little, and so they were faithful in much, even unto death; they could not serve two masters. Yesterday we honoured Peter and Paul; today we remember the ordinary believers who died in the same persecution. On Saturday we will keep the Dukhrana of Mar Thoma, and on Sunday the Season of Kaitha begins. Let us close the Season of the Apostles by asking whom we really serve, and by making one concrete choice for God over wealth.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much; and whoever is dishonest in a very little is dishonest also in much.”

The calling that reaches us in every condition of life, the dignity of those bought with a price, and the choice between God and wealth.

1. Lead the life to which God called you

  • Holiness where you are.
  • Obeying God's commandments is everything.
  • Remain there with God.
“But seek the welfare of the city where I have sent you into exile, and pray to the Lord on its behalf, for in its welfare you will find your welfare.” Jeremiah 29:7
“However that may be, let each of you lead the life that the Lord has assigned, to which God called you. This is my rule in all the churches.” 1 Corinthians 7:17

Apply: Offer your work to God each morning this week and do one part of it with special care.

2. Bought with a price

  • Slave and master equal before Christ.
  • The seed of human dignity.
  • Do not sell yourself again.
“For they are my servants, whom I brought out of the land of Egypt; they shall not be sold as slaves are sold.” Leviticus 25:42
“You were bought with a price; do not become slaves of human masters.” 1 Corinthians 7:23

Apply: Name one human master you serve, of opinion, habit or money, and take one step away from it.

3. You cannot serve God and wealth

  • Faithful in a very little.
  • Make friends with your money.
  • God knows your hearts.
“Remove far from me falsehood and lying; give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with the food that I need,” Proverbs 30:8
“No slave can serve two masters; for a slave will either hate the one and love the other, or be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and wealth.” Luke 16:13

Apply: Give to someone who cannot repay you, and refuse one expense that is only for show.

God calls us where we are; bought by Christ, we belong to no other master.

Faithful in little and generous with what we have, we serve God alone, as the apostles and the first martyrs did.

Take-home resolution: This week, offer your daily work to God, refuse one form of slavery, and use your money to make a friend among the poor.

Full homily

Dear friends, some of the first Christians thought that to follow Christ they had to change everything outside: their marriage, their customs, their work. Saint Paul says: “let each of you lead the life that the Lord has assigned, to which God called you” (1 Corinthians 7:17). Holiness is not somewhere else. It is where you are, if you remain there with God.

Paul also says: “You were bought with a price; do not become slaves of human masters” (1 Corinthians 7:23). Christ has paid for us with his Blood. So we must not sell ourselves to the opinion of others, to a habit, or to money.

In the Gospel Jesus says: “Whoever is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much” (Luke 16:10). Character is built in small things: the change returned, the honest exam, the hour of work truly worked.

And he says clearly: “You cannot serve God and wealth” (Luke 16:13). Money is a good servant but a bad master. Jesus tells us to use it to make friends with the poor, who will welcome us into heaven.

Today the Latin calendar remembers the first martyrs of Rome, ordinary believers who died in the same persecution as Peter and Paul. They served one Master only. As the Season of the Apostles comes to its end, let us ask ourselves whom we serve, and choose God. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus says: if you are honest in small things, you will be honest in big things. Return the extra change, tell the truth, share your things. And remember: money is a helper, not a boss. Only God is our boss, and he loves us very much!

Prayer

Lord God, you call each of us in the place and condition where we stand, and you ask us to remain there with you. Make our ordinary work, our families and our studies a path of holiness. You bought us with the precious Blood of your Son: do not let us become slaves of any human master, of opinion, of money or of any habit. Teach us to be faithful in very little, so that you may entrust to us the true riches. Loosen the hold of wealth on our hearts, and help us to make friends of the poor, who will welcome us into the eternal homes. With the first martyrs of Rome and the apostles Peter and Paul, may we serve you alone. Through Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
