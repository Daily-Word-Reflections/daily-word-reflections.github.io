Third Wednesday of the Dedication of the Church

18 November 2026 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 6:1–11 · You were washed, sanctified, justified
  • Gospel Luke 16:13–17 · Entering the Kingdom requires effort

Word for the day

“But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and in the Spirit of our God.” 1 Corinthians 6:11

Reflection

Paul lists the sins that exclude from the Kingdom, then adds with tenderness: “And this is what some of you used to be. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified.” Baptism is a real change: a new identity in Christ. Christians are called to live as who they already are.

Jesus says that since John the Baptist “the good news of the kingdom of God is proclaimed, and everyone tries to enter it by force.” The Kingdom is a gift, but it is entered with resolve: turning from sin, refusing to serve money, choosing God daily. Grace does not remove effort; it makes effort possible.

Homily outline

Theme: “Washed, and called to enter.”

Baptism gives a new identity; the Kingdom asks for resolve.

1. A new heart

  • God promised to cleanse his people and give a new spirit.
  • Baptism fulfils that promise.
“I will put my spirit within you, and you shall live.” Ezekiel 37:14
“Jesus answered, “Very truly, I tell you, no one can enter the kingdom of God without being born of water and Spirit.”” John 3:5

Apply: Thank God for your baptism today.

2. Live as the washed

  • Paul reminds the Corinthians who they now are.
  • Sin contradicts our baptismal identity.
“You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy.” Leviticus 19:2
“So if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation: everything old has passed away; see, everything has become new!” 2 Corinthians 5:17

Apply: Renounce one old habit that contradicts your baptism.

3. Enter the Kingdom with resolve

  • Grace makes effort possible.
  • No one can serve two masters.
“Choose this day whom you will serve… but as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD.” Joshua 24:15
“The law and the prophets were in effect until John came; since then the good news of the kingdom of God is proclaimed, and everyone tries to enter it by force.” Luke 16:16

Apply: Cut off one occasion of sin today.

We were washed; now we walk in newness of life.

Let us enter the Kingdom with all our heart.

Take-home resolution: Renew your baptismal promises at night prayer this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, after listing the sins that exclude from the Kingdom, Paul adds with tenderness: and this is what some of you used to be. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and in the Spirit of our God (1 Corinthians 6:11).

Baptism is a real change: a new identity in Christ. Christians are called to live as people who have been washed, not taking their quarrels before unbelievers, but dealing justly with their brothers and sisters. Jesus also says that everyone tries to enter the Kingdom by force (Luke 16:16), with eager resolve.

Our past does not decide our future; God's grace does. Today let us renew our baptismal promises in our hearts, and take the first step to settle a family quarrel with love. Amen.

For children

Children, at Baptism God washed us and made us new. When you enter the church today, make the sign of the cross and remember: I am God's child.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you washed, sanctified and justified me in baptism. Give me the resolve to live as your child and to enter your Kingdom with all my heart. Amen.
