Readings

  • Epistle 2 Thessalonians 1:3–12 · Worthy of the kingdom for which you suffer
  • Gospel Matthew 20:29–34 · Lord, let our eyes be opened

Word for the day

“Moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes. Immediately they regained their sight and followed him.” Matthew 20:34

Reflection

Jesus is leaving Jericho, the last town before the climb to Jerusalem, where the Cross awaits him. A large crowd follows him, and by the roadside sit two blind men. They cannot see him, but they hear that he is passing by, and they cry out: “Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!” The crowd, perhaps embarrassed, perhaps impatient, tells them to be quiet. “But they shouted even more loudly, ‘Have mercy on us, Lord, Son of David!’” Their prayer is short, repeated, and stubborn. It is the prayer the Church has never stopped praying: in our Holy Qurbana and in the Prayer of the Hours we say again and again, Lord, have mercy; the Christian East has made of it the Jesus Prayer, repeated with every breath. There is always a crowd that tells us to be quiet: the voice of shame, of busyness, of people who think prayer is useless. The blind men teach us to cry louder. And notice what happens next: “Jesus stood still.” The One who has set his face towards Jerusalem stops for two beggars. No one is too small to halt the Lord on his way.

“What do you want me to do for you?” Jesus knows what they need, yet he asks. He wants them to say it, to name their deepest desire. “Lord, let our eyes be opened.” Matthew gives the reason for what follows in one phrase: “Moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes.” The word for compassion is the one Matthew uses when Jesus sees the crowds like sheep without a shepherd: a love that moves the depths of the heart. He does not heal from a distance; he touches. “Immediately they regained their sight and followed him.” They do not go home to enjoy their new sight; they follow him on the road that leads to Jerusalem, to the Cross and to the Resurrection. In this first week of the Season of the Apostles, the Gospel shows us what the apostles became: people whose eyes had been opened by Jesus and who followed him wherever he went. Isaiah had said that the Servant would come “to open the eyes that are blind” (Isaiah 42:7). Every Baptism, which the ancient Church called illumination, is such an opening of the eyes; every confession and every Qurbana can be another touch of the Lord's compassion.

Paul writes to the young Church of Thessalonica with a pastor's pride: “your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing.” They are suffering persecution, and he does not hide it, but he sees their “steadfastness and faith during all your persecutions and the afflictions that you are enduring” as a sign that God is making them “worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you are also suffering.” The strong language about God's judgment that follows is not a call to revenge; it is the assurance that God sees, that injustice will not have the last word, and that the Lord Jesus will be revealed “to be glorified by his saints and to be marveled at on that day among all who have believed.” Christians in many places, and in our own country, have known such afflictions; the faithful of Kandhamal in Odisha in 2008 are still remembered. Paul's answer to suffering is prayer: “To this end we always pray for you, asking that our God will make you worthy of his call and will fulfill by his power every good resolve and work of faith, so that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you, and you in him”. Faith that grows, love that increases, steadfastness under trial, and a prayer for one another: this is the portrait of an apostolic Church.

Homily outline

Theme: “Moved with compassion, Jesus touched their eyes. Immediately they regained their sight and followed him.”

The stubborn prayer of the blind, the compassion that touches and opens the eyes, and a Church made worthy of its call.

1. Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David

  • Blind, but not deaf to his passing.
  • The crowd that says, Be quiet.
  • Crying louder.
“the LORD opens the eyes of the blind. The LORD lifts up those who are bowed down; the LORD loves the righteous.” Psalm 146:8
“When they heard that Jesus was passing by, they shouted, “Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!”” Matthew 20:30

Apply: Pray the Jesus Prayer every day this week, morning and evening.

2. What do you want me to do for you?

  • Jesus stands still.
  • Naming our deepest desire.
  • Compassion that touches.
“Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped;” Isaiah 35:5
“Jesus stood still and called them, saying, “What do you want me to do for you?”” Matthew 20:32

Apply: Tell Jesus honestly, in a quiet moment, the one thing you most need from him.

3. Worthy of his call

  • Faith growing, love increasing.
  • Steadfast in persecution.
  • Praying for one another.
“The steadfast love of the LORD never ceases, his mercies never come to an end;” Lamentations 3:22
“To this end we always pray for you, asking that our God will make you worthy of his call and will fulfill by his power every good resolve and work of faith,” 2 Thessalonians 1:11

Apply: Pray by name this week for three people in your parish, that God may make them worthy of his call.

The Lord who stood still for the blind of Jericho stands still for us, opens our eyes and calls us to follow.

Let us follow him with faith that grows and love that increases, steadfast in every trial.

Take-home resolution: This week, pray the Jesus Prayer daily, name your need to the Lord, and pray for the persecuted and for your parish.

Full homily

Dear friends, Jesus is leaving Jericho on his way to Jerusalem and the Cross. Two blind men by the road hear that he is passing and cry out: “Lord, have mercy on us, Son of David!” (Matthew 20:30).

The crowd tells them to be quiet. But they shout even louder. There is always a crowd that tries to silence our prayer: shame, tiredness, people who say prayer is useless. Let us learn from the blind men to cry louder. Our liturgy repeats again and again, Lord, have mercy.

“Jesus stood still.” The Lord on his way to the Cross stops for two beggars. He asks, “What do you want me to do for you?” They say, “Lord, let our eyes be opened.” Moved with compassion, he touches their eyes, and they see, and they follow him (Matthew 20:32–34).

Paul praises the Thessalonians: their faith is growing and their love increasing, even under persecution. He prays that God will make them “worthy of his call” (2 Thessalonians 1:11). That is the portrait of the Church of the apostles: eyes opened, faith growing, love increasing, steadfast in trial.

In this Season of the Apostles let us cry to the Lord for mercy, tell him honestly what we need, and when he opens our eyes, follow him. Let us also pray for our brothers and sisters who suffer for their faith. Amen.

For children

Children, two blind men shouted to Jesus, “Lord, have mercy on us!” People said, “Be quiet!” but they shouted louder. Jesus stopped, touched their eyes, and they could see! Jesus always stops when we call him.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Son of David, you stood still on the road for two blind beggars and opened their eyes with the touch of your compassion. Have mercy on us. Open the eyes of our hearts to see you, and let us follow you on the road you walk. Make our faith grow and our love for one another increase; keep steadfast all who suffer for your name, and make us worthy of your call, that your name may be glorified in us. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
