Word for the day

“He said to them, “Why were you searching for me? Did you not know that I must be in my Father's house?”” Luke 2:49

Reflection

At twelve Jesus stays behind in the Temple; Mary and Joseph search for him for three days in anguish. When they find him, he answers: “Did you not know that I must be in my Father's house?” Even as a boy he knows who his Father is. Then he goes down with them to Nazareth and is obedient to them, growing in wisdom and favour.

Paul gives the rule of Christian family and community life: “Bear one another's burdens, and in this way you will fulfil the law of Christ… let us not grow weary in doing what is right… let us work for the good of all.” The home at Nazareth was such a place: love that carried, patience that waited, and obedience that grew.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul tells the Galatians: bear one another's burdens, and in this way you will fulfil the law of Christ (Galatians 6:2). Christian life is not lived alone. Each of us carries something heavy, and each of us is called to help carry another's load.

Paul is practical: if anyone is detected in a transgression, you who have received the Spirit should restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness. Take care that you yourselves are not tempted (Galatians 6:1). Correction must be gentle and humble.

He adds: let us not grow weary in doing what is right, for we will reap at harvest time, if we do not give up. So then, whenever we have an opportunity, let us work for the good of all, and especially for those of the family of faith (Galatians 6:9–10).

In the Gospel the twelve-year-old Jesus stays behind in the Temple. Mary and Joseph search for three days, anxious. When they find him, Mary says: your father and I have been searching for you in great anxiety. Jesus answers: did you not know that I must be in my Father's house? (Luke 2:48–49).

Even the Holy Family knew anxiety and misunderstanding. Mary did not fully understand, but she treasured all these things in her heart. Then Jesus went down with them to Nazareth and was obedient to them, and increased in wisdom and in years (Luke 2:51–52).

Many parents today search anxiously for children who are lost: in faith, in addiction, in wrong company, far away abroad. Let us bear one another's burdens: pray for these families, support them, and never grow weary. And let us ask Mary to teach us to treasure and ponder, even when we do not understand. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Paul describes a community in which people carry one another’s burdens, and Luke shows the home where such love was learned. At twelve Jesus reveals who his Father is, and then goes down to Nazareth and is obedient. Both readings speak of patient, daily faithfulness that bears fruit in its own time.

Galatians 6:1–10 · Bear one another’s burdens

Setting.

Paul has written to the Churches of Galatia with deep concern, because some were teaching that Gentile Christians must be circumcised and keep the Law. He has just contrasted the works of the flesh with the fruit of the Spirit (5:16–26). Now, near the end, he shows what life in the Spirit looks like when someone in the community falls.

Structure.

  • vv. 1–5: restoring the one who falls, carrying burdens, examining oneself.
  • v. 6: support for those who teach.
  • vv. 7–10: sowing and reaping: do not grow weary in doing good.

Key words.

  • katartizete, restore: The word used for mending nets (Mark 1:19) and, by medical writers, for setting a broken bone. Correction aims to heal, and it must be done gently.
  • barē / phortion, burdens / load: Two different words. Barē in v. 2 are heavy weights that no one should carry alone. Phortion in v. 5 is a person’s own pack, the word used for a soldier’s kit. We help with the first; no one else can answer for the second.
  • nomos tou Christou, the law of Christ: Paul has just said that the whole Law is summed up in love of neighbour (5:14). Christ’s law is his own command of love, and his own life of carrying our burdens.

Verse by verse.

“if anyone is detected in a transgression, you who have received the Spirit should restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness.” (6:1)

The sign of having the Spirit is not spotless pride, but gentleness toward someone who has fallen. The warning follows: “Take care that you yourselves are not tempted.”

“Bear one another’s burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ.” (6:2)
“So let us not grow weary in doing what is right, for we will reap at harvest time, if we do not give up.” (6:9)

The sower does not see the harvest on the day of sowing. Paul knows that good people get tired, and he gives them a promise.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism calls the New Law a law of love (CCC 1972), and fraternal correction belongs to charity (CCC 1829). St Augustine devoted a whole reflection to this verse, teaching that nothing tests a friend so much as bearing the burden of another (in substance). The verse gives us a simple rule for parish and home: help first, then speak.

Luke 2:41–52 · In my Father’s house

Setting.

This is the only story in the Gospels from the years between Jesus’ infancy and his public ministry. The Law asked men to go up three times a year for the festivals (Deuteronomy 16:16), and Luke notes that Joseph and Mary went every year. Nazareth lay several days’ journey from Jerusalem. People travelled in groups of relatives and neighbours, which explains why they could walk a whole day without missing him.

Structure.

  • vv. 41–45: the pilgrimage, and the boy missed.
  • vv. 46–47: found in the Temple among the teachers.
  • vv. 48–50: Mary’s question and Jesus’ first words in this Gospel.
  • vv. 51–52: the return to Nazareth; he is obedient and grows.

Key words.

  • odynōmenoi, in great anxiety: A strong word: in pain, in anguish. Simeon’s sword (2:35) has already begun to pierce.
  • en tois tou patros mou, in my Father’s house: Literally “in the things of my Father”. It may mean his house, as the NRSV has it, or his affairs, as the POC has it. Both are true; the Greek allows both.
  • hypotassomenos, obedient: Luke’s verb says “he kept on being obedient”. It is the same word that Colossians 3:18 uses for ordering life under another. The eternal Son places himself under a carpenter and a village mother.

Verse by verse.

“After three days they found him in the temple, sitting among the teachers, listening to them and asking them questions.” (2:46)

He listens first. “After three days” may also make the reader think of Easter (24:7, 46), when other seekers look for him and are told where he truly is.

“Child, why have you treated us like this? Look, your father and I have been searching for you in great anxiety.” (2:48)

A mother’s honest question. St Augustine notes that Mary, out of respect for her husband, names Joseph first, and that Joseph is rightly called father because of their true marriage (in substance).

“Why were you searching for me? Did you not know that I must be in my Father’s house?” (2:49)

Mary says “your father”, and Jesus answers “my Father”. His first words in Luke name the Father, as his last words on the Cross do (23:46). “Must” (dei) is the word Luke uses for God’s plan.

“Then he went down with them and came to Nazareth, and was obedient to them. His mother treasured all these things in her heart.” (2:51)

They did not understand (v. 50), yet Mary keeps what she does not understand. That is a model for all faith.

“And Jesus increased in wisdom and in years, and in divine and human favor.” (2:52)

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism says that the finding in the Temple is the only event that breaks the silence of the Gospels about the hidden years, and that it lets us glimpse Jesus’ total consecration to the mission that flows from his divine sonship (CCC 534). His obedience to Mary and Joseph is an image in time of his obedience to the Father (CCC 532). Jesus truly grew in human knowledge, while his human mind also knew the Father in a unique way (CCC 472–473).

Questions people ask

Did the boy Jesus disobey his parents by staying behind?

The Gospel does not treat it as a fault. He acts out of a higher obedience to the Father, and at once afterward he goes home and is obedient to them. The episode shows that even the best family ties serve God’s call, and that a call may cost parents sorrow. Jesus was without sin (Hebrews 4:15).

For study and teaching

  • Whose burden can I help to carry this week, and is there someone I should correct gently rather than criticize?
  • Mary kept in her heart what she did not understand. What am I being asked to keep, without yet understanding it?
