Word for the day

“For the kingdom of God depends not on talk but on power.” 1 Corinthians 4:20

Reflection

The first day of June opens with a father's letter. Paul writes to a quarrelsome community he loves, and he does not hide his feelings: “For though you might have ten thousand guardians in Christ, you do not have many fathers. Indeed, in Christ Jesus I became your father through the gospel.” The guardian in a Greek household was the slave who walked the children to school and kept an eye on them; he could be strict and useful, but he was not their father. Paul claims something deeper. He did not merely supervise the Corinthians; he begot them in faith by preaching Christ to them. That is why he can say, without pride, “I appeal to you, then, be imitators of me.” Faith is handed on not only by teaching but by a life that can be copied. Paul sends Timothy, “my beloved and faithful child in the Lord, to remind you of my ways in Christ Jesus.” This is the apostolic pattern of the whole Season of the Apostles: a father in the faith, a son trained by him, and a community that learns the ways of Christ by watching those who walk in them. In these days, when schools reopen across Kerala and children set out with new bags and new books, the Church reminds parents, godparents, teachers and priests that they are more than guardians. Children learn the faith first from the way we pray, speak and forgive at home.

Then Paul turns to those who have grown arrogant, thinking that he will never come back: “But I will come to you soon, if the Lord wills, and I will find out not the talk of these arrogant people but their power. For the kingdom of God depends not on talk but on power.” Corinth loved clever speech. Some had made themselves important by talking well about spiritual things. Paul asks a simple question: what has your talk produced? The kingdom of God is shown by the power of the Spirit that changes lives, heals divisions and makes people holy. Words about God are cheap; a life changed by God is costly and convincing. Then the tender ending: “What would you prefer? Am I to come to you with a stick, or with love in a spirit of gentleness?” A good father can correct, but he would much rather come with gentleness. The choice, Paul says, is partly theirs.

In the Gospel Jesus speaks some of the sternest words he ever spoke, and he speaks them in defence of the little ones: “If any of you put a stumbling block before one of these little ones who believe in me, it would be better for you if a great millstone were fastened around your neck and you were drowned in the depth of the sea.” The millstone was the heavy stone turned by a donkey to grind grain. Jesus does not use such language lightly. To lead a child, or any simple believer, into sin or out of faith is a grave evil. The Church has had to hear these words with shame in our own time, when some who should have been fathers in the faith harmed the little ones entrusted to them; every parish and institution is bound to protect children and vulnerable people, to listen to them and to act. Then Jesus turns the warning inward: “If your hand or your foot causes you to stumble, cut it off and throw it away.” He is not asking for mutilation; he is asking for decisiveness. Whatever in my life leads me into sin, a habit, a company, an app on the phone, must be cut off firmly, because eternal life is worth more than anything we give up.

Today the Church remembers St Justin, one of the first great Christian thinkers. Born in Samaria early in the second century, he searched for the truth among the philosophers of his day, until an old man he met by the sea pointed him to the prophets and to Christ. He became, as he said, a philosopher of the true philosophy, opened a school in Rome, and wrote to the emperor to defend the Christians. In those writings he left the earliest full description of the Sunday Eucharist outside the New Testament: the reading of the memoirs of the apostles and the prophets, the homily, the prayers, the bread and wine brought to the presider, the great thanksgiving, the Amen of the people, and the gifts carried to the absent. Around the year 165 he was arrested with several companions and beheaded under the prefect Rusticus for refusing to sacrifice to the gods. The readings of his feast fit his life: “the message about the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God” (1 Corinthians 1:18), and “everyone who acknowledges me before others, the Son of Man also will acknowledge before the angels of God” (Luke 12:8). Justin's power was not in talk, though he talked well; it was in the witness of his blood.

Homily outline

Theme: “For the kingdom of God depends not on talk but on power.”

A father in the faith, the power of the Spirit that changes lives, the little ones whom Jesus defends, and St Justin, who found the truth in Christ.

  • Guardians and fathers.
  • Be imitators of me.
  • Faith handed on at home.

“For though you might have ten thousand guardians in Christ, you do not have many fathers. Indeed, in Christ Jesus I became your father through the gospel.” 1 Corinthians 4:15

Apply: Pray once this week with a child, slowly and aloud.

2. Not talk but power

  • The arrogant talkers of Corinth.
  • The Spirit who changes lives.
  • A stick or gentleness.
“He said to me, “This is the word of the Lord to Zerubbabel: Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, says the Lord of hosts.” Zechariah 4:6
“For the kingdom of God depends not on talk but on power.” 1 Corinthians 4:20

Apply: Ask the Holy Spirit each morning this week for one concrete change in your life.

3. Woe to the one who causes the little ones to stumble

  • The millstone.
  • Protecting children and the vulnerable.
  • Cutting off what causes sin.
“I will seek the lost, and I will bring back the strayed, and I will bind up the injured, and I will strengthen the weak, but the fat and the strong I will destroy. I will feed them with justice.” Ezekiel 34:16
“If any of you put a stumbling block before one of these little ones who believe in me, it would be better for you if a great millstone were fastened around your neck and you were drowned in the depth of the sea.” Matthew 18:6

Apply: Remove one occasion of sin from your life this week.

The faith is handed on by fathers and mothers whose lives can be copied, and the kingdom is shown in the Spirit's power.

With St Justin, let our Amen be sealed by a life that witnesses to Christ.

Take-home resolution: This week, pray with a child, protect the little ones, and cut off one thing that leads you into sin.

Full homily

Dear friends, today Saint Paul writes like a father. He says to the Corinthians: you have many guardians, but not many fathers; I became your father in Christ through the Gospel (1 Corinthians 4:15). Faith is not only taught; it is passed on by people who love us and whose lives we can copy.

This week children go back to school. Let us ask ourselves: who are the fathers and mothers in faith for our children? Not only priests and catechism teachers, but parents, grandparents and godparents. Children learn to pray by watching us pray.

Paul says: the kingdom of God depends not on talk but on power (1 Corinthians 4:20). It is easy to talk about God. The real sign of the kingdom is a life changed by the Holy Spirit: a temper calmed, a quarrel healed, a habit broken.

In the Gospel Jesus defends the little ones with strong words: it would be better to have a millstone around one's neck than to lead a little one into sin. Let us protect our children in every way. And let us be firm with ourselves: whatever leads us into sin, let us cut it off.

Today we remember Saint Justin. He searched for the truth among the philosophers, found it in Christ, and gave his life for him in Rome. He also told us how the first Christians celebrated the Eucharist on Sunday. When we say Amen at the Qurbana, we say it with Justin. May our faith, like his, be not only talk but power. Amen.

For children

Children, Saint Paul was like a father to the first Christians, because he told them about Jesus. Who told you about Jesus? Say thank you to them today!

Jesus loves children very much. If anyone ever makes you feel unsafe or asks you to do something wrong, tell your parents or a teacher you trust.

Prayer

Father of all, you gave the Corinthians a father in the faith through the Gospel, and you gave St Justin the wisdom to find in Christ the truth he was seeking. Give our children faithful fathers and mothers, teachers and priests, whose lives they can copy. Keep the little ones from every stumbling block, and give us courage to cut off whatever leads us into sin. Let your kingdom be seen among us not in talk but in the power of your Holy Spirit. Through Christ our Lord, to whom with you and the Holy Spirit be glory for ever. Amen.
