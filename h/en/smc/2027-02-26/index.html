Word for the day

“There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.” Romans 8:1

Reflection

After the flood God speaks to Noah and binds himself by a promise, not only to Noah's family but “with every living creature that is with you, the birds, the domestic animals, and every animal of the earth with you.” He gives a sign: “I have set my bow in the clouds, and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and the earth.” The Hebrew word for the rainbow is the same as for a warrior's bow; God hangs up his weapon in the sky. And the sign is for God himself: “When the bow is in the clouds, I will see it and remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is on the earth.” God binds himself to remember. The second reading tells how the walls of Jericho fell, not by siege engines but by obedience. Seven priests with seven trumpets walk before the ark, around the city, once a day for six days, and the people are told: “You shall not shout or let your voice be heard, nor shall you utter a word, until the day I tell you to shout.” On the seventh day they go round seven times; the trumpets sound, the people shout, “and the wall fell down flat.” Rahab and her family are spared “because she hid the messengers”. These pages of conquest are hard to read, and the Church reads them in the light of Christ, not as a pattern for treating people but as a picture of the walls of sin that fall before patient, silent obedience. During these weeks pilgrims climb Malayattoor hill, where tradition says St Thomas prayed, many carrying wooden crosses, step after step, calling on the golden cross. Like the circling of Jericho, Lenten prayer is often slow and repeated, and its fruit comes on a day we do not choose.

Paul opens the eighth chapter of Romans with the most freeing sentence a sinner can hear: “There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has set you free from the law of sin and of death.” What the law could not do, God did, “by sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh.” The choice before us is a matter of where we set our minds: “To set the mind on the flesh is death, but to set the mind on the Spirit is life and peace.” In the Gospel the Sadducees, who deny the resurrection, bring Jesus a riddle about a woman married in turn to seven brothers: whose wife will she be? Jesus answers: “Is not this the reason you are wrong, that you know neither the scriptures nor the power of God?” The risen life is not this life repeated; it is new. And he goes back to the burning bush, where God called himself the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, long after they had died: “He is God not of the dead, but of the living.” On this Friday we walk the Way of the Cross to the tomb, but the tomb is not the end of the road. Those we have lost are alive to God, and we pray for them in that hope.

Full homily

Dear friends, after the flood God set his bow in the clouds as a sign of the covenant (Genesis 9:13). God hung up his weapon in the sky and promised: I will remember. Our God is a God who remembers his promises.

The walls of Jericho fell not by force but by obedience: seven days of walking in silence around the city, and then a shout. Lenten prayer is like that. We walk the Way of the Cross, Friday after Friday, and the walls of sin fall on a day we do not choose.

Paul gives us the most freeing word a sinner can hear: there is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1). Set your mind on the Spirit, and you will find life and peace.

Jesus tells the Sadducees that God is not God of the dead, but of the living (Mark 12:27). Our Way of the Cross ends at a tomb, but the tomb is not the end. Let us pray today for our departed, who are alive to God. Amen.

For children

Children, after the flood God put a rainbow in the sky as a promise. God always keeps his promises! And Jesus tells us that people who die in God's love are alive with him.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, in you there is no condemnation. Remember your everlasting covenant with us, as you remembered Noah; bring down the walls of our sin through patient obedience; set our minds on the Spirit, who gives life and peace. You are God of the living: give eternal rest to our departed and bring us all to the resurrection. Amen.
