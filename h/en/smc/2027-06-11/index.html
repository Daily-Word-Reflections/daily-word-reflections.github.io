Word for the day

“for he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith. And a great many people were brought to the Lord.” Acts 11:24

Reflection

Today the Church keeps the feast of St Barnabas, whom the Acts of the Apostles calls an apostle together with Paul (Acts 14:14), though he was not one of the Twelve. His real name was Joseph. He was a Levite from Cyprus, and the apostles gave him a new name, Barnabas, “which means ‘son of encouragement’” (Acts 4:36). We first meet him in the earliest days of the Church in Jerusalem, when he sells a field and lays the money at the apostles' feet. It is a small gesture, but it tells us who he is: a man who gives without holding back, and who trusts the community of the apostles. All through Acts he lives up to his name. Wherever Barnabas appears, someone is encouraged, welcomed, trusted, or given a second chance.

The clearest example is Saul. After his conversion on the road to Damascus, the disciples in Jerusalem were afraid of him; they remembered the persecutor and did not believe he had changed. “But Barnabas took him, brought him to the apostles, and described for them how on the road he had seen the Lord” (Acts 9:27). Barnabas risked his own reputation to vouch for a former enemy. Without that act of trust, the Church might have kept at a distance the greatest missionary she has ever had. Today's reading shows him again in the same role. When the Gospel reaches Gentiles in Antioch, the church in Jerusalem sends Barnabas to see what is happening. “When he came and saw the grace of God, he rejoiced, and he exhorted them all to remain faithful to the Lord with steadfast devotion; for he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith.” He does not come as an inspector looking for faults, but as a brother looking for grace, and when he finds it he rejoices. Then he does something humble and wise: he goes to Tarsus to look for Saul, and brings him to Antioch, and the two of them teach there for a whole year. “It was in Antioch that the disciples were first called ‘Christians.’”

From Antioch the Holy Spirit sends them out. “While they were worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, ‘Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.’ Then after fasting and praying they laid their hands on them and sent them off” (Acts 13:2–3). Barnabas and Paul go first to Cyprus, Barnabas' own island, and then into Asia Minor. In this Season of the Apostles we see the Church's mission born from worship, fasting and the laying on of hands: not from a human plan, but from the Spirit speaking in a praying community. The Epistle tells how, years later, Paul and Barnabas went up to Jerusalem to lay before the leaders the gospel they preached among the Gentiles, and how “James and Cephas and John, who were acknowledged pillars, recognized the grace that had been given to me, they gave to Barnabas and me the right hand of fellowship.” The unity of the Church, with Peter among the pillars, guarded the truth of the Gospel. And the leaders asked only one thing: “that we remember the poor.” Barnabas, who had sold his field for the poor, would not have needed reminding.

Barnabas was not without weakness. Paul tells that at Antioch “even Barnabas was led astray” for a time when some held back from eating with Gentile believers (Galatians 2:13). And Acts honestly records that Paul and Barnabas had a sharp disagreement over John Mark, who had left them in the middle of their first journey; “they parted company; Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed away to Cyprus” (Acts 15:39). Barnabas gave Mark a second chance, as he had once given Saul a first one. Later Paul himself would write, “Get Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful in my ministry” (2 Timothy 4:11). The encourager had been right about his cousin. Tradition holds that Barnabas died a martyr in Cyprus, at Salamis.

The Gospel is the Lord's warning to those he sends: “See, I am sending you out like sheep into the midst of wolves; so be wise as serpents and innocent as doves.” The apostles will be handed over and hated, but they will not be alone: “it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father speaking through you.” And the psalm of the first reading gives the apostle's song: “O give thanks to the Lord, call on his name, make known his deeds among the peoples.” The weekday Gospel of this Fourth Friday, the raising of Jairus' daughter, adds a word that Barnabas lived: “Do not fear, only believe” (Mark 5:36). Every parish needs a Barnabas: someone who sees grace and rejoices, who welcomes the newcomer and the convert, who speaks for the person others are afraid of, and who gives a second chance to those who have failed. Today let us ask him to make us sons and daughters of encouragement.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we celebrate St Barnabas. His name was Joseph, a Levite from Cyprus, but the apostles called him Barnabas, “son of encouragement” (Acts 4:36). He sold his field and gave the money to the apostles for the poor. Wherever he went, people were encouraged.

When Saul was converted, the Christians in Jerusalem were afraid of him. But Barnabas took him by the hand, brought him to the apostles and spoke for him (Acts 9:27). Without Barnabas, the Church might have kept Saul, the future Saint Paul, at a distance.

In today's reading the Church sends Barnabas to Antioch, where many Gentiles have believed. He does not come to find faults. “When he came and saw the grace of God, he rejoiced” (Acts 11:23). Scripture says, “he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith.” Then he goes to look for Saul, and they teach together for a year. In Antioch the disciples were first called Christians.

While the church at Antioch was praying and fasting, the Holy Spirit said: “Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul” (Acts 13:2). The mission of the Church is born at prayer. The apostles in Jerusalem gave them the right hand of fellowship, and asked only that they remember the poor (Galatians 2:9–10).

Barnabas also gave a second chance to young Mark, who had once left the mission. Paul later wrote that Mark was useful in his ministry (2 Timothy 4:11). Jesus warns that his apostles go out like sheep among wolves, but the Spirit of the Father speaks through them (Matthew 10:16–20). Tradition says Barnabas died a martyr in Cyprus.

Every family and every parish needs a Barnabas. Let us look for God's grace in others and rejoice in it; let us welcome the newcomer and the one others fear; let us give a second chance; and let us remember the poor. St Barnabas, pray for us. Amen.

For children

Children, Barnabas was called the son of encouragement because he always made people feel brave and loved. When everyone was afraid of Saul, Barnabas became his friend.

This week, be a Barnabas! Sit with the child who is alone, say something kind to someone who is sad, and help a friend who made a mistake to try again.

Prayer

Lord God, you chose St Barnabas, a good man full of the Holy Spirit and of faith, to encourage the young Church, to welcome Saul, to rejoice in your grace among the Gentiles, and to carry the Gospel to the nations. By his prayers make us sons and daughters of encouragement: generous with the poor, welcoming to newcomers, quick to see your grace, slow to judge, and ready to give a second chance. Send labourers into your harvest, keep them wise and innocent among dangers, and let your Spirit speak through them. Through Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you in the unity of the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
