Passion Friday (Good Friday)

  • Gospel Luke 22:63–23:12; Matthew 27:19; Luke 23:13–23; Matthew 27:24–25; Luke 23:24–45; Matthew 27:51–54; John 19:23–30 · The Passion of Christ (Part I)

Word for the day

“When Jesus had received the wine, he said, “It is finished.” Then he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.” John 19:30

Reflection

The Church begins this day of sorrow with a father and a son climbing a mountain. God says to Abraham, “Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains that I shall show you.” Every word tightens the knot: your son, your only son, whom you love. Abraham lays the wood on Isaac's shoulders and carries the fire and the knife himself, “So the two of them walked on together.” Then the boy asks the question that echoes through the whole Bible: “The fire and the wood are here, but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?” Abraham answers, “God himself will provide the lamb for a burnt offering, my son.” On Moriah the hand of Abraham is stopped and a ram caught in a thicket dies in Isaac's place. On Calvary no hand is stopped. The Father does not spare his own Son, and the Son carries the wood of his own sacrifice up the hill. The question of Isaac is answered at last by John the Baptist: “Here is the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” What Abraham was asked to give and was spared, God gives and does not spare. That is why the Church reads Genesis 22 today: to show that the Cross is not a human tragedy that God turned to good, but the gift of the Father who so loved the world that he gave his only Son.

Isaiah's fourth song of the Servant is read on this day by Christians everywhere, because it reads like an eyewitness account written centuries before. “He was despised and rejected by others; a man of suffering and acquainted with infirmity.” The prophet's community confesses its mistake: they thought he was punished by God for his own sins. Then the truth breaks in: “But he was wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the punishment that made us whole, and by his bruises we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have all turned to our own way, and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all.” And the Servant goes silent to his death: “like a lamb that is led to the slaughter, and like a sheep that before its shearers is silent, so he did not open his mouth.” Paul draws out what this means for us. Christ did not die for the good and the deserving: “For while we were still weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly.” “But God proves his love for us in that while we still were sinners Christ died for us.” The Cross is the proof. Whoever doubts that God loves him should look at the crucifix and read what is written there: you were worth this to me. And Paul goes further: through one man's disobedience sin and death entered; through the one man Jesus Christ the free gift of grace overflows to the many.

The Passion is read today in two parts, woven from the Gospels of Luke, Matthew and John. Jesus is mocked, blindfolded and struck; he is led to the council, to Pilate, to Herod, and back to Pilate. Three times Pilate finds no case against him. Pilate's wife sends a message: “Have nothing to do with that innocent man, for today I have suffered a great deal because of a dream about him.” Pilate washes his hands, and the crowd cries, “His blood be on us and on our children!” The Church reads these words with great care. The Second Vatican Council teaches that what happened in the Passion “cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today” (Nostra Aetate 4), and the Catechism reminds us that sinners, all of us, were the authors of Christ's sufferings (CCC 597–598). Indeed the blood of Christ is on us: not as a curse but as the blood that cleanses, sprinkled on all who are saved. Then come the words from the Cross. To his executioners: “Father, forgive them; for they do not know what they are doing.” To the thief who asks to be remembered: “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in Paradise.” To his Mother and the beloved disciple: “Woman, here is your son… Here is your mother.” Then, “I am thirsty”, and they lift a sponge of sour wine to his lips. “It is finished.” Darkness covers the land, the curtain of the temple is torn, the earth shakes, and a Roman officer says, “Truly this man was God's Son!” In the second part a soldier pierces his side, “and at once blood and water came out”, and Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus lay him in a new tomb in a garden. The Fathers saw in that blood and water the Holy Qurbana and Baptism, and the Church herself born from the side of the new Adam asleep on the Cross.

How a family can follow the rites of Passion Friday, step by step (the order and names differ a little between dioceses, so follow your parish). First, the fast: on this day the faithful keep fast and abstinence, and many keep silence, switch off the television and take only kanji. Second, the service of the Passion in church, usually in the morning or afternoon: the prayers of the day, the readings from Genesis, Isaiah and Romans, and the long reading of the Passion (Part I), followed by the Passion sermon. There is no Holy Qurbana on this day: the altar stands bare, for the one Sacrifice is contemplated on the Cross itself. Third, the veneration of the Cross (Sliva Vandanam): the crucifix is shown to the people, and one by one they come forward, kneel and kiss the wounds of the Lord. Fourth, the procession with the Cross through the streets or around the church, praying the Way of the Cross; many walk to hills of pilgrimage such as Malayattoor with wooden crosses on their shoulders. Fifth, the second part of the Passion (John 19:31–42) and the burial: the body of the Lord, the crucifix or an image, is laid in a tomb prepared with flowers, and the people pass by in prayer. Sixth, the bitter drink (kaippuneer): at the end each one takes a sip of a bitter drink, recalling the sour wine offered to the thirsty Lord on the Cross, and promising to share his bitterness rather than run from it. At home, many families read or sing the Puthen Pana of Arnos Pathiri, especially the lament of the Mother, and keep the evening in quiet. Nothing in these rites is theatre. Each step is a way of standing with Mary and John at the foot of the Cross, where the world was redeemed.

Homily outline

Theme: “When Jesus had received the wine, he said, ‘It is finished.’ Then he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.”

The lamb that God provides, the Servant wounded for us, the words from the Cross, and the Church born from the pierced side.

1. God himself will provide the lamb

  • Your son, your only son, whom you love.
  • Isaac carries the wood.
  • On Calvary no hand is stopped.
“Abraham said, “God himself will provide the lamb for a burnt offering, my son.” So the two of them walked on together.” Genesis 22:8
“For God so loved the world that he gave his only Son, so that everyone who believes in him may not perish but may have eternal life.” John 3:16

Apply: Look at a crucifix for five minutes today and say only: you were given for me.

2. By his bruises we are healed

  • Despised and rejected.
  • The sins of all laid on one.
  • Silent like a lamb.
“But he was wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the punishment that made us whole, and by his bruises we are healed.” Isaiah 53:5
“But God proves his love for us in that while we still were sinners Christ died for us.” Romans 5:8

Apply: Bring one hidden sin to the Cross today, and plan to confess it in Easter week.

3. Father, forgive them

  • Forgiveness for his executioners.
  • Paradise for a thief.
  • No one is to blame but sin.
“All we like sheep have gone astray; we have all turned to our own way, and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all.” Isaiah 53:6
“Then Jesus said, “Father, forgive them; for they do not know what they are doing.”” Luke 23:34

Apply: Forgive one person by name today, and if possible tell them.

4. Blood and water

  • The pierced side.
  • The Church born from the new Adam.
  • Standing with Mary.
“They gave me poison for food, and for my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink.” Psalm 69:21
“Instead, one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once blood and water came out.” John 19:34

Apply: Take the kaippuneer today as a promise not to run from your own cross.

What Abraham was spared, God gave; what we deserved, Christ bore; what was torn open on the Cross is the door of the Church.

Kneel, kiss the wounds, forgive, and stand with Mary until the morning comes.

Take-home resolution: Today keep the fast with love, venerate the Cross with your family, forgive one person by name, and keep the evening in silence.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today there is no Qurbana. The altar is bare and the tabernacle is empty. The Church stands in silence at the foot of the Cross, where the one Sacrifice was offered once for all.

We began with Abraham and Isaac on the mountain. The boy asked, “Where is the lamb for a burnt offering?” and his father answered, “God himself will provide the lamb.” On Moriah the knife was stopped. On Calvary it was not. The Father gave his only Son, whom he loves, and the Son carried the wood of his own sacrifice.

Isaiah saw him centuries before: “He was wounded for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities… and by his bruises we are healed.” He did not open his mouth. And Paul tells us why: “God proves his love for us in that while we still were sinners Christ died for us.” Not for the good, but for sinners. For you and for me.

Listen to his words from the Cross. “Father, forgive them.” He forgives those who are killing him. “Today you will be with me in Paradise.” He opens heaven to a thief. “Here is your mother.” He gives us Mary. “I am thirsty.” He thirsts for our love. “It is finished.” Not ‘I am finished’, but ‘the work is complete’.

Who killed him? The Church answers honestly: not a people, not a nation, but sin, our sin. That is why today we do not point at anyone. We beat our own breasts, and then we come to kiss the wounds that heal us.

When you take the bitter drink today, remember the thirst of the Lord. Do not run from the bitterness of your own cross. Stand with Mary, forgive someone by name, and keep this evening in silence. We adore you, O Christ, and we bless you, because by your holy Cross you have redeemed the world. Amen.

For children

Children, today Jesus died on the cross. He did it because he loves us so much and wanted to take away all our sins.

On the cross Jesus said sorry for the people who hurt him: ‘Father, forgive them.’ Can you forgive someone today?

When we kiss the cross today, let us say: ‘Thank you, Jesus, for loving me so much!’

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Lamb of God, you carried the wood of your sacrifice up the hill, and you were wounded for our transgressions. We adore you and we bless you, for by your holy Cross you have redeemed the world. Forgive us as you forgave those who crucified you; remember us in your kingdom as you remembered the thief; give us your Mother as you gave her to the beloved disciple. Let the blood and water from your side wash us and feed us, and let us not run from your Cross but stand beside it with Mary, until we see your glory. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
