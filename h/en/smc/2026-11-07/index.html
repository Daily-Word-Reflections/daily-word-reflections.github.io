First Saturday of the Dedication of the Church

Readings

  • Epistle Romans 12:9–18 · Let love be genuine
  • Gospel Luke 10:25–37 · Love is the way to eternal life: the Good Samaritan

Word for the day

“He said, “The one who showed him mercy.” Jesus said to him, “Go and do likewise.”” Luke 10:37

Reflection

A lawyer asks, “What must I do to inherit eternal life?” and then, “Who is my neighbour?” Jesus answers with a story in which the despised Samaritan is the one who stops, binds the wounds and pays the cost. The question changes: not “Who is my neighbour?” but “Will I be a neighbour?”

Paul describes the same love from the inside: “Let love be genuine… outdo one another in showing honour… extend hospitality to strangers… live peaceably with all.” The Church, God's house, is meant to be the inn where the wounded are cared for, and every Christian is sent out to the road to Jericho.

Homily outline

Theme: “Go and do likewise.”

The Good Samaritan and Paul's portrait of genuine love.

1. Love your neighbour as yourself

  • The commandment is already in the Law of Moses.
  • Jesus joins love of God and love of neighbour.
“You shall not take vengeance or bear a grudge against any of your people, but you shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am the LORD.” Leviticus 19:18
“He answered, “You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your strength, and with all your mind; and your neighbor as yourself.”” Luke 10:27

Apply: Do one act of love for God and one for your neighbour today.

2. Love that stops

  • The priest and Levite pass by; the Samaritan is moved with pity.
  • Mercy costs time, effort and money.
“For I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings.” Hosea 6:6
“He said, “The one who showed him mercy.” Jesus said to him, “Go and do likewise.”” Luke 10:37

Apply: Give time to someone others pass by.

3. The Church as the inn

  • The Fathers saw the inn as the Church and the Samaritan as Christ.
  • Every parish is meant to care for the wounded.
“He has told you, O mortal, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?” Micah 6:8
“Come to me, all you that are weary and are carrying heavy burdens, and I will give you rest.” Matthew 11:28

Apply: Welcome a newcomer or migrant worker in your parish.

The Samaritan is Christ; the inn is the Church; the road to Jericho is ours.

Go and do likewise.

Take-home resolution: Visit or phone someone lonely this week.

Full homily

Dear friends, a lawyer asks: what must I do to inherit eternal life? And then: who is my neighbour? Jesus answers with a story in which a despised Samaritan is the one who stops, binds the wounds and pays the cost (Luke 10:30–35). The question changes: not who is my neighbour, but to whom can I be a neighbour?

Paul says: let love be genuine; love one another with mutual affection; rejoice with those who rejoice, weep with those who weep (Romans 12:9–15). This is the way of life in God's house.

The Fathers of the Church compared the Church to the inn where the Samaritan brought the wounded man: a house of healing. This week let us practise love that stops: when we meet a need on the road, let us not pass by. Amen.

For children

Children, the good Samaritan stopped and helped the hurt man. If you see someone sad today, go close and help!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Good Samaritan of humanity, you stopped for me when I was wounded. Make me a neighbour to all, with genuine love that stops, binds and pays the cost. Amen.
