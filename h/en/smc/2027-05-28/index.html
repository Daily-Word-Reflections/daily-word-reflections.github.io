Word for the day

“Very truly, I tell you, whoever believes has eternal life. I am the bread of life.” John 6:47–48

Reflection

Yesterday we celebrated the Body and Blood of Christ; today the Gospel takes a step back in the same discourse at Capernaum and shows us the road that leads to the altar. Jesus quotes the prophets: “It is written in the prophets, ‘And they shall all be taught by God.’ Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me.” The words come from Isaiah's promise to the restored Jerusalem, “All your children shall be taught by the Lord” (Isaiah 54:13). Before anyone eats the bread of life, they must first listen and learn. That is why every Holy Qurbana has two tables: first the table of the Word, with the readings and the homily, and then the table of the Body and Blood. Jesus adds at once that this learning is not mere information: “Not that anyone has seen the Father except the one who is from God; he has seen the Father.” Only the Son has seen the Father face to face. To be taught by God is to be taught by the Son, who makes the Father known.

Then comes the simple, tremendous claim: “Very truly, I tell you, whoever believes has eternal life. I am the bread of life.” Jesus contrasts himself with the gift Israel remembered with gratitude: “Your ancestors ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died. This is the bread that comes down from heaven, so that one may eat of it and not die.” The manna was a true gift of God; it kept a whole people alive for forty years in the desert. But it fed bodies that would one day die. The bread that Jesus gives, which is himself, feeds a life that death cannot end. Notice that faith comes before eating in this passage: whoever believes has eternal life. The Eucharist is truly Christ whatever our state, but it bears fruit only in a heart that believes. We come to the altar as people who have listened, learned and believed, and the Lord who is received into a believing heart gives life that lasts.

Paul's words to the Corinthians sound at first like an insult: “If you think that you are wise in this age, you should become fools so that you may become wise. For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God.” The Corinthians were proud of their learning and had divided into parties: some for Paul, some for Apollos, some for Cephas. Paul says that boasting about human leaders is foolish, and then he turns the whole question upside down: “For all things are yours, whether Paul or Apollos or Cephas or the world or life or death or the present or the future, all belong to you, and you belong to Christ, and Christ belongs to God.” The apostles do not own the Church; they serve it. The believer does not belong to a party, a preacher or a movement, but to Christ. In our own day the temptation is the same. We can make idols of favourite preachers, of retreat centres, of a style of liturgy, of a family or a community, and forget that all these are gifts given to us so that we may belong more fully to Christ. To become a fool for Christ is to let God teach us, as children are taught, and to stop measuring the things of God by the wisdom of the market, the media or the examination hall.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we celebrated the Body and Blood of Christ. Today Jesus tells us how we come to that bread: “It is written in the prophets, ‘And they shall all be taught by God.’ Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me” (John 6:45).

Before we eat, we listen. That is why every Qurbana begins with the Word: the readings, the Gospel and the homily. God teaches us first, and then feeds us. Let us not arrive late for the readings, as if only the second part mattered.

Then Jesus says: “I am the bread of life.” The ancestors ate manna in the desert and still died. But whoever eats the bread that comes down from heaven will not die. Jesus gives a life that death cannot destroy. And he says first: whoever believes has eternal life. We come to the altar with faith.

Paul tells the proud Corinthians: the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. They were fighting over their favourite preachers, Paul, Apollos and Cephas. Paul says: all of them are yours, and you belong to Christ (1 Corinthians 3:21–23).

So let us become humble learners, like children. Let us not divide our parishes over preachers and movements. All the gifts of the Church are given to us, so that we may belong more fully to Christ, who is the bread of life. Amen.

For children

Children, in the desert God gave his people a special bread called manna. But Jesus says he is an even better bread: the bread of life! At the Holy Qurbana we first listen to God's word, and then we receive Jesus. So listen carefully to the readings next Sunday!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you alone have seen the Father, and you make him known to us. Teach us, as the prophets promised, so that we may hear and learn from the Father and come to you. You are the bread of life: give us faith to receive you, and life that death cannot end. Deliver us from the wisdom of this world that is foolishness with God, from boasting and from divisions, and keep us always yours, as you are the Father's. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Both readings ask where true wisdom comes from. Paul tells the Corinthians, proud of their favourite teachers, that the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God, and that all things are theirs only because they belong to Christ. In John, Jesus says that those who are taught by God come to him, because he alone has seen the Father and is himself the bread of life. True wisdom is not something we own; it is Someone we receive.

1 Corinthians 3:18–23 · All things are yours

Setting.

The Corinthian church was divided into groups: “I belong to Paul”, “I belong to Apollos”, “I belong to Cephas” (1:12). Each group boasted of its leader as if he were a school of wisdom. Paul has shown that the cross looks like foolishness to the world (1:18–25), and that teachers are only servants and fellow workers in God’s field (3:5–9). Now he draws his conclusion.

Structure.

  • v. 18: do not deceive yourselves; become a fool in order to become wise.
  • vv. 19–20: two proofs from Scripture: God catches the wise in their craftiness (Job 5:13); the Lord knows their thoughts are futile (Psalm 94:11).
  • vv. 21–23: do not boast in human leaders: all things are yours, you are Christ’s, and Christ is God’s.

Key words.

  • sophia tou kosmou, the wisdom of this world: Not learning as such, which Paul himself had. It is a way of thinking that measures everything by success, status and cleverness, and so cannot understand a crucified Messiah.
  • kauchaomai, to boast: A favourite word of Paul in this letter. The only right boast is in the Lord (1:31, quoting Jeremiah 9:24).
  • panta hymōn, all things are yours: The Corinthians wanted to belong to one teacher. Paul turns it round: the teachers belong to them, and so does the whole world, life, death, present and future, because they belong to Christ.

Verse by verse.

“If you think that you are wise in this age, you should become fools so that you may become wise.” (3:18)

Humility is the door of wisdom. Whoever thinks he already knows cannot be taught.

“For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God.” (3:19)

Paul reverses the Corinthians’ scale of values. What the world calls foolish, the cross, is God’s wisdom (1:24).

“whether Paul or Apollos or Cephas or the world or life or death or the present or the future” (3:22)

A breathtaking list. Even death is “ours”, because in Christ it has become the way to life.

“and you belong to Christ, and Christ belongs to God.” (3:23)

Christ “belongs to God” as the Son who receives everything from the Father and returns everything to him (15:28). The chain of belonging ends in the Father’s love.

How the Church reads it.

The Second Vatican Council closes its reflection on human activity spoiled by sin with these verses: all things are yours, and you are Christ’s, and Christ is God’s (Gaudium et Spes 37). The Christian does not despise the world or human wisdom; he receives them as gifts and orders them to Christ. St John Chrysostom, preaching on this passage, notes that Paul humbles the proud teachers by making them belong to their hearers, and lifts the hearers up by giving them all things (in substance, Homilies on 1 Corinthians).

John 6:45–50 · Taught by God

Setting.

Jesus is still speaking in the synagogue at Capernaum. He has said that no one can come to him unless the Father draws him (6:44). Now he explains how the Father draws: by teaching. He quotes the prophets and then repeats, more plainly, the claim that gave offence: “I am the bread of life.”

Structure.

  • v. 45: it is written, “they shall all be taught by God”; whoever has learned from the Father comes to me.
  • v. 46: only the one who is from God has seen the Father.
  • v. 47: whoever believes has eternal life.
  • vv. 48–50: I am the bread of life; your ancestors ate manna and died; this bread is for not dying.

Key words.

  • didaktoi theou, taught by God: Jesus is quoting Isaiah 54:13, a promise for the restored Jerusalem: “All your children shall be taught by the Lord.” Jeremiah too promised a covenant in which God himself would teach every heart (Jeremiah 31:34).
  • egō eimi, I am: In John, Jesus often says “I am” with an image: bread, light, gate, shepherd, resurrection, way, vine. The words also echo the divine name revealed to Moses (Exodus 3:14), and sometimes stand alone (8:58).
  • manna, manna: The bread God gave Israel in the wilderness (Exodus 16). It was a true gift of God, but it fed bodily life only. Jesus does not despise it; he says it pointed beyond itself.

Verse by verse.

“It is written in the prophets, ‘And they shall all be taught by God.’ Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me.” (6:45)

The Father’s drawing is not blind force; it is teaching that the heart can hear and accept. Every true seeker of God is on the way to Christ.

“Not that anyone has seen the Father except the one who is from God; he has seen the Father.” (6:46)

The Prologue said the same: “No one has ever seen God. It is God the only Son, who is close to the Father’s heart, who has made him known” (1:18).

“Your ancestors ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died.” (6:49)

Jesus speaks to his own people of “your ancestors”, as Moses and the prophets did. The point is not blame but contrast: the manna sustained them for a time; the bread he gives sustains for ever.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that Jesus, because he has seen the Father, is the only one who knows him and can reveal him (CCC 151, quoting 6:46), and that the Church sees in the manna in the desert a prefiguring of the Eucharist, the true bread from heaven (CCC 1094). St Augustine taught that Christ is the teacher within: human teachers speak to the ear, but it is Christ dwelling in the heart who teaches us to recognise the truth (in substance, The Teacher).

For study and teaching

  • Paul says, “All things are yours… and you belong to Christ.” Which gifts in my life do I hold as if they were mine alone, rather than as gifts held in Christ?
  • Jesus says that all who are taught by God come to him. How do I let God teach me: in Scripture, in prayer, in the Church’s teaching, in the people he sends?
