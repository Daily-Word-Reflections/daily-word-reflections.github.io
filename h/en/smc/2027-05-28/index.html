Readings

  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 3:18–23 · The wisdom of this world is foolishness with God
  • Gospel John 6:45–50 · I am the bread of life

Word for the day

“Very truly, I tell you, whoever believes has eternal life. I am the bread of life.” John 6:47–48

Reflection

Yesterday we celebrated the Body and Blood of Christ; today the Gospel takes a step back in the same discourse at Capernaum and shows us the road that leads to the altar. Jesus quotes the prophets: “It is written in the prophets, ‘And they shall all be taught by God.’ Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me.” The words come from Isaiah's promise to the restored Jerusalem, “All your children shall be taught by the Lord” (Isaiah 54:13). Before anyone eats the bread of life, they must first listen and learn. That is why every Holy Qurbana has two tables: first the table of the Word, with the readings and the homily, and then the table of the Body and Blood. Jesus adds at once that this learning is not mere information: “Not that anyone has seen the Father except the one who is from God; he has seen the Father.” Only the Son has seen the Father face to face. To be taught by God is to be taught by the Son, who makes the Father known.

Then comes the simple, tremendous claim: “Very truly, I tell you, whoever believes has eternal life. I am the bread of life.” Jesus contrasts himself with the gift Israel remembered with gratitude: “Your ancestors ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died. This is the bread that comes down from heaven, so that one may eat of it and not die.” The manna was a true gift of God; it kept a whole people alive for forty years in the desert. But it fed bodies that would one day die. The bread that Jesus gives, which is himself, feeds a life that death cannot end. Notice that faith comes before eating in this passage: whoever believes has eternal life. The Eucharist is truly Christ whatever our state, but it bears fruit only in a heart that believes. We come to the altar as people who have listened, learned and believed, and the Lord who is received into a believing heart gives life that lasts.

Paul's words to the Corinthians sound at first like an insult: “If you think that you are wise in this age, you should become fools so that you may become wise. For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God.” The Corinthians were proud of their learning and had divided into parties: some for Paul, some for Apollos, some for Cephas. Paul says that boasting about human leaders is foolish, and then he turns the whole question upside down: “For all things are yours, whether Paul or Apollos or Cephas or the world or life or death or the present or the future, all belong to you, and you belong to Christ, and Christ belongs to God.” The apostles do not own the Church; they serve it. The believer does not belong to a party, a preacher or a movement, but to Christ. In our own day the temptation is the same. We can make idols of favourite preachers, of retreat centres, of a style of liturgy, of a family or a community, and forget that all these are gifts given to us so that we may belong more fully to Christ. To become a fool for Christ is to let God teach us, as children are taught, and to stop measuring the things of God by the wisdom of the market, the media or the examination hall.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we celebrated the Body and Blood of Christ. Today Jesus tells us how we come to that bread: “It is written in the prophets, ‘And they shall all be taught by God.’ Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me” (John 6:45).

Before we eat, we listen. That is why every Qurbana begins with the Word: the readings, the Gospel and the homily. God teaches us first, and then feeds us. Let us not arrive late for the readings, as if only the second part mattered.

Then Jesus says: “I am the bread of life.” The ancestors ate manna in the desert and still died. But whoever eats the bread that comes down from heaven will not die. Jesus gives a life that death cannot destroy. And he says first: whoever believes has eternal life. We come to the altar with faith.

Paul tells the proud Corinthians: the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. They were fighting over their favourite preachers, Paul, Apollos and Cephas. Paul says: all of them are yours, and you belong to Christ (1 Corinthians 3:21–23).

So let us become humble learners, like children. Let us not divide our parishes over preachers and movements. All the gifts of the Church are given to us, so that we may belong more fully to Christ, who is the bread of life. Amen.

For children

Children, in the desert God gave his people a special bread called manna. But Jesus says he is an even better bread: the bread of life! At the Holy Qurbana we first listen to God's word, and then we receive Jesus. So listen carefully to the readings next Sunday!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you alone have seen the Father, and you make him known to us. Teach us, as the prophets promised, so that we may hear and learn from the Father and come to you. You are the bread of life: give us faith to receive you, and life that death cannot end. Deliver us from the wisdom of this world that is foolishness with God, from boasting and from divisions, and keep us always yours, as you are the Father's. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
