Word for the day

“I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” John 10:11

Reflection

“When morning dawned, the angels urged Lot, saying, ‘Get up, take your wife and your two daughters who are here, or else you will be consumed in the punishment of the city.’” The prophet Ezekiel would later name the sin of Sodom as pride, excess of food, prosperous ease, and neglect of the poor and needy (Ezekiel 16:49), and Genesis has just shown its violence against strangers. Lot knows the city is doomed, and still: “But he lingered.” It is one of the most human sentences in the Bible. We too linger at the door of conversion, knowing what we must leave and not quite leaving it. And then mercy acts: “so the men seized him and his wife and his two daughters by the hand, the LORD being merciful to him, and they brought him out and left him outside the city.” God does not wait for Lot to be brave; he takes him by the hand. Genesis adds that God did this because “God remembered Abraham” (Genesis 19:29), whose bold prayer for the city comes just before this story (Genesis 18:23–32). The command is urgent: “Flee for your life; do not look back or stop anywhere in the Plain.” Lot bargains even now for a small town nearby, and God grants it. “But Lot's wife, behind him, looked back, and she became a pillar of salt.” She was out of the city, but her heart was still in it. Jesus gave his disciples a sermon of three words on her: “Remember Lot's wife” (Luke 17:32). One week before Hosanna Sunday, the Great Fast takes us by the hand and says: do not look back, do not stop on the plain; the hills of Easter are ahead.

The second reading closes the long account of the conquest with a sentence of pure gratitude: “Thus the LORD gave to Israel all the land that he swore to their ancestors that he would give them.” And then: “Not one of all the good promises that the LORD had made to the house of Israel had failed; all came to pass.” Joshua sends the tribes who had settled east of the Jordan back to their homes, but not without a charge: “to love the LORD your God, to walk in all his ways, to keep his commandments, and to hold fast to him, and to serve him with all your heart and with all your soul.” The promised land is not the end of the covenant but a place to keep it. Paul then turns to a quarrel in the church of Rome. Some believers ate everything; others, out of conscience, would not eat meat or drink wine; each side judged the other. Paul tells the strong: “Let us therefore no longer pass judgment on one another, but resolve instead never to put a stumbling block or hindrance in the way of another.” And he gives a principle that every Lent needs: “For the kingdom of God is not food and drink but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit.” Our families keep the Great Fast in different ways, from the strict fifty days of our grandparents to the lighter practice of busy young people abroad; the measure is not whose plate is emptier but whose heart is more loving. And Paul's warning reaches further: “it is good not to eat meat or drink wine or do anything that makes your brother or sister stumble.” Many families in Kerala have been wounded by drink. A father who gives up alcohol for the sake of his son, or a host who serves no liquor at a feast for the sake of one guest who struggles, is doing exactly what Paul asks: “Do not let what you eat cause the ruin of one for whom Christ died.”

In the Gospel Jesus speaks the words that the Church loves to hear at the threshold of his Passion: “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” He contrasts himself with the hired hand who “sees the wolf coming and leaves the sheep and runs away,” because “a hired hand does not care for the sheep.” A shepherd is known by what he does when danger comes. Then he speaks of a knowledge as deep as the life of God: “I know my own and my own know me, just as the Father knows me and I know the Father. And I lay down my life for the sheep.” His gaze goes beyond the flock in front of him: “I have other sheep that do not belong to this fold. I must bring them also, and they will listen to my voice. So there will be one flock, one shepherd.” The St Thomas Christians, one Church for many centuries, have been divided into several Churches since the seventeenth century; we pray with respect and hope for the day when all will be one flock. Finally Jesus says what makes the coming week intelligible: “No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord. I have power to lay it down, and I have power to take it up again.” The Passion we will celebrate is not an accident or a defeat; it is the free self-offering of the Son, who lays down his life in order to take it up again. Every bishop of our Church carries a shepherd's staff, and every priest stands at the altar to make present that one sacrifice. Let us pray for our shepherds, that they may not be hired hands, and let us listen this week for the voice of the Good Shepherd, who is already walking toward Jerusalem for us.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, at dawn the angels urge Lot to leave Sodom. But he lingered (Genesis 19:16). How often we too linger, knowing what we must leave. And the angels take him by the hand, the LORD being merciful to him, and bring him out. God's mercy does not wait for us to be brave.

They tell him: do not look back. But Lot's wife looked back, and became a pillar of salt (Genesis 19:26). She was out of the city, but her heart was still in it. Jesus says: remember Lot's wife (Luke 17:32). One week before Hosanna, what am I still looking back at?

Joshua tells Israel: not one of all the good promises of the LORD had failed; all came to pass (Joshua 21:45). And he gives them a charge: love the LORD, hold fast to him, and serve him with all your heart. God keeps every promise; let us keep faith with him.

Paul tells the Romans: the kingdom of God is not food and drink but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit (Romans 14:17). Let us not judge how others fast, and let us never be a stumbling block. If my drink or my habit makes my brother fall, love tells me to give it up.

Jesus says: I am the good shepherd; the good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep (John 10:11). No one takes his life from him; he lays it down of his own accord, and takes it up again. Next week we will see him do it.

Let us pray for our shepherds, our bishops and priests. Let us pray for the unity of all the sheep. And let us follow the voice of the Good Shepherd as he walks toward Jerusalem for us. Amen.

For children

Children, Lot was slow to leave a bad city, so the angels took him by the hand and led him out. God takes our hand too when we are slow to do good!

Jesus is our Good Shepherd. He knows each of us by name, and he loves us so much that he gave his life for us. Let us listen to his voice!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Good Shepherd, you lay down your life for the sheep of your own accord, and you take it up again. When we linger at the door of conversion, take us by the hand as the angels took Lot, and do not let us look back. Keep us faithful to your promises, every one of which is Yes in you. Make us careful never to place a stumbling block before those for whom you died, and gather all your scattered sheep into one flock under your one staff. As you walk toward Jerusalem, let us hear your voice and follow you. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
