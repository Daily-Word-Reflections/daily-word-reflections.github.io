Word for the day

“He whom God has sent speaks the words of God, for he gives the Spirit without measure.” John 3:34

Reflection

Yesterday the Church celebrated the Ascension of our Lord. The disciples stood on the Mount of Olives looking up, and two men in white asked them, “Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking up toward heaven?” (Acts 1:11). Then they went back to Jerusalem, to the upper room, and began to pray. Today's Gospel is a commentary on what they had just seen. “The one who comes from above is above all; the one who is of the earth belongs to the earth and speaks about earthly things. The one who comes from heaven is above all.” These words, at the end of the third chapter of John, describe Jesus as the One who came down from the Father; after the Ascension we hear them also as a description of the One who has gone back up. He came from above, and he has returned above. He is not one teacher among many, not a holy man who rose a little higher than the rest of us: he is the One who is above all. That is why the Church can call him Lord, and why the Holy Qurbana is offered to him and through him to the Father. The Gospel adds a sad line: “yet no one accepts his testimony.” But it also adds a promise: “Whoever has accepted his testimony has certified this, that God is true.” Every act of faith is a little seal pressed on the truth of God.

Then comes the verse that opens the nine days before Pentecost: “He whom God has sent speaks the words of God, for he gives the Spirit without measure.” The prophets received the Spirit for a time and for a task. The Son receives the Spirit without measure, and he gives the Spirit without measure. Before he went to the Father he had promised, “if I do not go away, the Advocate will not come to you; but if I go, I will send him to you” (John 16:7). The Ascension is not an absence; it is the condition of the gift. So the disciples do not sit idle in the upper room. They pray, “together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus” (Acts 1:14). The Church has always seen in those days the first novena, nine days of prayer with the Mother of the Lord, waiting for the Spirit who would make them a Church. The Gospel closes with the Father's love: “The Father loves the Son and has placed all things in his hands.” The hands that were nailed to the Cross now hold all things. On this First Friday we look also at the pierced side of the same Lord: from his open Heart came blood and water (John 19:34), and from that Heart the Spirit is poured out.

Paul writes to the Philippians as a man who has met that Lord and cannot stand still. “Not that I have already obtained this or have already reached the goal; but I press on to make it my own, because Christ Jesus has made me his own.” Paul does not boast of what he has reached. He speaks like a runner in the last lap: “forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal for the prize of the heavenly call of God in Christ Jesus.” The Greek words behind “the heavenly call” can mean “the upward call”: God calls us upward, where Christ has gone before us. The Ascension gives our whole life a direction. We do not run in circles; we run toward the place where our Head has already arrived. And Paul adds a gentle word for those who see things differently: “if you think differently about anything, this too God will reveal to you.” The one who runs toward Christ is patient with fellow runners. The novena begins today: let it be nine days of running toward the Lord, forgetting the failures behind us, and asking the Father for the Spirit who is given without measure.

Homily outline

Theme: “He whom God has sent speaks the words of God, for he gives the Spirit without measure.”

The day after the Ascension and the first day of the novena: the Lord above all, the Spirit poured out from his open Heart, and the upward call that sets our life in motion.

1. The One from above is above all

  • He came from the Father and has returned to him.
  • Not one teacher among many.
  • Faith as a seal on the truth of God.
“Lift up your heads, O gates! and be lifted up, O ancient doors! that the King of glory may come in.” Psalm 24:7
“The one who comes from above is above all; the one who is of the earth belongs to the earth and speaks about earthly things. The one who comes from heaven is above all.” John 3:31

Apply: Read one page of the Gospel slowly each day of the novena, and end with an act of faith in the Lord who speaks the words of God.

2. He gives the Spirit without measure

  • The Ascension as the condition of the gift.
  • Nine days with Mary in the upper room.
  • From the pierced Heart, rivers of living water.
“A new heart I will give you, and a new spirit I will put within you; and I will remove from your body the heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh.” Ezekiel 36:26
“He whom God has sent speaks the words of God, for he gives the Spirit without measure.” John 3:34

Apply: Pray “Come, Holy Spirit” each day until Pentecost, with Mary, and make the First Friday with confession and Holy Communion.

3. I press on toward the goal

  • Not yet arrived.
  • Forgetting what lies behind.
  • The upward call of God in Christ Jesus.
“But those who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength, they shall mount up with wings like eagles, they shall run and not be weary, they shall walk and not faint.” Isaiah 40:31
“I press on toward the goal for the prize of the heavenly call of God in Christ Jesus.” Philippians 3:14

Apply: Name one failure you keep carrying, give it to the Heart of Jesus, and do not take it with you into the novena.

The Lord who has gone up is above all, and from his open Heart he gives the Spirit without measure.

With Mary in the upper room, let us pray, forget what lies behind and run toward the upward call.

Take-home resolution: For these nine days, pray daily with Mary for the Holy Spirit, and let one old failure go into the Heart of Jesus.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday we celebrated the Ascension of our Lord. The disciples watched him go up, and then they went back to Jerusalem, to the upper room, to pray. Today we begin to pray with them.

The Gospel says: “The one who comes from above is above all.” Jesus came from the Father, and now he has returned to the Father. He is not one good teacher among many. He is above all. And “the Father loves the Son and has placed all things in his hands.” The hands that were nailed to the Cross now hold the whole world, and they hold each of us.

Then comes a beautiful line: “he gives the Spirit without measure.” Jesus had said, if I go, I will send the Advocate to you. So the Ascension is not a goodbye; it is the beginning of a gift. For nine days the apostles prayed with Mary, the mother of Jesus, waiting for that gift. This was the first novena of the Church. Let us pray it with them until Pentecost.

Today is also the First Friday. We look at the Heart of Jesus, pierced on the Cross, from which came blood and water. From that open Heart the Spirit is poured out on the Church. Let us come to confession, receive him in the Holy Qurbana, and ask his Heart for the gift of the Spirit.

And Paul tells us how to live these days: “forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal.” God's call is an upward call: where Christ has gone, we are called to follow. Let us not sit down on the track and cry over yesterday's fall. Let us get up and run toward him. Come, Holy Spirit! Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, yesterday Jesus went up to heaven. But he did not leave us alone! He promised to send the Holy Spirit.

For nine days the disciples prayed with Mary, waiting for the Holy Spirit. Can you pray with them? Every day until Pentecost, say: ‘Come, Holy Spirit, fill my heart!’

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you came from above and you have returned to the Father; you are above all, and all things are placed in your hands. As your disciples waited in the upper room with Mary your Mother, we wait for the Spirit whom you give without measure. Open the treasures of your Sacred Heart to us on this First Friday; make us forget what lies behind and run toward the upward call of God. Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of your faithful and kindle in them the fire of your love. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be glory for ever. Amen. Alleluia!
