Word for the day

“So faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ.” Romans 10:17

Reflection

In the synagogue of Capernaum the people are “astounded at his teaching, for he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.” The scribes quoted the teachers before them; Jesus speaks as the one whose word does what it says. A man with an unclean spirit cries out: “What have you to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are, the Holy One of God.” Jesus rebukes the spirit: “Be silent, and come out of him!” and the man is set free. The crowd asks: “What is this? A new teaching, with authority!” In Denha we see that the word of Jesus is not mere advice but power: it drives out evil and makes people whole.

Paul explains how this word reaches every nation: “For one believes with the heart and so is justified, and one confesses with the mouth and so is saved.” And “Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved.” But no one can call on a Lord of whom they have never heard. Paul asks: “And how are they to proclaim him unless they are sent?” and answers with Isaiah: “How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!” Then he sums it up: “So faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the word of Christ.” Our own faith came by hearing, through apostles, missionaries, parents and catechists who were sent. Now it is our turn: to open our mouths for Christ, to support those who are sent, and to let our feet carry the good news.

Full homily

Dear friends, in the synagogue at Capernaum the people were astounded: Jesus taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes (Mark 1:22). His word does not only explain; it acts.

A man with an unclean spirit cries out: I know who you are, the Holy One of God. Jesus says: be silent, and come out of him! And the man is freed (Mark 1:24–25). Evil recognises its Master. The people ask: what is this? A new teaching, with authority!

St Paul tells us how this word reaches us: everyone who calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved. But how can they call on one of whom they have never heard? And how are they to hear without someone to proclaim him? So faith comes from what is heard (Romans 10:13–14, 17).

Our faith came to us because someone was sent: an apostle, a missionary, a grandmother, a catechist. Now we are the ones sent. Let us believe with the heart, confess with the mouth, and let our feet carry the good news. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus' words are so strong that even bad spirits obey him! And somebody once told you about Jesus. This week you can tell a friend: Jesus loves you!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Holy One of God, you teach with authority and your word drives out every evil. Let your word live in my heart and on my lips; free me from all that binds me, and make my feet beautiful in carrying your good news to others. Amen.
