Second Monday of Denha

11 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 11:17–22 · When you come together as a church, there are divisions among you
  • Gospel John 17:20–26 · That they may all be one

Word for the day

“that they may all be one. As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” John 17:21

Reflection

At the Last Supper, before he goes out to his Passion, Jesus prays not only for the apostles but “also on behalf of those who will believe in me through their word”: that is, for us. And what does he ask? “That they may all be one. As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” The unity of Christians is not a matter of good organisation; its source and model is the communion of the Father and the Son. And it has a missionary purpose: when Christians are one, the world can recognise that Jesus comes from the Father. Denha is the season of manifestation, and our unity is one of the ways in which Christ is still made manifest.

Paul shows us the opposite picture. In Corinth, “when you come together as a church, I hear that there are divisions among you”. At the very meal of the Lord the better-off eat their own supper first, and “one goes hungry and another becomes drunk”. Paul is blunt: “Do you show contempt for the church of God and humiliate those who have nothing?” To divide the Body at the table of the Body is a contradiction. Our parishes and families are not free of such divisions: rich and poor, old families and newcomers, groups that no longer speak to one another, quarrels even about holy things. Jesus' prayer is for us. The Holy Qurbana is the sacrament of unity, and we cannot receive it with a clear heart while we despise a brother or sister.

Homily outline

Theme: “That they may all be one.”

Jesus' prayer for unity, and Paul's warning against division at the Lord's table.

1. Divisions at the table

  • The Lord's supper in Corinth.
  • One hungry, another drunk.
  • Contempt for the poor is contempt for the Church.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“For, to begin with, when you come together as a church, I hear that there are divisions among you; and to some extent I believe it.” 1 Corinthians 11:18

Apply: Make peace with one person before your next Holy Qurbana.

2. That they may all be one

  • Jesus prays for us.
  • The Father in the Son, the Son in the Father.
  • Unity is a gift before it is a task.
“I will make them one nation in the land, on the mountains of Israel; and one king shall be king over them all. Never again shall they be two nations, and never again shall they be divided into two kingdoms.” Ezekiel 37:22
“that they may all be one. As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” John 17:21

Apply: Pray daily for the unity of all Christians.

3. So that the world may believe

  • A light to the nations.
  • Unity makes Christ visible.
  • Division hides him.
“he says, “It is too light a thing that you should be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob and to restore the survivors of Israel; I will give you as a light to the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth.”” Isaiah 49:6
“I in them and you in me, that they may become completely one, so that the world may know that you have sent me and have loved them even as you have loved me.” John 17:23

Apply: Show kindness this week to someone outside your usual circle.

Unity is Jesus' own prayer and the Holy Spirit's gift.

A united Church makes Christ believable to the world.

Take-home resolution: Before the Unity Octave begins, be reconciled with one person and pray daily for Christian unity.

Full homily

Dear friends, the Corinthians were a lively, gifted community, yet Paul has to write to them: when you come together as a church, I hear that there are divisions among you (1 Corinthians 11:18). At the Lord's supper the better-off ate their own meal while the poor went hungry.

Paul asks: do you show contempt for the church of God and humiliate those who have nothing? (1 Corinthians 11:22). The meal of unity had become a sign of division.

In the Gospel Jesus prays for us, who believe through the apostles' word: that they may all be one, as you, Father, are in me and I am in you, so that the world may believe that you have sent me (John 17:21). Our unity is meant to be a window through which the world sees God.

In a week the Church begins the Week of Prayer for Christian Unity. Let us start at home: one phone call, one greeting, one apology before we next come to the altar. Amen.

For children

Children, before Jesus died, he prayed for you! He asked his Father that we would all be one big family. So be kind to everyone, even the child nobody plays with.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, on the night before you suffered you prayed that we may all be one, as you and the Father are one. Heal the divisions in my family, my parish and your Church, and never let me despise a brother or sister at your table, so that the world may believe that the Father has sent you. Amen.
