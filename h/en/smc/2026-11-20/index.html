Third Friday of the Dedication of the Church

20 November 2026 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle Hebrews 12:4–13 · Discipline yields the fruit of righteousness
  • Gospel John 15:1–8 · Whoever abides in me bears much fruit

Word for the day

“I am the vine, you are the branches. Those who abide in me and I in them bear much fruit, because apart from me you can do nothing.” John 15:5

Reflection

Hebrews reminds suffering Christians that “the Lord disciplines those whom he loves.” Discipline “always seems painful rather than pleasant at the time, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness.” Hardship received in faith is the Father's training, not his rejection.

Jesus uses the image of the vine: the Father prunes every fruitful branch so that it bears more fruit. Pruning hurts, but its purpose is fruit. And the secret of fruitfulness is simple: abide in me. Prayer, the Word and the Qurbana keep the branch joined to the vine; “apart from me you can do nothing.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Pruned to bear more fruit.”

Discipline from the Father's love and the vine that gives life to the branches.

1. The Lord reproves the one he loves

  • Discipline is a sign of sonship.
  • It yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness.
“My child, do not despise the LORD's discipline or be weary of his reproof, for the LORD reproves the one he loves, as a father the son in whom he delights.” Proverbs 3:11–12
“He removes every branch in me that bears no fruit. Every branch that bears fruit he prunes to make it bear more fruit.” John 15:2

Apply: Offer one present difficulty to God as his pruning.

2. The vine the Lord planted

  • Israel was God's vine brought out of Egypt.
  • Jesus is the true vine; we are his branches.
“You brought a vine out of Egypt; you drove out the nations and planted it.” Psalm 80:8
“I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinegrower.” John 15:1

Apply: Thank God for being grafted into Christ by baptism.

3. Abide and bear fruit

  • Without him we can do nothing.
  • Fruit glorifies the Father.
“Let me sing for my beloved my love-song concerning his vineyard: My beloved had a vineyard on a very fertile hill.” Isaiah 5:1
“My Father is glorified by this, that you bear much fruit and become my disciples.” John 15:8

Apply: Visit the Blessed Sacrament today.

The Father prunes in love; the Son gives life.

Let us abide in him and bear fruit.

Take-home resolution: Begin each day this week with: “Lord, without you I can do nothing.”

Full homily

Dear friends, the Letter to the Hebrews reminds suffering Christians that the Lord disciplines those whom he loves. Discipline always seems painful rather than pleasant at the time, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness (Hebrews 12:6, 11). Hardship received in faith is the Father's training.

Jesus says: I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinegrower. Every branch that bears fruit he prunes to make it bear more fruit. Abide in me; apart from me you can do nothing (John 15:1–5).

Pruning hurts, but it is a sign of the gardener's love. Offer Jesus one difficulty you are going through now, and stay united to the vine through daily prayer and the Holy Qurbana. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is the vine and we are the branches. If we stay close to Jesus we bear good fruit: kindness, patience and joy!

Prayer

Father, vinedresser of my soul, prune me with love, keep me joined to your Son, the true vine, and make me bear much fruit for your glory. Amen.
