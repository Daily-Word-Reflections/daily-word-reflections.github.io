Third Saturday of the Great Fast

27 February 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle Ephesians 4:25–32 · The evils to be put away
  • Gospel Matthew 21:28–32 · The kingdom of God belongs to those who repent

Word for the day

“and be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you.” Ephesians 4:32

Reflection

Paul writes about the most ordinary place of holiness: the way we talk to one another. “So then, putting away falsehood, let all of us speak the truth to our neighbors, for we are members of one another.” A lie told to a brother is like one hand deceiving the other. Then a realistic word about anger: “Be angry but do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger, and do not make room for the devil.” Anger is not always sin, but anger kept overnight becomes a room rented to the devil. The thief is not only to stop stealing but to work honestly “so as to have something to share with the needy.” And speech is to build up: “Let no evil talk come out of your mouths, but only what is useful for building up, as there is need, so that your words may give grace to those who hear.” Paul reminds the baptised that they carry a seal: “do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, with which you were marked with a seal for the day of redemption.” The Holy Spirit is grieved by bitterness at the dinner table as much as by great crimes. Many Syro-Malabar families keep a beautiful custom that answers this word: after the night prayer the children greet their parents and elders, “Praise be to Jesus Christ”, and receive the answer, “Now and for ever, Amen.” It is hard to go to sleep angry after saying that. Paul ends with the verse that could be written over every Christian home: “Put away from you all bitterness and wrath and anger and wrangling and slander, together with all malice, and be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you.”

In the Gospel Jesus tells a short parable in the temple. A father asks his two sons to work in the vineyard. One says, “I will not”; but later he changed his mind and went. The other answers politely, “I go, sir”; but he did not go. “Which of the two did the will of his father?” The answer is obvious, and it is aimed at people who had always said yes to God with their lips. “Truly I tell you, the tax collectors and the prostitutes are going into the kingdom of God ahead of you.” They had said no to God for years, but when John came preaching repentance, they changed their minds and believed. The religious leaders saw it happen, “and even after you saw it, you did not change your minds and believe him.” The parable has two warnings and one hope. Saying yes is not the same as going; and a past no is not the last word. The Great Fast is the time of “later he changed his mind.” On Saturday evenings in these weeks, the queues for confession grow longer in many parishes. Every person standing in that line is the son who once said no, and is now going into the vineyard.

Homily outline

Theme: “be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you.”

Truth and anger among members of one body, words that give grace, and the son who changed his mind.

1. Before the sun goes down

  • Members of one another.
  • Anger is not yet sin.
  • Do not make room for the devil.
“A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.” Proverbs 15:1
“Be angry but do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger,” Ephesians 4:26

Apply: End no day this week without settling the day's quarrels.

2. Words that give grace

  • No evil talk.
  • Words that build up.
  • The Spirit who sealed us.
“Set a guard over my mouth, O LORD; keep watch over the door of my lips.” Psalm 141:3
“Let no evil talk come out of your mouths, but only what is useful for building up, as there is need, so that your words may give grace to those who hear.” Ephesians 4:29

Apply: Say one word of encouragement to each member of your family today.

3. He changed his mind and went

  • I go, sir, and did not go.
  • I will not, and went.
  • Tax collectors going in first.
“Again, when the wicked turn away from the wickedness they have committed and do what is lawful and right, they shall save their life.” Ezekiel 18:27
“For John came to you in the way of righteousness and you did not believe him, but the tax collectors and the prostitutes believed him; and even after you saw it, you did not change your minds and believe him.” Matthew 21:32

Apply: Do today what you promised God and have not yet done.

We are members of one another: let the sun never set on our anger, and let our words give grace.

A yes without going is empty; a no can still become a yes. Now is the time to go into the vineyard.

Take-home resolution: Make your Lenten confession, if you have not yet made it, before the Fourth Sunday of the Great Fast.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul speaks to us about our everyday words. Speak the truth to your neighbours, for we are members of one another. Be angry but do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger (Ephesians 4:25–26).

Let no evil talk come out of your mouths, he says, but only words that build up and give grace to those who hear (Ephesians 4:29). Bitterness at the table grieves the Holy Spirit with whom we were sealed.

Jesus tells us of two sons. One says I will not, but later changes his mind and goes. The other says I go, sir, but does not go (Matthew 21:28–30). Which one did the will of his father?

Saying yes is not the same as going, and a past no is not the last word. The Great Fast is the time to change our minds. Let us make peace before the sun goes down, and go into the Father's vineyard today. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus told a story about two sons. One said no but then went to help his father. The other said yes but never went! Let us not only say yes to God, let us do it.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you sealed us with your Holy Spirit for the day of redemption. Keep us from grieving him with bitterness, anger and slander; let our words give grace to those who hear them, and let the sun never set on our anger. Where we have said yes to you and not gone, and where we have said no, change our minds and send us into your vineyard. Amen.
