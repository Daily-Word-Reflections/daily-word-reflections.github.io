Word for the day

“When they had finished breakfast, Jesus said to Simon Peter, “Simon son of John, do you love me more than these?” He said to him, “Yes, Lord; you know that I love you.” Jesus said to him, “Feed my lambs.”” John 21:15

Reflection

On the second Friday of Denha the Church honours the two pillars who made Christ known to the world: Peter and Paul. Peter had denied Jesus three times by a charcoal fire. Now, by another charcoal fire at the lake, the risen Lord asks him three times: “Simon son of John, do you love me?” Each answer is followed by a mission: feed my lambs, tend my sheep, feed my sheep. Jesus does not choose a perfect man; he chooses a forgiven one, and builds his flock on love. Then he foretells that Peter too will be led where he does not wish to go: martyrdom in Rome.

Paul tells his own story in Galatians: once he “was violently persecuting the church of God”, but God, “who had set me apart before I was born and called me through his grace, was pleased to reveal his Son to me, so that I might proclaim him among the Gentiles.” In Acts, Peter preaches to the Gentile household of Cornelius: “God shows no partiality.” Two very different men, one denier and one persecutor, both transformed by grace, both martyred in Rome. Through them Israel's calling to be “a priestly kingdom and a holy nation” reaches all nations, including us.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the Fridays of Denha the Church honours the great witnesses of Christ. Today: Peter and Paul, the pillars of the Church.

Peter denied Jesus three times beside a charcoal fire. After the Resurrection, beside another charcoal fire at the lake, Jesus asks him three times: Simon son of John, do you love me? Peter answers: yes, Lord; you know that I love you. And Jesus says: feed my lambs (John 21:15). Jesus builds his Church not on a perfect man but on a forgiven man who loves.

Paul tells us he once violently persecuted the Church. But God, who had set him apart before he was born and called him through his grace, revealed his Son to him (Galatians 1:15–16). Grace turned the persecutor into the apostle of the nations.

In Acts Peter preaches to a Roman family: God shows no partiality; everyone who believes receives forgiveness of sins through his name (Acts 10:34, 43). Israel's calling to be a priestly kingdom and a holy nation (Exodus 19:6) reaches all peoples.

Both died as martyrs in Rome. Let us pray today for the Pope, Peter's successor, and for our Major Archbishop. And let us answer Jesus' question from our hearts: Lord, you know that I love you. Amen.

For children

Children, Peter once said he did not know Jesus. But Jesus forgave him and asked: do you love me? Peter said yes! Jesus forgives us too. Tell Jesus today: I love you!

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you forgave Peter and called Paul by grace, and made them pillars of your Church. Through their prayers, make us love you above all, feed your flock, and proclaim you to all nations. Bless our Holy Father the Pope and all our shepherds. Amen.
