Word for the day

“And Jesus increased in wisdom and in years, and in divine and human favor.” Luke 2:52

Reflection

The two Sundays of Nativity look at the Child growing up. Today's readings are all set in the presence of God. Moses comes down from forty days on Sinai and does not know “that the skin of his face shone because he had been talking with God.” The boy Samuel, sleeping in the temple where the ark was, hears the Lord call him three times, until he learns to answer: “Speak, for your servant is listening.” Those who stay near God are changed by him and learn to listen.

In the Gospel Jesus, at twelve, goes up to Jerusalem for the Passover and stays behind. After three days of anxious searching, Mary and Joseph find him in the Temple, “sitting among the teachers, listening to them and asking them questions.” His first recorded words are: “Why were you searching for me? Did you not know that I must be in my Father's house?” The Son knows who his Father is. Yet he goes down to Nazareth “and was obedient to them”, and grows “in wisdom and in years, and in divine and human favor.” Holiness and ordinary obedience belong together.

St Paul turns this into a programme for every Christian: “present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship. Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your minds.” The Temple is now our life offered to God. The feast of St Kuriakose Elias Chavara today shows a son of our Church who lived this: priest, founder, educator, a man of prayer whose face, like Moses', shone with God among his people.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, on this Second Sunday of Nativity all our readings take place in the presence of God. Moses comes down from Mount Sinai after forty days, and the skin of his face shines because he has been talking with God (Exodus 34:29). He does not even know it.

The boy Samuel sleeps in the temple where the ark of God is. Three times the Lord calls him, and three times he runs to old Eli. Finally he learns to say: speak, for your servant is listening (1 Samuel 3:10). Those who stay near God are changed by him, and they learn to listen.

In the Gospel Jesus is twelve. After the Passover he stays behind in Jerusalem. For three days Mary and Joseph search for him with great anxiety. Every parent here knows that feeling. They find him in the Temple, sitting among the teachers, listening and asking questions.

His first words in the Gospel are: why were you searching for me? Did you not know that I must be in my Father's house? (Luke 2:49). At twelve, Jesus knows who his Father is. And yet he goes down with them to Nazareth and is obedient to them, and grows in wisdom and in years and in favour with God and people (Luke 2:51–52). Holiness does not despise ordinary family life; it is lived there.

St Paul tells us how to live this: present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship. Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your minds (Romans 12:1–2). Our whole life becomes worship.

Today we also remember St Kuriakose Elias Chavara, a son of our Church who stayed close to God in prayer and built schools beside churches so that children could grow in wisdom. Let us ask for his spirit: to listen to God, to love the Father's house, and to serve our families with obedience and love. Amen.

For children

Children, when Jesus was twelve, Mary and Joseph lost him for three days! They found him in the Temple, learning about God. Then he went home and obeyed them.

Let us love to come to church, the Father's house, and let us obey our parents with a smile.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, at twelve you were found in your Father's house, listening and asking. Then you went down to Nazareth and were obedient. Teach us to listen like Samuel, to shine with God's presence like Moses, and to offer our lives as a living sacrifice. Through the prayers of St Kuriakose Elias Chavara, make our homes and our parish places where you grow in us. Glory to you with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
