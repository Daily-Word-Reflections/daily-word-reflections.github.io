Word for the day

“The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor.” Luke 4:18

Reflection

Three days before the feast of Denha the Church already looks toward the Jordan, where the Spirit will come down upon Jesus. Today's readings speak of God the liberator and of those he sends. At the burning bush God tells Moses: “I have observed the misery of my people who are in Egypt; I have heard their cry on account of their taskmasters. Indeed, I know their sufferings, and I have come down to deliver them.” Then he sends a shepherd who protests, “Who am I that I should go to Pharaoh”, and who receives the only answer that matters: “I will be with you.”

Isaiah goes further. God calls a pagan king, Cyrus of Persia, his anointed, takes him by the right hand and says: “I call you by your name, I surname you, though you do not know me.” If God can use a foreign king to bring his people home from exile, nothing lies outside his reach. Yet Moses and Cyrus are only signs. In the synagogue of Nazareth Jesus unrolls the scroll of Isaiah and reads: “The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor.” Then he sits down and, with every eye fixed on him, says: “Today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing.” The true Anointed, the Mshiha, has come. The freedom he brings is freedom from sin and death, and it begins with the poor.

St Paul shows how this freedom reaches every generation. He reminds Timothy of the faith he received at home: “from childhood you have known the sacred writings that are able to instruct you for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus.” Faith passes from parent to child and from teacher to pupil, and it holds firm through persecution. St Kuriakose Elias Chavara, whose feast falls today, lived this: a son of Kainakary who, like Moses, heard the cry of his people, and who asked that a school stand beside every church, so that every child might come to know the Scriptures and the way of salvation.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, in three days we will celebrate Denha, when the Spirit came down upon Jesus at the Jordan. Today's readings prepare us by showing God as the one who hears, comes down and sets free.

Moses is keeping sheep in the wilderness when he sees a bush that burns without being consumed. From the fire God calls him by name and says: I have observed the misery of my people; I have heard their cry; I know their sufferings, and I have come down to deliver them (Exodus 3:7–8). Our God is not deaf to the cries of his people.

Moses protests: who am I that I should go to Pharaoh? God does not tell him how gifted he is. He says only: I will be with you (Exodus 3:11–12). That promise is enough for every mission, every vocation, every difficult task.

In Isaiah God does something more surprising still. He calls Cyrus, a foreign king, his anointed, and says: I call you by your name, though you do not know me (Isaiah 45:4). God can use anyone to save his people. But Cyrus is only a shadow. In Nazareth Jesus stands up in the synagogue and reads: the Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. Then he says: today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing (Luke 4:18, 21). The true Anointed, the Mshiha, is here.

How does this good news reach us? St Paul reminds Timothy that from childhood he has known the sacred writings that instruct him for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus (2 Timothy 3:15). Faith comes to us through families, catechism teachers and parishes, from one generation to the next.

Today we also celebrate St Kuriakose Elias Chavara. Like Moses, he heard the cry of his people, the poor and the unlettered, and he answered by asking that a school stand beside every church. Let us ask for his spirit: to hear the cry of the poor, to trust the promise I will be with you, and to hand on the word of God to our children. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus went to the synagogue in his home town and read from the Bible: God has sent me to bring good news to the poor! Then he said: today it has come true.

St Chavara wanted every child to learn to read, so that they could know God's word. Let us read a little of the Bible with our family this week.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, Anointed of the Father, the Spirit of the Lord is upon you, and you came to bring good news to the poor and release to the captives. You hear our cry as God heard Israel at the burning bush. Send us, as you sent Moses, with the promise that you will be with us; keep us in the faith we learned from childhood; and through the prayers of St Kuriakose Elias Chavara make our homes and schools places where your word is loved. Glory to you with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
