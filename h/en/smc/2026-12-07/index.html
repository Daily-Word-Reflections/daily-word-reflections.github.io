Word for the day

“If in my name you ask me for anything, I will do it.” John 14:14

Reflection

Paul's goal for the Church is maturity: “until all of us come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to maturity, to the measure of the full stature of Christ.” No longer “children, tossed to and fro”, but “speaking the truth in love, we must grow up in every way into him who is the head.”

Jesus promises that the one who believes in him will do the works he does, and even greater, and that whatever we ask in his name he will do. St Ambrose, bishop of Milan, lived this: chosen bishop while still a catechumen, he grew into a pastor of great faith who stood firm before emperors and led St Augustine to baptism.

Full homily

Dear friends, St Paul has a beautiful goal for the Church: that all of us may come to the unity of the faith and the knowledge of the Son of God, to maturity, to the measure of the full stature of Christ (Ephesians 4:13). God wants us to grow up.

Paul describes spiritual childishness: being tossed to and fro and blown about by every wind of doctrine, by people's trickery (Ephesians 4:14). Today the winds blow through our phones: forwarded messages, half-truths, aggressive opinions, new teachings every day. An immature faith is pushed around by every wave.

What is the alternative? Speaking the truth in love, we must grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ (Ephesians 4:15). Mature faith is rooted in Christ and the Church's teaching, and it speaks truth always with love: not harsh, not timid.

In the Gospel Jesus promises: whoever believes in me will do the works that I do and, in fact, will do greater works than these; if in my name you ask me for anything, I will do it (John 14:12–14). Asking in Jesus' name means asking as he would ask: for the Father's glory and our true good.

Today we remember St Ambrose, bishop of Milan. He was a mature Christian leader: deeply rooted in Scripture, brave enough to correct the emperor Theodosius after a massacre, gentle enough to welcome his repentance, and wise enough to lead the young Augustine to faith.

How do we grow to maturity? By regular prayer, by knowing the Scriptures, by learning the Catechism, by receiving the sacraments, and by belonging to a community. Faith that is only emotion or only custom stays childish.

This Nombu, let us choose one way to grow: read one chapter of a Gospel each day, join a Bible study, or read the Catechism of the Catholic Church on Christmas. And let us always speak the truth in love. Amen.
