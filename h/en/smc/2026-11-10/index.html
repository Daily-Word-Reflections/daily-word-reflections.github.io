Full homily

Dear friends, near the end of his life Peter remembers the mountain: we had been eyewitnesses of his majesty; we ourselves heard this voice come from heaven while we were with him on the holy mountain (2 Peter 1:16–18). The faith of the Church rests on eyewitnesses and on the prophetic word.

That word is like a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts (2 Peter 1:19). On the mountain of the Transfiguration Jesus' face shone, and a voice came from the cloud: this is my Son, my Chosen; listen to him (Luke 9:35).

In the dark places of life we have God's word as our lamp. The Father's command, listen to him, is for us too. This week let us read the Gospel for ten minutes each day and quietly contemplate the face of Jesus. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Peter, near the end of his life, remembers the mountain where he saw the glory of Christ and heard the Father's voice. Luke tells the same event: Jesus at prayer, transfigured, speaking with Moses and Elijah about his “departure” at Jerusalem. The Epistle is the witness; the Gospel is the scene. The Church's faith rests on apostles who saw and heard, and on Scriptures the Spirit inspired.

2 Peter 1:12–21 · We had been eyewitnesses

Setting.

2 Peter is written as Peter's testament: a farewell letter in which a dying leader reminds his people of what matters (compare Paul's farewell in Acts 20 and 2 Timothy 4). Its readers were troubled by teachers who mocked the hope of Christ's coming (3:3–4). Many scholars think it was written after Peter's death by a disciple, in his name and tradition; the Church receives it as inspired Scripture and as the witness of Peter.

Structure.

  • vv. 12–15: Peter's purpose: to remind them before his death.
  • vv. 16–18: not myths but eyewitness: the Transfiguration.
  • vv. 19–21: the prophetic word, a lamp in a dark place, given by the Holy Spirit.

Key words.

  • skēnōma, tent, body: Peter calls his body a tent (vv. 13–14), soon to be folded away. The NRSV says “this body”; the Malayalam POC keeps the image of the tent.
  • epoptai, eyewitnesses: A rare word, used in Greek religion for those who had reached the highest grade of initiation and had “seen” the mysteries. Peter's mystery is not a secret rite: he saw Christ's majesty with his own eyes.
  • phōsphoros, morning star: Literally “light-bearer”, the morning star that announces the day: Christ rising in the heart (compare Revelation 22:16).

Verse by verse.

“For we did not follow cleverly devised myths when we made known to you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but we had been eyewitnesses of his majesty.” (1:16)

Peter sets myth against eyewitness: the Christian faith rests on what happened in history.

“We ourselves heard this voice come from heaven, while we were with him on the holy mountain.” (1:18)

The mountain is “holy” because God revealed himself there, as on Sinai. Later tradition identified it with Mount Tabor.

“no prophecy ever came by human will, but men and women moved by the Holy Spirit spoke from God.” (1:21)

“Moved” (pheromenoi) is the word used for a ship driven by the wind (Acts 27:15, 17). The human authors truly wrote, but the Spirit filled their sails.

How the Church reads it.

The Second Vatican Council cites this passage when it teaches that the books of Scripture were written under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit and have God as their author, while the human writers used their own powers as true authors (Dei Verbum 11; CCC 105–106). Verse 20 also gives a rule for reading: Scripture is to be read within the living Tradition of the whole Church, not by private interpretation alone (Dei Verbum 12; CCC 113).

Luke 9:28–36 · This is my Son, my Chosen

Setting.

About eight days earlier Peter had confessed Jesus as the Messiah of God, and Jesus had begun to speak of his suffering and of taking up the cross (9:18–27). The Transfiguration confirms both: Jesus is the Son in glory, and the way to glory goes through Jerusalem. Only Luke says that Jesus went up to pray and was transfigured while praying, and only Luke tells us what Moses and Elijah spoke about.

Structure.

  • vv. 28–29: Jesus goes up to pray; his face and clothes change.
  • vv. 30–31: Moses and Elijah speak of his departure.
  • vv. 32–33: the sleepy disciples; Peter's three dwellings.
  • vv. 34–36: the cloud, the voice, and Jesus alone.

Key words.

  • exodos, departure: Literally “exodus”, a going out. Jesus' death, resurrection and ascension are the new Exodus, to be “accomplished” at Jerusalem.
  • skēnai, dwellings, tents: Booths like those of the Feast of Tabernacles. Peter wants to make the moment last; but the true tent of God's presence will be Christ's own glorified body.
  • episkiazō, to overshadow: The cloud overshadows them as the power of the Most High overshadowed Mary (Luke 1:35), and as the cloud covered the tent of meeting (Exodus 40:35, in the Greek Bible).

Verse by verse.

“And while he was praying, the appearance of his face changed, and his clothes became dazzling white.” (9:29)

Prayer is the place of transfiguration. Luke shows Jesus praying at every turning point: at his baptism, before choosing the Twelve, before Peter's confession (3:21; 6:12; 9:18).

“They appeared in glory and were speaking of his departure, which he was about to accomplish at Jerusalem.” (9:31)

Moses and Elijah stand for the Law and the Prophets, and both speak of the Cross.

“Then from the cloud came a voice that said, “This is my Son, my Chosen; listen to him!”” (9:35)

“My Chosen” recalls the Servant of Isaiah 42:1; “listen to him” recalls Moses' promise of a prophet like himself (Deuteronomy 18:15).

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism sees in the Transfiguration a foretaste of Christ's glorious coming and a strengthening of the disciples before the Passion (CCC 554–556, 568). It quotes St Thomas Aquinas: the whole Trinity appeared, the Father in the voice, the Son in the man, the Spirit in the shining cloud (CCC 555). The voice from the cloud gives the Church her daily task: to listen to the Son.

For study and teaching

  • Peter writes so that his readers may “recall these things” after his death. Who handed on the faith to me, and how am I handing it on?
  • Jesus was transfigured while praying. What place does quiet prayer have in my day?
