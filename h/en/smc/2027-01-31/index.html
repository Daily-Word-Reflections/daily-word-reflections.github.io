Fifth Sunday of Denha

31 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Readings

  • First Reading Leviticus 4:13–21 · Forgiveness through sacrifice
  • Second Reading Daniel 9:1–9 · To the Lord our God belong mercy and forgiveness
  • Epistle Colossians 1:12–19 · Redemption and forgiveness through the Son
  • Gospel Mark 2:1–12 · The Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins

Word for the day

“When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, “Son, your sins are forgiven.”” Mark 2:5

Reflection

Today's readings are a single long meditation on forgiveness. In Leviticus, when the whole congregation sins unknowingly, a bull is offered, its blood sprinkled before the veil, and “the priest shall make atonement for them, and they shall be forgiven.” Israel learned that sin is serious and that forgiveness is costly. In exile Daniel reads Jeremiah, fasts in sackcloth, and confesses for all the people: “we have sinned and done wrong… To the Lord our God belong mercy and forgiveness, for we have rebelled against him.” Forgiveness is not owed; it belongs to God's mercy.

Then in Capernaum the house is so crowded that four friends dig through the roof and lower a paralysed man on his mat in front of Jesus. “When Jesus saw their faith”, the faith of the friends, “he said to the paralytic, ‘Son, your sins are forgiven.’” The scribes are right in their premise: “Who can forgive sins but God alone?” They are wrong in their conclusion. Jesus answers: “so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”, and the man stands up, takes his mat and walks out before them all. The God of the sin offering and of Daniel's prayer is present in person.

Paul tells the Colossians what this means: the Father “has rescued us from the power of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of his beloved Son, in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins. He is the image of the invisible God.” In Denha Christ is revealed as the one who forgives. That authority he gave to his Church: “If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them.” Every confession is Capernaum again. And like the four friends, we are called to carry to Christ those who cannot come by themselves. Today we also honour St John Bosco, who carried countless young people to Christ.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, today's readings speak of forgiveness from beginning to end. In Leviticus, when the people sin, a bull is offered and the priest shall make atonement for them, and they shall be forgiven (Leviticus 4:20). Sin is serious; forgiveness is costly.

In exile Daniel fasts and confesses for all the people: we have sinned and done wrong. To the Lord our God belong mercy and forgiveness (Daniel 9:5, 9). Forgiveness is never owed; it is God's mercy.

In Capernaum four friends dig through a roof to lower a paralysed man before Jesus. When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic: Son, your sins are forgiven (Mark 2:5). The scribes ask: who can forgive sins but God alone? They are right; and God is standing in front of them.

Jesus says: so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins, stand up, take your mat and go home (Mark 2:10–11). And the man walks out before them all.

Paul says: in the beloved Son we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins; he is the image of the invisible God (Colossians 1:14–15). Christ gave this authority to his Church. Every confession is Capernaum again.

Let us go to confession, and like the four friends, let us carry to Christ those who cannot come alone, as St John Bosco carried so many young people. Amen.

For children

Children, four friends carried a sick man to Jesus, even through the roof! Jesus forgave him and he could walk again.

Who can you help bring closer to Jesus this week?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, image of the invisible God, you have authority on earth to forgive sins. Forgive us, raise us up from all that paralyses us, and make us like the four friends who carried the sick to you. Through the prayers of St John Bosco, help us bring the young to you. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
