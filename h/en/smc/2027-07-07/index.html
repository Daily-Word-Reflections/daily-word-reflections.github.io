Word for the day

“My sheep hear my voice. I know them, and they follow me.” John 10:27

Reflection

Paul's letter to Timothy reaches its climax in a charge, and the charge begins with a title: “But as for you, man of God.” In the Old Testament this is the name of Moses, Samuel, Elijah and the prophets, people who belonged wholly to God. Timothy is a young bishop, and the title reminds him who he is. What follows is a list of what to run from and what to run after: “shun all this”, that is, the love of money which Paul has just called the root of all kinds of evil, and “pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, gentleness”. Six words, and not one of them is loud. Then the athlete's picture: “Fight the good fight of the faith; take hold of the eternal life, to which you were called and for which you made the good confession in the presence of many witnesses.” The fight is not against people. It is the long struggle to stay faithful, and the prize is already in the hand, because eternal life is something to be taken hold of today.

Paul sets Timothy's confession beside the confession of Christ himself, “who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate made the good confession”. Before the Roman governor Jesus said that he was a King, and that his kingdom was not of this world, and he said it knowing the cost. The charge ends in a hymn that Paul may have taken from the liturgy of his Church: Christ will be manifested “at the right time”; God is “the blessed and only Sovereign, the King of kings and Lord of lords. It is he alone who has immortality and dwells in unapproachable light, whom no one has ever seen or can see.” We stand at the beginning of Kaitha, the season in which the Church looks at her growth and waits for her Lord. The reading reminds us that the harvest belongs to the One who dwells in unapproachable light, and that our task is to keep the commandment “without spot or blame” until he appears.

In the Gospel Jesus walks in the portico of Solomon at the feast of the Dedication, the winter feast in which the Jewish people remember the cleansing and re-dedication of the Temple. Some of the leaders gather round him: “How long will you keep us in suspense? If you are the Messiah, tell us plainly.” Jesus has already told them, and he points them to his works: “The works that I do in my Father’s name testify to me.” Then he says something hard: “you do not believe, because you do not belong to my sheep.” This is not a rejection of the Jewish people, from whom Jesus, Mary and the apostles came; John's Gospel, in speaking of “the Jews”, usually means these particular leaders in Jerusalem, and the Church teaches that what happened in the Passion cannot be charged against all the Jews of that time, still less against the Jews of today (Nostra Aetate 4). The sentence names a danger for every listener, including us: one can stand beside Jesus, ask him questions, and never listen.

Then comes the shepherd's voice. “My sheep hear my voice. I know them, and they follow me. I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one will snatch them out of my hand.” Hearing, being known, following: three steps, and the whole of Christian life is in them. In the villages of Kerala a shepherd's call is not common now, but every mother knows how a child in a crowded market can pick out one voice from a hundred. Our phones fill every hour with voices, and many of them sound confident. The sheep of Christ learn his voice in the Gospel read at the Holy Qurbana, in daily prayer and in the family Rosary, and then they can tell it from the others. St Gregory the Great taught that when the Lord says “I know my sheep” he means “I love them”. We are not held by our own grip, which grows tired, but by the hand that no one can pry open.

Homily outline

Theme: “My sheep hear my voice. I know them, and they follow me.”

The man of God who pursues righteousness, the good confession made before many witnesses, and the voice of the Shepherd that no one can silence.

1. Man of God, pursue righteousness

  • Shun the love of money.
  • Six quiet virtues.
  • The good fight of the faith.
“Whoever pursues righteousness and kindness will find life and honor.” Proverbs 21:21
“But as for you, man of God, shun all this; pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, gentleness.” 1 Timothy 6:11

Apply: Practise one of the six virtues with one particular person this week.

2. The good confession

  • Many witnesses.
  • Christ before Pilate.
  • Keep the commandment without spot or blame.
“I will also speak of your decrees before kings, and shall not be put to shame.” Psalm 119:46
“Fight the good fight of the faith; take hold of the eternal life, to which you were called and for which you made the good confession in the presence of many witnesses.” 1 Timothy 6:12

Apply: Say one word of faith, kindly and clearly, where it is usually kept silent.

3. The voice of the Shepherd

  • Hear, be known, follow.
  • The works testify.
  • No one will snatch them from his hand.
“Know that the Lord is God. It is he that made us, and we are his; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.” Psalm 100:3
“My sheep hear my voice. I know them, and they follow me.” John 10:27

Apply: Read the Gospel of the day each morning before looking at the phone.

Faith is a fight fought with quiet virtues, and a confession made by a life.

The Shepherd knows his sheep, and no one can snatch them from his hand.

Take-home resolution: This week, practise one virtue, read the Gospel each morning, and follow his voice.

Full homily

Dear friends, in today's Gospel some people ask Jesus: “If you are the Messiah, tell us plainly.” Jesus answers that he has already told them by his works. The problem was not that they had not heard; it was that they were not ready to listen.

Then Jesus says something very beautiful: “My sheep hear my voice. I know them, and they follow me” (John 10:27). Three things: they hear, he knows them, they follow. This is the whole of the Christian life. Listening in prayer, being known and loved by the Lord, and walking behind him.

Saint Paul tells Timothy, “Fight the good fight of the faith; take hold of the eternal life” (1 Timothy 6:12). The fight is not with people. It is the daily struggle to stay faithful: to be gentle when we want to shout, to be honest when it costs us, to pray when we are tired.

Many voices call us today: the phone, advertisements, fear, our own pride. But the sheep who spend time with the Shepherd know his voice. Let us read the Gospel every day and say the family prayer together, and we will know which voice to follow.

And the Lord promises: “No one will snatch them out of my hand” (John 10:28). We may be weak, but his hand is strong. Let us hold to him in this season of Kaitha, and he will bring us home. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is the Good Shepherd, and you are his little sheep! A shepherd knows each sheep by name, and the sheep know his voice. Speak with Jesus every morning and every night, and you will learn his voice. Then, when someone asks you to do something wrong, you will hear Jesus in your heart say: ‘Come this way, little sheep.’ And nobody can take you out of his hand!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Good Shepherd, you spoke plainly to those who asked, and you still speak to us in your word. Open our ears to your voice among the many voices of the world; let us hear you, be known by you, and follow you. Make us men and women of God, pursuing righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance and gentleness. Teach us to fight the good fight of the faith and to make the good confession by the way we live. Hold us in your hand, from which no one can snatch us, until the day when you are revealed, King of kings and Lord of lords, who with the Father and the Holy Spirit live and reign for ever. Amen.
