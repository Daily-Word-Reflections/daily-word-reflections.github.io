Third Sunday of the Great Fast

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading Genesis 7:6–24 · Noah and his family are saved
  • Second Reading Joshua 5:13–6:5 · The walls of wickedness fall by the Lord's power
  • Epistle Romans 7:14–25 · Who will rescue me?
  • Gospel Matthew 20:17–28 · Discipleship is sharing in his sufferings

Word for the day

“just as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.”” Matthew 20:28

Reflection

The flood comes in the first reading with terrible exactness: “In the six hundredth year of Noah's life, in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month, on that day all the fountains of the great deep burst forth, and the windows of the heavens were opened. The rain fell on the earth forty days and forty nights.” The number is not an accident. Forty days of rain, forty years in the wilderness, forty days of Moses on the mountain, forty days of Jesus in the desert: in Scripture forty is the length of God's great washings and testings, and the Great Fast is our share in it. Before the waters rise, one small sentence holds the whole story: “and the LORD shut him in.” Noah did not seal the ark himself; God closed the door. Inside, the ark “rose high above the earth”, and “the ark floated on the face of the waters.” Outside, “only Noah was left, and those that were with him in the ark.” The Fathers saw in the ark an image of the Church, and the Catechism still says that the Church is prefigured by Noah's ark, “which alone saves from the flood” (Catechism 845). In the season of the fast we are not saved by our own swimming. We are saved by being inside, where the Lord has shut us in.

In the second reading Joshua stands near Jericho and sees a man with a drawn sword. He asks the natural question: “Are you one of us, or one of our adversaries?” The answer is: “Neither; but as commander of the army of the LORD I have now come.” The question is not whether God is on our side, but whether we are on his. Joshua falls on his face and is told: “Remove the sandals from your feet, for the place where you stand is holy.” Many of our people still leave their footwear at the door of the church, and in that small gesture they say what Joshua learned: before we fight any battle, we must first bow. Then the strategy for Jericho is given, and it is not a military one. The people are to walk round the city, with seven priests carrying seven trumpets before the ark, once a day for six days and seven times on the seventh, and “the wall of the city will fall down flat.” A procession, trumpets, the ark, patience: it looks like liturgy, not war. The walls of sin in our lives, the habits we have built up over years, do not fall to one angry blow. They fall when we walk around them again and again in prayer, carrying the Lord with us.

Paul knows such walls from the inside. “I do not understand my own actions. For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate.” “For I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I do.” Every honest Christian has said these words in some form in the third week of a fast. Paul's cry breaks out: “Wretched man that I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death?” And at once the answer: “Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!” The rescue is not a technique but a Person. In the Gospel that Person is walking up to Jerusalem, and for the third time he tells the twelve what awaits him: he will be handed over, mocked, flogged and crucified, “and on the third day he will be raised.” At that very moment the mother of James and John asks for the two best seats in his kingdom. Jesus does not mock her; he asks the brothers, “Are you able to drink the cup that I am about to drink?” They say, “We are able,” and he answers, “You will indeed drink my cup.” James would be the first of the twelve to die for him. Then Jesus turns the world's idea of greatness upside down: “whoever wishes to be great among you must be your servant, and whoever wishes to be first among you must be your slave; just as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.” Halfway through the Great Fast, this is the question: not which seat we shall have, but whether we will drink his cup and serve.

Homily outline

Theme: “just as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.”

The ark shut by God, the bare feet of Joshua, the cry of Paul, and the cup of the Servant.

1. The LORD shut him in

  • Forty days and forty nights.
  • God closed the door.
  • Saved inside the ark.
“And those that entered, male and female of all flesh, went in as God had commanded him; and the LORD shut him in.” Genesis 7:16
“For as in those days before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day Noah entered the ark, and they knew nothing until the flood came and swept them all away, so too will be the coming of the Son of Man.” Matthew 24:38–39

Apply: Do not miss Sunday Holy Qurbana for the rest of the Great Fast.

2. Remove your sandals

  • Are you for us or for our adversaries?
  • Bow before you fight.
  • Walls that fall by procession.
“The commander of the army of the LORD said to Joshua, “Remove the sandals from your feet, for the place where you stand is holy.” And Joshua did so.” Joshua 5:15
“By faith the walls of Jericho fell after they had been encircled for seven days.” Hebrews 11:30

Apply: Pray every day for seven days about the habit of sin that has resisted you longest.

3. Who will rescue me?

  • I do not do what I want.
  • The war within.
  • Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ.
“I waited patiently for the LORD; he inclined to me and heard my cry. He drew me up from the desolate pit, out of the miry bog, and set my feet upon a rock, making my steps secure.” Psalm 40:1–2
“Wretched man that I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death? Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!” Romans 7:24–25

Apply: Bring your struggle honestly to the sacrament of reconciliation this Lent.

4. To serve and to give his life

  • The third announcement of the Passion.
  • Are you able to drink my cup?
  • The greatest is the servant.
“Out of his anguish he shall see light; he shall find satisfaction through his knowledge. The righteous one, my servant, shall make many righteous, and he shall bear their iniquities.” Isaiah 53:11
“just as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many.”” Matthew 20:28

Apply: Take on one humble task this week that usually falls to someone else.

We are saved inside the ark, by the Lord who shuts the door and carries us above the flood.

Bow before you fight, cry to the One who rescues, and choose to serve.

Take-home resolution: Stay faithful to Sunday Holy Qurbana, pray for seven days against one habit of sin, and take the lowest task at home this week.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, the first reading tells us that the rain fell on the earth forty days and forty nights (Genesis 7:12). And before the waters rose, one small sentence: and the LORD shut him in (Genesis 7:16). Noah did not close the door himself. God did. Noah was saved because he was inside.

That is how we are saved in these forty days of the Great Fast. Not by our own swimming, but by staying inside the ark of the Church: in her sacraments, her prayer, her Sunday Qurbana.

Joshua near Jericho asks the commander of the LORD's army: are you for us or for our enemies? The answer is: neither (Joshua 5:13–14). The question is not whether God is on our side, but whether we are on his. Joshua removes his sandals and bows. And the walls of Jericho fall, not by force, but by a procession of prayer around the city.

Saint Paul cries out: I do not do the good I want, but the evil I do not want is what I do. Wretched man that I am! Who will rescue me? And he answers: thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord (Romans 7:19, 24–25). Our rescue is a Person.

In the Gospel, while Jesus speaks of his cross, his disciples ask for the best seats. Jesus asks: are you able to drink the cup that I am about to drink? And he teaches: whoever wishes to be great among you must be your servant; for the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life a ransom for many (Matthew 20:22, 26–28).

This week, let us stay inside the ark, walk around the walls of our sins in prayer, and choose the lowest place in order to serve. Then we will share his cup, and his glory. Amen.

For children

Children, God shut Noah and his family safe inside the ark when the big flood came. The Church is like the ark: inside, with Jesus, we are safe!

Jesus says that the greatest person is the one who serves others. How will you help someone at home this week without being asked?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you shut Noah into the ark and carried him above the flood; keep us within your Church in these forty days. Teach us to remove our sandals before you and to walk around the walls of our sin in patient prayer until they fall. When we cry, who will rescue me?, be our answer. Give us courage to drink your cup and the humility to serve, as you came not to be served but to serve and to give your life a ransom for many. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
