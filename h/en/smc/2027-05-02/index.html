Word for the day

“that they may all be one. As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” John 17:21

Reflection

The Sixth Sunday of Resurrection stands at a threshold. On Thursday the Church will celebrate the Ascension of the Lord, and the readings of this Sunday already have the sound of a farewell and a sending. The first reading is a cry of joy from the prophet who comforted Israel in exile: “How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of the messenger who announces peace, who brings good news, who announces salvation, who says to Zion, ‘Your God reigns.’” The watchmen on the ruined walls of Jerusalem see the Lord coming home, and the ruins themselves are told to sing: “Break forth together into singing, you ruins of Jerusalem; for the Lord has comforted his people, he has redeemed Jerusalem.” Then a promise that reaches far beyond one city: “The Lord has bared his holy arm before the eyes of all the nations; and all the ends of the earth shall see the salvation of our God.” Paul hears in this text the vocation of every preacher of the Gospel: “How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!” (Romans 10:15). The feet of the apostles were dusty and tired; one pair of them, the Church in India believes, walked the coast of Malabar. On this Sunday we give thanks for every messenger who brought the good news to us, and we remember that our own feet are meant for the same road.

The Acts of the Apostles shows how far that road would go. Peter is praying on a rooftop at Joppa around noon. He is hungry, and in a trance he sees “the heaven opened and something like a large sheet coming down, being lowered to the ground by its four corners”, full of animals that the Law of Moses forbade him to eat. A voice says, “Get up, Peter; kill and eat.” Peter answers as a faithful Jew who has kept the commandments all his life: “By no means, Lord; for I have never eaten anything that is profane or unclean.” And the voice replies, “What God has made clean, you must not call profane.” Three times this happens. Peter does not yet understand, but at that very moment messengers from a Roman centurion named Cornelius are at the gate. The vision is not mainly about food; it is about people. Soon Peter will stand in a Gentile house and say, “I truly understand that God shows no partiality, but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him” (Acts 10:34–35). Notice how gently God leads his apostle. Peter's hesitation came from love of God's Law, not from hatred; God opens his heart step by step. The Church has never ceased to honour the people of Israel, from whom Peter, Mary and Jesus himself came; “the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable” (Romans 11:29). What the vision opens is the door of that same family to all the nations.

Paul writes to the Ephesians, most of them Gentiles by birth, and asks them to remember what they once were: “without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers to the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God in the world.” Then comes the great turning: “But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ. For he is our peace; in his flesh he has made both groups into one and has broken down the dividing wall, that is, the hostility between us.” The image comes from the Temple in Jerusalem, where a barrier kept Gentile visitors from the inner courts. In the Cross, Christ creates “in himself one new humanity in place of the two”, reconciling both to God in one body. And Paul draws the conclusion that every baptised Indian Christian can read as his own: “So then you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are citizens with the saints and also members of the household of God, built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone.” Our society still knows many dividing walls: of caste, of language, of wealth, of region and politics. Sadly, they have sometimes stood even inside our churches. The Ephesian letter says plainly that in the household of God those walls have already been broken down by the blood of Christ. It is our task not to rebuild them.

The Gospel lets us listen to Jesus praying on the night before he died. After praying for his disciples, he looks beyond them to us: “I ask not only on behalf of these, but also on behalf of those who will believe in me through their word, that they may all be one. As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” The unity Jesus asks for is not a mere agreement or a good organisation; it is a share in the communion of the Father and the Son. And it is missionary: “so that the world may believe.” Divided Christians make the Gospel hard to believe. We feel this pain in Kerala, where the descendants of the Christians of St Thomas now belong to several Churches. The Catholic Church prays and works for the full unity Christ willed, with respect and love for our brothers and sisters of the other Churches (Unitatis Redintegratio 1). The prayer ends with a desire that turns our minds to the Ascension: “Father, I desire that those also, whom you have given me, may be with me where I am, to see my glory, which you have given me because you loved me before the foundation of the world.” Where he goes on Thursday, he wants us to follow. Until then, the sign that we belong to him is the love that makes us one: “so that the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them.”

Homily outline

Theme: “that they may all be one. As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.”

On the last Sunday before the Ascension: the beautiful feet of the messenger, the door opened to the nations, the dividing wall broken down, and the prayer of Jesus for our unity.

1. How beautiful are the feet

  • The messenger who announces peace.
  • The ruins told to sing.
  • All the ends of the earth shall see salvation.
“How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of the messenger who announces peace, who brings good news, who announces salvation, who says to Zion, “Your God reigns.”” Isaiah 52:7
“How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!” Romans 10:15

Apply: Bring one word of hope this week to someone who is discouraged.

2. What God has made clean

  • Peter on the rooftop at Joppa.
  • A faithful man led step by step.
  • The door opened to the nations.
“for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples.” Isaiah 56:7
“The voice said to him again, a second time, “What God has made clean, you must not call profane.”” Acts 10:15

Apply: Examine whom you look down on, and greet that person with respect this week.

3. He is our peace

  • Once far off, now brought near.
  • The dividing wall broken down.
  • Citizens with the saints.
“Peace, peace, to the far and the near, says the Lord; and I will heal them.” Isaiah 57:19
“But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ.” Ephesians 2:13

Apply: Take one step to heal an old quarrel in your family or neighbourhood.

4. That they may all be one

  • Jesus prays for us.
  • Unity in the life of the Father and the Son.
  • So that the world may believe.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“that they may all be one. As you, Father, are in me and I am in you, may they also be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me.” John 17:21

Apply: Pray each day this week for the unity of all Christians.

Christ has broken down the walls that divide us and prayed that we may be one.

As we prepare for his Ascension on Thursday, let us carry his peace and live as one household, so that the world may believe.

Take-home resolution: This week, pray daily for unity, heal one quarrel, and take part in the Holy Qurbana of the Ascension on Thursday.

Full homily

Dear friends, this is the last Sunday before the Ascension. On Thursday we will celebrate the Lord going up to heaven. Today's readings are like a farewell and a sending.

Isaiah sings: “How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of the messenger who announces peace” (Isaiah 52:7). The apostles were such messengers; Mar Thoma brought the good news to our land. Now our feet are called to carry it.

In Acts, Peter sees a vision and hears: “What God has made clean, you must not call profane” (Acts 10:15). God was teaching him that the Gospel is for every nation. Soon Peter entered the house of a Roman officer, Cornelius, and baptised his family.

Paul tells the Ephesians that Christ “is our peace” and “has broken down the dividing wall” (Ephesians 2:14). In Christ there are no strangers. We too have walls: caste, money, language, old quarrels. Christ has broken them down. Let us not build them again.

In the Gospel Jesus prays for us: “that they may all be one... so that the world may believe that you have sent me” (John 17:21). When Christians are divided, people find it hard to believe. When we love one another, the world sees Jesus.

So this week, let us pray for unity, make peace with one person, and prepare to celebrate the Ascension on Thursday. The Lord wants us to be with him where he is. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus prayed for you! He asked his Father that all his friends would be one big family.

When you share, forgive and play kindly with others, you help Jesus' prayer come true.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, before your Passion you prayed to the Father that all who believe in you may be one. By your blood you have broken down the walls that divide us and made us members of one household. Make our feet beautiful with the good news of peace; open our hearts as you opened the heart of Peter; heal the divisions among Christians and in our society; and draw us after you to the glory where you are, until the world believes that the Father sent you. Amen. Alleluia!
