Readings

  • Epistle 1 John 1:1–3 · We declare to you the word of life
  • Gospel Luke 14:25–35 · The cost of discipleship

Word for the day

“Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” Luke 14:27

Reflection

The First Letter of John opens with a breathless sentence, the words of an old man who cannot forget what he saw as a young one: “We declare to you what was from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what we have looked at and touched with our hands, concerning the word of life.” Heard, seen, looked at, touched: four verbs piled one on another. John insists that the faith rests on a real encounter with a real person. The Word who was with the Father from the beginning became flesh, ate with fishermen, invited Thomas to put his hand into his side. “This life was revealed, and we have seen it and testify to it.” Now the Lord has ascended and is no longer seen in that way. How then do we meet him? John answers: through the witness of the apostles, handed on in the Church. “We declare to you what we have seen and heard so that you also may have fellowship with us; and truly our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ.” The chain is clear: the apostles saw; they declared; we who hear them enter into fellowship with them, and through them with the Father and the Son. Our Church received that witness from Thomas, the apostle who touched the wounds. In these days before Pentecost, the Spirit is preparing the apostles to begin declaring.

The Gospel shows what that fellowship costs. “Now large crowds were traveling with him; and he turned and said to them, ‘Whoever comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters, yes, and even life itself, cannot be my disciple.’” The word “hate” shocks us. In the language of the Bible it is a way of saying “prefer less”; Matthew gives the same saying as “Whoever loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me” (Matthew 10:37). Jesus is not asking us to stop loving our families; he who gave us the commandment to honour father and mother asks that no love, not even the dearest, take his place. Then he speaks plainly: “Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” A crowd follows for a day; a disciple follows to the end.

Jesus gives two little parables of good sense. A man who wants to build a tower first sits down and estimates the cost, so that he is not mocked: “This fellow began to build and was not able to finish.” A king going to war with ten thousand against twenty thousand first sits down and considers whether he can win. Both sit down before they begin. Discipleship is not an enthusiasm of one evening; it is a decision taken with open eyes. “So therefore, none of you can become my disciple if you do not give up all your possessions.” Finally he speaks of salt: “Salt is good; but if salt has lost its taste, how can its saltiness be restored?” A disciple who has stopped paying the cost loses his flavour. Many in Kerala know what that means: families where the faith has become only a custom, where the name is Christian but the taste is gone. Salt, Leviticus says, was offered with every sacrifice as “the salt of the covenant” (Leviticus 2:13). Today the Latin Church remembers Our Lady of Fatima, who asked three poor children to pray, to do penance and to offer sacrifices for sinners. Mary is the disciple who counted the cost at Nazareth and paid it at the foot of the Cross. In the upper room she prayed for the Spirit who alone can give us the courage to finish the tower we have begun.

Homily outline

Theme: “Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.”

The seventh day of the novena: the word of life heard, seen and touched, the cost of following Christ to the end, and the salt that must not lose its taste.

1. The word of life

  • Heard, seen, looked at, touched.
  • The witness handed on.
  • Fellowship with the Father and the Son.
“I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you.” Job 42:5
“We declare to you what was from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what we have looked at and touched with our hands, concerning the word of life.” 1 John 1:1

Apply: Tell one person this week, simply, what Jesus has done in your life.

2. Carry the cross and follow me

  • The crowd and the disciple.
  • No love in the place of Christ.
  • Following to the end.
“Do not press me to leave you or to turn back from following you! Where you go, I will go; where you lodge, I will lodge; your people shall be my people, and your God my God.” Ruth 1:16
“Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” Luke 14:27

Apply: Give God back his first place in one area where another love has pushed him aside.

3. Sit down and count the cost

  • The tower and the king.
  • Finishing what we begin.
  • Salt that keeps its taste.
“You shall not omit from your grain offerings the salt of the covenant with your God; with all your offerings you shall offer salt.” Leviticus 2:13
“Salt is good; but if salt has lost its taste, how can its saltiness be restored?” Luke 14:34

Apply: Finish one Christian commitment you have left half done, and restore one family practice of prayer.

The apostles touched the Word of life and handed him on to us; the Spirit gives us courage to follow him to the end.

With Mary, who paid the cost at the Cross, let us carry our cross and keep our salt.

Take-home resolution: This week, bear witness once, put Christ first in one thing, finish one tower, and pray the rosary for peace.

Full homily

Dear friends, the First Letter of John begins with an old apostle's memory: “what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what we have looked at and touched with our hands, concerning the word of life.” John had touched Jesus. Our faith rests on a real meeting with a real person.

Now the Lord has ascended. How do we meet him? Through the witness of the apostles, handed on in the Church. “We declare to you what we have seen and heard so that you also may have fellowship with us.” We received this witness from St Thomas, who touched the Lord's wounds.

In the Gospel, large crowds follow Jesus. He turns and says: unless you put me before father and mother, before even your own life, you cannot be my disciple. He does not ask us to love our families less, but to love him most. “Whoever does not carry the cross and follow me cannot be my disciple.” A crowd follows for a day; a disciple follows to the end.

Jesus speaks of a man who builds a tower without counting the cost, and cannot finish. Many of us have begun towers: a promise at a retreat, a resolution after confession. Let us finish them. And he speaks of salt that loses its taste. Faith that becomes only a habit loses its taste. Let us restore it.

Today the Latin Church remembers Our Lady of Fatima. Mary asked three children to pray and offer sacrifices. She herself counted the cost at Nazareth and paid it at the Cross. In the upper room she prayed for the Spirit. Let us ask her to pray for us, that the Spirit may give us courage to follow to the end. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus said: before you build a big tower, think if you can finish it! Following Jesus is like building a tower: we must keep going even when it is hard.

At Fatima, Mary asked three children to pray every day. You can pray too! Today, say a Hail Mary for someone who is far from Jesus.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, Word of life, you were heard, seen and touched by your apostles, and through their witness we have fellowship with the Father and with you. Give us the courage to put you before every other love, to carry our cross and follow you to the end. Teach us to count the cost and to finish what we have begun, and keep the salt of our faith from losing its taste. Through the prayers of the Virgin Mary, who paid the cost of discipleship at the foot of the Cross and prayed with the apostles for the Spirit, send us your Holy Spirit. Amen. Alleluia!
