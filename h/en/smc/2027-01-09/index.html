Word for the day

“And he answered them, “Go and tell John what you have seen and heard: the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, the poor have good news brought to them.” Luke 7:22

Reflection

From prison John the Baptist sends two disciples to ask Jesus: “Are you the one who is to come, or are we to wait for another?” Even the great witness has a moment of darkness. Perhaps he expected a Messiah of fire and judgement. Jesus does not argue. He shows what he is doing: the blind see, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor hear good news. These are the signs Isaiah promised (Isaiah 35:5–6; 61:1). And he adds: “blessed is anyone who takes no offense at me.”

The epistle opens a door in heaven. John the seer sees a throne, a rainbow, twenty-four elders and four living creatures who “Day and night without ceasing… sing, ‘Holy, holy, holy, the Lord God the Almighty, who was and is and is to come.’” The Church on earth joins this song in every Qurbana. When we doubt, as John did in prison, we look at the works of Christ among the poor and we lift our eyes to the throne: he is the one who is to come.

Full homily

Dear friends, from Herod's prison John the Baptist sends two disciples to Jesus with a question: are you the one who is to come, or are we to wait for another? (Luke 7:19). Even the great prophet who saw the heavens open has a moment of darkness.

Jesus does not scold him and does not argue. He tells the messengers: go and tell John what you have seen and heard: the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, the poor have good news brought to them (Luke 7:22). These are the signs Isaiah promised.

Then he adds: blessed is anyone who takes no offense at me (Luke 7:23). The Messiah does not always act as we expect. He comes in mercy before he comes in judgement.

The epistle opens a door in heaven. Around the throne the living creatures sing day and night: holy, holy, holy, the Lord God the Almighty, who was and is and is to come (Revelation 4:8). We join that song at every Qurbana. When doubts come, let us bring them honestly to Jesus, look at his works of mercy, and lift our eyes to his throne. Amen.

For children

Children, even John the Baptist had a question for Jesus. Jesus answered: look at what I do! I help the blind and the sick. You can ask Jesus your questions too.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you answered John's doubt with deeds of mercy. When I am in darkness, show me your works, lift my eyes to your throne, and let me join the angels' song: Holy, holy, holy. Amen.
