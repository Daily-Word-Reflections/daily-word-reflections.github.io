Fourth Friday of Denha · Commemoration of St Stephen, Protomartyr

  • Second Reading 1 Kings 21:1–13 · Naboth is stoned to death
  • Epistle Acts 6:8–15; 7:1–2a, 51–60 · Stephen is stoned to death
  • Gospel Luke 23:33–43 · Father, forgive them

Word for the day

“He replied, “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in Paradise.”” Luke 23:43

Reflection

On the Fridays of Denha the Church remembers those who manifested Christ by their lives, and today it is Stephen, the first martyr. The readings draw a line of innocent blood. Abel, the righteous brother, is killed by Cain, and God says: “Listen; your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground!” Naboth refuses to sell his ancestral vineyard to King Ahab, is falsely accused by hired witnesses, and is stoned outside the city. Stephen too is accused by false witnesses, his face shining “like the face of an angel”, and is stoned outside Jerusalem.

But Stephen dies differently from Abel and Naboth. He sees “the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God!” And as the stones fall he prays: “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit… Lord, do not hold this sin against them.” These are almost the words of Jesus on the cross in today's Gospel: “Father, forgive them”, and to the repentant thief, “today you will be with me in Paradise.” Hebrews says the blood of Jesus “speaks a better word than the blood of Abel”: not vengeance, but forgiveness. A young man named Saul held the coats of those who stoned Stephen; Stephen's prayer was answered in Paul. The martyr's forgiveness becomes the seed of the Church.

Homily outline

Theme: “Today you will be with me in Paradise.”

Innocent blood, the martyr's vision, and forgiveness like Christ's.

1. The blood that cries out

  • Abel the righteous.
  • Naboth the faithful.
  • God hears the innocent.
“And the LORD said, “What have you done? Listen; your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground!” Genesis 4:10
“and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, and to the sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel.” Hebrews 12:24

Apply: Speak up for someone treated unjustly.

2. The face of an angel

  • False witnesses.
  • A shining face.
  • Heaven opened.
“But Naboth said to Ahab, “The LORD forbid that I should give you my ancestral inheritance.”” 1 Kings 21:3
“And all who sat in the council looked intently at him, and they saw that his face was like the face of an angel.” Acts 6:15

Apply: Stay calm and faithful when criticised for your faith.

3. Do not hold this sin against them

  • Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.
  • Christ's own prayer.
  • The seed of the Church.
“Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his faithful ones.” Psalm 116:15
“Then he knelt down and cried out in a loud voice, “Lord, do not hold this sin against them.” When he had said this, he died.” Acts 7:60

Apply: Pray by name for someone who has wronged you.

Christ's blood and the martyr's prayer speak forgiveness.

Forgiveness opens heaven and plants the Church.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day this week for persecuted Christians.

Full homily

Dear friends, today's readings trace a line of innocent blood. God says to Cain: listen; your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground! (Genesis 4:10). Naboth is falsely accused and stoned for refusing to give up his ancestral vineyard.

Stephen too is falsely accused, yet his face shines like the face of an angel (Acts 6:15). He sees the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God.

As the stones fall he prays: Lord Jesus, receive my spirit. Lord, do not hold this sin against them (Acts 7:59–60). These are the words of Jesus on the cross: Father, forgive them; and to the thief: today you will be with me in Paradise (Luke 23:43).

Hebrews says Jesus' blood speaks a better word than the blood of Abel: not vengeance but forgiveness. Saul held the coats of Stephen's killers; Stephen's prayer was answered when Saul became Paul. Let us forgive, and see heaven opened. Amen.

For children

Children, St Stephen loved Jesus so much that he forgave the people who hurt him. When someone hurts us, let us pray for them like Stephen did.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you forgave those who crucified you and promised Paradise to the thief. Through the prayers of St Stephen, give us faith to see the heavens opened, and love to pray for those who wrong us: Lord, do not hold this sin against them. Amen.
