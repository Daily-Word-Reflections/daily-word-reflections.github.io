Fourth Friday of Denha · Commemoration of St Stephen, Protomartyr

29 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading 1 Kings 21:1–21 · Naboth is stoned to death
  • Second Reading Acts 7:54–60 · The martyrdom of Stephen
  • Epistle 2 Corinthians 4:8–18 · Carrying in the body the death of Jesus
  • Gospel Matthew 23:34–39 · The blood of the righteous

Word for the day

“always carrying in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be made visible in our bodies.” 2 Corinthians 4:10

Reflection

On the Fridays of Denha the Church remembers those through whom Christ was made visible, and today it is Stephen, the first martyr. The first reading tells of another innocent man. Naboth will not sell his family vineyard to King Ahab: “The LORD forbid that I should give you my ancestral inheritance.” Queen Jezebel arranges a religious fast, seats two scoundrels opposite Naboth to accuse him of cursing God and the king, and has him stoned outside the city. Then Elijah meets Ahab in the stolen vineyard with God's question: “Have you killed, and also taken possession?” In the Gospel Jesus laments over a city that repeats this story: “Jerusalem, Jerusalem, the city that kills the prophets and stones those who are sent to it! How often have I desired to gather your children together as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, and you were not willing!” He speaks of all the righteous blood shed on earth, “from the blood of righteous Abel” onward.

Stephen stands at the end of that line, but he does not die as a victim only. Filled with the Holy Spirit, “he gazed into heaven and saw the glory of God and Jesus standing at the right hand of God.” As the stones fall he prays, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit”, and kneeling, “Lord, do not hold this sin against them.” At his feet the witnesses lay their coats, beside a young man named Saul. Paul, once that young man, later describes the martyr's life from the inside: “We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed; always carrying in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be made visible in our bodies.” The stones were real, but so was the glory: “this slight momentary affliction is preparing us for an eternal weight of glory beyond all measure, because we look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen.” In Denha Stephen shows us Christ: the face of a forgiving God, visible in a dying man.

Homily outline

Theme: “The life of Jesus may also be made visible in our bodies.”

Innocent blood, the martyr's vision, and the glory hidden in affliction.

1. The blood of the righteous

  • Naboth's inheritance.
  • False witnesses.
  • The prophets stoned.
“But Naboth said to Ahab, “The LORD forbid that I should give you my ancestral inheritance.”” 1 Kings 21:3
“so that upon you may come all the righteous blood shed on earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah son of Barachiah, whom you murdered between the sanctuary and the altar.” Matthew 23:35

Apply: Stand up for someone treated unjustly by the powerful.

2. Heaven opened

  • Filled with the Spirit.
  • Jesus standing.
  • Lord, do not hold this sin against them.
“Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his faithful ones.” Psalm 116:15
““Look,” he said, “I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God!”” Acts 7:56

Apply: Pray by name for someone who has wronged you.

3. Afflicted but not crushed

  • Carrying the death of Jesus.
  • The inner nature renewed.
  • An eternal weight of glory.
“Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the LORD rescues them from them all.” Psalm 34:19
“We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed;” 2 Corinthians 4:8–9

Apply: Offer one affliction today, looking at what cannot be seen.

The martyrs show Christ's face in suffering.

Look at what cannot be seen, and forgive.

Take-home resolution: Pray each day this week for persecuted Christians.

Full homily

Dear friends, Naboth refuses to sell his family vineyard to the king: the LORD forbid that I should give you my ancestral inheritance (1 Kings 21:3). He is falsely accused and stoned. Jesus laments over Jerusalem, the city that kills the prophets and stones those sent to it, which he longed to gather as a hen gathers her brood (Matthew 23:37).

Stephen is the next in that line. But filled with the Holy Spirit he looks up and says: I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God (Acts 7:56). He dies praying: Lord, do not hold this sin against them.

Saul held the coats. Years later, as Paul, he writes: we are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; always carrying in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be made visible in our bodies (2 Corinthians 4:8, 10).

So let us look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen. The stones are real, but the glory is greater. Let us carry Christ in our troubles and forgive as Stephen forgave. Amen.

For children

Children, St Stephen looked up and saw Jesus in heaven, even when people hurt him. He forgave them! When someone hurts us, let us look to Jesus and pray for them.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, standing at the right hand of God, you opened the heavens to your servant Stephen as he died forgiving his killers. Through his prayers, keep us from being crushed in affliction, let your life be made visible in our bodies, and give us the grace to say: Lord, do not hold this sin against them. Amen.
