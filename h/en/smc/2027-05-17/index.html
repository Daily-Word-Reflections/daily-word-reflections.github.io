Word for the day

“for God is a God not of disorder but of peace.” 1 Corinthians 14:33

Reflection

On the day after Pentecost the Church looks at the woman who was in the upper room when the Spirit came. Luke had named her among the praying disciples: “together with certain women, including Mary the mother of Jesus” (Acts 1:14). At the Cross Jesus had given her to the beloved disciple with the words “Woman, here is your son,” and to the disciple, “Here is your mother” (John 19:26–27). The Church has always understood that in that disciple all disciples are given a Mother. On 21 November 1964, at the Second Vatican Council, St Paul VI solemnly proclaimed Mary Mother of the Church, that is, of the whole people of God, faithful and pastors. In 2018 Pope Francis placed this memorial on the Monday after Pentecost, so that the Church, newly born of the Spirit, might begin her journey with her Mother. Our First Set calendar lists the day without proper readings, and so we hear the readings of the first weekday of the Season of the Apostles; they speak, as it turns out, of the Church at prayer and of the humble heart, the two things Mary teaches best. She is not above the Church as a goddess, nor outside it as a spectator; she is its first and most perfect member, and for that very reason its Mother.

Paul writes to an assembly that has received many gifts of the Spirit and is in danger of turning them into noise. Everyone wants to speak at once. “When you come together, each one has a hymn, a lesson, a revelation, a tongue, or an interpretation. Let all things be done for building up.” Tongues only with interpretation, prophets one by one, the others weighing what is said, the first speaker silent when another receives a revelation: “For you can all prophesy one by one, so that all may learn and all be encouraged.” The reason is theological, not merely practical: “for God is a God not of disorder but of peace.” The Spirit who came at Pentecost with wind and fire is the same Spirit of order and peace. Our Syro-Malabar liturgy, with its fixed prayers, its roles for priest, deacon, choir and people, and its moments of silence, is a school in Paul's teaching: each voice in turn, everything for building up. Mary is the model of such a gift. She speaks little in the Gospels; she treasures and ponders (Luke 2:19). Her few words build up: “Do whatever he tells you” (John 2:5). In a Church tempted by noise and rivalry, the Mother of the Church teaches silence that listens and speech that serves.

The Gospel returns us to the prayer of Jesus: “I thank you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because you have hidden these things from the wise and the intelligent and have revealed them to infants”. If anyone was such an infant before God, it was Mary, who sang that the Lord “has looked with favor on the lowliness of his servant”. Jesus continues: “Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me; for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls.” Where did the human heart of Jesus learn its gentleness? From the Father, first of all; and also in the home of Nazareth, from a mother who was gentle and humble. The Mother of the Church does not draw us to herself; she leads us to her Son, who gives rest to the weary. Many in our parishes carry heavy burdens: illness, debt, a broken marriage, children far away, loneliness in old age. On this first day of the Season of the Apostles, the Church, with Mary her Mother, says to them what her Son says: Come to him. Learn from him. His yoke is easy and his burden light, because he carries it with you.

Homily outline

Theme: “for God is a God not of disorder but of peace.”

On the day of Mary, Mother of the Church: the Mother given from the Cross, an assembly built up in peace, and the gentle and humble heart of Christ.

1. Here is your mother

  • The beloved disciple and all disciples.
  • Mary in the upper room.
  • Mother of the whole people of God.
“The man named his wife Eve, because she was the mother of all living.” Genesis 3:20
“Then he said to the disciple, “Here is your mother.”” John 19:27

Apply: Entrust your family to the Mother of the Church with a decade of the rosary each day this week.

2. Let all things be done for building up

  • Many gifts, one assembly.
  • Each in turn, listening to one another.
  • Mary's silence and her few words.
“How very good and pleasant it is when kindred live together in unity!” Psalm 133:1
“Let all things be done for building up.” 1 Corinthians 14:26

Apply: In every meeting and discussion this week, speak only what builds up.

3. Learn from me, gentle and humble in heart

  • Revealed to infants.
  • The humble servant of the Lord.
  • Rest for the weary.
“As a mother comforts her child, so I will comfort you;” Isaiah 66:13
“Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me; for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls.” Matthew 11:29

Apply: Visit or call one person who is carrying a heavy burden, and bring them the comfort of Christ.

The Church born at Pentecost begins her journey with her Mother, who teaches her to listen, to build up and to be humble.

With Mary, let us bring every burden to the gentle heart of her Son.

Take-home resolution: This week, pray a daily decade with your family, speak only words that build up, and comfort one who is weary.

Full homily

Dear friends, yesterday was Pentecost, the birthday of the Church. Today, the first weekday of the Season of the Apostles, the Church honours Mary as Mother of the Church. She was in the upper room with the apostles when the Spirit came (Acts 1:14).

At the Cross Jesus said to the beloved disciple, “Here is your mother” (John 19:27). In that disciple, we were all given a Mother. St Paul VI proclaimed her Mother of the Church in 1964, and Pope Francis set this memorial on the Monday after Pentecost.

Paul tells the Corinthians that when they come together each one has a gift, and “Let all things be done for building up” (1 Corinthians 14:26). Speak in turn, listen to one another, “for God is a God not of disorder but of peace” (14:33). Mary is the model: she pondered in silence, and her few words built up: “Do whatever he tells you.”

Jesus thanks the Father for revealing his secrets to infants, and says: “learn from me; for I am gentle and humble in heart” (Matthew 11:29). Mary was the little one, the humble servant. She learned and lived the gentleness of God, and she leads us to her Son, who gives rest to the weary.

Let us take Mary into our homes as the beloved disciple did. Let our parish meetings and prayer groups be places of peace, where each gift builds up. And let us bring our burdens to Jesus, with our Mother beside us. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus gave his own mother to us! Mary is the Mother of the whole Church. When you are sad or afraid, you can say: “Mother Mary, pray for me,” and she will take you to Jesus.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, from the Cross you gave your Mother to the beloved disciple and to all your disciples. Grant that your Church, born of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, may grow in faith and love under the care of Mary, her Mother. Make our assemblies places of peace and not of disorder, where every gift builds up the body. Teach us, with her, to be little before the Father, and give us your gentle and humble heart, that the weary may find rest in you through us. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
