Word for the day

“When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, “Son, your sins are forgiven.”” Mark 2:5

Reflection

Paul's picture in the Epistle is taken from the Law of Moses: “Do not be mismatched with unbelievers.” The Greek word means to be yoked unequally, like an ox and a donkey under one yoke, which the Law of Moses forbids (Deuteronomy 22:10). Then follows a series of questions, each with the answer no: “what partnership is there between righteousness and lawlessness? Or what fellowship is there between light and darkness? What agreement does Christ have with Beliar? Or what does a believer share with an unbeliever? What agreement has the temple of God with idols?” In Corinth the practical question was sharing in the feasts held in pagan temples, with their sacrifices to idols and their immorality. Paul is not telling Christians to cut off their neighbours; in the first letter he says that then they would have to go out of the world (1 Corinthians 5:10). He asks them to refuse partnership in idolatry and sin.

In India, where Christians live among neighbours of many faiths, this needs care. The Church “rejects nothing that is true and holy” in other religions and urges respect, friendship and dialogue with them (Nostra Aetate 2). What we refuse is not a neighbour but a yoke: the bribe, the cruelty, the lie, the unclean practice, anything that would put another master in the place of Christ. Paul's reason is a gift before it is a command: “For we are the temple of the living God; as God said, ‘I will live in them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.’” God walked among Israel in the tent and the Temple, as he walked with Adam in the garden. Now he lives in us, in the Church and in each baptised person. A temple is not a place where everything is permitted; it is a place that belongs to God. The reading ends in a promise of tenderness: “I will welcome you, and I will be your father, and you shall be my sons and daughters, says the Lord Almighty.”

The Gospel is one of the most vivid scenes in Mark. Jesus is “at home” in Capernaum, and the house is so full that there is no room even at the door. Four men arrive carrying a paralysed friend. They cannot get through the crowd, so they climb the outside stairs, dig through the roof, made of beams, branches and clay, and lower the man on his mat to the feet of Jesus. We do not know their names, only that they would not give up. Mark says: “When Jesus saw their faith.” It is their faith, and the paralytic's, that Jesus sees. Here is a mystery that every parish knows: someone is carried to Jesus by the faith of others, by a mother's prayer, a friend's visit, a catechist, a neighbour who sits by a sickbed.

Jesus does not begin with the healing of the legs. “Son, your sins are forgiven.” The man had come for his body; Jesus first gives him what he needs most, and calls him “Son”. Some scribes, sitting there, think: “It is blasphemy! Who can forgive sins but God alone?” Their premise is correct, and they ask honestly; only God can forgive sins. Jesus answers by doing the visible thing to prove the invisible: “But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins… I say to you, stand up, take your mat and go to your home.” The man stands, takes his mat and walks out before all of them, and all are amazed: “We have never seen anything like this!” Jesus forgives as God forgives, and this authority has been given to his Church in the sacrament of Reconciliation (John 20:23). On this Saturday, when many families remember the Mother of the Lord, we remember that she is the one who, at Cana, brought the need of others to her Son; she is the best of all those who carry friends to Jesus.

Theme: “When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, ‘Son, your sins are forgiven.’”

We are the temple of the living God; four friends and a hole in the roof; and the Son of Man who has authority to forgive.

  • Not mismatched.
  • God lives in us and walks among us.
  • A place that belongs to God.

“What agreement has the temple of God with idols? For we are the temple of the living God; as God said, “I will live in them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.”” 2 Corinthians 6:16

Apply: Treat your body and your home as his dwelling this week.

2. Come out from sin, and welcome your neighbour

  • Not from people, but from idols and impurity.
  • Respect for neighbours of every faith.
  • A Father who welcomes.
“Depart, depart, go out from there! Touch no unclean thing; go out from the midst of it, purify yourselves, you who carry the vessels of the Lord.” Isaiah 52:11
“Therefore come out from them, and be separate from them, says the Lord, and touch nothing unclean; then I will welcome you.” 2 Corinthians 6:17

Apply: Step out of one dishonest partnership.

3. When Jesus saw their faith

  • Four friends.
  • The hole in the roof.
  • Faith that carries another.
“who forgives all your iniquity, who heals all your diseases,” Psalm 103:3
“When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, “Son, your sins are forgiven.”” Mark 2:5

Apply: Carry one named person to Jesus by prayer and a visit.

4. The authority to forgive

  • Only God forgives.
  • The visible sign of the invisible gift.
  • The sacrament of mercy.

“But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins” Mark 2:10

Apply: Plan your next confession.

God dwells in us as in a temple, and he forgives in the person of his Son.

Let us be the four friends who carry others to him.

Take-home resolution: This week, carry one person to Jesus, step out of one false yoke, and go to confession.

Full homily

Dear friends, in today's Gospel four friends carry a paralysed man to Jesus. The house is full, so they climb up, make a hole in the roof, and let their friend down. We do not know their names. But Mark says: “When Jesus saw their faith” (Mark 2:5). Faith can carry another person.

And what does Jesus say to the man? He does not first say, ‘Walk.’ He says, “Son, your sins are forgiven.” The man had come for his legs, but Jesus gave him what he needed most. Only God can forgive sins, and Jesus does it, and proves it by making the man walk.

This forgiveness is given to us in the sacrament of Reconciliation. If we have been away for a long time, today's Gospel tells us that there is no hole in the roof too hard to make. The Lord is waiting.

Saint Paul tells the Corinthians that we are “the temple of the living God” (2 Corinthians 6:16). God lives in us. So we refuse everything that is not worthy of his dwelling: lies, cheating, impurity, idols. At the same time we respect and love our neighbours of every faith.

On this Saturday, let us ask Mary, the Mother of Jesus, to teach us to be like the four friends: to carry others to her Son. Let us name one person today and carry them in prayer. Amen.

For children

Children, do you remember the four friends who made a hole in the roof? They loved their friend so much that they did not give up. Jesus saw how much they believed and made the man walk. You can also carry your friends to Jesus: when a friend is sick or sad, say a prayer for him. And remember, Jesus lives in you like in a little church, so be kind and clean in your words and deeds.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you see the faith of those who carry others to you, and you say to the weak: Son, your sins are forgiven. Make us temples of the living God, clean from every idol, and give us the grace to refuse every partnership with sin. Teach us to carry our friends, our families and our parish to you, and never to give up when the crowd is in the way. Heal us in body and soul, and give us the courage to come to the sacrament of your mercy. Through the prayer of your Mother, who brings the needs of her children to you, make us your dwelling place, for you are God for ever and ever. Amen.
