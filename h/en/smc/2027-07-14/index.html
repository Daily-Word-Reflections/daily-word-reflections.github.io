Readings

  • Epistle 2 Corinthians 4:14–5:1 · Our inner nature is renewed day by day; a building from God
  • Gospel John 12:23–28 · The grain of wheat that falls into the earth and dies

Word for the day

“Very truly, I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.” John 12:24

Reflection

Kaitha is the season of harvest, and today the readings tell us how a harvest begins: with a seed that goes under the ground. Paul writes to the Corinthians from a life of daily pressure, and the verse that opens our reading is the root of his courage: “because we know that the one who raised the Lord Jesus will raise us also with Jesus, and will bring us with you into his presence.” Just before, he had described himself and his companions as treasure carried in clay jars: “We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed.” What keeps the jar from breaking is not its own strength but the faith that the One who raised Jesus will raise us. And Paul adds that none of this is for himself: “Yes, everything is for your sake, so that grace, as it extends to more and more people, may increase thanksgiving, to the glory of God.” A suffering that is offered for others is already becoming thanksgiving.

Then comes one of the most consoling sentences in Scripture: “So we do not lose heart. Even though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day.” Anyone who has watched a parent grow old, or who feels the years in his own knees at the Sunday Qurbana, knows the first half of that sentence. Paul wants us to believe the second half just as firmly: the person who prays, forgives and gives is growing younger in what matters. He does not make light of pain; he himself had been beaten, shipwrecked and stoned. But he weighs it against glory: “For this slight momentary affliction is preparing us for an eternal weight of glory beyond all measure, because we look not at what can be seen but at what cannot be seen; for what can be seen is temporary, but what cannot be seen is eternal.” The word “preparing” matters: suffering joined to Christ is not wasted; it is work being done in us.

The reading ends with an image from Paul's own trade, for he was a tentmaker (Acts 18:3): “For we know that if the earthly tent we live in is destroyed, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens.” A tent is a home for a journey, folded away when the journey ends. Paul does not despise the body, for the Church believes in the resurrection of the body, the very flesh we now carry; but he knows that our present life is a tent, and that God is preparing a house which no monsoon, no fire and no death can bring down. For the families of Kerala who have laid parents and children in the churchyard, this is no poetry. It is the ground on which they stand when they say at the grave: we believe in the resurrection of the dead and the life of the world to come.

The Gospel puts this hope in the hands of Jesus. Some Greeks had come to Philip saying, “Sir, we wish to see Jesus”, and Jesus answers by saying that the hour has come: “The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.” Then he shows what glory looks like: “Very truly, I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.” Every farmer who sows in the monsoon soil knows this. A seed kept in a tin is safe and useless; a seed buried in wet earth seems lost and becomes a field. Jesus is speaking first of himself, for the Cross is the grain falling, and the Church is the harvest. Then he opens the saying to all who follow: “Those who love their life lose it, and those who hate their life in this world will keep it for eternal life.” To “hate” one's life is a way of speaking in Semitic idiom, meaning to love it less than him. “Whoever serves me must follow me, and where I am, there will my servant be also.” The martyrs of the Church are the grain that fell: the blood of the martyrs, the early Christians said, is the seed of the Church. Even Jesus is not spared the trembling: “Now my soul is troubled.” He does not pretend. He asks the question that every one of us has asked and then gives himself to the Father: “No, it is for this reason that I have come to this hour. Father, glorify your name.” And the voice from heaven answers: “I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.”

Homily outline

Theme: “Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit.”

The courage of faith in the face of wasting and loss, the house that God prepares, and the Lord who is the grain that falls into the earth.

1. So we do not lose heart

  • The One who raised Jesus will raise us.
  • Treasure in clay jars.
  • Suffering offered for others becomes thanksgiving.
“May those who sow in tears reap with shouts of joy.” Psalm 126:5
“So we do not lose heart. Even though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day.” 2 Corinthians 4:16

Apply: Place one discouragement of this week in the hands of the risen Lord each morning.

2. Look at what cannot be seen

  • A slight momentary affliction.
  • An eternal weight of glory.
  • The earthly tent and the house from God.
“I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worth comparing with the glory about to be revealed to us.” Romans 8:18
“For we know that if the earthly tent we live in is destroyed, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens.” 2 Corinthians 5:1

Apply: Pray for your dead and for the sick in your parish, and visit one of them.

3. The grain of wheat

  • The hour has come: the Cross is the glory.
  • Whoever serves me must follow me.
  • The voice: I will glorify it again.
“Fool! What you sow does not come to life unless it dies.” 1 Corinthians 15:36
“Whoever serves me must follow me, and where I am, there will my servant be also. Whoever serves me, the Father will honor.” John 12:26

Apply: Choose one small dying to self this week, and do it quietly.

The One who raised Jesus will raise us; our inner nature is renewed, and the house from God is prepared.

Take-home resolution: This week, offer one trouble to the risen Lord each morning, pray for the sick and the dead, and let one small seed of yourself fall for someone else.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul tells us today, “We do not lose heart.” Whoever has watched a parent grow old, or has felt the weight of years, understands how much those words are worth. Our outer nature is wasting away, he says, but our inner nature is being renewed day by day (2 Corinthians 4:16).

Why does Paul speak with such confidence? Because he knows that the One who raised the Lord Jesus will raise us also (2 Corinthians 4:14). Our troubles are real, but they are slight compared with the glory being prepared. We look not at what can be seen, but at what cannot be seen.

Paul was a tentmaker, and he says that our earthly body is like a tent. When it is folded up, God has a building ready, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens (2 Corinthians 5:1). That is why we stand at the grave of our dear ones with hope.

In the Gospel Jesus says, “Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit” (John 12:24). A seed in a tin is safe and useless. A seed in the wet earth looks lost and becomes a field. Jesus himself is that grain, and so are all who give their lives for others.

This week let us find one place where we can let a seed fall: a sharp word we keep back, an hour we give to someone old or sick, a quarrel we end. He who serves Jesus must follow him, and where he is, there will his servant be also (John 12:26). Amen.

For children

Children, a seed looks lost when the farmer puts it in the mud, but then it becomes a green plant with many grains! Jesus said that is how love works. When we give something up for someone else, God makes something beautiful grow from it.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, grain of wheat fallen into the earth, you died to bring forth a great harvest. Give us the courage not to lose heart when our bodies grow weak or our hopes seem buried. Renew our inner life day by day; teach us to look at what cannot be seen; and make us willing to fall into the earth and die to ourselves, so that the grace we receive may grow into thanksgiving for many. Prepare for us, and for all our beloved dead, the house not made with hands. You live and reign with the Father and the Holy Spirit, one God, for ever. Amen.
