Word for the day

“Jesus said to him, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.”” John 14:6

Reflection

Today the Church honours two of the Twelve together, Philip and James. The Gospels tell us more about Philip than about many of the apostles, and almost all of it comes from John. Philip was “from Bethsaida, the city of Andrew and Peter” (John 1:44), a fishing town on the Sea of Galilee. Jesus found him and said simply, “Follow me”, and Philip at once became a messenger: he found Nathanael and told him, “We have found him about whom Moses in the law and also the prophets wrote, Jesus son of Joseph from Nazareth.” When Nathanael scoffed, “Can anything good come out of Nazareth?”, Philip did not argue; he said, “Come and see” (John 1:45–46). Before the feeding of the five thousand, Jesus tested him: “Where are we to buy bread for these people to eat?”, and Philip, practical to the end, did the arithmetic: “Six months’ wages would not buy enough bread for each of them to get a little” (John 6:5–7). When some Greeks came to the feast and said, “Sir, we wish to see Jesus”, it was to Philip that they came (John 12:21). An early tradition, recorded by the historian Eusebius from a letter of Bishop Polycrates of Ephesus at the end of the second century, says that Philip was buried at Hierapolis in Asia Minor. The second apostle is James, the son of Alphaeus (Matthew 10:3). The Latin tradition has long identified him with James ‘the less’ and with James ‘the brother of the Lord’, who led the Church in Jerusalem (Acts 15:13; Galatians 1:19) and to whom the risen Lord appeared (1 Corinthians 15:7); the Eastern tradition has generally distinguished these figures. We need not settle the question to honour him: an apostle chosen by Christ, a witness of the Resurrection, and a pillar of the first Church. The two have been honoured together in the West since the dedication of the Basilica of the Holy Apostles in Rome, where their relics are kept, in the sixth century.

The Gospel of the feast is the conversation of the Last Supper in which Philip spoke. Jesus begins with words that Christians have read at countless deathbeds: “Do not let your hearts be troubled. Believe in God, believe also in me. In my Father’s house there are many dwelling places.” He is going to prepare a place, and he will come again and take us to himself, “so that where I am, there you may be also.” Thomas, honest as always, objects: “Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?” And Jesus gives the answer that sums up the whole Gospel: “I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.” In the longer reading, Philip asks the question that lies under every human search for God: “Lord, show us the Father, and we will be satisfied.” Jesus answers with gentle sorrow: “Have I been with you all this time, Philip, and you still do not know me? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.” Three days before the Ascension these words take on a special weight. Jesus is going to the Father; but he is not leaving a map, he is leaving himself. The way to the Father is not a technique or a set of rules but a Person, the Son made man. The Church in India lives among many sincere seekers who speak of many paths to God. We honour every true search, for God is at work in it; but we cannot keep silent about the One who said, ‘I am the way.’

The two Old Testament readings sing of the victory God gives his faithful ones. Exodus gives the song of Moses on the shore of the sea, which the Church sings in the night of Easter: “I will sing to the Lord, for he has triumphed gloriously; horse and rider he has thrown into the sea. The Lord is my strength and my might, and he has become my salvation.” The apostles saw a greater exodus: the Lord passing through death and bringing his people out of the slavery of sin. Wisdom then describes the day when the despised righteous will be vindicated: “Then the righteous will stand with great confidence in the presence of those who have oppressed them and those who make light of their labors.” Their persecutors will say in amazement, “Why have they been numbered among the children of God? And why is their lot among the saints?” Tradition remembers that both Philip and James gave their lives for Christ. James, the brother of the Lord, was put to death in Jerusalem in the early sixties of the first century, as the Jewish historian Josephus records; of Philip's death the accounts are later and differ. The world thought their lives were wasted. The Church sings with Moses that the Lord has triumphed in them.

In the Epistle Paul explains why an apostle cannot be silent: “If I proclaim the gospel, this gives me no ground for boasting, for an obligation is laid on me, and woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel!” He makes himself “a slave to all” so that he might win more of them; to Jews he becomes as a Jew, to those outside the law as one outside the law, to the weak weak: “I have become all things to all people, that I might by all means save some.” This is not compromise of the truth but the humility of love, which goes to people where they are. Philip lived it when he said, “Come and see”, and when he brought the Greeks to Jesus. The weekday readings of this Sixth Monday add a word for us all. Peter calls the baptised “living stones” built into “a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ”, and in Matthew Jesus warns against a religion that is only outward, cleaning “the outside of the cup” while the inside stays unclean. The apostles' faith was not a show. It was a life given. On their feast we ask for that same apostolic faith, the faith of the Church that is built on the foundation of the apostles, and for the courage to say to our friends, as Philip did, ‘Come and see.’

Homily outline

Theme: “Jesus said to him, ‘I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.’”

On the feast of the apostles Philip and James: the Father seen in the Son, the way that is a Person, the duty to proclaim, and the confidence of the righteous.

1. Lord, show us the Father

  • The longing of every human heart.
  • Philip's honest question.
  • Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.
“Moses said, “Show me your glory, I pray.”” Exodus 33:18
“Whoever has seen me has seen the Father.” John 14:9

Apply: Spend some minutes today before a crucifix or icon, asking the Lord to show you the Father.

2. I am the way, and the truth, and the life

  • Do not let your hearts be troubled.
  • Many dwelling places.
  • A Person, not a map.
“You show me the path of life. In your presence there is fullness of joy; in your right hand are pleasures forevermore.” Psalm 16:11
“Jesus said to him, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.”” John 14:6

Apply: Bring one troubled worry to Jesus in prayer today and leave it with him.

3. Woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel

  • An obligation laid on me.
  • All things to all people.
  • Come and see.
“If I say, “I will not mention him, or speak any more in his name,” then within me there is something like a burning fire shut up in my bones” Jeremiah 20:9
“woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel!” 1 Corinthians 9:16

Apply: Invite one person this week to come and see: to the Qurbana, to adoration or to a prayer meeting.

4. The righteous will stand with confidence

  • The song of Moses at the sea.
  • The despised vindicated.
  • The apostles who gave their lives.
“Then the righteous will stand with great confidence in the presence of those who have oppressed them and those who make light of their labors.” Wisdom 5:1
“Blessed is anyone who endures temptation. Such a one has stood the test and will receive the crown of life that the Lord has promised to those who love him.” James 1:12

Apply: Pray today for Christians who suffer for their faith, that they may stand firm.

Philip and James saw the Father in the face of Jesus and gave their lives proclaiming him.

May we walk the way that is Christ and invite others to come and see.

Take-home resolution: This week, pray the Creed slowly each day, invite one friend to meet Jesus, and pray for persecuted Christians.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we honour two apostles, Philip and James. Philip was a practical man from Bethsaida. When Jesus called him, he ran to his friend Nathanael and said, “Come and see” (John 1:46). James led the Church in Jerusalem, and the risen Lord appeared to him (1 Corinthians 15:7).

At the Last Supper Philip asked, “Lord, show us the Father, and we will be satisfied.” Jesus answered, “Whoever has seen me has seen the Father” (John 14:8–9). We do not need to search far for God. He has shown his face in Jesus.

Jesus also said, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life” (John 14:6). On Thursday he will go up to the Father. He does not leave us a map; he leaves us himself. To follow him is to be on the way to the Father's house.

Paul says, “woe to me if I do not proclaim the gospel!” (1 Corinthians 9:16). The apostles could not keep silent. They gave their lives for Christ. Like the righteous in the Book of Wisdom, they now stand with great confidence before God.

Each of us can be a little apostle. We can say to a friend, ‘Come and see.’ Let us ask Sts Philip and James for their courage and their faith. Alleluia! Amen.

For children

Children, Philip found Jesus and ran to his friend: ‘Come and see!’

Who is a friend you can bring to Jesus? Invite them to church, or tell them a story about Jesus.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you are the way, the truth and the life, and whoever sees you sees the Father. On the feast of your apostles Philip and James, keep us in the faith they proclaimed and sealed with their lives. Calm our troubled hearts, show us the Father, and give us the courage to say to our friends, Come and see. Make us living stones in your Church, clean within and without, until we reach the dwelling place you have gone to prepare for us. Amen. Alleluia!
