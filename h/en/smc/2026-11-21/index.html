Word for the day

Reflection

Today the Syro-Malabar Church remembers the arrival of the apostle Thomas in India. According to the ancient tradition of the St Thomas Christians he landed in AD 52 at Kodungallur (Maliankara, near ancient Muziris), preached the Gospel along the Malabar coast, founded Christian communities, and was martyred at Mylapore. Our Church calls him “our Father in faith”.

The readings show what an apostle is: someone called and sent. God calls Moses and sends him to Pharaoh with a promise: “I will take you as my people.” Isaiah's God says to his servant, chosen “from the ends of the earth”: “Do not fear, for I am with you.” Paul tells the Gentiles that they are “no longer strangers and aliens,” but members of God's household, “built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone.” That is our story: a people at the ends of the earth, built into God's house on the foundation of Thomas.

In the Gospel it is Thomas who asks the honest question: “Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?” And Jesus answers with one of the greatest sayings of the Gospel: “I am the way, and the truth, and the life.” Thomas brought to India not a philosophy but a Person, the Way himself. Later, touching the risen Lord's wounds, he would confess, “My Lord and my God!” That confession is the foundation of our faith.

Today is also the Presentation of the Blessed Virgin Mary: the Church remembers, from ancient tradition, Mary's dedication to God from childhood. Mary and Thomas both teach us the same thing: to belong wholly to God and to follow the Way.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, today our Church remembers a day that changed our land: the arrival of the apostle Thomas in India. Our ancestors handed down the memory that he landed at Kodungallur, preached on this coast, founded Christian communities, and gave his life at Mylapore. We are his children in faith.

The readings tell us what an apostle is. God calls Moses and sends him with a promise. God says to his servant, chosen from the ends of the earth, “Do not fear, I am with you.” St Paul tells the Gentiles: you are no longer strangers; you are members of God's household, built on the foundation of the apostles. That is our story. We were at the ends of the earth, and through Thomas we became God's household.

In the Gospel Thomas asks an honest question: “Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?” Jesus answers: “I am the way, and the truth, and the life.” Thomas did not bring our ancestors a new philosophy; he brought them a Person, the Way himself. No wonder our fathers called the faith “Margam”, the Way.

Later, touching the wounds of the risen Lord, Thomas confessed, “My Lord and my God!” That confession crossed the sea and became ours. Every time the Body of Christ is raised at the Qurbana, we say it again.

Today we also remember the Presentation of Mary, who belonged wholly to God from childhood. Let us ask Mary and Thomas to help us belong wholly to Jesus and to carry the Way to others. My Lord and my God! Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, today we remember with gratitude the coming of the apostle Thomas to our land. According to the ancient tradition of our Church, he landed on the coast of Kerala, preached Christ, founded communities and finally gave his life as a martyr. Let us look at Thomas in the Gospel to understand the faith he brought us.

Thomas is famous for his doubt. When the other disciples said they had seen the risen Lord, he said: unless I see the mark of the nails in His hands, and put my finger in the mark of the nails and my hand in His side, I will not believe (John 20:25). A week later Jesus came and showed him His wounds. And Thomas made the greatest confession of faith in all the Gospels: my Lord and my God (John 20:28). Doubt became adoration.

But Thomas was also courageous. When Jesus decided to go back to Judea, where His enemies wanted to stone Him, it was Thomas who said: let us also go, that we may die with Him (John 11:16). And at the Last Supper it was Thomas who asked: Lord, we do not know where you are going; how can we know the way? Jesus answered: I am the way, and the truth, and the life (John 14:5–6).

Our ancestors called the Christian life Mar Thoma Margam, the Way of Thomas. It is the way of a faith that is honest enough to ask questions, brave enough to follow Christ to death, and humble enough to kneel and say my Lord and my God. At the elevation in every Holy Qurbana, many of us whisper those very words.

Today Thomas Christians live all over the world. The faith he brought to one coast has been carried to the Gulf, to Europe, to America, to Australia. In every place, let us keep it, live it and hand it on to our children. Like Thomas, may we see the wounds of Christ in the poor and suffering, and confess Him as our Lord and our God.

Dear brothers and sisters, on the day we remember the coming of St Thomas, let us learn something about the identity of our Church, because many young people do not know it.

According to our ancient and living tradition, the apostle Thomas preached the Gospel in India in the first century and died as a martyr. Historians discuss the details, but the faith of the community, the songs, the place names and the very name St Thomas Christians bear witness to a Christian presence in this land from very early times. For centuries our Church lived in communion with the Church of the East and used the East Syriac liturgy, the liturgy of the Holy Qurbana of Mar Addai and Mar Mari, one of the oldest Eucharistic prayers in the world.

Our history also had trials. From the sixteenth century there were contacts and tensions with Western missionaries, Latinisation of our customs and liturgy, divisions and painful separations. Yet the community remained faithful to Christ and to the Catholic communion. Gradually the Church recovered her identity: her own bishops, her own liturgical reforms, and in 1992 the Syro-Malabar Church was raised to the status of a Major Archiepiscopal Church, governed by its Synod of Bishops under the Major Archbishop, in full communion with the Pope.

We are one of the twenty-three Eastern Catholic Churches sui iuris. That means we have our own liturgy, our own canon law, our own spirituality and theology, and we are fully Catholic. The Second Vatican Council asked Eastern Catholics to preserve their traditions faithfully (Orientalium Ecclesiarum 6). This is not a matter of pride, but of fidelity.

Today our Church has missions in many parts of India and dioceses in the Gulf, Europe, North America and Australia. Let us thank God for the faith of our ancestors, pray for unity and peace in our Church, support her missions, and above all live the faith of Thomas so that our children and grandchildren may also say with him: my Lord and my God.
