Word for the day

“He said, “The one who showed him mercy.” Jesus said to him, “Go and do likewise.”” Luke 10:37

Reflection

Two weeks after Pentecost the Season of the Apostles shows us the Church on the move. The first reading is the beginning of Moses' great farewell speech on the plains of Moab, at the edge of the promised land. He recalls the word God spoke at Mount Horeb, forty years earlier: “You have stayed long enough at this mountain. Resume your journey, and go into the hill country of the Amorites.” Then the promise: “See, I have set the land before you; go in and take possession of the land that I swore to your ancestors, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.” Horeb, or Sinai, was the mountain of the covenant, the place where God had spoken. It would have been easy to stay there for ever. But the covenant was given for a journey. So it is with Pentecost. The apostles did not stay in the Upper Room; the Spirit sent them out to Samaria, to Antioch, to Rome, and, as our tradition holds, to the coasts of India with Mar Thoma. Every parish, every prayer group and every family is tempted to settle down on its own mountain of good experiences. The Lord says to us too: you have stayed long enough; resume your journey. The longer reading adds that Moses could not carry the people alone and appointed wise leaders, telling the judges: “hear out the small and the great alike; you shall not be intimidated by anyone, for the judgment is God’s.” The Church on the move shares her burdens and is just to the small.

Isaiah opens his book with a lawsuit in which heaven and earth are the witnesses: “Hear, O heavens, and listen, O earth; for the Lord has spoken: I reared children and brought them up, but they have rebelled against me. The ox knows its owner, and the donkey its master’s crib; but Israel does not know, my people do not understand.” The image is homely and painful. A farm animal knows the hand that feeds it and finds its way back to its own manger, but God's own children have forgotten him. The Fathers loved to see in this verse the ox and the donkey at the manger of Bethlehem, where the Lord of Israel lay in a feeding trough, recognised by animals and by shepherds. Isaiah describes a land wounded from head to foot, “bruises and sores and bleeding wounds; they have not been drained, or bound up, or softened with oil.” Remember those words when we come to the Gospel. And yet even here there is hope: “If the Lord of hosts had not left us a few survivors, we would have been like Sodom.” God always keeps a remnant, and through the remnant he begins again.

Paul answers questions the Corinthians had written to him about marriage. Some among them, it seems, were saying, “It is well for a man not to touch a woman,” as though the married love of husband and wife were somehow unholy. Paul disagrees. Husband and wife belong to each other: “The husband should give to his wife her conjugal rights, and likewise the wife to her husband.” What is striking, in a world where wives were often treated as the property of their husbands, is that Paul speaks of perfect mutuality: neither has authority over his or her own body; each belongs to the other. Married couples may abstain for a time by agreement in order to give themselves to prayer, and then come together again. Then he adds: “I wish that all were as I myself am. But each has a particular gift from God, one having one kind and another a different kind.” Marriage is a gift; consecrated celibacy is a gift. Neither is to be despised, and each is a true path to holiness. Our Syro-Malabar Church, which crowns the bride and groom in the sacrament of Marriage and whose families have given so many priests and religious sisters to the world, knows both gifts. Today let us pray for married couples, especially those going through difficulties, and for all who have given their bodies and lives to God in priesthood and religious life.

The Gospel is one of the best-loved stories Jesus ever told. A lawyer asks, “Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?” He knows the answer himself: love God with all your heart, soul, strength and mind, and your neighbour as yourself. But “wanting to justify himself, he asked Jesus, ‘And who is my neighbor?’” He wants a boundary: who counts, and who does not? Jesus answers with a story. A man going down from Jerusalem to Jericho is stripped, beaten and left half dead. A priest passes by on the other side; so does a Levite. We are not told why, and we should not mock them; perhaps they feared ritual impurity, perhaps robbers. Jesus, a Jew speaking to a Jew, then names the one person his hearers least expected to be the hero: “But a Samaritan while traveling came near him; and when he saw him, he was moved with pity.” The Samaritan does all that Isaiah said no one had done for wounded Israel: he binds the wounds and pours in oil and wine. He takes him to an inn, pays, and promises to come back. Then Jesus turns the lawyer's question around: not, who is my neighbour, but “Which of these three, do you think, was a neighbor to the man who fell into the hands of the robbers?” The lawyer cannot bring himself to say “the Samaritan”; he says, “The one who showed him mercy.” “Go and do likewise.” The Fathers saw in the Samaritan Christ himself, who came down to our wounded humanity, poured in the oil and wine of the sacraments, brought us to the inn of the Church and promised to return. Those who have been healed by such a Samaritan cannot pass by on the other side: not by the accident victim on the national highway, nor by the migrant worker far from home, nor by the neighbour of another caste or another religion.

Homily outline

Theme: “He said, ‘The one who showed him mercy.’ Jesus said to him, ‘Go and do likewise.’”

On the Third Sunday of the Apostles: a people sent on from the mountain, a people who forgot their owner, the gifts of marriage and celibacy, and the Samaritan who became a neighbour.

1. You have stayed long enough at this mountain

  • Horeb, the mountain of the covenant.
  • Resume your journey.
  • The apostles sent to the nations.
“The Lord our God spoke to us at Horeb, saying, “You have stayed long enough at this mountain.” Deuteronomy 1:6
“And he said to them, “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.” Mark 16:15

Apply: Take one step out of your comfortable circle this week in the service of the Gospel.

2. The ox knows its owner

  • Heaven and earth as witnesses.
  • Children who forgot their Father.
  • The remnant God keeps.
“The ox knows its owner, and the donkey its master’s crib; but Israel does not know, my people do not understand.” Isaiah 1:3
“And she gave birth to her firstborn son and wrapped him in bands of cloth, and laid him in a manger, because there was no place for them in the inn.” Luke 2:7

Apply: Begin and end each day this week with prayer at the family altar.

3. Each has a particular gift from God

  • Married love is holy.
  • Husband and wife belong to each other.
  • Marriage and celibacy, two gifts.
“Therefore a man leaves his father and his mother and clings to his wife, and they become one flesh.” Genesis 2:24
“I wish that all were as I myself am. But each has a particular gift from God, one having one kind and another a different kind.” 1 Corinthians 7:7

Apply: Married couples, pray together each night this week; everyone, pray for a priest or a sister by name.

4. Go and do likewise

  • Who is my neighbour?
  • Moved with pity, he came near.
  • Christ the Samaritan, the Church the inn.
“You shall not take vengeance or bear a grudge against any of your people, but you shall love your neighbor as yourself: I am the Lord.” Leviticus 19:18
“But a Samaritan while traveling came near him; and when he saw him, he was moved with pity.” Luke 10:33

Apply: Do one concrete act of mercy this week for someone outside your own group.

The Spirit of the apostles moves us off our mountains, reminds us whose we are, blesses every vocation and makes us neighbours.

Healed by Christ the Samaritan, we cannot pass by on the other side.

Take-home resolution: This week, step out of your comfort, pray daily at the family altar, pray for marriages and vocations, and show mercy to someone outside your circle.

Full homily

Dear friends, two weeks after Pentecost, the readings show us a Church on the move. At Mount Horeb God said to his people, “You have stayed long enough at this mountain. Resume your journey” (Deuteronomy 1:6–7). The apostles did not stay in the Upper Room; the Spirit sent them out, and Mar Thoma came even to our shores.

We too can settle on our own mountain: our group, our habits, our comfort. The Lord says to us: you have stayed long enough. Go out to the family you do not know, to the parishioner who has stopped coming, to those who have never heard of Christ.

Isaiah weeps over a people who forgot God: “The ox knows its owner, and the donkey its master’s crib; but Israel does not know” (Isaiah 1:3). A farm animal knows who feeds it. Do we remember the One who feeds us every day? Let our homes show to whom they belong.

Saint Paul speaks to married couples: husband and wife belong to each other, and should give themselves to each other with love; they may pray together and support each other. And he says, each has a particular gift from God (1 Corinthians 7:7). Marriage is a gift; the life of priests and sisters is a gift. Let us honour both and pray for both.

In the Gospel a man lies wounded on the road. A priest and a Levite pass by. A Samaritan, the one the listeners least expected, stops, binds his wounds, pours in oil and wine, and takes him to an inn. Jesus asks: who was a neighbour? “The one who showed him mercy.” “Go and do likewise” (Luke 10:37).

The Fathers saw in the Samaritan Jesus himself, who stopped for us, healed us with the sacraments and brought us to the inn of the Church. Healed by him, we cannot pass by on the other side. Let us become neighbours to the injured, the migrant, the lonely and the stranger, whatever their caste or religion. Amen.

For children

Children, a man was hurt on the road. Two people walked past without helping. But a Samaritan stopped, cleaned his wounds and took him to a safe place.

Jesus says to us: “Go and do likewise.” This week, look for someone who needs help, at school, at home or on the road, and help them!

Prayer

Lord God, you led your people from the mountain of the covenant toward the land you promised, and at Pentecost you sent your apostles to the ends of the earth. Do not let us settle on our own mountain, but send us out. Forgive us when we have forgotten you, though the ox knows its owner. Bless husbands and wives with faithful love, and those you have called to give themselves wholly to you with joy in their gift. Lord Jesus, true Samaritan, you came near to our wounds and poured in oil and wine: make us neighbours to everyone we meet, until you come again. Glory to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
