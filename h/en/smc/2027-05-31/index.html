Word for the day

“And blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” Luke 1:45

Reflection

On the last day of the month of Mary the Church follows her on a journey. Luke has just told of the Annunciation: the angel has left, the Word has become flesh in her womb, and she has heard that her elderly kinswoman Elizabeth, who was called barren, is in her sixth month. Mary does not stay at home to guard her secret. “In those days Mary set out and went with haste to a Judean town in the hill country.” It was a long journey, perhaps four or five days on foot from Nazareth to the hill country of Judea, which tradition places at Ain Karem near Jerusalem. The haste is not anxiety; it is the haste of love and of joy. St Ambrose says that she went joyful in her desire, devout in her duty, eager in her gladness. The first thing Mary does after receiving Christ is to carry him to someone who needs her. That is the pattern of every Christian life, and especially of this Season of the Apostles: the one who has received Christ goes out in haste to bring him to others. Pope Francis took Mary's haste as the theme of the World Youth Day at Lisbon in 2023, “Mary arose and went with haste”, and told the young that it is the haste of one who cannot keep a great gift to herself.

Then the meeting: Mary “entered the house of Zechariah and greeted Elizabeth. When Elizabeth heard Mary’s greeting, the child leaped in her womb. And Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit and exclaimed with a loud cry, ‘Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb. And why has this happened to me, that the mother of my Lord comes to me?’” Two women meet, one old and one young, both carrying a child that only God could give. But the real meeting is between the two children: John, still unborn, leaps for joy before the unborn Christ. The Church sees here John the Baptist filled with the Holy Spirit from his mother's womb, as the angel had promised (Luke 1:15). Elizabeth's words have become our own daily prayer: in every Hail Mary we say with her, “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb.” And she gives Mary the title that the Council of Ephesus would later solemnly proclaim: the mother of my Lord, the Mother of God.

Luke tells the story with words that recall the ark of the covenant. When King David brought the ark up to Jerusalem, he said, “How can the ark of the Lord come into my care?” (2 Samuel 6:9); he “danced before the Lord with all his might” (2 Samuel 6:14), and the ark remained three months in the house of Obed-edom, bringing blessing. Elizabeth asks, “why has this happened to me, that the mother of my Lord comes to me?” John leaps for joy, and Mary remains with Elizabeth about three months (Luke 1:56). The Fathers and many interpreters see the meaning: Mary is the new ark, carrying not tablets of stone but the living Word of God. The Syriac Fathers, St Ephrem among them, sing of her in images like these. St John Paul II added that at the Visitation Mary became in some way the first tabernacle in history; her journey has also been called the first Eucharistic procession. How fitting that this feast falls in the week of the Body and Blood of Christ, and that the weekday Gospel today has Jesus saying, “I am the bread of life” (John 6:35). Mary carried the Bread of Life to Elizabeth's house; we carry him from the altar into ours.

Elizabeth's last word is a beatitude, the first in Luke's Gospel: “And blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her by the Lord.” Mary is blessed first not because of a privilege, but because she believed. She is the model of faith for every disciple. And from that faith comes love in action, which is what Paul describes in the Epistle chosen for the feast: “Let love be genuine; hate what is evil, hold fast to what is good; love one another with mutual affection; outdo one another in showing honor.” Then: “Contribute to the needs of the saints; extend hospitality to strangers… Rejoice with those who rejoice, weep with those who weep. Live in harmony with one another; do not be haughty, but associate with the lowly.” Every phrase could describe the three months at Ain Karem: a young woman who serves her older relative, rejoices with her, shares her house and her joys, and makes herself lowly though she carries the Lord. The weekday Epistle says the same in its own way: “If one member suffers, all suffer together with it; if one member is honored, all rejoice together with it” (1 Corinthians 12:26). As the month of Mary ends, the best homage we can give her is to do what she did: to believe the word of the Lord, and to set out in haste to serve someone who needs us.

Full homily

Dear friends, today is the last day of the month of Mary, and the Church celebrates the Visitation. The angel has left Nazareth, the Son of God has taken flesh in Mary's womb, and Mary hears that Elizabeth, old and once barren, is expecting a child. What does she do? “Mary set out and went with haste” (Luke 1:39).

The first thing Mary does after receiving Jesus is to carry him to someone who needs her. Her haste is the haste of love and joy. In this Season of the Apostles, the Church does the same: whoever has received Christ goes out to bring him to others.

When Mary greets Elizabeth, the child in Elizabeth's womb leaps for joy, and Elizabeth is filled with the Holy Spirit. She cries out, “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb.” We say these words in every Hail Mary. And she calls Mary “the mother of my Lord” (Luke 1:43), the Mother of God.

Luke tells the story like the story of the ark of the covenant. David danced before the ark; John leaps before Jesus. The ark stayed three months in a house and brought blessing; Mary stays three months with Elizabeth. Mary is the new ark, the first tabernacle, carrying the living Bread to Elizabeth's home.

Elizabeth says, “blessed is she who believed” (Luke 1:45). Mary believed, and her faith became service. Saint Paul tells us how: let love be genuine, extend hospitality, rejoice with those who rejoice, weep with those who weep, associate with the lowly (Romans 12:9–16).

As we close the month of Mary, let us give her the gift she loves most: to believe God's word as she did, and to set out in haste to someone who needs us, carrying Jesus whom we have received in the Holy Qurbana. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us. Amen.

For children

Children, Mary walked a long way over the hills to help her old cousin Elizabeth. When Mary said hello, baby John jumped for joy inside Elizabeth, because Jesus was there!

Like Mary, hurry to help someone this week: help Grandma, a little brother, or a friend. And every time you pray the Hail Mary, remember Elizabeth's happy words!

Prayer

Lord God, you inspired the Blessed Virgin Mary, carrying your Son in her womb, to set out in haste to visit Elizabeth, and at her greeting John leaped for joy and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit. Give us Mary's faith, to believe that your word will be fulfilled, and her haste, to carry Christ to those who need him. Make our love genuine, our homes hospitable and our hearts lowly. As we close this month of Mary, keep us under her protection, and let us magnify you with her all our days. Through Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
