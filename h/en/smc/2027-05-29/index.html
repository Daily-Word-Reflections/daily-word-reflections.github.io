Word for the day

“Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread.” 1 Corinthians 10:17

Reflection

All week the readings have followed the sixth chapter of John; today, at the end of the week of the Body and Blood of Christ, we go back to its beginning, the sign that opened the whole discourse. A large crowd follows Jesus to the far side of the Sea of Galilee, and John notes that “the Passover, the festival of the Jews, was near.” The feeding is a Passover sign, pointing to the new Passover of the Last Supper. Jesus asks Philip, “Where are we to buy bread for these people to eat?” He asks it, John says, to test him, “for he himself knew what he was going to do.” Philip does the arithmetic: “Six months’ wages would not buy enough bread for each of them to get a little.” Andrew finds a boy: “There is a boy here who has five barley loaves and two fish. But what are they among so many people?” Barley loaves were the bread of the poor. The boy gives what he has, and it is not enough, and Jesus does not ask for more. He takes it, gives thanks and distributes it, and some five thousand eat “as much as they wanted.” The words are the words of the Eucharist: he took the loaves, gave thanks, and distributed them. Every Holy Qurbana begins with our small offering, bread and wine and the gifts of the faithful, and the Lord does with it far more than we could imagine.

Two details are easy to miss. First: “When they were satisfied, he told his disciples, ‘Gather up the fragments left over, so that nothing may be lost.’ So they gathered them up, and … they filled twelve baskets.” The Lord who gives in abundance does not waste. The twelve baskets, one for each apostle, are carried by the Twelve; they will feed the twelve tribes of a new Israel with what the Lord has blessed. And the words “so that nothing may be lost” echo what Jesus says later in the chapter about people: “that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me” (John 6:39). Second: when the crowd wants to come and take him by force to make him king, Jesus withdraws to the mountain alone. He will not be a king who wins votes with bread. His kingship will be revealed on the Cross, and the bread he will give is his own flesh.

Paul draws the conclusion for a Church living among many altars. Corinth was full of temples, and meat sacrificed to idols was sold in the market and served at banquets. Paul writes: “Therefore, my dear friends, flee from the worship of idols.” Then he gives the reason: “The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a sharing in the blood of Christ? The bread that we break, is it not a sharing in the body of Christ? Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread.” The word translated sharing is koinonia, communion. Holy Communion is not a private devotion between me and Jesus; it makes us one body. That is why Paul says: “You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons.” He is not scorning the neighbours of the Corinthians; he is warning Christians against a divided heart. Our idols today are rarely made of stone or metal. They are money and success, the craving for approval, trust in charms and lucky days in place of trust in God. Whoever has eaten the one bread cannot give the heart to them. And whoever has eaten the one bread cannot ignore the hungry: the bread we break at the altar sends us to break our bread with the poor, and to gather up the fragments, so that nothing and no one is lost.

Full homily

Dear friends, a huge crowd followed Jesus, and there was nothing to eat. Philip counted the cost: six months' wages would not be enough. Andrew found a boy with five barley loaves and two fish, and said, “But what are they among so many people?” (John 6:9).

The boy gave what he had. It was not enough. But Jesus took the loaves, gave thanks and distributed them, and five thousand ate as much as they wanted. Those are the words of the Holy Qurbana: he took, gave thanks, and gave. Never think your gift is too small for God.

Then Jesus said, “Gather up the fragments left over, so that nothing may be lost” (John 6:12). Twelve baskets were filled. The Lord gives in abundance, but nothing is wasted, and no one is to be lost. Let us waste no food, and gather up the ones the world forgets.

Saint Paul says: the bread that we break is a sharing in the body of Christ; because there is one bread, we who are many are one body (1 Corinthians 10:16–17). Communion makes us one. So we cannot give our hearts to idols, to money, success or charms, and we cannot forget the hungry.

This is the last Saturday of the month of Mary. She gave the Lord her whole self, and he took flesh from her. Let us give him our five loaves today, and let him make them bread for many. Amen.

For children

Children, a boy gave Jesus his lunch: five small loaves and two fish. Jesus thanked God and fed five thousand people! And there were twelve baskets left over. Give Jesus what you have, even if it is small, and see what he can do with it!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you took the boy's five barley loaves, gave thanks and fed the multitude, and you told your disciples to gather the fragments so that nothing might be lost. Take the little we have and make it enough for others. You are the one bread that makes us one body: keep our hearts undivided, turned away from every idol, and open to the hungry. Through the prayers of Mary, your Mother, who gave you flesh, make us faithful to the table of the Lord. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
