Word for the day

“But the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you everything, and remind you of all that I have said to you.” John 14:26

Reflection

On the eve of the feast of Peter and Paul the Church remembers a bishop who loved the apostles' teaching more than his own life, and who handed it on so faithfully that he is called today the Doctor of Unity. Irenaeus was born in Asia Minor, probably at Smyrna, in the first half of the second century. As a boy he listened to Polycarp, the bishop of Smyrna, who had known the apostle John. Many years later Irenaeus wrote to a friend that he could still remember the place where Polycarp sat when he taught, how he went in and out, and how he spoke of his conversations with John and with others who had seen the Lord. That is a chain of only three links between Irenaeus and Jesus: the Lord, the beloved disciple, Polycarp, Irenaeus. He went to Gaul, served as a priest in Lyons, and after the persecution of 177, in which the old bishop Pothinus died in prison, he became bishop of that Church. He wrote five books against the false teachings of his day, usually called Against Heresies, and a shorter work, the Demonstration of the Apostolic Preaching. When a dispute about the date of Easter threatened to divide the Church of Rome from the Churches of Asia, he wrote to Pope Victor asking for patience and peace; the historian Eusebius says that he lived up to his name, which means peacemaker. He died around the year 200 and has long been honoured as a martyr, though the details of his death are not known. In 2022 Pope Francis declared him a Doctor of the Church, with the title Doctor of Unity.

The readings chosen for him speak of the priest and the teacher. In Exodus the high priest Aaron wears the breastpiece engraved with the names of the twelve tribes: “So Aaron shall bear the names of the sons of Israel in the breastpiece of judgment on his heart when he goes into the holy place, for a continual remembrance before the Lord.” A true shepherd carries his people on his heart into the presence of God. Malachi recalls God's covenant with Levi and describes the priest as God wanted him: “True instruction was in his mouth, and no wrong was found on his lips. He walked with me in integrity and uprightness, and he turned many from iniquity. For the lips of a priest should guard knowledge, and people should seek instruction from his mouth, for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts.” Irenaeus was such a priest: he guarded knowledge, not secret knowledge for a few, but the public faith of the whole Church.

Paul's words to Timothy could be Irenaeus' own motto: “But as for you, continue in what you have learned and firmly believed, knowing from whom you learned it.” The Gnostic teachers of the second century claimed to possess secret knowledge passed down privately, and they despised the body, the created world and the God of the Old Testament. Irenaeus answered that the apostles had hidden nothing: their teaching is public, kept in the Churches they founded, and handed on by the bishops who succeeded them. He listed the bishops of Rome, “the very great, very ancient and universally known Church founded and organised at Rome by the two most glorious apostles, Peter and Paul” (in substance), and said that every Church must agree with it because of its preeminent authority. So tomorrow's feast and today's saint belong together. He defended the goodness of creation and of the flesh, which the Son of God took from the Virgin Mary; he saw Mary as the new Eve, whose obedience untied the knot of Eve's disobedience; he taught that Christ sums up and renews the whole of human history in himself. And he gave us the sentence that sums up his joy: the glory of God is the human being fully alive, and the life of the human being is the vision of God. He knew that the Spirit was the guarantee of this teaching, as Jesus promised in today's Gospel: “the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you everything, and remind you of all that I have said to you.” All scripture, Paul adds, “is inspired by God and is useful for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness.”

The weekday readings fit the peacemaker well. Paul writes about a member of the Corinthian Church who had caused great pain and been punished by the community. Now Paul asks for mercy: “so now instead you should forgive and console him, so that he may not be overwhelmed by excessive sorrow. So I urge you to reaffirm your love for him.” Discipline in the Church is never meant to crush, only to heal; and an unforgiving community gives the devil an opening: “we do this so that we may not be outwitted by Satan; for we are not ignorant of his designs.” In the Gospel the scribes and Pharisees ask Jesus for a sign. He gives them only the sign of Jonah, three days in the belly of the sea monster, a sign of his own death and resurrection, and he reminds them that the people of Nineveh repented at Jonah's preaching and that the queen of the South came “from the ends of the earth to listen to the wisdom of Solomon, and see, something greater than Solomon is here!” Irenaeus, who carried the Gospel from Asia to the far west of the empire, and we in India, who received it from Mar Thoma at the eastern edge of the known world, know what it means to come from the ends of the earth to hear the One who is greater than Solomon. Let us listen, repent, forgive, and continue in what we have learned.

Homily outline

Theme: “But the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you everything, and remind you of all that I have said to you.”

On the commemoration of St Irenaeus: the shepherd who carries his people on his heart, the faith that is received and handed on, the mercy that forgives and consoles, and the One greater than Jonah.

1. Forgive and console

  • Discipline that heals.
  • Reaffirm your love.
  • Not outwitted by Satan.

“so now instead you should forgive and console him, so that he may not be overwhelmed by excessive sorrow.” 2 Corinthians 2:7

Apply: Console one person this week who has been made to feel the weight of their wrongdoing.

2. Something greater than Jonah

  • The demand for a sign.
  • Three days and three nights.
  • Nineveh repented.
“And the people of Nineveh believed God; they proclaimed a fast, and everyone, great and small, put on sackcloth.” Jonah 3:5
“The people of Nineveh will rise up at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, because they repented at the proclamation of Jonah, and see, something greater than Jonah is here!” Matthew 12:41

Apply: Make one concrete act of repentance this week instead of asking God for signs.

3. Names on the shepherd's heart

  • Aaron's breastpiece.
  • Irenaeus, bishop and peacemaker.
  • The Good Shepherd.
“So Aaron shall bear the names of the sons of Israel in the breastpiece of judgment on his heart when he goes into the holy place, for a continual remembrance before the Lord.” Exodus 28:29
““I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.” John 10:11

Apply: Pray each day this week for your bishop and your parish priest by name.

4. Continue in what you have learned

  • Public tradition, not secret knowledge.
  • The Churches of the apostles.
  • The Spirit who reminds.
“For the lips of a priest should guard knowledge, and people should seek instruction from his mouth, for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts.” Malachi 2:7
“But the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you everything, and remind you of all that I have said to you.” John 14:26

Apply: Thank God by name for those who handed on the faith to you, and contact one of them.

St Irenaeus received the faith from those who knew the apostles and handed it on whole; he made peace in the Church.

Let us forgive and console, repent at the word of the One greater than Jonah, and continue in what we have learned.

Take-home resolution: This week, console someone, make one act of repentance, pray for your shepherds by name, and thank those who taught you the faith.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we remember Saint Irenaeus, a bishop of the second century. As a boy he listened to Polycarp, who had known the apostle John, who had known Jesus. Think of it: only a few links between Irenaeus and the Lord.

False teachers in his time said that they had secret knowledge that ordinary Christians did not have. Irenaeus answered: the apostles hid nothing. Their teaching is public, kept in the Churches they founded and handed on by the bishops who came after them. Paul says the same to Timothy: “continue in what you have learned and firmly believed, knowing from whom you learned it” (2 Timothy 3:14).

The readings for his feast describe a true priest. Aaron carried the names of the tribes on his heart before God. Malachi says, “the lips of a priest should guard knowledge” (Malachi 2:7). And Jesus promises the Holy Spirit, who “will teach you everything, and remind you of all that I have said to you” (John 14:26).

Irenaeus was also a peacemaker; his name means peaceful. When a quarrel about the date of Easter threatened to divide the Church, he begged for patience. In the weekday reading Paul too asks for mercy: forgive and console the one who did wrong, reaffirm your love for him, so that Satan may not outwit you (2 Corinthians 2:7–11).

Irenaeus wrote: the glory of God is the human being fully alive. Tomorrow we celebrate Peter and Paul, the apostles whose Church in Rome Irenaeus honoured. Today let us thank God for those who handed on the faith to us, let us forgive, and let us continue in what we have learned. Amen.

For children

Children, Saint Irenaeus heard about Jesus from an old bishop who had known Saint John, Jesus' friend. The faith came to him like a chain, from hand to hand. Today, say thank you to the person who taught you to pray. And be a peacemaker, like Irenaeus!

Prayer

Lord God, you gave your Church the bishop Saint Irenaeus to guard the faith of the apostles and to make peace among your people. Through his prayers keep us faithful to what we have learned and firmly believed, and grateful to those from whom we learned it. Give our bishops and priests the heart of Aaron, to carry your people before you, and the lips of a true priest, to guard knowledge and to teach. Send us the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, to teach us everything and to remind us of all that your Son has said. Make us quick to forgive and console, so that Satan may not outwit us, and let us live fully, for your glory, until we see you face to face. Through Christ our Lord, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
