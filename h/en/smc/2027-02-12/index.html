Word for the day

“Then the Lord said to Cain, “Where is your brother Abel?” He said, “I do not know; am I my brother's keeper?”” Genesis 4:9

Reflection

Yesterday God asked Adam, “Where are you?” Today he asks Cain, “Where is your brother Abel?” Sin that began as distrust of God quickly turns against the brother. Cain and Abel both bring offerings; God regards Abel's, and Cain's face falls. God speaks to him like a father warning a son: “If you do well, will you not be accepted? And if you do not do well, sin is lurking at the door; its desire is for you, but you must master it.” Cain does not master it. He leads his brother into the field and kills him. When God asks where Abel is, Cain answers with a lie and a shrug: “I do not know; am I my brother's keeper?” But the earth cannot keep the secret: “Listen; your brother's blood is crying out to me from the ground!” And yet even here God shows mercy: he puts a mark on Cain “so that no one who came upon him would kill him.” Jesus speaks to the root of Cain's sin, the comparing and judging eye: “Do not judge, so that you may not be judged.” “Why do you see the speck in your neighbor's eye, but do not notice the log in your own eye?” “You hypocrite, first take the log out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to take the speck out of your neighbor's eye.”

On the Fridays of the Great Fast our parishes walk the Way of the Cross, often in the evening, and many keep a stricter fast. Abel's blood cried from the ground for justice; on the Cross we see another blood, “the sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel”, the blood that cries for mercy. The second reading shows the Jordan in flood at harvest time. As soon as the feet of the priests carrying the ark touch the edge of the water, the river stands up “in a single heap”, and the whole people crosses on dry ground. Twelve men lift twelve stones from the riverbed, so that when the children ask, “What do those stones mean to you?”, the answer will be told. Paul holds up Abraham as the father of all who believe: “Hoping against hope, he believed.” He knew his body was “already as good as dead” and Sarah was barren, yet “No distrust made him waver concerning the promise of God, but he grew strong in his faith as he gave glory to God, being fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised.” These words were written for us too, who believe in the God who raised Jesus, “who was handed over to death for our trespasses and was raised for our justification.” Every Friday of the Great Fast walks toward that Friday, and through it to Easter.

Full homily

Dear friends, God asked Adam: where are you? Today he asks Cain: where is your brother Abel? (Genesis 4:9). Sin against God soon becomes sin against the brother. Cain answers: am I my brother's keeper? God's answer is yes.

God had warned Cain: sin is lurking at the door; its desire is for you, but you must master it (Genesis 4:7). Jealousy began with comparing. That is why Jesus says: do not judge. First take the log out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly (Matthew 7:5).

Abraham teaches us another way: hoping against hope, he believed (Romans 4:18). He did not look at his weakness but at God's promise. And Israel crossed the Jordan because God held back the waters.

Today is a Friday of the Great Fast. As we walk the Way of the Cross, we see the blood of Jesus, which speaks a better word than the blood of Abel. Let us become keepers of our brothers and sisters. Amen.

For children

Children, God asks each of us: where is your brother? where is your sister? Jesus wants us to take care of each other. Do something kind for your brother, sister or friend today.

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, your blood speaks a better word than the blood of Abel. Master the sin that lurks at the door of our hearts, take the log from our eyes, and make us keepers of our brothers and sisters. Give us the faith of Abraham, hoping against hope, and lead us through the waters of these days to your Easter. Amen.
