Word for the day

“When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, “Take heart, son; your sins are forgiven.”” Matthew 9:2

Reflection

The Gospel begins with a group of people we never meet by name. Jesus crosses the lake to his own town, Capernaum, “and just then some people were carrying a paralyzed man lying on a bed.” The man cannot walk to Jesus; others must carry him. Matthew notes something remarkable: “When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralytic, ‘Take heart, son; your sins are forgiven.’” It is the faith of the carriers that Jesus sees. In many of our parishes, during the Great Fast, the priest goes from house to house to hear the confessions of the sick and the elderly and to bring them Holy Communion; in others the young people bring the housebound to church for the penitential service, in wheelchairs, in autorickshaws, sometimes on a chair carried by four. Every such journey repeats this Gospel. Some of us walk to Christ; some of us must be carried; and some of us are called to carry.

Then comes the surprise. The friends had brought the man for his legs, but Jesus speaks first about his sins. The scribes are scandalised: “This man is blaspheming.” Only God can forgive sins, and they are right about that. Jesus answers their unspoken thoughts: “Which is easier, to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Stand up and walk’? But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins” he said to the paralytic, “Stand up, take your bed and go to your home.” The visible healing proves the invisible one. The deepest paralysis is the one that keeps us from God, and Jesus heals that first. The crowd's response is full of meaning for us: “they glorified God, who had given such authority to human beings.” The authority to forgive, which belongs to God alone, Christ has placed in human hands. On Easter evening he will breathe on his apostles and say: “If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them.” Every confession heard in a Kerala parish this Lent stands on this verse.

Paul tells us what forgiveness is for. It is not a licence to go on sinning: “Should we sin because we are not under law but under grace? By no means!” The forgiven are set free, not to belong to nobody, but to belong to God. “For just as you once presented your members as slaves to impurity and to greater and greater iniquity, so now present your members as slaves to righteousness for sanctification.” The man in the Gospel rose and carried the bed that had carried him. So it is with us: the members of our body that once served sin, the hands, the eyes, the tongue, are now to serve holiness. And the end of this road is not wages but a gift: “the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

Full homily

Dear friends, today some people carry a paralysed man to Jesus. He cannot come by himself. And the Gospel says: when Jesus saw their faith, he said, take heart, son; your sins are forgiven (Matthew 9:2). Their faith carried him.

The friends wanted healing for his legs, but Jesus heals his heart first. The deepest paralysis is sin, which keeps us from God. Then, to show that he has authority to forgive sins, Jesus tells the man: stand up, take your bed and go home (Matthew 9:6). And he does.

The crowd glorified God, who had given such authority to human beings (Matthew 9:8). That authority continues in the Church. When the priest says the words of absolution, it is Christ who says to us: your sins are forgiven.

Saint Paul tells us what the forgiven are to do: present your members as slaves to righteousness for sanctification (Romans 6:19). Let us go to confession this Lent, and let us carry someone else to Christ. Amen.

For children

Children, some friends carried a sick man to Jesus because they believed Jesus could help. Jesus forgave him and made him walk. Who can you help to come closer to Jesus this week?

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you saw the faith of those who carried the paralysed man and you forgave his sins. Give us friends who carry us to you, and make us friends who carry others. Thank you for giving to human beings the authority to forgive in your name. Raise us from every paralysis of sin, and let us present our members to you for holiness. Glory to you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, for ever. Amen.
