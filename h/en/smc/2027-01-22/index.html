Third Friday of Denha · Commemoration of the Evangelists

Readings

  • Second Reading Acts 5:12–16 · Signs and wonders through the apostles
  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 4:9–16 · In Christ Jesus I became your father through the gospel
  • Gospel Matthew 10:1–15 · Jesus sends out the Twelve

Word for the day

“For though you might have ten thousand guardians in Christ, you do not have many fathers. Indeed, in Christ Jesus I became your father through the gospel.” 1 Corinthians 4:15

Reflection

On the third Friday of Denha the Church honours the four Evangelists, Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, through whom the good news has reached every land. The readings show what the Gospel is: God making himself known. On Mount Carmel Elijah repairs the broken altar with twelve stones, “according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Jacob”, soaks the offering with water and prays: “Answer me, O LORD, answer me, so that this people may know that you, O LORD, are God, and that you have turned their hearts back.” Fire falls, and the whole people cries: “The LORD indeed is God; the LORD indeed is God.” This is Denha: the true God revealed. In Acts the same God works “many signs and wonders” through the apostles, and the sick are laid in the streets so “that Peter’s shadow might fall on some of them”.

In the Gospel Jesus calls the Twelve by name; among them are “James son of Zebedee, and his brother John” and “Thomas and Matthew the tax collector”. He sends them out: “As you go, proclaim the good news, ‘The kingdom of heaven has come near.’” And he adds: “You received without payment; give without payment.” The Evangelists did in writing what the Twelve did by voice: Matthew and John from among the Twelve, Mark and Luke from the circle of Peter and Paul. Paul tells the Corinthians what it cost: “We are fools for the sake of Christ”, hungry, homeless, reviled; yet “in Christ Jesus I became your father through the gospel.” Through their four books the Evangelists are fathers in faith to us too. In Kerala the faith first preached by the Apostle Thomas has been fed for centuries by these Gospels, read at every Holy Qurbana.

Full homily

Dear friends, on the third Friday of Denha we honour the four Evangelists. The first reading takes us to Mount Carmel. Elijah rebuilds the broken altar of the LORD with twelve stones, pours water on the offering three times, and prays: answer me, O LORD, so that this people may know that you are God. The fire falls, and the people cry: the LORD indeed is God (1 Kings 18:39).

Denha means manifestation: God showing himself. In Acts he shows himself again: many signs and wonders are done through the apostles, and people lay the sick in the streets hoping Peter's shadow will fall on them (Acts 5:12–15).

In the Gospel Jesus calls the Twelve by name and sends them: as you go, proclaim the good news, the kingdom of heaven has come near. You received without payment; give without payment (Matthew 10:7–8). Matthew and John were among them. Mark and Luke learned from Peter and Paul. What the apostles preached, the Evangelists wrote, so that the good news could reach us.

It was not easy. Paul says: we are fools for the sake of Christ, hungry, poorly clothed, homeless. But he also says: in Christ Jesus I became your father through the gospel (1 Corinthians 4:15). Each time we open a Gospel, these fathers in faith speak to us. Let us read one Gospel this month, and pass on freely what we have freely received. Amen.

For children

Children, Matthew, Mark, Luke and John wrote down the story of Jesus for us! Jesus said: you received for free, so give for free. Share something with a friend today, and read a little of the Gospel every day.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you sent the Twelve to proclaim that the kingdom of heaven has come near, and through the four Evangelists your Gospel has reached us. Let the fire of your Spirit fall on our hearts, so that we may say with all your people: the Lord indeed is God. Make us glad to give freely what we have freely received. Amen.
