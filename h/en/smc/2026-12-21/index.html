Word for the day

“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” John 1:1

Reflection

Four days before Christmas the Church reads the opening of John's Gospel. Before Bethlehem there is eternity: “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… All things came into being through him… The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it.” The child in the manger is the eternal Son, Creator of all.

Paul asks the Ephesians to “pray in the Spirit at all times” and to pray for him, “that when I speak, a message may be given to me to make known with boldness the mystery of the gospel.” In these last days of waiting, prayer prepares the heart to receive the Word made flesh.

Full homily

Dear friends, with four days to Christmas, St Paul asks us to pray: pray in the Spirit at all times in every prayer and supplication; keep alert and always persevere in supplication for all the saints. Pray also for me, so that I may make known with boldness the mystery of the gospel (Ephesians 6:18–19).

Paul wrote this from prison, in chains. Yet he does not ask for release; he asks for boldness to speak. Prayer gave him courage.

In the Gospel we hear the opening of St John: in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. All things came into being through him. In him was life, and the life was the light of all people. The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it (John 1:1–5).

This is the deepest meaning of Christmas. The baby in the manger is not merely a good person who later became holy. He is the eternal Word, through whom all things were made. The One who made the stars lies under the star of Bethlehem.

And the darkness did not overcome it. Whatever darkness you face this Christmas, sickness, loneliness, debt, grief, sin, the light of Christ is stronger. It may seem small, like one candle, but darkness cannot put it out.

These last days before Christmas, let us pray more: at home, in the church, before the crib. Let us pray for missionaries, for persecuted Christians, for our priests, that they may proclaim the Gospel with boldness. And let us light a candle in our home each evening, saying: the light shines in the darkness. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus is the light of the world. When you light the star or lamp this Christmas, remember that Jesus is the true Light!

Prayer

Eternal Word of the Father, light of every person, shine in my darkness and prepare my heart to welcome you at Christmas. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

With four days to go before the feast of the Lord's birth, the readings set two things side by side: a prisoner asking his friends to pray for him, and one of the highest statements in all of Scripture about who is coming. The Word who was with God from the beginning is the one the Church now waits to welcome in a manger. And so she prays, at all times.

Ephesians 6:18–20 · Pray at all times

Setting.

These verses come straight after the armour of God (6:10–17): the belt of truth, the shield of faith, the sword of the Spirit. Grammatically v. 18 still belongs to that sentence. Prayer is not one more piece of the armour. It is how the soldier stays awake inside it.

Structure.

  • v. 18: pray always, in the Spirit, for all the saints.
  • vv. 19–20: pray also for me, that I may speak boldly.

Key words.

  • agrypnountes, keep alert: Literally, staying awake, losing sleep. It is the word for a watchman on duty through the night. The word "all" sounds four times in the Greek of v. 18: every prayer, all times, all perseverance, all the saints.
  • parrēsia, boldness: The right of a citizen to speak openly in the assembly. It appears in v. 19, and the related verb in v. 20. Even Paul asks for this; it is not something he assumes he has.
  • presbeuō en halysei, I am an ambassador in chains: An ambassador was protected and honoured, so this is a striking picture. His captors have put the envoy of the King of kings in chains, and he goes on with his embassy.

Verse by verse.

“Pray in the Spirit at all times in every prayer and supplication.” (6:18)

Praying in the Spirit means letting the Spirit pray in us (see Romans 8:26). Paul asks for a whole life held in prayer, not a life without other work.

“Pray also for me, so that when I speak, a message may be given to me to make known with boldness the mystery of the gospel,” (6:19)

The great Apostle does not ask for release from prison. He asks for the right word at the right moment. Those who preach and teach need this prayer from their people.

“Pray that I may declare it boldly, as I must speak.” (6:20)

"As I must": the Gospel is a duty laid on him. Compare 1 Corinthians 9:16.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism quotes this verse when it teaches that we must pray constantly and that this is possible, because prayer and Christian life are one (CCC 2742, 2745). It describes prayer for others as a sharing in the prayer of Jesus, and it recalls that the first Christian communities lived this form of prayer intensely (CCC 2634–2636).

John 1:1–5 · In the beginning was the Word

Setting.

The Gospel of John opens with its famous Prologue (1:1–18), which many scholars think has the form of a hymn. Matthew begins Jesus' story with Abraham, and Luke with Zechariah in the Temple. John goes back before all of them, to the first words of the Bible: "In the beginning" (Genesis 1:1).

Structure.

  • vv. 1–2: the Word with God, and the Word as God.
  • v. 3: all things through him.
  • vv. 4–5: life and light; the darkness does not overcome it.

Key words.

  • logos, Word: Greek readers would hear in it reason, the order of the world. Jewish readers would hear the word by which God created (Genesis 1; Psalm 33:6) and the Wisdom who was with him (Proverbs 8:22–31). John gathers all this up and then says what no one had yet said: this Word became flesh (1:14).
  • pros ton theon / theos ēn ho logos, with God / the Word was God: The two phrases must be kept together. "With God" means that the Word is distinct from the Father. "Was God" means that he is truly divine. Without the article, theos here says what the Word is, and it does not lessen his divinity. Here we meet the seed of the Church's faith in the Trinity.
  • katelaben, overcome: The Greek verb can mean both to overcome and to grasp, to understand. Both meanings fit the Gospel: the darkness has not put out the light, and it has not understood it either.

Verse by verse.

“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.” (1:1)

The verb "was" in Greek describes a state that continues. At the beginning, the Word already was. He does not begin to exist; he is there when everything else begins.

“All things came into being through him, and without him not one thing came into being.” (1:3)

Nothing lies outside his reach, which is why nothing created is evil in itself. A note on the text: the earliest manuscripts have no punctuation. NRSV takes the end of v. 3 with v. 4, as many early writers did, while the Malayalam POC ends the sentence at v. 3.

“What has come into being in him was life, and the life was the light of all people.” (1:3–4)

This light is for "all people", and not for one people alone.

“The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it.” (1:5)

"Shines" is in the present tense. The verse is not only about the past. On the darkest day of our own lives, it is still true.

How the Church reads it.

The Council of Nicaea (325) confessed, against Arius, that the Son is true God, of one being with the Father, and that all things were made through him (CCC 242, 465). The Catechism teaches that God created everything by his eternal Word (CCC 291). St Augustine says in his Confessions that he found something like "In the beginning was the Word" in the books of the Platonists, but that he did not find there that the Word became flesh (in substance; Confessions 7.9). That is exactly what the Church is about to celebrate.

Questions people ask

If the Word is God and is also with God, are there two Gods?

No. The Church confesses one God in three Persons. John holds together that the Word is distinct from the Father and that he is truly God. The Father and the Son are one in being (John 10:30), and they are distinct as Persons (CCC 253–254).

For study and teaching

  • Paul asks for prayer, not for release. Who in my parish, perhaps my priest or my catechist, needs me to pray that they may speak boldly?
  • The light still shines in the darkness (John 1:5). Which darkness in my family or my town do I most want the Lord's birth to reach?
