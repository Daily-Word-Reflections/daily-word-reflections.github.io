Word for the day

“And he said to them, “Go into all the world and proclaim the good news to the whole creation.”” Mark 16:15

Reflection

St Francis Xavier (1506–1552), Jesuit companion of St Ignatius, arrived in Goa in 1542 and spent years among the fishing communities of the southern coast of India, including Travancore, teaching the catechism and baptising thousands. He went on to Malacca and Japan and died on the island of Sancian, within sight of China. His body rests in Goa. The Church honours him as patron of the missions.

The readings for his feast speak of mission: God's own people, holy to the Lord (Deuteronomy); the call to “all the ends of the earth” to turn and be saved (Isaiah); the apostle who is “poor, yet making many rich” (2 Corinthians); and the Twelve sent two by two with nothing but a staff (Mark). In this season of announcements, Xavier reminds us that the Good News must be announced to everyone.

Full homily

Dear friends, today we celebrate St Francis Xavier, who preached along the southern coasts of India, including Travancore, more than four hundred and eighty years ago. Isaiah calls: turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth (Isaiah 45:22). Francis brought that call to our shores.

St Paul describes the life of an apostle: in hardships, sleepless nights and hunger; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, yet possessing everything (2 Corinthians 6:4–10). Francis lived exactly so: tired, poor, yet rich in God.

In the Gospel Jesus sends out the Twelve with nothing for the journey except a staff: no bread, no bag, no money (Mark 6:8). They must trust God and the hospitality of the people. And they preached repentance, cast out demons and healed the sick.

We are heirs of missionaries. Let us thank God for those who brought and kept the faith in India, pray for missionaries today, and share the Gospel in our own surroundings, simply and with joy. Amen.
