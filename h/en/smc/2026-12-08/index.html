Word for the day

“For the Mighty One has done great things for me, and holy is his name. His mercy is for those who fear him from generation to generation.” Luke 1:49–50

Reflection

On 8 December 1854 Pope Pius IX defined as a dogma revealed by God that “the most Blessed Virgin Mary, in the first instant of her conception, by a singular grace and privilege of almighty God, in view of the merits of Jesus Christ, the Saviour of the human race, was preserved free from all stain of original sin” (Ineffabilis Deus). Mary was redeemed too, but in a more sublime way: preserved rather than cleansed. The Eastern tradition has long honoured her as “all-holy” (Panagia) and “all-pure”.

The readings prepare a dwelling for the Word. Wisdom says: “The Creator of all things… chose the place for my tent. He said, ‘Make your dwelling in Jacob.’” Isaiah promises the sign of Immanuel, born of the young woman. Hebrews proclaims that “in these last days God has spoken to us by a Son,” who became like us in every respect. A pure dwelling was prepared for the Son of God: the Immaculate Virgin.

At the very beginning, after the Fall, God promised enmity between the serpent and the woman (Genesis 3:15). The Church sees in Mary the woman in whom the serpent never had a share. And in the Magnificat Mary herself gives the reason for her greatness: not her merits, but God's mercy. “He has looked with favour on the lowliness of his servant… the Mighty One has done great things for me.”

For us the feast is a feast of hope: what God did in Mary at the beginning, he wants to accomplish in us at the end, presenting us “holy and blameless before him in love” (Ephesians 1:4).

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, today we celebrate the Immaculate Conception of the Blessed Virgin Mary. Many people confuse this feast with the virginal conception of Jesus. Today's feast is about Mary herself: from the first moment of her life in her mother's womb, God preserved her from original sin.

Why? Because God was preparing a home for his Son. The book of Sirach describes Wisdom looking for a place to pitch its tent. Isaiah promised a sign: the young woman will bear a son, Immanuel. When the Son of God came, he needed a mother, and God prepared her with a singular grace.

The Church teaches that Mary was preserved from sin in view of the merits of Jesus Christ. So Mary too was saved by her Son, but in a wonderful way: not lifted out of the mud, but kept from falling into it. That is why the angel called her “full of grace”.

And Mary herself tells us the secret of her greatness in the Magnificat: “He has looked with favour on the lowliness of his servant… the Mighty One has done great things for me.” Not pride, but gratitude. Everything is God's mercy.

What God did in Mary at the beginning, he wants to do in us at the end: to make us holy and blameless before him in love. So in this season, let us prepare a clean heart for Jesus by a good confession. Holy Mary, conceived without sin, pray for us who have recourse to you. Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, the early Christians did not have the dogma of the Immaculate Conception in its later words, but they had a deep intuition about Mary, drawn from the Scriptures. Let us look at two images that the Fathers loved: Mary as the new Eve, and Mary as the Ark of the Covenant.

In the garden, Eve listened to the serpent and believed his lie. Through her disobedience, sin and death entered the world. But God promised that the offspring of a woman would crush the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15). In Nazareth another woman listened, not to a serpent but to an angel, and said: here am I, the servant of the Lord; let it be with me according to your word (Luke 1:38). St Irenaeus wrote that the knot of Eve's disobedience was untied by Mary's obedience. Where Eve said no, Mary said yes.

The second image is the Ark of the Covenant. God commanded Moses to make it of acacia wood, covered with pure gold inside and out (Exodus 25:10–11). Inside were the tablets of the law, a jar of manna and Aaron's rod (Hebrews 9:4). When the Ark entered a house, it brought blessing; David danced before it. Mary carried in her womb the Word of God, the true Bread from heaven and the eternal High Priest. When she entered Elizabeth's house, the child leaped for joy (Luke 1:41). If the Ark had to be pure gold, how much more the living Ark of the Son of God.

This is what the Church proclaims today: that from the first moment of her conception Mary was preserved from every stain of original sin, by a singular grace of God and in view of the merits of Jesus Christ. She too was saved by her Son, but in a more exalted way: preserved rather than cleansed.

And what God did perfectly for Mary, He wants to begin in us. In Baptism we were washed; in confession we are cleansed again; in the Holy Qurbana we receive Christ as Mary did. This Advent, let us prepare a clean dwelling for Him. Make a good confession before Christmas, and ask Mary Immaculate to teach you to say yes.

Dear brothers and sisters, many Catholics think that today's feast is about the conception of Jesus in Mary's womb. It is not. That we celebrate on 25 March, the Annunciation. Today we celebrate the conception of Mary herself in the womb of her mother, whom tradition names Anne.

On 8 December 1854, Pope Pius IX solemnly defined that the most Blessed Virgin Mary, from the first moment of her conception, by a singular grace and privilege of almighty God, and in view of the merits of Jesus Christ the Saviour of the human race, was preserved free from all stain of original sin (CCC 491). Every word matters. Singular grace: it was God's gift, not Mary's achievement. In view of the merits of Jesus Christ: Mary too was saved by her Son.

Where is this in the Bible? The angel greets Mary as full of grace (Luke 1:28). The Greek word means one who has been, and remains, filled with God's favour. The Church understood that the woman chosen to be the Mother of God was prepared by grace from the beginning. The Catechism says that Mary was redeemed from the moment of her conception, and that by God's grace she remained free of every personal sin her whole life long (CCC 492–493). The Eastern Fathers call her Panagia, the All-Holy.

Four years after the definition, in 1858, Our Lady appeared to a poor girl, Bernadette, at Lourdes. When Bernadette asked her name, she answered: I am the Immaculate Conception. Bernadette did not understand the words, but the parish priest did. Heaven confirmed what the Church had taught.

Why does this matter? Because it shows what God's grace can do. Mary is not a goddess; she is a creature, entirely filled with grace. She shows what the Church is called to become: holy and without blemish (Ephesians 5:27). So let us honour her this Advent with the Rosary, and ask her to help us prepare for her Son at this Holy Qurbana and at Christmas.
