Word for the day

“The grass withers, the flower fades; but the word of our God will stand forever.” Isaiah 40:8

Reflection

On the Fridays of Denha the Church honours the great witnesses who made Christ known. The first is John the Baptist, who saw the heavens open over Jesus at the Jordan. Isaiah had foretold his mission: a voice crying in the wilderness to prepare the way of the Lord, so that “the glory of the LORD shall be revealed, and all people shall see it together.” In the synagogue of Antioch in Pisidia St Paul tells the whole story of Israel, from Egypt to David, and then names the forerunner: “before his coming John had already proclaimed a baptism of repentance to all the people of Israel.” And John himself said: “I am not he. No, but one is coming after me; I am not worthy to untie the thong of the sandals on his feet.”

John paid for his witness with his life. He told Herod plainly: “It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife.” Herod feared him, “knowing that he was a righteous and holy man”, and even liked to listen to him; but at a birthday banquet, out of regard for his oaths and his guests, he sent a soldier to behead him in prison. Herod's power withered like grass; John's witness still speaks. “The grass withers, the flower fades; but the word of our God will stand forever.” St Paul, himself “a prisoner for Christ Jesus”, shows what such witness builds: a household in which strangers become “citizens with the saints and also members of the household of God, built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone.” John, the last of the prophets, belongs to that foundation. The question he leaves us is simple: will I speak the truth with love, whatever it costs?

Full homily

Dear friends, on the Fridays of Denha the Church remembers the witnesses of Christ. Today we honour John the Baptist, who saw the heavens open at the Jordan. Long before, Isaiah had heard a voice crying: in the wilderness prepare the way of the Lord (Isaiah 40:3). John was that voice.

In Antioch St Paul tells the story of Israel, from Egypt to King David, and then says: before the coming of Jesus, John proclaimed a baptism of repentance to all the people. And John said: I am not he; one is coming after me, and I am not worthy to untie his sandals (Acts 13:24–25). The greatness of John is that he points away from himself.

John told Herod: it is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife (Mark 6:18). Herod knew that John was a righteous and holy man, and even liked to listen to him. But at his birthday banquet, to keep a rash oath and to save face before his guests, he had John beheaded in prison. Truth spoken with love can be costly.

Where is Herod's power now? The grass withers, the flower fades; but the word of our God will stand forever (Isaiah 40:8). St Paul, writing as a prisoner for Christ, tells us that we are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone (Ephesians 2:20). John's witness, sealed in blood, is part of that foundation.

Let us ask for John's humility, to point always to Christ, and for his courage, to speak the truth with love. And let us pray for Christians who today are imprisoned or killed for their faith. Amen.

For children

Children, John the Baptist was very brave. He told the king the truth even when it was dangerous. Let us always tell the truth, kindly and bravely.

Prayer

O God, you sent John the Baptist to prepare the way of your Son and to bear witness to the truth even to death. Give us his humility, to point always to Christ, and his courage, to speak the truth with love; and build us with all your saints into a dwelling place for you. Through Christ our Lord. Amen.
