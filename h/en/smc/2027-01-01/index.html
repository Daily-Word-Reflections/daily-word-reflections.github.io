The Holy Name of Jesus (Circumcision of our Lord) · New Year's Day

1 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading Genesis 17:1–14 · God changes Abram's name and gives the covenant of circumcision
  • Second Reading Isaiah 43:1–7 · I have called you by name, you are mine
  • Epistle Philippians 2:5–11 · The name that is above every name
  • Gospel Matthew 1:18–25; Luke 2:21 · He was named Jesus

Word for the day

“She will bear a son, and you are to name him Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins.” Matthew 1:21

Reflection

On the eighth day after his birth the Child was circumcised and “he was called Jesus, the name given by the angel before he was conceived in the womb.” The Syro-Malabar Church begins the civil year with this feast of the Holy Name. In the first reading God changes Abram's name to Abraham and gives him the covenant sign; a new name means a new mission. Through Isaiah God says to his people: “I have called you by name, you are mine.” Names in the Bible are not labels; they reveal who someone is before God.

The name Jesus, Yeshua, means “the LORD saves”. The angel explains it to Joseph: “he will save his people from their sins.” And St Paul sings the hymn of Philippians: the Son emptied himself, took the form of a slave, became obedient to death on a cross, “Therefore God also highly exalted him and gave him the name that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend.” The way to the exalted Name passed through humility and the Cross.

Today is also the last Friday of the Nativity season, kept as the Divine Motherhood of Mary. The woman who gave the Child his human flesh is the Mother of God; she stands with the Apostles in prayer at the beginning of the Church (Acts 1:14). As the new year opens, the Church places on our lips the Name in which there is salvation, and under our eyes the Mother who keeps us close to her Son.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, a blessed new year to you all. On this first day of the year the Church does not begin with our plans but with a Name: the Holy Name of Jesus. Eight days after his birth, the Child was circumcised and named Jesus, the name given by the angel before he was conceived (Luke 2:21).

In the Bible a name is never only a label. In the first reading God changes Abram's name to Abraham, father of a multitude, and gives him the covenant (Genesis 17:5). And through Isaiah he says to his people: I have called you by name, you are mine (Isaiah 43:1). Each of us was called by name at Baptism. God knows you, not as a number, but by name.

The name Jesus means the Lord saves. The angel says to Joseph: you are to name him Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins (Matthew 1:21). We may want many things from the new year: health, a job, success for our children. Jesus comes first to save us from sin, the root of every other sorrow.

St Paul tells us how this Name was won. Christ Jesus emptied himself, took the form of a slave, humbled himself and became obedient to death on a cross. Therefore God highly exalted him and gave him the name that is above every name (Philippians 2:7–9). The way to glory is the way of humility.

Today is also the last Friday of Nativity, when our Church honours the Divine Motherhood of Mary. She who gave Jesus his human flesh is truly the Mother of God, and she stands with the Apostles in prayer (Acts 1:14). Let us begin this year with her.

So let us make a simple resolution: to call on the Name of Jesus every day. Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner. Let this Name be on our lips in joy and in trouble, and this year will be blessed. Amen.

For children

Children, today is Jesus' name day! The name Jesus means God saves us. Let us say his name with love every day.

Do you know your own name's meaning? Ask your parents tonight, and thank God for calling you by name.

Prayer

Lord Jesus, your Name means “the Lord saves”. At the beginning of this new year we bow before your holy Name, which is above every name. Write your Name on our hearts, save us from our sins, and keep us all through this year under the mantle of your Mother, the Mother of God. To you be glory with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and for ever. Amen.
