Third Tuesday of the Great Fast

23 February 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • Epistle 1 Corinthians 10:1–13 · Whoever thinks he stands, let him not fall
  • Gospel Luke 21:29–36 · Be watchful at all times

Word for the day

“So if you think you are standing, watch out that you do not fall.” 1 Corinthians 10:12

Reflection

Paul looks back at Israel in the desert and sees a mirror of the baptised. “Our ancestors were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, and all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink.” He even says of the water from the rock: “the rock was Christ.” Every privilege was theirs, and yet “God was not pleased with most of them, and they were struck down in the wilderness.” They craved, they turned to idols, they tested God, they grumbled. Paul does not tell the story to frighten the Corinthians but to wake them: “These things happened to them to serve as an example, and they were written down to instruct us, on whom the ends of the ages have come.” We too have passed through the water of baptism and eat the spiritual food of the Holy Qurbana. These gifts do not make us safe if we stop watching. Hence the sober line: “So if you think you are standing, watch out that you do not fall.” But Paul never leaves a warning without a promise: “No testing has overtaken you that is not common to everyone. God is faithful, and he will not let you be tested beyond your strength, but with the testing he will also provide the way out so that you may be able to endure it.” In the third week of the fast, when we too are tempted to grumble in the desert, that promise is worth learning by heart.

In the Gospel Jesus points to the fig tree: when it puts out leaves, everyone knows summer is near. The signs of God's kingdom are just as plain for those who look. “Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away.” Then comes a very practical warning: “Be on guard so that your hearts are not weighed down with dissipation and drunkenness and the worries of this life, and that day does not catch you unexpectedly, like a trap.” Three things make the heart heavy: too much pleasure, too much drink, too much worry. The Church in Kerala has long urged the faithful to keep the Great Fast free of alcohol, and many men who drink keep these fifty days dry; for some it has been the beginning of freedom. Jesus' last word is a programme for the whole fast: “Be alert at all times, praying that you may have the strength to escape all these things that will take place, and to stand before the Son of Man.” The Latin calendar remembers today St Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna and disciple of the apostle John, who after eighty-six years of serving Christ refused to deny him and was put to death. He was able to stand at the end because he had watched and prayed all his life.

Homily outline

Theme: “So if you think you are standing, watch out that you do not fall.”

Israel in the desert as a lesson for us, the faithfulness of God in temptation, and the watchfulness Jesus asks.

1. Written down to instruct us

  • Under the cloud and through the sea.
  • The rock was Christ.
  • Gifts are not a guarantee.
“For he is our God, and we are the people of his pasture, and the sheep of his hand. O that today you would listen to his voice! Do not harden your hearts, as at Meribah, as on the day at Massah in the wilderness,” Psalm 95:7–8
“These things happened to them to serve as an example, and they were written down to instruct us, on whom the ends of the ages have come.” 1 Corinthians 10:11

Apply: Notice when you grumble today, and turn each complaint into a short prayer.

2. God is faithful

  • Testing common to everyone.
  • Not beyond our strength.
  • Always a way out.
“When you pass through the waters, I will be with you; and through the rivers, they shall not overwhelm you; when you walk through fire you shall not be burned, and the flame shall not consume you.” Isaiah 43:2
“No testing has overtaken you that is not common to everyone. God is faithful, and he will not let you be tested beyond your strength, but with the testing he will also provide the way out so that you may be able to endure it.” 1 Corinthians 10:13

Apply: Decide now what your way out will be when your usual temptation comes.

3. Be alert at all times

  • The fig tree in leaf.
  • Hearts weighed down.
  • Praying for strength to stand.
“my soul waits for the LORD more than those who watch for the morning, more than those who watch for the morning.” Psalm 130:6
“Be alert at all times, praying that you may have the strength to escape all these things that will take place, and to stand before the Son of Man.”” Luke 21:36

Apply: Keep the fifty days free of alcohol, or of whatever most weighs your heart down.

The baptised are not safe because they once crossed the water, but because they keep watching.

God is faithful: watch, pray, and take the way out he provides.

Take-home resolution: Name your heaviest burden of pleasure, drink or worry, and give it up for the rest of the Great Fast.

Full homily

Dear friends, Paul reminds us of Israel in the desert. They passed through the sea, they ate bread from heaven and drank from the rock, and the rock was Christ (1 Corinthians 10:4). Yet many of them fell, because they grumbled and turned away.

We too have been baptised and fed at the altar. These gifts are not a guarantee if we stop watching. So if you think you are standing, watch out that you do not fall (1 Corinthians 10:12).

But Paul gives us comfort: God is faithful, and he will not let you be tested beyond your strength, and with the testing he will provide the way out (1 Corinthians 10:13). There is always a way out, if we look for it.

Jesus warns us not to let our hearts be weighed down with too much pleasure, drink and worry. Be alert at all times, praying (Luke 21:36). Like St Polycarp, who served Christ for eighty-six years, may we watch and pray, and stand firm to the end. Amen.

For children

Children, Jesus asks us to stay awake inside, like a watchman at night. When something wrong tempts you, remember: God always gives you a way out. Ask him for it!

Prayer

Lord Jesus Christ, you are the rock that followed your people in the desert, and you feed us with spiritual food. Keep us from grumbling and from false security; do not let our hearts grow heavy with pleasure, drink or worry. When we are tested, show us the way out, and through the prayers of St Polycarp give us strength to stand before you at the end. Amen.
