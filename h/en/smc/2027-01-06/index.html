Denha · The Epiphany and Baptism of our Lord

6 January 2027 · Syro-Malabar Rite

Open in the Daily Word app

Readings

  • First Reading Exodus 19:16–25 · The Lord descends upon Mount Sinai
  • Second Reading Joel 2:28–32 · I will pour out my spirit on all flesh
  • Epistle 2 Timothy 1:6–10 · Grace revealed through the appearing of our Saviour
  • Gospel Mark 1:1–11 · You are my Son, the Beloved

Word for the day

“And a voice came from heaven, “You are my Son, the Beloved; with you I am well pleased.”” Mark 1:11

Reflection

Denha is a Syriac word meaning dawn, rising, manifestation. The Eastern Churches celebrate on this day not first the Magi but the Baptism of Jesus in the Jordan, because there the whole mystery of God shone out: the Son in the water, the Spirit descending like a dove, the Father's voice from heaven. “You are my Son, the Beloved; with you I am well pleased.” At the Jordan the Holy Trinity is revealed, and the public ministry of Jesus begins. The Sun of Justice rises over the world.

The readings show God coming down. At Sinai “the LORD had descended upon it in fire”, with thunder, smoke and trembling, and the people had to keep their distance. At the Jordan the heavens are “torn apart”, and God comes close in gentleness: the Spirit descends like a dove upon a man standing in the water among sinners. Joel's promise begins to be fulfilled: “I will pour out my spirit on all flesh.” What was terrifying at Sinai becomes intimate at the Jordan.

Jesus did not need baptism; he was without sin. He went down into the water to sanctify the waters for us and to stand with sinners. St Paul tells Timothy to “rekindle the gift of God that is within you”, for grace “has now been revealed through the appearing of our Savior Christ Jesus, who abolished death and brought life and immortality to light.” Our ancestors' customs, the lamps on plantain stems with the cry “El Paiya”, God is light, and the night bath recalling the Jordan, were ways of saying: our Baptism made us children of light. Denha calls each of us to remember our Baptism and to live as beloved sons and daughters.

Full homily

Dear brothers and sisters, a blessed feast of Denha to you. Denha means dawn, rising, manifestation. Our Church celebrates today the Baptism of Jesus in the Jordan, because there the whole mystery of God was revealed.

Look at the scene in Mark. Jesus comes from Nazareth and is baptised by John. Just as he is coming up out of the water, he sees the heavens torn apart and the Spirit descending like a dove on him. And a voice comes from heaven: you are my Son, the Beloved; with you I am well pleased (Mark 1:10–11). Son, Spirit and Father: the Holy Trinity is revealed.

The first reading takes us to Sinai. The LORD descended upon it in fire; the mountain shook violently, and the people had to keep away (Exodus 19:18). At the Jordan God comes down again, but now so gently: a dove, a voice of love, a man standing in the river among sinners. What was terrifying at Sinai becomes tender at the Jordan.

Why was Jesus baptised? He had no sin. He went down into the water to stand with us sinners and to sanctify the waters for our Baptism. The prophet Joel had promised: I will pour out my spirit on all flesh (Joel 2:28). That outpouring begins with Jesus and reaches us in Baptism and Confirmation.

Our ancestors kept this feast beautifully. They lit lamps on plantain stems and walked around crying El Paiya, God is light. On the eve they bathed in rivers and ponds, remembering the Jordan. These customs said: we are baptised; we are children of light.

St Paul tells Timothy: rekindle the gift of God that is within you (2 Timothy 1:6). Many of us were baptised as infants and rarely think of it. Today, let us remember: God has said to you, you are my beloved son, my beloved daughter. Let us live as people who have heard that voice. Amen.

For children

Children, today is Denha! Jesus was baptised in the river Jordan. The Holy Spirit came down like a dove, and God said: you are my beloved Son!

When you were baptised, God said the same to you: you are my beloved child. Ask your parents when your Baptism day is!

Prayer

O Christ, Sun of Justice, today you rose upon the world at the Jordan. The Father bore witness to you and the Spirit descended upon you. Sanctify us who were baptised into you; rekindle in us the gift of God; and let us live as beloved children of light. To you, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, be praise and glory, now and always and for ever. Amen.
