Word for the day

“And a voice from heaven said, “This is my Son, the Beloved, with whom I am well pleased.”” Matthew 3:17

Reflection

Denha is a Syriac word meaning dawn, rising, manifestation. The Eastern Churches celebrate on this day not first the Magi but the Baptism of Jesus in the Jordan, because there the whole mystery of God shone out: the Son in the water, the Spirit descending like a dove, the Father's voice from heaven: “This is my Son, the Beloved, with whom I am well pleased.” At the Jordan the Holy Trinity is revealed, and the public ministry of Jesus begins. The Sun of Justice rises over the world.

The readings show a dawn long promised. The seer Balaam, a stranger to Israel, looks out from the hills of Moab and sees far ahead: “I see him, but not now; I behold him, but not near”, and he announces that “a star shall come out of Jacob”. Isaiah sees a shoot from the stump of Jesse and promises: “The spirit of the LORD shall rest on him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might”. At the Jordan both promises meet. The star has risen, and the Spirit comes down and rests on Jesus, as Matthew says: “he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and alighting on him.”

John protests: “I need to be baptized by you, and do you come to me?” Jesus had no sin. Yet he answers that “it is proper for us in this way to fulfill all righteousness”: he goes down into the water to stand with sinners and to sanctify the waters for us. St Paul tells Titus what this means for us: God “saved us, not because of any works of righteousness that we had done, but according to his mercy, through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit. This Spirit he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior”. Our ancestors' customs, the lamps on plantain stems with the cry El Paiya, God is light, and the night bath recalling the Jordan, were ways of saying: our Baptism has made us children of light. Denha calls each of us to remember our Baptism and to live as beloved sons and daughters.

Full homily · Homily of the day

Dear brothers and sisters, a blessed feast of Denha to you. Denha means dawn, rising, manifestation. Our Church celebrates today the Baptism of Jesus in the Jordan, because there the whole mystery of God was revealed.

Look at the scene in Matthew. Jesus comes from Galilee to John at the Jordan to be baptised. John tries to stop him: I need to be baptised by you, and do you come to me? Jesus answers: let it be so now; it is proper for us in this way to fulfil all righteousness (Matthew 3:14–15). The sinless one takes his place in the line of sinners.

And when he comes up from the water, the heavens are opened, the Spirit of God descends like a dove and alights on him, and a voice from heaven says: this is my Son, the Beloved, with whom I am well pleased (Matthew 3:16–17). Son, Spirit and Father: the Holy Trinity is revealed.

Long before, Balaam had seen from afar: a star shall come out of Jacob (Numbers 24:17). Isaiah had promised a shoot from the stump of Jesse on whom the spirit of the Lord would rest, the spirit of wisdom and understanding (Isaiah 11:1–2). At the Jordan the star rises and the Spirit rests. The dawn has come.

Our ancestors kept this feast beautifully. They lit lamps on plantain stems and walked around crying El Paiya, God is light. On the eve they bathed in rivers and ponds, remembering the Jordan. These customs said: we are baptised; we are children of light.

St Paul tells Titus that God saved us, not because of works we had done, but according to his mercy, through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit, poured out on us richly (Titus 3:5–6). Many of us were baptised as infants and rarely think of it. Today, let us remember: God has said to you, you are my beloved son, my beloved daughter. Let us live as people who have heard that voice. Amen.

Dear friends, long ago Isaiah cried out: O that you would tear open the heavens and come down (Isaiah 64:1). Israel longed for God to come close. At the Jordan the prayer is answered: as Jesus came up from the water, he saw the heavens torn apart and the Spirit descending like a dove on him (Mark 1:10).

This is the great manifestation of the Trinity. The Son stands in the water, the Spirit descends, and the Father's voice says: this is my Son, the Beloved, with whom I am well pleased (Matthew 3:17). Every time we make the sign of the cross, we confess what was revealed at the Jordan.

Jesus did not need to be cleansed. The Fathers said: he went into the water not to be made holy by it, but to make the waters holy for us. From that day, water became the doorway into God's life.

Paul says: he saved us through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit (Titus 3:5). What happened at the Jordan happened to us at the font. Heaven opened over us; the Father called us his children.

Today let us thank God for our Baptism, make the sign of the cross with faith, and come to the Holy Qurbana as children of an open heaven. Amen.

Dear brothers and sisters, most of us were baptised as infants. We do not remember the water, the oil or the candle. Today, as we celebrate the Lord's own baptism, the Church invites us to remember what God did to us.

Paul asks: do you not know that all of us who have been baptised into Christ Jesus were baptised into his death? We were buried with him by baptism, so that we too might walk in newness of life (Romans 6:3–4). The font is both a tomb and a womb.

He also says: as many of you as were baptised into Christ have clothed yourselves with Christ (Galatians 3:27). That is why we received a white garment. We are called to wear Christ every day, at home, at work, in our words.

At Baptism a candle was lit for us, and our godparents spoke promises on our behalf: to reject sin and to believe. Now that we are grown, those promises are ours to keep. Once you were darkness, but now in the Lord you are light (Ephesians 5:8).

So let us find the date of our Baptism, thank our godparents, and tonight renew our promises: I reject sin; I believe in God. Then come to the Holy Qurbana as children of the light. Amen.

Exegesis

The readings as one lesson

Denha is the Syriac word for a rising or a dawning. The East Syriac tradition celebrates on this day the Baptism of the Lord, because at the Jordan the mystery of God shines out: the Son stands in the water, the Spirit comes down, and the Father’s voice is heard.

The readings show the dawn from far off to close at hand. A seer from outside Israel sees a star rising from Jacob, “but not now”. Isaiah sees a shoot from a cut-down stump, with the Spirit resting on him. In Matthew that person stands in the Jordan, and the Spirit rests on him as the Father speaks. And Titus tells us where we come into it: “the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit”. The key words are star, Spirit, righteousness and appeared.

Numbers 24:15–25 · A star shall come out of Jacob

Setting.

Israel is camped on the plains of Moab at the end of the desert journey. Balak, the king of Moab, hires Balaam son of Beor, a famous seer from the Euphrates region (22:5), to curse them. Three times Balaam opens his mouth and blesses them instead. Now, unasked, he speaks a fourth oracle about the future.

A plaster inscription found at Deir ‘Alla in Jordan in 1967, from about the eighth century BC, speaks of a seer called Balaam son of Beor. So he was remembered outside the Bible as well.

Structure.

  • vv. 15–16: Balaam introduces himself as one who sees.
  • v. 17: the star and the scepter.
  • vv. 18–19: Edom and Seir.
  • vv. 20–24: brief oracles on Amalek, the Kenites, Asshur and the ships from Kittim. The Hebrew is obscure in places, especially in the last verses.
  • v. 25: Balaam goes home.

Key words.

  • kôkāb, star: In the ancient Near East a star was often an image for a king, and “scepter” in the parallel line confirms this. Compare “the bright morning star” in Revelation 22:16.
  • šēbeṭ, scepter: The staff of a ruler (compare Genesis 49:10). Many scholars think that the oracle first had David in view, who subdued Moab and Edom (2 Samuel 8:2, 13–14). Later readers looked beyond him.
  • šətum hā‘āyin, whose eye is clear: The Hebrew word in v. 15 is rare and its meaning uncertain. It could mean “opened” or “closed”. The NRSV reads “clear”.

Verse by verse.

“I see him, but not now; I behold him, but not near” (24:17)

The seer knows that what he sees is far off. Those who come to the Jordan and see Jesus are living in the “now” that he could not reach.

“a star shall come out of Jacob, and a scepter shall rise out of Israel;” (24:17)

The Greek Old Testament says that a star “shall rise”, and this is the language of dawn. Matthew’s Magi say, “we observed his star at its rising” (Matthew 2:2). Zechariah speaks of “the dawn from on high” (Luke 1:78). Jewish readers around the time of Jesus also read this verse of a coming leader, as the Dead Sea Scrolls show.

“Then Balaam got up and went back to his place” (24:25)

He goes home. The seer who saw so far does not walk in that light; later Scripture remembers him unfavourably (Numbers 31:16; 2 Peter 2:15). Seeing truly is one thing, and following is another.

How the Church reads it.

St Justin Martyr (Dialogue with Trypho 106) and Origen (Against Celsus 1) link this oracle with the star of the Magi (in substance). The Catechism says that Epiphany celebrates the manifestation of Jesus as Messiah of Israel, Son of God and Saviour of the world, and that the Magi represent the nations who come to him (CCC 528). The East Syriac feast of Denha centres on the Jordan, but the theme is the same: the light that had been promised is seen.

Isaiah 11:1–5 · The spirit of the Lord shall rest on him

Setting.

Isaiah preaches in Jerusalem in the eighth century BC, in the shadow of Assyria. Just before this reading (10:33–34) the Lord cuts down a great forest, a picture of proud powers brought low. The royal house of David itself will look like a stump. But from that stump something grows.

Structure.

  • v. 1: the shoot from Jesse’s stump.
  • v. 2: the Spirit and his gifts.
  • vv. 3–4: how this king judges: not by appearance, but with justice for the poor.
  • v. 5: righteousness and faithfulness are his clothing.

Key words.

  • geza‘ yišay, stump of Jesse: The prophet says Jesse, not David. He goes back behind the royal glory to the shepherd’s father in Bethlehem. God starts again from the beginning.
  • nēṣer, branch: Some scholars think that this word lies behind Matthew’s saying that Jesus would be called a Nazorean (Matthew 2:23), though this cannot be proved.
  • rûaḥ YHWH, spirit of the Lord: In Israel’s history the Spirit came on judges and kings for a moment. Here he “shall rest” on this one, and remain.

Verse by verse.

“A shoot shall come out from the stump of Jesse, and a branch shall grow out of his roots.” (11:1)

New life comes from what looks dead. This is the way God works in Scripture, and the feast of Denha rests upon it.

“The spirit of the Lord shall rest on him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and the fear of the Lord.” (11:2)

The Hebrew has six gifts in three pairs. The Greek and Latin versions render “fear of the Lord” in two ways, adding “piety”, and so the Church’s list of seven gifts of the Holy Spirit took shape (CCC 1831).

“He shall not judge by what his eyes see, or decide by what his ears hear;” (11:3)

Compare “the Lord looks on the heart” (1 Samuel 16:7). A king who judges by appearances will favour the rich and the well-connected.

“but with righteousness he shall judge the poor, and decide with equity for the meek of the earth;” (11:4)

The test of the Spirit’s presence in a ruler is what happens to the poor.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism teaches that the prophets announced that the Spirit of the Lord would rest on the hoped-for Messiah, and that the descent of the Spirit on Jesus at his baptism was the sign that he was the one to come (CCC 1286; see also CCC 712). The gifts “belong in their fullness to Christ, Son of David” (CCC 1831), and they are given to us who are joined to him.

Titus 3:1–7 · The water of rebirth

Setting.

The letter instructs Titus on how the Christians of Crete are to live among their neighbours. The Church has received it among Paul’s letters, while many scholars think that a disciple wrote the Pastoral Letters in his name and others defend Paul’s own authorship. In the middle of practical advice comes one of the most compact summaries of salvation in the New Testament (3:4–7). The verses that follow it were read on the eve of this feast (3:8–11).

Structure.

  • vv. 1–2: how to behave in society: respect, gentleness, courtesy to all.
  • v. 3: what we once were.
  • vv. 4–7: what God has done: he appeared, he saved, he poured out the Spirit, and we have become heirs.
  • ¶ The structure is a “before” and an “after”. The hinge is the word “But” in v. 4.

Key words.

  • epephanē, appeared: From the same root as epiphaneia, “manifestation”, which gives this feast its Greek name. For Titus the manifestation is of God’s kindness itself.
  • philanthrōpia, loving kindness: Literally “love of human beings”. The Greeks praised it in kings. Titus says that it has appeared in God our Saviour.
  • loutron palingenesias, water of rebirth: Loutron means a washing or bath. Palingenesia, new birth, is used in the New Testament only here and in Matthew 19:28. This is Baptism.

Verse by verse.

“But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared,” (3:4)
“he saved us, not because of any works of righteousness that we had done, but according to his mercy, through the water of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.” (3:5)

Grace comes first, and it comes through a real sacramental act. Water is not a mere symbol here; God saves through it.

“This Spirit he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior,” (3:6)

Notice the three: God our Saviour, the Holy Spirit, and Jesus Christ. The Jordan’s revelation of the Trinity becomes the believer’s own experience.

“so that, having been justified by his grace, we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life.” (3:7)

What the Father says of the Son at the Jordan now reaches us: those who are baptized into him are heirs with him (Romans 8:17).

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism names Baptism with these very words, “the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit” (CCC 1215). Through it the baptized become a new creature and an adopted child of God (CCC 1265). Paul’s teaching on justification (Romans 3:24) is echoed here: it comes from God’s grace and mercy (CCC 1987, 1996).

Matthew 3:13–17 · This is my Son, the Beloved

Setting.

John the Baptist has been preaching repentance at the Jordan and has announced one more powerful who will baptize “with the Holy Spirit and fire” (3:11). Now, as a grown man, Jesus appears in Matthew’s Gospel for the first time since his childhood. John’s baptism was a sign of repentance. This raises a question which only Matthew records, and John himself asks it.

Structure.

  • v. 13: Jesus comes to John from Galilee.
  • v. 14: John tries to prevent him.
  • v. 15: Jesus’ first words in Matthew’s Gospel.
  • vv. 16–17: the heavens opened, the Spirit, the voice.

Key words.

  • dikaiosynē, righteousness: A key word in Matthew (5:6; 6:33): doing God’s saving will. “All righteousness” means all that the Father’s plan asks.
  • peristera, dove: Luke says “in bodily form” (3:22). The dove may recall the Spirit over the waters of creation (Genesis 1:2) or the dove after the flood (Genesis 8:8–12). The Catechism sees here a prelude of the new creation (CCC 1224).
  • ho agapētos, the Beloved: The voice combines Psalm 2:7, of the royal son, and Isaiah 42:1, of the servant in whom God delights. Many also hear Genesis 22:2, the beloved son of Abraham. King and Servant meet in one person.

Verse by verse.

“I need to be baptized by you, and do you come to me?” (3:14)

John sees that the order seems to be wrong.

“Let it be so now; for it is proper for us in this way to fulfill all righteousness.” (3:15)

Jesus has no sin to confess. He comes to stand where sinners stand, in solidarity with them. The word “us” includes John: each has a part in the Father’s plan.

“suddenly the heavens were opened to him and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and alighting on him.” (3:16)

Isaiah had cried, “O that you would tear open the heavens and come down” (Isaiah 64:1). What Isaiah 11 promised is happening: the Spirit rests on him.

“This is my Son, the Beloved, with whom I am well pleased.” (3:17)

In Mark and Luke the voice says “You are”. In Matthew it says “This is”, as an announcement to all. This is the manifestation that gives Denha its name.

How the Church reads it.

The Catechism says that Jesus’ baptism is his acceptance and inauguration of his mission as God’s suffering Servant, that he lets himself be numbered among sinners, and that the Spirit comes to remain on him (CCC 536). It sees here the manifestation of his self-emptying, and a prelude of the new creation (CCC 1224). St Ignatius of Antioch says that he was baptized so that by his passion he might purify the water (To the Ephesians 18, in substance). St Gregory Nazianzen says in his Epiphany sermons: Christ is baptized; let us go down with him so as to rise with him (in substance). Mar Ephrem sings in a hymn of faith of fire and Spirit in the womb that bore him and in the river where he was baptized (in substance). In Baptism, the Christian is sacramentally joined to Jesus (CCC 537).

Questions people ask

Why was the sinless Jesus baptized?

Not for his own sins. He took his place among sinners, began his mission as the Servant, and by entering the water made it holy for us (CCC 536, 1224). The Catechism sees this as a first form of the self-giving that will be fulfilled on the cross.

Is Denha the same feast as the Latin Epiphany of the Magi?

It falls on the same date and comes from the same ancient feast of God’s manifestation. The Catechism says that Epiphany celebrates the Magi, the Baptism and the wedding at Cana together (CCC 528). The Latin tradition places the Magi first, and the Syriac and other Eastern traditions place the Baptism first. These are two windows on one light.

Why would God speak through a seer like Balaam?

Because God is not limited by who is speaking. Balaam was hired to curse, and he was made to bless. Scripture does not hide his later failure. God’s word can be true on the lips of one who does not live by it, and this is a warning to all who speak in God’s name.

For the Syro-Malabar reader

The Syro-Malabar Church keeps Denha as one of her great feasts, and its season runs on from here, showing week by week who Jesus is. In Kerala the feast was kept with lights and water. In pindikuthi, lamps were set on a plantain stem and the family gathered around them, confessing Christ as the light. In rakkuli, families bathed by night in river or pond to recall the Jordan. Both customs teach the same thing as the readings: the Light has risen, and we belong to him through water. At the Holy Qurbana today, we who were baptized into the Son hear the Father’s word again, and we may hear it as said over us too.
